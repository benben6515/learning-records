Autonomy Levels L1 → L2 → L3

Report-only first, then assisted, then unattended — the logic of phased rollout, and why you never skip L1.

Phase B (reliability) covered the parts and the two pillars. Phase C is about "operating, risk, and patterns." This lesson tackles the central operating question first: how much should you let go? The answer is a phased ladder — and it's not a one-way ratchet; when things go wrong you demote.

Autonomy isn't "higher is better" — it's "prove it, then promote." Never skip L1 for a new pattern on a production repo.

Four levels, mapped to checklist sections

The design checklist splits readiness into four levels, each gated by which checklist sections pass:

LevelWhat it doesRequires
L0 — DraftDocumented intent only§1
L1 — Reporttriage → state, no auto-action§1–3, §5
L2 — AssistedSmall auto-fixes with a verifier§1–7
L3 — UnattendedRuns without you watchingAll sections

The upgrade path: prove it, then take the next step

The operating-loops upgrade path gives each step a precondition:

L1 report-only→ stable triage 1–2 weeks
L2 small auto-wins+ verifier + worktree + max attempts
L2+ connectors→ PRs / tickets updated automatically
L3 unattended+ denylist + budget + metrics + human gate
Demote when it breaks — this is not a one-way ratchet.

L1 → L2 is the most consequential step

On this ladder, the L1 → L2 crossing is the most consequential — because it's the turn where the loop starts acting instead of just reporting. Once it edits files and opens PRs, the risk profile changes. So this step has the hardest preconditions: a verifier, worktree isolation, and a "max attempts" cap.

Why report-only first

"Daily Triage at L1, report only" is the docs' first-loop recommendation, for two reasons:

  1. It teaches discipline, risk-free — it forces you to build the read/write/prune state muscle without auto-merge's destructive power. "Low risk, excellent pair."
  2. See the value before acting — in that report-only week you'll see: stale PRs, flaky tests, a chronically red main. Prove it "reads accurately" first, then promote it to fix.
Not a one-way ratchet Autonomy can and should be demoted. A production incident in progress (the loop may fight a hotfix), a breaking schema migration underway, or a key reviewer OOO while auto-merge was on (don't) — these are all signals to drop back to report-only.

loop-audit mechanically blocks L3

"Can I let go" isn't just your call. loop-audit mechanically blocks L3 until three cost signals exist: loop-budget.md, loop-run-log.md, and a budget section in LOOP.md. In other words, without budget and observability in place, the system won't let you reach unattended — that's an enforced guardrail, not advice.

One sentence to take away

Autonomy is a ladder of "prove it then promote, demote when it breaks." The biggest jump is L1→L2 (the loop starts acting); and L3 isn't a goal — it's a destination you're allowed to reach only once budget, metrics, and gates are in place. The next lesson covers the risks this ladder amplifies.

Ask your teacher "Roughly what level am I at when I use agents today?" "I want to try L2 — which preconditions (verifier, worktree, caps) am I missing?" "What kinds of work should just stay at L1 report-only?" Anything unclear is fair game.
