Wayfinder: Fog & Decision Tickets

The on-ramp for foggy, multi-session efforts. It plans; it doesn’t build.

An on-ramp for the hardest cases: an effort too big for one agent session, wrapped in fog, where the way from here to the destination isn't visible yet. /wayfinder plans, it doesn't build.

When to reach for it — and when not

  • Yes — greenfield project, huge feature build, too big to hold in one session, route still foggy. You can feel the shape of the work but can't yet write it down as a spec or plan.
  • No — a well-scoped feature. Where /grill-with-docs sharpens an idea you can hold in one session, wayfinder is for the idea you can't. It's slower and denser — save it for exactly that.

The map is an index, fog is the frontier

The map is a single wayfinder:map issue whose tickets are its child issues — one shared URL the whole team can watch. It's an index, not a store: each decision lives in exactly one place (its ticket); the map only gists and links, never restates.

Beyond the live tickets lies the fog of war — decisions you can tell are coming but can't yet pin down. The test for whether something is a ticket or still fog: can you state the question precisely now, not whether you can answer it.

Resolving a ticket clears the fog ahead of it, graduating whatever's now specifiable into fresh tickets. The frontier is the open, unblocked, unclaimed tickets — the edge of the known. Fog only gathers toward the destination; work past it is ruled out of scope, closed, never graduating.

Decisions, not deliverables

Every ticket resolves a decision — a question to settle, not a slice of a build to execute. The map is done when nothing is left to decide before someone goes and builds the thing. That's the defining move: wayfinder produces decisions, not deliverables.

HITL vs AFK

Every ticket is HITL (human in the loop — grilling, prototype) or AFK (agent alone — research). A HITL ticket only resolves through a live exchange, so the agent never answers its own questions. Research stays a real ticket — a shared blocker downstream decisions hang on — but because it's AFK, a session fires a /research subagent to burn it down in parallel, keeping the frontier fast.

It hands off, it doesn't build

The handoff rule When the map clears, wayfinder hands off to /to-spec, which collapses the map's linked decisions into a buildable plan — then /to-tickets and /implement as usual. Looping the map straight into /implement skips that collapse and throws the linked detail away.

If the opening grill surfaces no fog, it stops and tells you the journey is small enough to skip the map — go straight to /to-spec or even /implement if it turned out genuinely small.

One session resolves at most one ticket

(Research tickets excepted.) It records the answer as a resolution comment, closes the ticket, and appends a one-line pointer to Decisions so far. Naming the destination is the first act, before any ticket exists — because it fixes the scope every ticket is measured against.
