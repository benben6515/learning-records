The Full Routing Drill

Five scenarios. Route each to the right skill(s), in order.

Five realistic scenarios. For each, pick the skill(s) to reach for and the order. This is the whole course compressed into one drill — get all five and the mission's success looks like is met.

Scenario 1

You have a codebase. A feature request is fuzzy in your head — you can feel the shape but three or four decisions are unresolved, and the project's vocabulary for it isn't settled yet. You want to build it.

Scenario 2

An intermittent test flakes about one in twenty CI runs. Nobody can reproduce it locally. It's been green for two days and just failed again.

Scenario 3

Your issue tracker has 30 unreviewed bug reports and external PRs from contributors. Two are duplicates, one is already fixed, and several lack reproduction steps.

Scenario 4

You're starting a greenfield project that will take months. You can feel the shape but cannot yet write it down as a spec — there are too many unknowns, and the route to the destination is foggy.

Scenario 5

You're mid-grilling a feature and the session is at ~110k tokens — still sharp, but you're only halfway through the decision tree and approaching the smart zone. You don't want to lose the thread.

Bonus — the two-axis review

You've finished the course

Fifteen lessons covering all 25 promoted skills (Engineering + Productivity; Misc stays un-promoted). If you routed all five scenarios correctly, you can reach for the right skill at the right moment — the mission is met.

The two things to keep doing:

  • Revisit the reference docs — Skill Map, Main Flow, Four Failure Modes, Glossary. They are the compressed essence; lessons fade, references stay.
  • Type /ask-matt in the real moment — the router is the cure for cognitive load. You don't have to hold all 22 skills in your head; the router does.
