Solo Speaking Techniques

When you have no conversation partner — which is most days — these four techniques let you still train your mouth, your rhythm, and your courage. Keep this sheet open while you practise.

1 · Shadowing

The daily workhorse. Best for: pronunciation, rhythm, real-time processing.

  1. Pick a 1–3 min clip you understand (podcast, video, TED-Ed).
  2. Listen once, fully. Check at most 2–3 unknown words.
  3. Shadow with transcript ×2: read along, speak with the voice.
  4. Shadow without transcript ×1–2: eyes up, echo the sounds & music.

Do NOT pause the audio — that's what makes it shadowing. Lag a half-second behind if you must.

2 · Self-talk (narration)

Best for: the "think in English" reflex, vocabulary recall, confidence.

  1. Describe what you're doing, out loud, in real time: "I'm opening my laptop… now I'm checking the build…"
  2. When a word won't come, say it in another way (circumlocution) — don't switch to Chinese.
  3. Aim for 60–90 seconds unbroken. Build to 3 minutes.

Do it while cooking, commuting, or waiting. It's free reps with zero fear.

3 · The 4/3/2 fluency sprint

Best for: fluency on a specific topic. Classic, research-backed.

  1. Pick a familiar topic. Talk about it for 4 minutes.
  2. Same topic, different (imaginary) listener: 3 minutes.
  3. Again, a third listener: 2 minutes.

Same content, shrinking time → forces you to compress and speed up. Record it; you'll hear yourself getting smoother.

4 · Record & shadow yourself

Best for: noticing your own errors — the feedback loop solo learners lack.

  1. Record 1 minute of free speech on your phone.
  2. Listen back the next day. Mark 2 things to fix (a sound, a hesitation).
  3. Re-record the same 1 minute applying the fixes.

Your ears are sharper than your mouth thinks. Distance in time makes errors obvious.
