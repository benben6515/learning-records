Read Faster by Reading Smarter

Reading speed is not about moving your eyes faster — it's about recognising instead of decoding. Keep this sheet beside you when you read.

Why you're slow (3 real reasons)

  1. Decoding, not recognising. Every word you only half-know is a speed bump — your brain spells it out instead of knowing it instantly.
  2. Translating to your first language. English in → Chinese → meaning is two trips per sentence. Fluent readers go English → meaning directly.
  3. Reading word-by-word. Your eyes can grab 2–4 words per stop. If you stop at every single word, you read 2–4× slower than you could.

The fix: three habits

  • Read in chunks. Let your eyes land on phrase groups ("when you type / a web address") not single words.
  • Tolerate gaps. Don't stop for every unknown word — guess from context, keep moving.
  • Build sight vocabulary. The more words you recognise instantly, the faster you read. Nation (2006): you need ~98% of words known to read comfortably without a dictionary.

The drill: Repeated Reading (3 passes)

  1. Pick a short passage (~200 words) you understand ~95% of.
  2. Pass 1: read for meaning. Don't stop. Time it.
  3. Pass 2: read the same passage again. Time it.
  4. Pass 3: read once more for flow. Time it.

Your words-per-minute jumps between passes — that jump is what automaticity feels like. Practise on one short passage a few times a week and your first-pass speed climbs over months.

Two rules that protect comprehension

  • The 3× rule. Only look up a word if it appears 3+ times or blocks the whole meaning. Otherwise keep moving.
  • Read at the right level. If you're looking up words constantly, the text is too hard — drop to an easier one. Easy reading builds speed; hard reading builds frustration.
