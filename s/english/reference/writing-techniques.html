Solo Writing Techniques

Writing is the one output skill you can capture, send, and get corrected — so it gives you the tightest feedback loop of all. Keep this sheet beside you when you write.

1 · Free-writing sprint

The daily workhorse. Best for: the "think in English on the page" reflex, fluency, confidence.

  1. Pick one prompt you already have thoughts on.
  2. Set a timer: 5 min (3 on a busy day).
  3. Write without stopping — no editing, no translating, no dictionary. If you stall, write "I'm stuck" until something comes. Lacking a word? Write around it.
  4. Send the raw draft for 2–3 marks, not 20.

Do NOT edit while you write. Producing and editing are two brain modes that sabotage each other. An ugly first draft is the goal.

2 · The read-aloud revision

Best for: catching what reads like translation. The single highest-value edit.

  1. After the sprint (a separate pass), read your draft out loud.
  2. Mark every spot you stumble or run out of breath.
  3. Rewrite only those — usually a sentence is too long, or a verb is weak.

Your mouth finds errors your eyes miss. If you can't say it smoothly, a reader can't read it smoothly.

3 · Sentence combining

Best for: lifting choppy, short-sentence writing into flowing prose.

  1. Take three short sentences you wrote: "It was late. I was tired. I kept coding."
  2. Combine into one, keeping the strongest idea main: "Even though I was tired, I kept coding late into the night."
  3. Do this to 2–3 choppy spots per draft.

Short sentences are fine in bursts; a diet of only short ones feels like a child writing. Combine for flow.

Why you freeze (3 real reasons)

  • Translating from your first language. Thought → Chinese → English → page is two trips. Fluent writers go thought → English → page.
  • The inner editor. Stopping mid-sentence to fix grammar breaks the chain. Editing belongs in a separate pass.
  • Dictionary fear. Reaching for a translation app kills flow. Write around the gap; look it up later.

The two rules of revision

  • Separate the modes. Generate first (messy), then revise (clean). Never both at once.
  • Cut needless words; prefer strong verbs. Strunk & White: "omit needless words." Replace make a decision → decide; is running fast → sprints.
