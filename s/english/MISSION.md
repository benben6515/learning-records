# Mission: Level Up English (B1–B2 → solid C1)

## Why
The goal is for English to stop being a friction and start being a tool — to make **productive English** (speaking and writing) feel natural and automatic. The learner already uses English heavily for front-end web development, but everyday spoken and written English still feels effortful. Four forces pull in the same direction: career growth, formal study, general personal growth, and major life transitions that often carry hard language requirements.

## Success looks like
Concrete, observable markers, calibrated to the official **CEFR C1** descriptors (Council of Europe). These are the north star; progress is measured against them.

**Speaking (the priority):**
- Talk for 5+ minutes straight on a familiar topic *without freezing* or silently translating from the first language.
- Hold a spontaneous work conversation (e.g., a 1:1 or standup) without rehearsing sentences in advance.
- Sound natural — control of stress, rhythm, and intonation — not just "correct".

**Writing:**
- Write a clear 150–250 word email / message / post in English *without a dictionary*, in one sitting.
- Express and defend an opinion in writing with structure and precision.

**Confidence (the meta-goal):**
- Reach for English first — thinking, drafting notes, and talking to oneself in English — without the reflex to switch to the first language.
- Feel calm, not anxious, when asked to speak English on the spot.

## Constraints
- **Time: ~15 minutes/day.** This is the binding constraint and it is non-negotiable for design. Every lesson and habit must fit a micro-session. (The science favours this anyway: small daily doses beat long cram sessions.)
- **Solo-first environment.** The learner is not currently immersed in English; the daily default is still the first language. Habits must work alone, then scale to community/tutor later.
- **Front-end dev context is a strength to exploit**, not a gap. Technical reading/listening is strong; we lean on it for input material.
- **Budget:** open to apps / online courses; tutor budget to be confirmed. Default to free + low-cost until proven needed.

## Out of scope (for now)
- Exam-specific drilling (IELTS/TOEFL) — *unless* study or a life transition reveals a required score. Revisit then.
- Academic / formal writing (essays, papers) beyond what career + study need.
- Reading and listening as *primary* targets — they are already strong and will be used as input fuel, not drilled.
- Accent elimination. The goal is *clear, natural, confident* speech — not passing as a native speaker.

## Working level definition (from CEFR, for reference)
- **B2 (current):** Can interact with a degree of fluency; can write clear detailed text on familiar subjects; can explain a viewpoint.
- **C1 (target):** Can express themselves *fluently and spontaneously*; can use language *flexibly and effectively for social and professional purposes*; can produce *clear, well-structured, detailed* text on complex subjects.

The B2→C1 gap is mostly **fluency, breadth/precision of vocabulary, and idiomaticity in production** — not new grammar rules.
