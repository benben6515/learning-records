# Shadowing attempted; connected speech (連音) is the main difficulty

Shadowing has been tried before, and **連音 (connected speech / liaison)** is reported as the hardest part. High-value signal: it names a specific, predictable stumbling block and confirms real engagement with the technique.

### Why it's hard (L1 = a Chinese variety)
Chinese is **syllable-timed** — each character is a discrete, roughly equal syllable. English is **stress-timed** — content words are stressed, function words shrink into weak forms (schwa), and neighbouring words **link and blur** ("want to" → "wanna", "did you" → "didja", consonant-to-vowel linking, elision). This rhythm mismatch is one of the biggest gaps for Chinese-L1 learners in both listening and speaking.

### Implications
- Shadowing is the correct cure for 連音 — the learner is on the right track; discouraging abandoning it.
- Near-term tweak: **start with slower, clearer audio** (and the transcript) so the links are audible before chasing natural-speed blur.
- Queue a dedicated **Lesson 04 — Connected speech / 連音 ear-training**: explicit practice on linking, weak forms, and reductions, with minimal-pair / listen-and-shadow drills.
- This connects to the reading translation habit (LR-0002): both stem from treating English as isolated units rather than connected flow.
