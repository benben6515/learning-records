The Writing Sprint: Your Daily Writing Workout

~10 min total 5 read · 5 write Solo · Free

The method is one rule — keep your fingers moving (Lesson 04). This is the daily workout that installs it: a timed sprint with a prompt, a live word count, and a send-it-to-me step. That last step makes it the tightest feedback loop of all your skills, and the fastest path to the Mission goal: a clean 150–250 word message, written in one sitting, without a dictionary.

The method — 4 steps

  1. Pick a prompt (one sentence). Use mine below, or invent your own: a standup update, your reaction to a dev article, what you did yesterday. Anything you already have thoughts on.
  2. Set a timer (5 min). Five is the default. Drop to 3 on a busy day; never zero.
  3. Write without stopping. No editing, no translating, no dictionary. If you stall, write "I'm stuck, I'm stuck…" until something comes — but keep moving. Lacking a word? Write around it ("the thing that…", "kind of…").
  4. Send it to me. Paste your draft in our chat. I'll mark 2–3 things to sharpen — never 20. That correction is the loop: produce → feedback → produce again tomorrow.

Total: about 10 minutes, read included. Then you're done for the day.

Step 2 (later): revise — but not now Resist the urge to fix your draft the moment the timer stops. Revising is a different brain mode from generating, and they trip each other up. Better: paste the raw draft to me, see my 2–3 marks, and then rewrite the weak spots. Over time you'll borrow my fixes until they become yours.
