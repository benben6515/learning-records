Lesson 01 · Speaking

Shadowing: Your Daily Speaking Workout

~10 min total 5 read · 5 practise Solo · Free

You told me your weak spots: speaking, writing, and confidence. Here's the good news hiding inside that: all three are output skills, and they share one cure — producing English every day, even badly. Confidence isn't a personality trait you wait for. It's the residue of repetition.

The jump from B2 to C1 isn't about more grammar rules — you already know most of them. It's about fluency: speaking without first translating from Chinese in your head. And the single best solo habit for that — one you can do in 15 minutes, alone, for free — is called shadowing.

What is shadowing?

Shadowing means listening to an English audio and repeating it out loud at the same time, like an echo chasing the original voice. You do not pause the audio — you speak over it.

Why it works
  • Trains your mouth muscles. English uses sounds Chinese doesn't (the "th", long vs short vowels). Shadowing builds the muscle memory to make them.
  • Trains prosody & rhythm — the "music" of English: which words get stressed, how pitch rises and falls. This is what makes you sound fluent, not just correct.
  • Forces real-time processing. You can't translate word-by-word if you must keep up. This builds the "think in English" reflex.
  • Builds confidence, safely. You're alone, so there's no fear of mistakes — every rep is a tiny win.

It's not folklore: a study at National Taiwan University found shadowing significantly improved students' intonation, fluency, and pronunciation (Hsieh, Dong & Wang, 2013). Read the method write-up →

The method — 4 steps

  1. Pick a short clip (1–3 min). Something you understand and enjoy. For you this is easy: a snippet from a front-end dev podcast/video, a TED-Ed animation, or any podcast you like. Make sure it has a transcript or subtitles.
  2. Listen once, fully. Get the meaning. Look up at most 2–3 key words — no more, or you'll run out of time.
  3. Shadow with the transcript (×2). Play the audio, read along, and speak with the voice. Match their speed and rhythm. Lagging a half-second behind is fine.
  4. Shadow without the transcript (×1–2). Eyes up. Just listen and echo. Focus on the sounds and music, not the meaning.

Total: about 10–15 minutes. Then you're done for the day.

The 5-minute rule If 15 minutes feels like too much today, do 5. Five minutes every day will beat two hours every Sunday, every single time. Consistency is the whole game.
Do it now — your first win

Your mission is speaking + confidence. So let's speak. Right now, do your first shadowing session. Tick each step as you finish it (your progress is saved on this device).

  • Listen to it once, fully. (Max 2–3 new words.)
  • Shadow with the transcript — two times.
