# English Level-Up Resources

Grounded in the official CEFR framework and the science of second-language acquisition (input + output hypotheses). Knowledge is drawn from these, not from guesses.

## Knowledge

- [CEFR self-assessment grid — Council of Europe](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/level-descriptions)
  The authoritative source for what B2 vs C1 "can do". Use for: defining success criteria and self-rating progress. The MISSION success markers are calibrated to it.
- [Shadowing: The Technique That Transforms Your English Speaking — Leonardo English](https://www.leonardoenglish.com/blog/what-is-shadowing)
  Clearest free write-up of the shadowing method, with citations to the original research (Argüelles; Hamada; Hsieh et al.). Use for: the daily solo speaking habit in Lesson 01.
- [Language Shadowing: A Superior Learning Method — Mezzoguild](https://www.mezzoguild.com/language-shadowing-a-superior-learning-method/)
  Explains Alexander Argüelles' original "shadowing while walking" method in depth. Use for: refining technique once the basic habit is established.
- [Comprehensible Output Hypothesis — Swain (overview)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comprehensible_output)
  The theory that *producing* language (not just hearing it) is what forces learners to notice gaps and grow. Use for: why this approach prioritises speaking + writing output, not more input.
- [_English Grammar in Use_ — Raymond Murphy (Cambridge)](https://www.cambridge.org/gb/cambridgeenglish/catalog/grammar-vocabulary-and-pronunciation/english-grammar-use-5th-edition/)
  The standard self-study grammar reference for B1–C1. Use for: targeted grammar lookups when a lesson surfaces a specific gap (e.g., present perfect vs past). Not for front-to-back reading.
- [_The Elements of Style_ — Strunk & White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Elements_of_Style)
  Short, opinionated guide to clear English writing. Use for: writing lessons — trimming wordy sentences, choosing strong verbs. Complements the writing sprint habit.

## Wisdom (Communities)

- [r/EnglishLearning](https://www.reddit.com/r/EnglishLearning/) — active, moderated subreddit. Use for: getting writing/speaking corrected by natives, asking "does this sound natural?", daily practice threads.
- [iTalki](https://www.italki.com/) — 1:1 video tutors from ~US$10/hr. Use for: real-time speaking practice and confidence reps once solo habits are solid. (Budget to confirm.)
- [Tandem](https://www.tandem.net/) / [HelloTalk](https://www.hellotalk.com/) — free language-exchange apps to find native English speakers who want to learn your first language. Use for: casual conversation reps at zero cost.
- **Tech-in-English practice (ties career + language):** contributing to open-source front-end projects, and lurking/posting in English-speaking dev communities (e.g., the React/JS Discord servers, r/Frontend). Use for: practicing English *inside* a domain the learner already dominates — low fear, high relevance.

## Gaps
- **Speaking feedback loop at home:** shadowing is solo but gives no *external* correction. A tutor (iTalki) or community (Tandem) is needed for real conversational feedback once habits are established. Flag for ~Lesson 04+.
- **Pronunciation diagnosis:** no resource yet for pinpointing a specific first-language→English interference (likely: /θ/ vs /s/, vowel length, final consonants, stress-timed rhythm). To source when relevant.

## Preferences
- The learner has not opted out of communities — open to them. Default to recommending low-friction ones (Reddit, Tandem) before paid tutors.
