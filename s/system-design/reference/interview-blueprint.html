The Interview Blueprint

The whole method in one card: the 45-minute time budget, the four steps with the questions each one must answer, the interviewer's scorecard, and the page index into the ByteByteGo archive. Compressed from the System Design Primer approach and archive pp.162/134. Print me, then rehearse from me.

The clock

Requirements — 5′→ Estimation — 5′→ High-level — 15′→ Deep dives — 20′
Roughly. Move minutes between steps deliberately, never silently.

The four steps — and the question each must answer

StepYou are answering…Say it like this
1 · Requirements"What are we building, for whom, at what scale?""Two features: __ and __. Assume __ DAU, read-heavy — correct me."
2 · Estimation"How big is it, to an order of magnitude?""10M DAU ≈ 100 QPS write, 10K read — so __."
3 · High-level"What are the boxes, and how does one request flow?""Walk with me: a user does __, which hits __, then __."
4 · Deep dives"What does this box cost, and what breaks first?""Here I'd pick __ over __ — buying __ at the price of __."

Requirements checklist (step 1 prompts)

  • Functional (pick 2–3, defer the rest aloud): what does the user do? Which feature is the interview really about?
  • Non-functional: scale (DAU), read:write ratio, latency target, consistency needs (can a follower serve this?), availability bar.
  • Assumptions to voice: traffic geography, growth rate, what's explicitly out of scope ("no billing — agreed?").

The interviewer's scorecard

SignalLooks likeFails like
Scopingasks, then states assumptionsstarts drawing at minute one

Data flowone request walked end-to-endstatic boxes, no story through them
Trade-off fluencyevery choice names its cost"we'd use Kafka" with no why
Depth on demandzooms into one box cleanlyrepeats the overview, louder

The one sentence worth memorizing

The trade-off shape"I'd choose X over Y here, because I'm buying this property at the price of that property — and in this problem, that price is worth paying." Every box you draw should be sayable in this shape.
