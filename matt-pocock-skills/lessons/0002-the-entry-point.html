The Entry Point: grill-with-docs

Every spec-driven journey starts here. It's three skills, nested.

In Lesson 1 you saw the map: the main flow starts with grill-with-docs. Now zoom in. When you bring a fuzzy project idea — "I want to rethink the onboarding flow" — this is the skill that sharpens it into something you can actually spec. The surprise: it isn't one skill. It's three, nested like Russian dolls.

The one idea: a composition

At the centre is a single primitive. Two wrappers sit around it, identical in how they interview you, different only in what they leave behind:

grill-with-docswith a codebase · writes CONTEXT.md + ADRs
grill-meno codebase · no paper trail
grilling — the primitivethe interview itself
one question at a time · looks up facts itself · decisions are yours · won't act until you both agree

Read it inside-out. grilling is the relentless interview. grill-me is that interview with no memory. grill-with-docs is that interview plus domain-modeling — so every term you settle and every decision you reach gets written to CONTEXT.md and (rarely) an ADR as you go. Same questions, different aftermath.

Which wrapper do I reach for? Have a codebase? grill-with-docs — always. The paper trail it leaves is exactly what to-spec, implement, and improve-codebase-architecture will read later. No codebase yet (greenfield idea, no repo)? grill-me — same sharpening, nothing to write to.

The four rules of a good grilling

These come straight from the grilling skill. They set your expectations — and tell you when a session is going off the rails:

  1. One question at a time. It waits for your answer before asking the next. A burst of five questions at once is a bug, not a feature — it's "bewildering" by design.
  2. It looks up facts; you own decisions. If something can be found by reading the filesystem or code, it should look it up rather than ask you. Only genuine decisions (trade-offs only you can make) get put to you.
  3. Every question comes with a recommended answer. You're never staring at a blank — you're approving, rejecting, or adjusting a suggestion.
  4. It will not act until you confirm shared understanding. Grilling produces alignment, not code. Nothing gets built in this stage.
Facts get looked up. Decisions get put to you. Nothing happens until you both agree.

Why this is the entry point — and why the window must stay whole

Recall Lesson 1's context-hygiene rule. Now you can see why it exists: grill-with-docs is where the shared thinking and the first glossary entries accumulate. If you compact or clear the conversation here, you throw away the very state the rest of the flow depends on. The unbroken window isn't a superstition — it's protecting the output of this stage.

Your tangible win — say these out loud

  1. Name the three nested skills, inside-out, and what each leaves behind.
  2. State the four rules of a good grilling (the one-question-at-a-time rule is the one people forget).
  3. Finish this sentence: "grill-with-docs is the stateful choice because ______."

If you can, you understand the entry point. Notice we haven't touched the rules of CONTEXT.md yet — that's deliberate. This lesson is about the interview; the next is about the glossary it writes.
