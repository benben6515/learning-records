THE RUIN OF KULLERVO

庫　勒　沃　的　毀　滅

THE WRONGED SLAVE · THE BROKEN KNIFE · INCEST · THE SWORD'S QUESTION

Into the Kalevala, between the glory of the Sampo and the coming of the new faith, the poem inserts its darkest story — the tragedy of Kullervo, a boy born of a massacred family, raised a slave, tormented by a stone baked into his bread, whose only inheritance from his father — a knife — snaps on that stone. He takes a terrible revenge, flees, seduces a maiden on the road who turns out to be his sister, and finally asks his own sword what blood it would drink. The sword answers. This is the wound at the heart of the Finnish epic. 在《卡勒瓦拉》之中，於桑波的榮光與新信仰的到來之間，這首詞插入了它最黑暗的故事——庫勒沃的悲劇，一個生於被屠滅家族的男孩，被養為奴隸，被人以烘進麵包的石頭所折磨，他唯一得自父親的遺產——一把刀——在那塊石頭上折斷。他展開可怖的報復，逃亡，在路上勾引了一個少女，那少女竟是他的妹妹，最後他問他自己的劍，它願飲何人之血。劍回答了。這是芬蘭史詩心臟裡的那道傷。

Chapter 1 · 第一章

The Wronged Birth

受虧負之生

Kullervo's story begins in massacre. His uncle, Untamo, quarreled with Kullervo's father Kalervo — a family feud, the poem tells us, over nothing worth the killing — and Untamo, in his rage, attacked his brother's household and destroyed it. He killed Kalervo. He killed Kalervo's wife — almost. He slaughtered the servants, burned the hall, left the family in ruins. And from the wreckage he took one thing: the unborn child in Kalervo's wife's womb, who was born into captivity, Untamo's slave from the moment he drew breath.

庫勒沃的故事始於屠殺。他的叔叔溫塔莫，與庫勒沃的父親卡勒沃起了爭執——一場家族宿怨，這首詞告訴我們，為了一件不值得殺戮之事——而溫塔莫，在他的狂怒之中，攻擊了他哥哥的家，毀了它。他殺了卡勒沃。他殺了卡勒沃的妻子——幾乎。他屠殺了僕人，燒了大廳，使那個家族化為廢墟。而從那片廢墟中他帶走了一樣東西：卡勒沃妻子腹中那個未生的孩子，他生於囚禁之中，從他呼吸的第一刻起便是溫塔莫的奴隸。

And the boy, Kullervo, was born wrong — or rather, born into wrong. He had, the poem tells us, a strength that was almost monstrous: as an infant he broke his cradle; as a child he could not be controlled. And Untamo, who had murdered his family and enslaved him, tried to break him — set him to impossible tasks, beat him, starved him, tried in every way to crush the spirit of a boy whose only crime was having survived the massacre of his house. And the spirit would not break. Kullervo grew up full of a rage so deep it had no bottom — the rage of a child born into a wrong he did not commit, raised by the very man who had committed it, with no memory of any other life.

而那個男孩，庫勒沃，生來便是錯的——或者說，生於錯之中。他有，這首詞告訴我們，一種幾乎是怪獸般的力量：作為嬰孩他打破了搖籃；作為孩子他無法被控制。而溫塔莫，那個屠殺了他家族、奴役了他的人，試圖擊垮他——給他設下不可能的役使，打他，餓他，用盡一切方法要去壓碎一個男孩的精神，那個男孩唯一的罪便是活過了他家族的屠殺。而那精神不肯被壓碎。庫勒沃長大，滿懷一種深到無底的怒——一個生於他未曾犯下之錯的孩子之怒，由那個犯下那錯之人所養，對任何其他生命毫無記憶。

This is the wound that defines Kullervo, and the Kalevala gives it to us without flinching. He is not a villain. He is a victim who became dangerous. The massacre made him; the slavery shaped him; and the rage, which had nowhere else to go, turned inward and outward at once — into a hatred of his enemies and a hatred of himself, into a violence that could not distinguish between the two. The poem does not excuse what Kullervo will do. But it insists that we understand where it came from. He is not evil. He is what evil, done to a child, produces. And his whole story is the unfolding of that production — the slow, inevitable working-out of a wrong that began before he was born.

這便是定義庫勒沃的傷，而《卡勒瓦拉》毫不退縮地把它給了我們。他不是反派。他是一個變得危險的受害者。那場屠殺造就了他；奴役塑造了他；而那怒，無處可去，同時向內與向外——變成對他仇敵的恨與對他自己的恨，變成一種無法分辨兩者的暴力。這首詞不為庫勒沃將要做之事開脫。但它堅持我們要明白它從何而來。他不是邪惡。他是「對一個孩子所做的邪惡」所生產之物。而他整個故事，便是那個生產的展開——一個在他出生之前便已開始的錯，那緩慢而無可避免的開展。

Chapter 2 · 第二章

The Slave & the Impossible Tasks

奴隸與不可能之役

Untamo, unable to break Kullervo, sold him. He sold the boy to Ilmarinen the smith — the same Ilmarinen who had forged the Sampo — and Ilmarinen's wife took the slave into her household and set him to work. And the work, from the first, went wrong, because Kullervo's rage made him incapable of doing anything the way he was told. Set to clear a field, he cut down the crops and left the weeds. Set to fence a pasture, he built the fence inside out, trapping the cattle. Set to herd, he lost the animals. Every task failed, and every failure was, the poem knows, a small act of resistance — the slave's only weapon, sabotage.

溫塔莫，無法擊垮庫勒沃，把他賣了。他把那個男孩賣給了鐵匠伊爾瑪利寧——那個曾鍛造桑波的伊爾瑪利寧——而伊爾瑪利寧的妻子把那個奴隸納入她的家中，派他去工作。而那工作，從一開始，便出錯，因為庫勒沃的怒使他無法以被告知的方式做任何事。被派去清理一塊田，他砍倒了作物，留下了雜草。被派去圍一塊牧場，他把圍籬裡外建反了，困住了牲畜。被派去放牧，他弄丟了動物。每一項役使都失敗了，而每一場失敗，這首詞知道，都是一個小小的抵抗之舉——那個奴隸唯一的武器，破壞。

And Ilmarinen's wife, who was a harsh woman, took out her frustration on the slave. She cursed him, she underfed him, and — in the act that will define the next chapter — she baked a stone into his bread. But before that, the poem gives us a detail that breaks the heart. Kullervo, the poem tells us, had one possession in the world — one thing that was truly his, the only inheritance from his murdered father: a knife, a beautiful knife, the kind a man gives his son. And the boy carried that knife everywhere. It was the only link he had to the family he had never known. It was, in a life of total dispossession, the one object that made him someone's son.

而伊爾瑪利寧的妻子，一個嚴苛的女子，把她的挫折發洩在那個奴隸身上。她咒罵他，她少餵他，而——在那個將定義下一章的舉動裡——她在他的麵包裡烘了一塊石頭。但在那之前，這首詞給了我們一個令人心碎的細節。庫勒沃，這首詞告訴我們，在世上有一样財產——一样真正屬於他的東西，唯一得自他被謀殺之父親的遺產：一把刀，一把美麗的刀，那種一個男人給他兒子的刀。而那個男孩把那把刀帶到任何地方。那是他與他從未認識的家族之間唯一的連結。那是在一個被完全剝奪的生命裡，那個唯一的物件，使他成為某個人的兒子。

The knife matters. It is not a weapon, in this part of the story — it is a relic, a remnant, the only proof Kullervo has that he came from somewhere, that he was loved once, that there was a father who meant for him to have something. The whole tragedy of Kullervo, the poem is telling us, turns on this small object — on the one fragile thread that connects a massacred boy to a murdered father. And in the next chapter, that thread will snap. The stone in the bread will break the knife. And the breaking of the knife will be the breaking of Kullervo's last restraint.

那把刀很重要。在故事的這個部分，它不是一件武器——它是一件遺物、一個殘餘，是庫勒沃所擁有的唯一證據，證明他來自某處，他曾被愛過，曾有一個父親有意要給他某物。庫勒沃的整場悲劇，這首詞告訴我們，便繞著這個小物件而轉——繞著那條連接一個被屠殺的男孩與一個被謀殺的父親的、唯一的脆弱之線。而在下一章，那條線將斷。麵包裡的石頭將折斷那把刀。而那把刀的折斷，將是庫勒沃最後一道束縛的折斷。

Chapter 3 · 第三章

The Stone in the Bread

麵包中的石頭

And so the wife baked a stone into Kullervo's bread. She hated the slave who could do nothing right; she wanted to hurt him; and she knew, the poem tells us, that a man who has been starving all day, set to work in the cold, will bite hard into his bread when it is given to him. And so she took a stone — round, the size of a fist, smooth — and she baked it inside the loaf, and she handed the loaf to Kullervo as he went out to herd the cattle.

於是那位妻子在庫勒沃的麵包裡烘了一塊石頭。她恨那個什麼也做不好的奴隸；她想要傷害他；而她知道，這首詞告訴我們，一個整天挨餓、在寒冷中被派去工作的人，在他被給予麵包之時會狠狠地咬下去。於是她取了一塊石頭——圓的、拳頭大、光滑——她把它烘進那條麵包裡，而在庫勒沃出去放牛之時，她把那條麵包交給了他。

And out in the field, hungry, Kullervo sat down to eat. He took out his father's knife — the only thing that was his, the only link to the family he had lost — and he cut the bread. And the knife struck the stone. And the blade — the heirloom, the relic, the last gift of a murdered father to a son he would never see grow up — snapped in two. And the poem tells us that Kullervo looked at the broken knife, and something in him, the last restraint, the last thread connecting him to anything human, broke with it.

而在田裡，飢餓的庫勒沃坐下來吃飯。他取出他父親的刀——那件唯一屬於他的東西，那條與他所失去的家族之間唯一的連結——他切那條麵包。而那把刀撞上了石頭。而那個刀刃——那件傳家之寶、那件遺物、一位被謀殺之父給一個他永遠看不到長大的兒子的最後禮物——啪地斷成兩截。而這首詞告訴我們，庫勒沃望著那把斷了的刀，而他體內的某個東西，最後一道束縛，最後一條連接他與任何人之物的線，隨之斷了。

And his revenge was terrible. He drove the cattle — all of them, the entire herd — over a cliff, into a ravine, to their deaths. And he conjured wolves and bears, by his magic, to wait at the bottom, and they tore apart the cattle as they fell. And when the wife came out to see why her herd had not returned, Kullervo set the wolves and bears upon her, and they killed her too — tore her to pieces, the poem says, as her bread had torn his knife. It is the most violent scene in the Kalevala, and the poem does not soften it. The slave, pushed past his breaking point, took a revenge as total as the wrong done to him. The broken knife became a broken person. And the broken person became a killer. And then, knowing what he had done, Kullervo fled — into the forest, into exile, into the road that would lead him to the worst thing of all.

而他的報復是可怖的。他把那些牛——全部，整群——趕過一座懸崖，趕入一道溝壑，趕向死亡。而他以他的魔法召喚狼與熊，讓牠們在底下等著，牠們在牛落下之時將之撕碎。而當那位妻子出來看她的牛群為何未歸之時，庫勒沃把狼與熊放向她，而牠們也殺了她——把她撕成碎片，這首詞說，如同她的麵包撕了他的刀。這是《卡勒瓦拉》中最暴力的場面，而這首詞不軟化它。那個奴隸，被推過了他的斷裂點，採取了一場與加諸他之錯同等徹底的報復。那把斷了的刀成了一個斷了的人。而那個斷了的人成了一個殺人者。然後，知道了自己所做之事，庫勒沃逃了——逃入森林，逃入放逐，逃入那條將引他走向那最糟之事的路。

Chapter 4 · 第四章

The Maiden on the Road

路上的少女

Fleeing through the forest, Kullervo met a girl on the road — a young woman, traveling alone, and he stopped her, and he propositioned her. The poem gives us the encounter in cold detail: she refused him, at first; he offered her gold, then his magic, then his strength; she refused again; and finally he took her, by force or by the weight of his will, the versions vary in how much violence is done. And in the aftermath, talking, they began to tell each other who they were — their families, their origins.

逃亡穿過森林，庫勒沃在路上遇見一個女孩——一個年輕女子，獨行，而他攔住她，而向她求歡。這首詞以冷峻的細節給了我們這場相遇：她起初拒絕他；他向她獻金，繼而獻他的魔法，繼而獻他的力量；她又拒絕；而終於他得到了她，以力或以他意志之重，各個版本在做了多少暴力上有所不同。而在事後，談話著，他們開始告訴對方自己是誰——他們的家族、他們的出身。

And the girl, speaking, told him she was a daughter of the house of Kalervo — Kullervo's own father. She was his sister. The girl he had just violated was the sister he had never known he had — the one other survivor of the massacre, raised elsewhere, unknown to him, met by pure accident on a forest road. And the poem gives us, in the moment of recognition, one of its most devastating scenes. The girl, learning who Kullervo was, learning what she had done with her own brother, broke. She said, the poem tells us, that she would rather die than live with what had happened. And she ran from him, to the nearest water, and she drowned herself. The sister who had unknowingly slept with her brother chose the cold water, as Aino had chosen it, rather than live with the knowledge.

而那個女孩，說著話，告訴他她是卡勒沃家族之女——庫勒沃自己的父親。她是他的妹妹。那個他剛侵犯的女孩，是他從不知自己擁有的妹妹——那場屠殺的另一個倖存者，在別處被養大，他並不知曉，純粹出於意外在一條森林之路上相遇。而這首詞，在相認的那一刻，給了我們它最令人心碎的場面之一。那個女孩，得知庫勒沃是誰、得知她與她親哥哥做了什麼，崩潰了。她說，這首詞告訴我們，她寧願死也不願帶著所發生之事活下去。而她從他那裡跑開，跑到最近的水邊，而她淹死了自己。那個不知情地與哥哥同寢的妹妹，選擇了冷水，一如艾諾所選擇的，而不願帶著那份知曉活下去。

This is the wound that cannot heal, and the poem knows it. Kullervo did not know. The girl did not know. The incest was an accident of a massacre — two survivors of the same destroyed family, meeting on a road, neither aware of the other's existence. And yet the knowledge, once it came, was unbearable to both. The sister chose death. And Kullervo, left standing on the road with the body of his shame and his ignorance, was set on the path to his own end. The poem does not blame him, exactly, for the incest — he could not have known. But it knows that some acts, even unknowing, cannot be undone, and that the doer of them is ruined whether he meant to do them or not. The next chapter is the working-out of that ruin.

這是一道無法癒合的傷，而這首詞知道它。庫勒沃並不知情。那個女孩並不知情。那場亂倫是一場屠殺的意外——同一個被毀家族的兩個倖存者，在一條路上相遇，兩者皆不知對方的存在。然而那份知曉，一旦來到，對兩人都無法承受。妹妹選擇了死。而庫勒沃，帶著他的羞恥與無知之屍被留在路上站著，被推上了通往他自己終局的路。這首詞，並不確切地為那場亂倫責怪他——他不可能知道。但它知道，有些作為，即便不知，也無法被撤銷，而其作為者，無論是否有意，都已毀了。下一章，便是那場毀滅的開展。

Chapter 5 · 第五章

The Sword's Question

劍之問

And Kullervo, broken by everything — the massacre of his family, the slavery, the broken knife, the killing of the wife, the death of his sister — went to war. He went back to Untamo, the uncle who had destroyed his house, and he killed him. He killed every last member of Untamo's household. He took the full revenge he had been born wanting. And the poem tells us that it gave him no peace. The revenge was complete, and the rage was still there, because the rage had never really been about Untamo — it had been about being born into a wrong, and no amount of killing could undo a birth.

而庫勒沃，被一切所擊碎——他家族的屠殺、奴役、那把斷刀、那位妻子之死、他妹妹之死——去打仗了。他回到溫塔莫、那個毀了他家的叔叔那裡，而他殺了他。他殺了溫塔莫家中每一個最後的成員。他取了他生來便想要的全部報復。而這首詞告訴我們，那沒有給他安寧。報復完成了，而那怒仍在，因為那怒從來不真正是關於溫塔莫——它是關於生於一個錯之中，而再多的殺戮也無法撤銷一場誕生。

And he went home — to the family he had thought was lost, to seek if anyone was left. And the poem gives us, here, a small hope: he found his mother alive. His mother, who he had believed dead in the massacre, had survived. But she was old now, and the years had taken their toll. And he spoke to her, and she told him, one by one, that everyone else was gone — his father, his brothers, his other sister. And then, in the cruellest turn, the poem tells us that his mother too died, shortly after, leaving Kullervo utterly alone in the world, the last of his line, with no one left to love or to be loved by.

而他回家了——回到他以為已失去的家族，去尋找是否還有任何人留下。而這首詞，在此處，給了我們一個小小的希望：他發現他的母親還活著。他的母親，那個他以為在屠殺中已死的母親，活下來了。但她如今老了，而歲月已留下痕跡。而他對她說話，而她告訴他，一個一個地，其他人都已不在了——他的父親、他的兄弟們、他的另一個妹妹。然後，在最殘酷的轉折裡，這首詞告訴我們，他的母親也死了，不久之後，留下庫勒沃在世上徹底孤獨，他那血脈的最後一人，再也無人可愛、也無人愛他。

And Kullervo, alone, took his sword. And the poem gives us, in its closing of this tragedy, one of the most famous dialogues in literature: the warrior asking his weapon a question. "Tell me, O my sword," Kullervo said, "would you drink the blood of the guilty?" And the sword, understanding, answered that it would — that it had drunk the blood of the guilty before, and would drink it again. And Kullervo asked the second question, the one the sword had been dreading: "And would you drink the blood of the innocent — of one who has done no wrong, who was wronged from birth?" And the sword answered, yes. It would drink even that. And Kullervo fell upon his own blade. The warrior who could not undo his birth undid his life. And the poem tells us that the sword drank his blood, and was satisfied, and that the broken boy was broken no more. The Kalevala's darkest story ends. The new faith is coming.

而庫勒沃，獨自一人，取了他的劍。而這首詞，在它對這場悲劇的收束裡，給了我們文學中最著名的對話之一：那位武士向他的武器問一個問題。「告訴我，噢我的劍，」庫勒沃說，「你願飲有罪者之血嗎？」而那把劍，明白了，回答它願——它以前飲過有罪者之血，它會再飲。而庫勒沃問了第二個問題，那個劍一直所懼的問題：「那你願飲無辜者之血嗎——一個未曾作惡、卻生來受虧負之人之血？」而那把劍回答，願。它連那個也願飲。而庫勒沃倒在自己的劍刃之上。那位無法撤銷其誕生的武士，撤銷了他的命。而這首詞告訴我們，那把劍飲了他的血，滿足了，而那個破碎的男孩不再破碎了。《卡勒瓦拉》最黑暗的故事結束了。新信仰即將到來。

THE SWORD THAT WOULD DRINK ANY BLOOD

那把願飲任何血之劍

A boy was born into a massacre, and raised a slave, and given a stone in his bread, and his father's knife broke on the stone, and he drove the cattle off a cliff and set the wolves on the wife who had tormented him. He fled, and took a girl on the road, and she was his sister, and she drowned herself. He killed his uncle's whole house, and found his mother, and she died. And alone at last, he asked his sword whether it would drink the blood of one wronged from birth — and the sword said yes, and he fell on it, and the broken boy was broken no more. The wound at the heart of the Kalevala closes. The new faith approaches.
