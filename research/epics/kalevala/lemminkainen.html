LEMMINKÄINEN

勒　明　蓋　寧　之　死　與　復　生

THE RASH WARRIOR · THE RIVER OF TUONELA · THE MOTHER'S RAKE

Where Väinämöinen is wise and Ilmarinen is patient, the third hero of the Kalevala is none of these things. Lemminkäinen is young, handsome, reckless, ruled by his appetites — and the poem loves him and punishes him in equal measure. He rides north to woo a maiden, is set three impossible tasks, and is shot dead and chopped to pieces in the black river of death. And then the poem gives us its most tender scene: a mother, sensing her son's death from far away, who walks into the land of the dead and rakes the river for every piece of him, and sings him back to life. 維納莫寧是睿智的，伊爾瑪利寧是耐心的，而《卡勒瓦拉》的第三位英雄，這些都不是。勒明蓋寧年輕、俊美、魯莽，受其慾望所統治——而這首詞既愛他，也同等地懲罰他。他策馬北上求一個少女，被指派三項不可能的任務，在黑暗的死亡之河中被射殺、剁成碎片。然後這首詞給了我們它最溫柔的場面：一位母親，從遠方感應到兒子的死，她走入死者之國，用耙將河中的每一塊碎片撈起，以歌將他唱回人間。

Chapter 1 · 第一章

The Rash Hero

魯莽的英雄

Lemminkäinen is the third great hero of the Kalevala, and he is everything the other two are not. Väinämöinen is old and wise; Ilmarinen is patient and skilled; Lemminkäinen is young, beautiful, vain, and entirely ruled by desire. He is the warrior of the trio — the one who fights, who woos, who chases, who cannot resist a beautiful face or a dangerous challenge. And the poem introduces him with affection and exasperation both, as a mother might describe a son she loves and fears for: handsome as the morning, brave as a lion, and utterly incapable of listening to a single warning.

勒明蓋寧是《卡勒瓦拉》的第三位大英雄，而他完全是另外兩位所不是的一切。維納莫寧年老睿智；伊爾瑪利寧耐心嫻熟；勒明蓋寧年輕、俊美、虛榮，且完全受欲望所統治。他是三人中的戰士——那個戰鬥、求歡、追逐、無法抗拒一張美麗面孔或一個危險挑戰之人。而這首詞以既鍾愛又無奈的方式介紹他，如同一個母親描述一個她所愛又為之所懼的兒子：俊美如清晨，勇敢如獅，卻完全無法聽進任何一個警告。

And the poem gives us, throughout Lemminkäinen's story, the constant refrain of his mother's warnings. Every time he sets out on some new folly, she stands at the door and tells him what will go wrong: do not go to that feast, do not woo that woman, do not boast at that gathering, do not ride to the North. And every time, Lemminkäinen laughs, and does the thing anyway, and the thing goes wrong exactly as she said. This is not, the poem insists, a flaw to be condemned. It is the flaw of youth itself — the inability to believe, in one's own body, that the warnings apply to you. Lemminkäinen is every young person who has ever been told the fire is hot and reached for it anyway. The poem loves him because he is the part of every reader that refused to listen.

而這首詞，在勒明蓋寧故事的始終，給了我們他母親警告的不斷重複。每一次他著手某樁新的蠢事，她便站在門口告訴他會出什麼差錯：不要去那場宴會，不要去追那個女子，不要在那個聚會上吹噓，不要騎馬去北境。而每一次，勒明蓋寧一笑，還是做了那件事，而那件事便如她所說地出了差錯。這首詞堅持，這不是一個該受譴責的缺陷。它是青春本身的缺陷——無法在自己的身體裡相信，那些警告也適用於你。勒明蓋寧，是每一個曾被警告火是燙的、卻還是伸手去碰的年輕人。這首詞愛他，因為他是每一個讀者身上那個拒絕聽話的部分。

And this quality — the refusal to heed, the rush toward the beautiful and the dangerous — is exactly what will make Lemminkäinen essential to the theft of the Sampo. The wise singer and the patient smith can plan and forge, but neither of them can do the reckless thing that victory will require: the bold charge, the impudent theft, the willingness to ride into the frozen North and grab the wonder from under the witch's nose. Lemminkäinen is the one who will do that. But first, the poem tells us, his recklessness must be tested to the breaking point — and beyond it, into death itself, and back. The next chapters are the story of that test. And at the heart of it is his mother, whose love will prove deeper than the river of death.

而這個特質——拒絕聽從、衝向美麗與危險——正是使勒明蓋寧成為桑波之盜所不可或缺的特質。睿智的歌者與耐心的鐵匠能計劃與鍛造，但他們兩人都無法做勝利所需要的那件魯莽之事：大膽的衝鋒、厚顏的盜取、願意策馬進入冰封的北境、從那個女巫的鼻子底下把奇物搶走。勒明蓋寧是會做那件事的人。但首先，這首詞告訴我們，他的魯莽必須被試煉到斷裂點——以及超越它，進入死亡本身，再回來。接下來的章節，便是那場試煉的故事。而在其核心，是他的母親，她的愛，將被證明比死亡之河更深。

Chapter 2 · 第二章

The Quest for the North Maiden

北境少女之求

And so Lemminkäinen, hearing of the beauty of a maiden in the North — the daughter of Louhi, in some tellings, or a maiden of the cold lands — resolved to ride there and win her. His mother begged him not to go. She listed every danger: the long road, the cold, the witch's cruelty, the tasks the North would set, the likelihood of death. And Lemminkäinen, as always, laughed, and saddled his horse, and rode out. And the poem gives us the journey in detail — the forests, the frozen rivers, the long ride toward the darkening North — and at every turning, a new warning from his mother, carried on the wind, ignored by the son.

於是勒明蓋寧，聽聞北境一位少女之美——在某些版本中是婁希的女兒，或冷地的一位少女——決意策馬前去贏得她。他的母親求他別去。她列舉了每一個危險：漫長的路、寒冷、女巫的殘酷、北境會設下的任務、死亡的可能。而勒明蓋寧，一如往常，一笑，給他的馬套上鞍，策馬而去。而這首詞詳細地給了我們這趟旅程——森林、冰封的河流、朝著漸暗的北境的長途騎行——而在每一個轉折處，來自他母親的一個新警告，乘著風而來，被兒子所忽視。

And he came at last to the North, and to the hall of the maiden's mother — a formidable old woman, the poem tells us, who had no intention of giving her daughter to a handsome fool. And she set him tasks. "You may not woo my daughter," she said, "until you have done what I ask. Ski down the demon-stag of the elk of Hiisi. Bridle the demon-horse of the river. And then — shoot the swan of Tuonela, the swan that swims on the black river of death itself, and bring it to me." And Lemminkäinen, puffed with confidence, agreed to all of it.

而他終於來到了北境，來到了那少女母親的大廳——一位可怕的老婦人，這首詞告訴我們，她無意把女兒交給一個俊美的傻子。而她給他設下了任務。「你不可以求我的女兒，」她說，「直到你完成我所要求的。滑雪獵下希西之麋的惡魔雄鹿。給河中的惡魔之馬套上轡。然後——射下圖奧內拉的天鵝，那隻游在死亡本身之黑河上的天鵝，把它帶給我。」而勒明蓋寧，自信滿滿，全部答應了。

And the poem tells us, here, what the reader already suspects: that the third task is the trap. The swan of Tuonela is not a bird. It is the guardian of the river of death — the black water that flows through Tuonela, the land of the dead — and to shoot it is to enter the land of death itself. The mother of the maiden has set Lemminkäinen a task that cannot be done without dying. And Lemminkäinen, who has never heeded a warning, does not see it. He goes to do the first two tasks. And then, confident, careless, he walks toward the black river. The poem has been building to this his whole story: the boy who would not listen, walking toward the one danger listening could have saved him from.

而這首詞，在這裡，告訴我們讀者早已懷疑之事：那第三項任務，便是陷阱。圖奧內拉的天鵝不是一隻鳥。它是死亡之河的守護者——那條流經圖奧內拉、死者之國的黑河——而射殺它，便是進入死亡本身之國。那少女的母親，給勒明蓋寧設下了一項不做便不能完成、做了便會死的任務。而勒明蓋寧，從未聽從任何警告，看不見它。他去做前兩項任務。然後，自信、漫不經心，他走向那條黑河。這首詞，他整個故事以來，一直在為此鋪陳：那個不肯聽話的男孩，走向那個聽話本可救他的唯一危險。

Chapter 3 · 第三章

The Three Impossible Tasks

三項不可能之任務

The first task: ski down the demon-stag. The elk of Hiisi — a magical beast, the size of a mountain, that ran through the forests faster than the wind — was to be caught and skied down, a thing no human had ever done. And Lemminkäinen, with the help of a friendly forest-spirit who lent him magic skis, accomplished it. He ran the demon-stag to exhaustion, caught it, and brought it back. The poem gives us the chase with delight — the long pursuit through the snow, the magic skis flashing, the beast finally brought down. Lemminkäinen, triumphant, returned for the next task.

第一項任務：滑雪獵下惡魔雄鹿。希西之麋——一頭魔法之獸，山一般大，比風更快地穿行於森林——要被捉住並滑雪獵下，這是從無人做到之事。而勒明蓋寧，在一個友善的森林之靈借他魔法雪橇的幫助下，完成了它。他將那頭惡魔雄鹿跑到力竭，捉住它，帶了回來。這首詞欣喜地給了我們那場追逐——在雪中漫長的追趕、閃爍的魔法雪橇、那頭獸終於被制服。勒明蓋寧，得勝，回來領取下一項任務。

The second task: bridle the demon-horse of the river. A great stallion lived in the waters of a northern river — wild, untamable, breathing fire — and Lemminkäinen was to catch it and put a bridle on it. And again, with cunning and courage, he did it; he waded into the river, seized the horse by the mane, and forced the bit into its mouth. Two tasks done. And the poem tells us that Lemminkäinen, at this point, was almost giddy with success. He had done the impossible, twice. Surely the third task would be no harder.

第二項任務：給河中的惡魔之馬套上轡。一匹巨型的雄馬住在北方一條河的水中——狂野、不可馴、噴吐火焰——而勒明蓋寧要捉住它、給它套上轡。而再一次，憑著機智與勇氣，他做到了；他涉入河中，抓住那匹馬的鬃，把銜鐵塞入它口中。兩項任務完成。而這首詞告訴我們，勒明蓋寧，在此刻，幾乎因成功而暈眩。他做到了不可能，兩次。第三項任務，想必不會更難。

And so he set out for the black river of Tuonela — and the poem, here, lets us feel the change. The first two tasks were hard but they were tasks of the living world: a beast in a forest, a horse in a river. The third is something else. The swan of Tuonela swims on the river of the dead. To reach it, Lemminkäinen must walk down into the land of death itself. And the poem tells us that he felt, for the first time, a chill that was not the cold of the North — a deeper cold, the cold of a place where the living do not belong. But he had come too far to turn back. He had boasted too loudly. He walked on, toward the black water. And in the next chapter, the trap will close.

於是他動身前往圖奧內拉的黑河——而這首詞，在此處，讓我們感覺到那個轉變。前兩項任務雖難，卻是活人世界的任務：森林中的一頭獸、河裡的一匹馬。第三項，是別的東西。圖奧內拉的天鵝，游在死者之河上。要抵達它，勒明蓋寧必須走下死亡本身之國。而這首詞告訴我們，他頭一回感覺到一陣寒意，那不是北境之冷——一種更深的冷，一種活人不屬之地之冷。但他已走得太遠，無法回頭。他已吹噓得太響。他繼續走，走向那黑色的水。而在下一章，陷阱將關上。

Chapter 4 · 第四章

The Death in the Black River

黑河之死

And there, at the river, waited the one enemy Lemminkäinen had never accounted for: a blind old herdsman, a man he had wronged earlier in his journey, who had been waiting, with a snake for an arrow, at the water's edge. The poem does not let us forget that Lemminkäinen's recklessness had made enemies — that his careless victories had left wounds behind them — and that one of those wounds, now, had a man holding it. And the blind herdsman, hearing Lemminkäinen approach, drew his bow and loosed the snake.

而在那裡，河邊，等待著勒明蓋寧從未計入的那個敵人：一個盲目的老牧人，一個他在旅途早先所虧負之人，一直等在水邊，以一條蛇為箭。這首詞不讓我們忘記，勒明蓋寧的魯莽曾樹敵——他那漫不經心的勝利留下了傷口——而其中一道傷口，如今，有一個人握著它。而那個盲目的牧人，聽見勒明蓋寧走近，拉開他的弓，射出了那條蛇。

The snake struck him in the heart. And Lemminkäinen fell — the handsome warrior, the boy who would not listen, the third hero of the Kalevala — fell dead at the edge of the black river of death. And the blind herdsman, to make sure, took the body and chopped it to pieces, and cast every piece into the dark water, and watched the river of death carry them away. The pieces scattered. The body was gone. The hero was no more.

那條蛇擊中了他的心臟。而勒明蓋寧倒了——那個俊美的戰士、那個不肯聽話的男孩、《卡勒瓦拉》的第三位英雄——倒在死亡之黑河的邊緣，死了。而那個盲目的牧人，為求保險，將屍體剁成碎片，把每一塊都擲入那暗水之中，看著死亡之河將它們帶走。碎片散了。屍體沒了。英雄不在了。

And the poem, here, does something extraordinary. It does not end the story. It does not move on to another hero. It turns, instead, to the mother — to the woman who had warned him at every door, whose every warning he had ignored, who is now, far away in the warm south, going about her day. And the poem tells us that she felt it. A brush on her cheek, a chill in her blood, a wrongness in the world — and she knew, without being told, that her son was dead. This is the turn that makes the Kalevala the Kalevala: not the death, but the mother's knowing. The rash hero's story is, in the end, not about the hero at all. It is about the love that comes for him when he cannot save himself.

而這首詞，在此處，做了一件非凡之事。它沒有結束這個故事。它沒有轉向另一個英雄。它轉向，反而，那位母親——那個在每一扇門前警告過他、他的每一個警告都被他忽視、如今在遙遠的溫暖南方過著她的一日的女子。而這首詞告訴我們，她感覺到了。一陣拂過她臉頰的觸感、她血中的一陣寒、世界裡的一個不對勁——而她知道，無須人告訴，她的兒子死了。這個轉折，使《卡勒瓦拉》成為《卡勒瓦拉》：不是那場死亡，而是那位母親的知曉。那位魯莽英雄的故事，最終，根本不是關於那位英雄的。它是關於那份在他無法自救之時為他而來的愛。

Chapter 5 · 第五章

The Mother's Descent

母親的下凡

And the mother went. The poem gives us, in this chapter, one of the great passages of world literature — a mother's descent into the land of the dead to retrieve her son. She did not hesitate. She did not ask permission. She felt his death, and she set out, immediately, on the long road north, through the forests and the cold, toward the black river where she knew, in her blood, he had gone. And along the way she asked everything she met — the road, the trees, the moon, the sun — "Where is my son? Have you seen my son?" And finally the sun, who sees everything, told her: he is in the river of Tuonela, chopped to pieces, gone.

而那位母親去了。這首詞，在這一章裡，給了我們世界文學中最偉大的段落之一——一位母親下到死者之國去尋回她的兒子。她沒有猶豫。她沒有請求許可。她感覺到他的死，而她立即動身，踏上漫長的北路，穿過森林與寒冷，走向那條她血中知道他已前往的黑河。而一路上她詢問她所遇的一切——路、樹、月、日——「我的兒子在哪裡？你看見我的兒子了嗎？」而終於，那個看見一切的太陽告訴她：他在圖奧內拉的河裡，被剁成碎片，沒了。

And she came to the river of death. And she had brought with her a giant rake — a long-handled iron rake, the kind a farmer uses, the tool of ordinary human labor — and she began, the poem tells us, to rake the black water. Stroke by stroke, through the cold dark current of the river of the dead, she dragged the rake along the bottom. And slowly, piece by piece, the rake caught the fragments of her son — a hand here, a rib there, a piece of his beautiful face. She gathered them all. She missed nothing. The poem gives us, in patient detail, the long raking — the mother refusing to stop, refusing to leave any piece behind, working the river of death like a field until she had recovered every scrap of the boy she had raised.

她來到了死亡之河。而她隨身帶了一把巨大的耙——一把長柄的鐵耙，那種農夫所用的耙，普通人類勞動的工具——而她開始了，這首詞告訴我們，耙那黑色的水。一耙接一耙，穿過死者之河冰冷黑暗的水流，她沿著河底拖著那把耙。而慢慢地，一塊一塊，那耙勾住了她兒子的碎片——一隻手在這裡，一根肋骨在那裡，一塊他俊美的臉。她把它們全撈了起來。她什麼也沒漏掉。這首詞，以耐心的細節，給了我們那場漫長的耙梳——那位母親拒絕停下，拒絕留下任何一塊，像耕耘一塊田那樣耕耘死亡之河，直到她尋回她所撫養之男孩的每一片碎屑。

This is, I think, the most powerful image in the entire Kalevala, and one of the great images of maternal love in any literature. The mother does not defeat death with magic. She does not bargain with the gods. She takes an ordinary farm tool, and she works, patiently, in the worst place in the world, until she has gotten back what is hers. The weapon against death, the poem tells us, is not power. It is the refusal to leave the dead un-raked — the stubborn, exhausting, unglamorous labor of love that will not accept that the loved one is simply gone. And she raked the river until she had him, all of him, in pieces, on the bank. And the next chapter will tell us how she sang those pieces back into a living boy.

我想，這是整部《卡勒瓦拉》中最有力量的意象，也是任何文學中關於母愛的最偉大意象之一。那位母親不是以魔法擊敗死亡。她不是與諸神討價還價。她取了一把平凡的農具，而她工作著，耐心地，在世界上最糟的地方，直到她尋回屬於她之物。對抗死亡的武器，這首詞告訴我們，不是力量。它是拒絕任死者未被耙梳的那份執拗——那份不肯接受所愛之人就這樣沒了的、頑固、耗人、不體面的愛之勞動。而她耙著那條河，直到她擁有了他，他的全部，一塊一塊，在岸上。而下一章將告訴我們，她如何將那些碎片唱回成一個活的男孩。

Chapter 6 · 第六章

The Restoration to Life

復生

But the pieces were not enough. The mother had raked the river, and she had gathered every fragment of her son, and she had laid them out on the bank in the shape of a body — but it was still only a jigsaw of dead flesh, not a living boy. And so she set to work, the poem tells us, with a needle and thread, stitching the pieces together, joining hand to arm, arm to shoulder, piece to piece, until the body was whole again. But still it did not breathe. Still it was dead. The form was restored; the life was not.

但那些碎片還不夠。那位母親耙了那條河，她尋回了她兒子的每一塊碎片，而她把它們排放在岸上，排成一個身體的形狀——但它仍然只是一副死肉的拼圖，不是一個活的男孩。於是她開始工作，這首詞告訴我們，用一根針和線，把那些碎片縫在一起，把手縫到臂上，臂縫到肩上，一塊縫到一塊，直到那個身體再次完整。但它仍然不呼吸。它仍然是死的。形已復；命未復。

And so the mother did the only thing left to her: she called on the bee. In the Finnish tradition, the bee is a creature of healing — the bringer of the life-giving honey, the ointment that mends. And the mother spoke to a bee, and asked it to fly to the highest heaven, to the halls of the Creator, and bring back a drop of the life-giving balm. And the bee flew — up, up, past the clouds, past the stars, to the very dwelling of the god who had made the world — and it returned, the poem tells us, carrying on its legs a drop of the honey of life. And the mother anointed her son's lips with it. She anointed his wounds, his eyes, his heart.

於是那位母親做了她唯一能做的事：她呼喚那隻蜜蜂。在芬蘭傳統裡，蜜蜂是一種療癒之生靈——那生命之蜜的帶來者、那修補一切的藥膏。而那位母親對一隻蜜蜂說話，請它飛到最高的天上、飛到造物主的殿堂，帶回一滴賦命之膏。而那隻蜜蜂飛了——向上，向上，越過雲，越過星辰，直到那位造了世界之神所居之處——而它歸來了，這首詞告訴我們，腿上帶著一滴生命之蜜。而那位母親用它塗抹她兒子的嘴唇。她塗抹他的傷口、他的眼睛、他的心。

And Lemminkäinen opened his eyes. He drew a breath. He sat up. The dead boy lived. The mother, who had raked the river of death and stitched the body and called the bee and anointed the wounds, had done what no god had done for her: she had brought her son back from the land of the dead. And the poem, in its closing of this chapter, tells us that Lemminkäinen was changed — not humbled, exactly, the way a Greek hero might be, but deepened. He had died and returned. He knew, now, what his mother's love was worth, in a way he had never known before. And he would need that knowledge — that knowledge of death, and of the love that crosses it — when, in the next stage of our journey, the three heroes sail north together to steal the Sampo from the mountain of the witch.

而勒明蓋寧睜開了眼睛。他吸了一口氣。他坐了起來。那個死的男孩活了。那位母親，她耙了死亡之河、縫合了身體、呼喚了蜜蜂、塗抹了傷口，她做了沒有一位神為她做過的事：她把她的兒子從死者之國帶了回來。而這首詞，在這一章的結尾，告訴我們勒明蓋寧變了——不完全是謙卑了，如一個希臘英雄可能會的那樣，而是更深了。他死過又歸來了。他如今知道，他母親的愛值多少，那是以他從未知曉的方式知道的。而他將需要那份知識——那份關於死亡、關於跨越死亡之愛的知識——當我們旅程的下一階段裡，三位英雄一同航向北方，要從那個女巫的山中盜走桑波。

THE MOTHER WHO RAKED THE RIVER

耙梳那條河的母親

A boy who would not listen rode into the North, did the impossible twice, and walked toward the third task — and a blind man's snake struck his heart, and his body was chopped and scattered in the river of death. And far away, his mother felt it, and came, with nothing but a farm rake, and worked the black water until she had every piece of him. A bee flew to heaven for the honey of life. She anointed his lips. He breathed. The dead boy lived. And now the three heroes — the singer, the smith, and the one who has been to death and back — are ready to sail for the Sampo.
