THE WORLD & THE ANCIENT SINGER

世　界　與　古　老　的　歌　者

ILMATAR · THE WORLD-EGG · VÄINÄMÖINEN'S BIRTH

In the Kalevala, the world is not made by a god speaking a command. It is made by a maiden floating on the sea, and a duck that lays its eggs on her knee, and the shattering of those eggs into sky and earth and sun and moon. And then, from the same maiden, is born the ancient singer Väinämöinen — whose power is not the sword but the song, who can move stones and calm the sea by the depth of his knowing, and whose life will be the spine of the whole epic to come. 在《卡勒瓦拉》裡，世界不是由一位神說出命令而造的。它是由一位漂浮在海上的少女、一隻在她膝上產卵的鴨子，以及那些卵碎裂成天、地、日、月而成的。然後，從同一位少女身上，誕生了古老的歌者維納莫寧——他的力量不是劍，而是歌，他能以所知之深移動石頭、平息大海，而他的一生，將是整部史詩的脊梁。

Chapter 1 · 第一章

Ilmatar on the Cosmic Sea

太初之海上的伊爾瑪塔

In the beginning there was a maiden, and the maiden was called Ilmatar — daughter of the air, spirit of the wind — and she had lived for a long time in the upper spaces, alone, longing for something to hold and to love. And she let herself down out of the emptiness, and came to the great sea that covered everything, and she floated there, on the face of the water, for seven hundred years. The waves were her cradle and her prison. She could not find a shore because there was no shore yet. She could not rest because there was no land. She floated, and she floated, and the universe waited for her to grow tired enough to begin.

太初之時有一位少女，這位少女名叫伊爾瑪塔——空氣之女、風之靈——她長久以來獨自居住於上方的虛空之中，渴望著有什麼可以擁抱、可以去愛。於是她從那空無之中降下，來到那覆蓋一切的大海，她在那裡，在水面上，漂浮了七百年。波濤是她的搖籃，也是她的牢獄。她找不到岸，因為那時還沒有岸。她無法歇息，因為還沒有陸地。她漂著，漂著，而宇宙等待著她，等她疲累到足以開始。

This is the Finnish way of beginning a story, and it is utterly unlike any other in this garden. There is no creator-god here, no command that summons light from darkness. There is only a woman, and the water, and an unbearable longing, and seven hundred years of patience. The world is not made by power. It is made by a being who wanted something so much that her wanting could not stay unanswered forever. The Kalevala's deepest teaching — that all making begins in desire — is here, in its first image. The cosmos is born not of a word but of a want.

這是芬蘭人開始一個故事的方式，而它與這座園中任何其他方式都截然不同。這裡沒有造物之神，沒有一道將光從黑暗中召出的命令。只有一個女子，一片水，一份不可承受的渴望，以及七百年的耐心。世界不是由力量造成的。它是由一個「想要」某物的生靈造成的，她想要得如此之深，以至於她的「想要」不可能永遠沒有回應。《卡勒瓦拉》最深的教誨——一切創造皆始於欲望——便在這裡，在它的第一個意象之中。宇宙之生，不是始於一個字，而是始於一份渴。

And the poem gives us, with a tenderness that runs through all its pages, the long interior of those seven hundred years — Ilmatar moving her limbs in the water, lifting her head, watching the emptiness for any sign of land, and feeling, inside her own body, the slow forming of something else. For she was carrying, the whole time, a child — a son who would not be born until the world was ready to receive him. The world had to be made first. And the maker of it, the poem tells us, was the maiden herself, by the simple, ancient, terrible act of becoming a mother. Creation, in the Kalevala, is not an act of will over chaos. It is an act of motherhood — slow, painful, patient, made out of love for the thing that is coming.

而這首詩，以一種貫穿其所有書頁的溫柔，給了我們那七百年的漫長內裡——伊爾瑪塔在水中動著四肢，抬起頭，望著空無尋找任何陸地的跡象，並在她自己的身體裡，感覺著另一個東西的緩慢成形。因為她，這整段時間，都懷著一個孩子——一個要等到世界準備好迎接他之時才會出生的兒子。世界必須先被造好。而造它的人，這首詩告訴我們，正是那位少女自己，藉著成為母親這個簡單、古老、可怖之舉。在《卡勒瓦拉》裡，創造不是一個意志凌駕混沌之舉。它是一個母親之舉——緩慢、痛苦、耐心，出於對那個將要來臨之物的愛。

Chapter 2 · 第二章

The Duck & the World-Egg

鴨子與世界之卵

Then came the duck. A small bird — the poem does not name her, only that she flew over the endless water looking for a place to build her nest — and she found nowhere, for there was no land. And then she saw, rising barely above the waves, the knee of the floating maiden. And the duck took Ilmatar's knee for an island, and there, on it, she built her nest, and laid six golden eggs and one of iron. And Ilmatar, feeling the warmth of the eggs on her skin, held very still, for the love of the little life growing in the nest — and the duck sat, and warmed them, day after day.

然後鴨子來了。一隻小鳥——這首詩沒有給她名字，只說她飛過無盡的水面尋找築巢之地——而她到處都找不到，因為沒有陸地。然後她看見，剛剛露出波濤之上的，那位漂浮少女的膝蓋。而那隻鴨子把伊爾瑪塔的膝蓋當成了一座島，在那裡，在它上面，她築了巢，下了六顆金蛋和一顆鐵蛋。而伊爾瑪塔，感覺到那些蛋在她肌膚上的溫暖，非常非常地保持不動，出於對那個在巢中生長的小生命之愛——而那隻鴨子坐著，孵化著它們，日復一日。

And then the accident that makes the world. Ilmatar's knee grew hot — too hot, burning — and involuntarily she twitched, and the eggs rolled off, and struck the water, and shattered. And the poem, in one of its most beautiful passages, tells us what happened to the pieces. The fragments of the eggshells did not sink. The lower halves became the earth; the upper halves became the sky. The yolk became the sun; the white became the moon. The mottled parts became the stars, and the dark specks became the clouds. The whole world was made, in an instant, from the breaking of seven eggs on a startled woman's knee. The cosmos is a shattered nest.

然後那個造就世界的事故發生了。伊爾瑪塔的膝蓋變熱了——太熱，灼燒——她不由自主地一顫，那些蛋滾了下來，撞上水面，碎裂了。而這首詩，在其最美麗的段落之一裡，告訴了我們那些碎片後來如何。那些蛋殼的碎片沒有下沉。下半部成了大地；上半部成了天空。蛋黃成了太陽；蛋白成了月亮。斑駁之處成了星辰，而暗色的斑點成了雲。整個世界，在一瞬之間，由一個受驚女子膝上的七顆蛋的破碎而成。宇宙，是一個碎裂的巢。

This is one of the great creation-images of world mythology, and its tenderness is its point. The world is not commanded into being. It is dropped, by accident, from a mother's flinch. The sun is an egg-yolk; the moon is the white. And the whole thing rests on a moment of failed stillness — Ilmatar trying, for the love of the duck, to hold perfectly steady, and failing, and the failure being the making of everything. The Kalevala's worldview is, from its first page, a worldview of accident and care: the world is precious because it is fragile, and it exists at all because someone, briefly, loved a small thing enough to try to hold still.

這是世界神話中最偉大的創世意象之一，而它的溫柔，正是它的重點。世界不是被命令而存在的。它是由一位母親的一個瑟縮，意外地掉落的。太陽是一顆蛋黃；月亮是蛋白。而這整個事物，依賴於一個失敗的靜止之瞬——伊爾瑪塔，為了那隻鴨子的愛，試著完美地保持不動，卻失敗了，而那個失敗，正是一切的造就。《卡勒瓦拉》的世界觀，從它的第一頁起，便是一個關於意外與關懷的世界觀：世界之所以珍貴，是因為它脆弱；而它之所以存在，是因為有人，短暫地，愛一個小東西愛到足以試著保持不動。

Chapter 3 · 第三章

The Birth of Väinämöinen

維納莫寧之生

And now, with the world made, the child Ilmatar had been carrying all those centuries could finally be born — and his name was Väinämöinen. But his birth, like everything in the Kalevala, was slow. The poem tells us he lay in his mother's womb for thirty more years after the world was made, growing in wisdom, unwilling to come out into a world he could already see was imperfect. And when at last he was born, he did not arrive as a squalling infant but as an old man — white-haired, calm, already wise, already singing. He had spent thirty years in the dark, learning.

而如今，世界既成，伊爾瑪塔懷了那麼多個世紀的孩子，終於可以出生了——他的名字是維納莫寧。但他的出生，一如《卡勒瓦拉》中的一切，是緩慢的。這首詩告訴我們，在世界造成之後，他又在母親腹中躺了三十年，增長著智慧，不願來到一個他已能看見其不完美的世界。而當他終於出生之時，他不是作為一個啼哭的嬰孩到來，而是作為一個老人——白髮、平靜、已然睿智、已然在歌唱。他在黑暗中花了三十年，學習。

Väinämöinen is the great figure of the Kalevala, and he is not a warrior. He is a singer — a shaman, a sage, a man whose power is the depth of his knowledge and the strength of his songs. In the Finnish tradition, the most powerful act a person could perform was not to swing a sword but to remember and to sing: to know the origins of things, the hidden names, the words that could move iron and calm storms. Väinämöinen knows the roots of all things. His songs are so old and so deep that they predate the hills. He is, in a sense, the Finnish answer to every other epic's warrior-king — and the answer is that the deepest power is not strength but memory, not the blade but the word, not what you can break but what you can recall.

維納莫寧是《卡勒瓦拉》的偉大人物，而他不是一個戰士。他是一個歌者——一個薩滿、一個智者、一個其力量乃是其知識之深與其歌之強的人。在芬蘭的傳統裡，一個人所能從事的最有力之舉，不是揮劍，而是記憶與歌唱：知曉萬物的根源、隱藏的名字、那些能移動鐵與平息風暴的詞。維納莫寧知曉一切事物的根。他的歌如此古老、如此之深，以至於它們早於山丘。在某種意義上，他是芬蘭對其他每一部史詩之武士君王的回答——而那個回答是：最深的力量不是武力，而是記憶；不是刀刃，而是話語；不是你能擊碎什麼，而是你能召回什麼。

And the poem immediately shows us this power in action. Väinämöinen, the newborn old man, sets about completing the world his mother began. He plants the first forests, shapes the shores, orders the rivers. He does this not by labor but by song — by the speaking of the right words in the right order. The world is still being made, and it is being made by a singer. This is the spine of the whole epic: whatever the heroes of the Kalevala will do, they will do it by song, by knowledge, by the patient recollection of origins. The Sampo will be forged by a smith, yes — but the voyage to fetch it will be sung into being. And the final victory, when it comes, will be a song that defeats an enemy a sword could not.

而這首詩立刻向我們展示了這份力量的運作。維納莫寧，這個新生的老人，著手完成他母親所開始的世界。他種下最初的森林，塑造海岸，整理河流。他做這些，不是靠勞動，而是靠歌——靠以正確的順序說出正確的話。世界仍在被造之中，而造它的是一個歌者。這便是整部史詩的脊梁：無論《卡勒瓦拉》的英雄們將做什麼，他們都將以歌、以知識、以對根源的耐心召回而做。桑波，沒錯，將由一位鐵匠鍛造——但前去取它的航程，將被唱入存在。而最終的勝利，當它來時，將是一首擊敗刀劍所不能敗之敵的歌。

Chapter 4 · 第四章

The Contest of Songs

歌唱之爭

Into this world came a challenger — a young man named Joukahainen, proud, richly dressed, convinced that he had learned all there was to learn, and determined to prove himself against the famous ancient singer. He hitched up his sleigh and drove it down the road, boasting as he went that he would defeat Väinämöinen in a contest of songs and take his place as the greatest singer in the North. His old mother warned him not to go — "Väinämöinen's songs are deeper than the lakes," she said — but the young man laughed and drove on.

進入這個世界的，來了一位挑戰者——一個名叫尤卡海寧的年輕人，驕傲、衣著華麗、深信自己已學盡一切可學之物，並決心要向那位著名的古老歌者證明自己。他套上雪橇，沿路驅馳，一路吹噓他要在歌唱之爭中擊敗維納莫寧，取而代之成為北方最偉大的歌者。他的老母親警告他別去——「維納莫寧的歌比湖泊更深，」她說——但那年輕人一笑，繼續驅馳。

And the two met on the road, and neither would give way, and so they began the contest — the great singing-duel that is the Kalevala's signature scene. They sang of the origins of things. Joukahainen sang of what he knew, and he knew much: the names of hills, the shapes of clouds, the history of the stars. But every time he finished, Väinämöinen answered with a song that went deeper — to the roots beneath the hills, to the air before the clouds, to the dark before the stars. The young man sang of the present. The old man sang of the beginnings. And the beginnings, the poem tells us, are always deeper than whatever came after.

而兩人在路上相遇，誰也不肯讓路，於是他們開始了那場較量——那場偉大的歌唱決鬥，是《卡勒瓦拉》的標誌性場面。他們歌唱萬物的起源。尤卡海寧唱出他所知的，而他所知的不少：山丘之名、雲之形、星辰之史。但每當他唱完，維納莫寧總以一首更深之歌回應——到山丘之下的根，到雲之前的空氣，到星辰之前的黑暗。那年輕人歌唱當下。那老人歌唱開端。而開端，這首詩告訴我們，總是比其後來的任何事物都更深。

And finally Väinämöinen, growing weary of the contest, sang the young man into the ground. His song was so deep and so powerful that Joukahainen began to sink — first his sledge, then his horses, then his own legs — into the very marsh they were standing on, drawn down by the weight of a knowledge he could not match. Up to his waist, up to his chest, up to his chin in the bog, the young man begged for mercy. And Väinämöinen stopped, and asked what he would give. And Joukahainen, in his terror, made the promise that would set the next tragedy in motion: he promised Väinämöinen his sister, the beautiful Aino, in exchange for his life. The contest of songs, in the Kalevala, is not a game. It is a weapon. And the loser pays in people.

最後維納莫寧，對這場較量漸感厭倦，將那年輕人唱入了地。他的歌如此之深、如此有力，以至於尤卡海寧開始下沉——先是他的雪橇，然後他的馬，然後他自己的腿——沉入他們所站的那片沼澤之中，被一份他無法匹敵的知識之重所拉下。沉到腰際、沉到胸口、沉到下巴陷入泥沼之中，那年輕人乞求饒命。而維納莫寧停了，問他願意付出什麼。而尤卡海寧，在他的恐懼之中，立下了那個將啟動下一場悲劇的承諾：他承諾將他的妹妹、美麗的艾諾，許給維納莫寧，以換取自己的性命。在《卡勒瓦拉》裡，歌唱之爭，不是一場遊戲。它是一件武器。而輸者，以人償付。

Chapter 5 · 第五章

Aino's Fate

艾諾之命

And so Aino learned that she had been given, by her brother, to an old man she did not love — and the poem gives us, in the figure of Aino, one of its quietest and most devastating characters. She does not rage. She does not bargain. She weeps, in her mother's house, and tells her mother what has been done, and her mother — terribly — tells her to be grateful, that to marry a great singer is an honor, that she should go willingly. And Aino, hearing this, understands that there is no help for her in any human house. She puts on her best clothes, takes her jewelry, and walks alone to the cold lake.

於是艾諾得知，她已被她的哥哥給了一個她不愛的老人——而這首詩，在艾諾這個形象裡，給了我們其最沉靜也最令人心碎的角色之一。她不暴怒。她不討價還價。她在母親家中哭泣，告訴母親所發生之事，而她的母親——可怖地——告訴她要心存感激，嫁給一位偉大的歌者是榮幸，她應當心甘情願地去。而艾諾，聽了這話，明白在任何人的家中都沒有她的援手。她穿上最好的衣裳，帶上她的首飾，獨自走向冰冷的湖。

And there, by the water, she meets three water-nymphs — mermaids, in some tellings — who are bathing. And they speak to her, gently, and tell her that the water is kinder than the land, that the lake will not give her to a husband she does not want. And Aino, in the most famous and the most heartbreaking image of the early Kalevala, walks into the lake. She does not drown in horror. She walks in as if going home. The water closes over her. She is gone. She becomes, the poem says, a water-spirit herself — a fish in the cold river, a voice in the reeds — free of the bargain her brother made, free of the old man's house, free at the cost of her own life.

而在那裡，水邊，她遇見三位水仙女——美人魚，在某些版本中——她們正在沐浴。她們溫柔地對她說話，告訴她水比陸地仁慈，那座湖不會把她交給一個她不想要的丈夫。而艾諾，在《卡勒瓦拉》早期最著名也最令人心碎的意象之中，走進了湖裡。她不是在驚恐中溺沒。她走進去，如同回家。水在她頭上闔上。她不見了。她成了，這首詩說，一位水靈 herself——冷河中的一條魚，蘆葦中的一個聲音——擺脫了她哥哥所做的那筆交易，擺脫了那個老人的家，以她自己的生命為代價，自由了。

The death of Aino is the first real wound of the Kalevala, and it sets the pattern for everything that follows. The old singer, Väinämöinen, is grief-stricken — he never wanted the girl to die; he had merely accepted a bargain offered. And Joukahainen, the brother, is consumed by a guilt and rage that will, in the next chapter, turn to violence against the man he blames. The poem does not tell us who is at fault. Everyone is, a little: the brother for the bargain, the mother for the refusal of help, the old man for the accepting, the girl for the only way out she could see. This is the Kalevala's moral world — not heroes and villains, but people caught in a web of wants and bargains, where even the innocent pay, and where the deepest griefs come from no single evil but from the ordinary way human beings fail one another.

艾諾之死，是《卡勒瓦拉》的第一道真正的傷，而它為其後的一切定下了模式。那位古老的歌者維納莫寧，悲痛欲絕——他從不想要那個女孩死；他不過是接受了一筆被提出的交易。而哥哥尤卡海寧，被一種愧疚與憤怒所吞噬，那將在下一章化為對他所怪罪之人的暴力。這首詩不告訴我們誰有錯。每個人，都有一點：哥哥的錯在於那筆交易，母親的錯在於拒絕伸出援手，老人的錯在於接受，女孩的錯在於她所能看見的唯一出路。這便是《卡勒瓦拉》的道德世界——不是英雄與惡棍，而是被一張欲望與交易之網所困的人，在其中，連無辜者也付出代價，而最深的悲慟，不來自任何單一的惡，而來自人彼此虧負的平凡方式。

Chapter 6 · 第六章

The Sea & the North's Demand

大海與北境之求

Joukahainen, maddened by the loss of his sister, took his revenge the only way a weak man can: by ambush. He hid beside the road with his crossbow and waited for Väinämöinen to pass, on his way to the North. And when the old singer rode by on his magnificent stallion, Joukahainen fired. The first arrow missed. The second killed the horse. And Väinämöinen, thrown from his saddle, tumbled into the great sea — and the waves swallowed him, and he was carried out, helpless, into the deep.

尤卡海寧，因失去妹妹而發狂，以一個弱者所能的唯一方式報了仇：埋伏。他帶著弩藏於路旁，等待維納莫寧在前往北境的途中經過。而當那位老歌者騎著他那匹神駒經過之時，尤卡海寧射了。第一箭落空。第二箭殺了馬。而維納莫寧，被摔下馬鞍，跌入了大海——而波濤吞噬了他，他被帶走，無助地，進入深海。

And in the sea, the ancient singer was swallowed by a giant fish — a pike so vast the poem spends pages describing it — and there, in the belly of the fish, Väinämöinen waited, and sang, and worked. For the Kalevala's hero is never truly helpless: even in the belly of a monster, he can use his knowledge. He felt along the fish's insides until he found its heart, and with a knife he forged from the fish's own debris, he cut the great pike open from within and stepped out, alive, onto the surface of the sea.

而在海中，那位古老的歌者被一條巨魚吞下——一條如此巨大的狗魚，這首詩花了數頁描述它——而在那魚的腹中，維納莫寧等待著、歌唱著、行動著。因為《卡勒瓦拉》的英雄從未真正無助：即便在怪獸的腹中，他也能運用他的知識。他沿著魚的內裡摸索，直到找到它的心臟，用一把以魚自身的殘屑鍛成的刀，從內部將那條巨狗魚剖開，活了出來，踏上海面。

And there, on the water, he was found — by the people of Pohjola, the frozen North, and taken to the hall of their mistress, the great witch Louhi. And Louhi looked at the rescued singer and saw his power, and made him an offer: she would give him her daughter, the maiden of the North — but only if he would forge for her the Sampo, the wonder-mill that grinds out salt and gold and flour without end. And Väinämöinen, who was a singer and not a smith, had to confess he could not. But he promised her, in return for safe passage home, that he would send her the one man who could — the eternal smith, Ilmarinen, who had forged the very sky. And with that promise, the stage is set for the next act of the epic: the forging of the Sampo, the wonder that all the rest of the story will turn upon.

而在那裡，水面上，他被尋得了——被波赫尤拉、那冰封北境的人們，帶到他們的女主人、偉大的女巫婁希的大廳。而婁希望著這位被救起的歌者，看見了他的力量，向他提出一個提議：她會把她的女兒、北境的少女許給他——但條件是他要為她鍛造桑波，那座無盡磨出鹽、金、麵粉的奇蹟之磨。而維納莫寧，他是個歌者而非鐵匠，不得不承認他做不到。但他答應她，作為平安歸鄉的交換，他會把那個做得到的人送到她那裡——那位曾鍛造蒼穹本身的永恆鐵匠伊爾瑪利寧。而有了這個承諾，這部史詩下一幕的舞台便搭好了：桑波之鍛造，那件其餘整個故事都將圍繞其轉的奇蹟。

THE SINGER, NOT THE SWORD

是歌者，不是劍

A maiden floated on the sea for seven hundred years, and a duck laid its eggs on her knee, and the world was made from their breaking. From the same maiden an old man was born, already singing, and his songs were deeper than the lakes. He sang a proud youth into a marsh, and the youth's sister walked into the cold water to be free. And the youth shot him into the sea, and a great fish swallowed him, and he cut his way out with a knife forged from the fish's own bones. Now he stands in the hall of the frozen North, and its witch has asked him for a wonder he cannot make. The next stage will forge it.
