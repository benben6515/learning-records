THE FORGING OF THE SAMPO

桑　波　的　鍛　造

ILMARINEN · THE ETERNAL SMITH · THE MAGIC MILL

The Mistress of the frozen North demanded a wonder — a mill that would grind out salt and gold and flour without end, the prosperity of a whole land locked inside a single object — and the man who could make it was Ilmarinen, the smith who had forged the very dome of the sky. This is the story of how that wonder was made, on a rock in the cold North, and of how it was locked away there, and of the long theft that the rest of the epic exists to accomplish. 冰封北境的女主人要求一件奇物——一座會無盡磨出鹽、金、麵粉的磨，一整片土地的豐饒被鎖在一件物之中——而能造出它的人，是伊爾瑪利寧，那位曾鍛造蒼穹穹頂的鐵匠。這便是那件奇物如何被造出來的故事，在寒冷北境的一塊岩石之上，以及它如何被鎖在那裡，以及那場史詩其餘部分之所以存在的、漫長的盜取。

Chapter 1 · 第一章

Ilmarinen, the Smith of the Sky

鍛造蒼穹的伊爾瑪利寧

If Väinämöinen is the Kalevala's singer, Ilmarinen is its maker — and the two together, the wise man and the smith, the word and the hand, are the twin poles of the Finnish heroic imagination. Ilmarinen's first and greatest work was not any earthly thing. At the beginning of the world, when the sky was being hung above the earth, it was Ilmarinen who forged the firmament — hammering out, on his anvil, the very dome of the heavens, fitting it together so cleverly that no one has ever found where the joins are. He forged the stars into it. He forged the sun's track and the moon's. He made the sky itself.

如果維納莫寧是《卡勒瓦拉》的歌者，那麼伊爾瑪利寧便是它的造物者——而這兩者一起，智者與鐵匠，話語與手，是芬蘭英雄想像的雙極。伊爾瑪利寧的第一件、也是最偉大的作品，不是任何塵世之物。在世界之初，當天空被懸於大地之上時，是伊爾瑪利寧鍛造了那蒼穹——在他的鐵砧上，鎚出天穹的穹頂，如此巧妙地將之拼合，以至於從無人找得到接縫在哪裡。他把星辰鍛入其中。他把太陽與月亮的軌道鍛入其中。他造了天空本身。

And yet, the poem tells us, in one of its most poignant touches, the smith was not satisfied. When he had finished the sky and hung it up, he looked at it, and he wept — because, in all that vast and beautiful work, he had forgotten to hammer in the moon. The sky was perfect except for that one omission, and the omission grieved him for the rest of his life. This little detail is the key to Ilmarinen's character: he is the maker who can never be satisfied with what he has made, because he can always see the one flaw in it. The sky-god is also the god of imperfect work. And this restlessness — this constant reaching for a perfection that always escapes — is what will make him, in the next chapter, the man who can forge the Sampo.

然而，這首詩告訴我們，在其最令人心酸的一筆之中，這位鐵匠並不滿意。當他完成天空並將之掛起之後，他望著它，他哭了——因為，在那整件浩瀚而美麗的作品之中，他忘了把月亮鎚進去。那片天空，除了那一個遺漏之外是完美的，而那個遺漏，使他餘生都為之悲傷。這個小細節，是伊爾瑪利寧性格的鑰匙：他是那種永遠無法滿足於自己所造之物的造物者，因為他總能看見其中那唯一的瑕疵。那位天空之神，也是不完美之作的神。而這種不安——這種對一種總是逃逸之完美的持續追求——將使他在下一章裡，成為那個能鍛造桑波的人。

Ilmarinen is, in other words, the figure the Kalevala gives to craft itself — to the patient, flawed, endlessly-redone human labor of making things. He is not a magician, exactly; he is an artisan, the best there has ever been, and his power is the power of practice and patience and the refusal to accept "good enough." When Louhi demands the Sampo, it is Ilmarinen she is really demanding — the one being in the world whose skill is great enough, and whose dissatisfaction deep enough, to make a thing that grinds gold from stone. The singer Väinämöinen knows the words. The smith Ilmarinen knows the work. And the Sampo will need both.

換言之，伊爾瑪利寧，是《卡勒瓦拉》賦予「技藝」本身的形象——賦予那種製造事物的、耐心的、有瑕疵的、無盡重做的人類勞動。他不完全是個魔法師；他是個工匠，有史以來最好的，而他的力量，是練習、耐心，以及拒絕接受「夠好了」的力量。當婁希要求桑波時，她真正要求的其實是伊爾瑪利寧——世上唯一一個其技藝足夠偉大、其不滿足足夠深沉，以至於能造出一件能從石中磨出黃金之物的人。歌者維納莫寧知曉話語。鐵匠伊爾瑪利寧知曉工作。而桑波，將兩者皆需。

Chapter 2 · 第二章

The Trick & the Journey North

計謀與北行

Now Väinämöinen had promised Louhi the smith — but Ilmarinen, back home in the warm lands, had no wish to go to the frozen North and forge a wonder for a witch. He was content with his forge, his hammers, his unfinished work. So Väinämöinen, who was a singer and therefore also a trickster, used his craft to bring Ilmarinen to the North against his will. The poem gives us the scene with relish: Väinämöinen climbs a great pine tree and sings, and his song calls up a great wind, and the wind carries the sound of a hammer on an anvil, the ring of metal on metal — the sound of a forge that does not exist.

如今維納莫寧已向婁希承諾了那位鐵匠——但伊爾瑪利寧，回到溫暖故鄉的他，並不願前往冰封的北境、為一個女巫鍛造奇物。他滿足於他的鍛爐、他的鐵鎚、他未完成的工作。於是維納莫寧，身為一個歌者、因此也是個 trickster，運用他的技藝，違背伊爾瑪利寧之意將他帶到了北境。這首詩饒有興味地給了我們這個場面：維納莫寧爬上一棵大松樹，唱起歌，而他的歌喚起一陣大風，而那風帶來了鐵鎚敲在鐵砧上的聲響、金屬相擊的鏗鏘——一座並不存在的鍛爐之聲。

And Ilmarinen, hearing the phantom forge, could not resist. The smith in him leapt up. Here, he thought, was a master at work, ringing out sounds his own forge had never made — and he had to see it, had to learn from it, had to find whoever was making that music of metal. So he left his home, and followed the sound, and the sound led him North — past the last trees, into the cold, to the very hall of Louhi. And there the phantom forge fell silent, and Ilmarinen found himself, not at a rival's anvil, but at the witch's hall, with Väinämöinen smiling and Louhi waiting. The trick had worked. The smith had been called by the thing he could never resist: the sound of better work than his own.

而伊爾瑪利寧，聽見那幻影鍛爐，無法抗拒。他體內的鐵匠躍起。這裡，他想，有一位大師在工作，敲出他自己的鍛爐從未發出過的聲響——而他必須去看看，必須從中學習，必須找到是誰在製造那金屬的樂音。於是他離開家，追隨那聲響，而那聲響把他引向北方——越過最後的樹木，進入寒冷，直到婁希的大廳。而在那裡，那幻影鍛爐靜了下來，而伊爾瑪利寧發現自己，不是站在一個對手的鐵砧前，而是站在那個女巫的大廳裡，維納莫寧微笑著，婁希等待著。計謀奏效了。那位鐵匠，被他所無法抗拒之物所召喚：比自己更好的工作之聲。

This is the Kalevala's affectionate joke about its own greatest maker: that the one way to move a master craftsman is to make him believe someone else is making something finer. Ilmarinen is not tempted by gold, or by the maiden Louhi offers, or by fame. He is tempted by craft — by the possibility that there is work in the world better than his. And that temptation, the poem knows, is the secret of all making: the good craftsman is never satisfied because he is always imagining a better work, and he will follow that imagination anywhere, even into the cold North, even to a witch's hall. The forge of the Sampo begins, then, not with a command but with a longing — the smith's longing to hear the sound he cannot make.

這是《卡勒瓦拉》對自己最偉大造物者的一個充滿感情的玩笑：移動一位大師工匠的唯一方法，是讓他相信別人正在製造更精細之物。伊爾瑪利寧不被黃金所誘，不被婁希所許的少女所誘，不被名聲所誘。他被技藝所誘——被世上存在著比自己更好的工作這個可能性所誘。而那種誘惑，這首詩知道，是一切製造的秘密：好的工匠從不滿足，因為他總在想像一件更好的作品，而他會追隨那個想像到任何地方，即便進入寒冷的北境，即便到一個女巫的大廳。於是，桑波的鍛造，不是始於一個命令，而是始於一份渴望——鐵匠渴望聽見他所不能製造之聲。

Chapter 3 · 第三章

The Forging on the Rock

岩石之上的鍛造

And now the great work began. Louhi brought Ilmarinen to a bare rock on the shore of the Northern sea, and there he built his forge — the bellows, the anvil, the fire — and set to work. And the poem gives us the forging in detail, day after day, because the detail is the point. On the first day, Ilmarinen worked, and from his forge came a crossbow, golden, beautiful — but it asked, the poem says, for a sin, for blood, for a human life to be taken. And Ilmarinen, seeing that the work wanted evil, broke it up and put it back into the fire.

而如今，那件偉大的工作開始了。婁希將伊爾瑪利寧帶到北海岸邊的一塊裸岩之上，而在那裡他建起了他的鍛爐——風箱、鐵砧、火——開始工作。而這首詩逐日地給了我們鍛造的細節，因為細節便是重點。第一天，伊爾瑪利寧工作著，而從他的鍛爐裡出來了一把弩，金色的、美麗的——但它要求，這首詩說，一樁罪，一份血，一條人命被取。而伊爾瑪利寧，看見那件作品想要邪惡，便將之拆碎，放回火中。

On the second day, he worked again, and from the fire came a red ship — but it too wanted evil, it wanted to sail to war and to carry men to their deaths. And he broke it up, and put it back. And on the third day came a golden heifer, and it too was stained with the desire to harm. And the fourth day brought a golden plow, and it too wanted blood. And Ilmarinen, the great maker, broke each of them, one after another, because none of them was the thing he had been asked for, and all of them, the moment they were made, revealed that they wanted to do harm. The forge was producing wonders, but the wonders were all wrong. The Sampo would not come.

第二天，他又工作著，而從火中出來了一艘紅船——但它也想要邪惡，它想要駛向戰爭，運載人們去送死。而他將之拆碎，放回去。第三天出來一頭金色的小母牛，而它也沾染著傷害之欲。第四天帶來一把金犁，而它也想要血。而伊爾瑪利寧，那位偉大的造物者，將它們一個接一個地拆碎，因為它們沒有一個是他被要求製造之物，而它們全部，在被造出來的那一刻，便顯露出它們想要為害。鍛爐正在生產奇物，但那些奇物全都錯了。桑波不願降臨。

And on the fifth day — or the ninth, in some versions, for the poem loves to dwell — Ilmarinen set the bellows working and stepped back, and this time the forge produced, not a weapon, not a beast, but a thing the poem can barely describe: the Sampo. A mill. A wonder. It had three surfaces, the poem says — one ground out salt, one ground out money, one ground out flour. And it ground them endlessly, without stopping, as long as it was set going. Here, at last, was a wonder that wanted no blood — a wonder that only wanted to feed, to enrich, to make the land prosperous. The long sequence of broken wrongs had led, at last, to a right. The Sampo was forged. And the whole rest of the epic — the marriage, the theft, the sea-battle, the breaking — flows from this single object, hammered into being on a cold rock by a smith who refused, four times, to accept a wonder that did harm.

而在第五天——或第九天，在某些版本中，因為這首詩樂於停留——伊爾瑪利寧讓風箱運作，退後一步，而這一次，鍛爐產出的，不是一件武器，不是一頭野獸，而是一件這首詩幾乎無法描述之物：桑波。一座磨。一件奇物。它有三個面，這首詩說——一面磨出鹽，一面磨出錢，一面磨出麵粉。而它無盡地磨著，不停歇地，只要它被啟動。這裡，終於，是一件不想要血的奇物——一件只想要餵養、想要富足、想要使土地繁盛的奇物。那一長串被拆碎的錯，終於引向了一個對。桑波鍛成了。而這部史詩其餘的全部——那樁婚姻、那場盜取、那場海戰、那場碎裂——都從這件單一的物物流出，由一位拒絕了四次、不肯接受一件會為害之奇物的鐵匠，在一塊冷岩之上，鎚入存在。

Chapter 4 · 第四章

The Sampo & the Locked Hill

桑波與封鎖之丘

Ilmarinen gave the Sampo to Louhi, and Louhi took it — and the poem gives us, here, one of the defining images of the whole epic. The witch did not set the wonder-mill out in the open, to grind prosperity for everyone. She took it, the poem says, and locked it inside a great hill of solid rock — inside the mountain of Pohjola, behind nine locks, deep in the earth where no one could reach it. And there the Sampo sat, grinding out its salt and gold and flour for Louhi alone, for the North alone, while the lands to the south — the warm Kalevala, the lands of the heroes — grew poorer by the year.

伊爾瑪利寧將桑波交給婁希，而婁希收下了它——而這首詩，在這裡，給了我們整部史詩最具定義性的意象之一。那個女巫並沒有把那座奇蹟之磨放在露天，為每一個人磨出豐饒。她把它，這首詩說，鎖進了一座堅岩的大丘之中——波赫尤拉的山裡，九重鎖之後，大地深處，無人能及之處。而桑波便在那裡坐著，只為婁希、只為北境，磨出它的鹽、金、麵粉，而南方諸地——溫暖的卡勒瓦拉、英雄們的土地——卻一年比一年貧窮。

What is the Sampo? The poem deliberately refuses to say exactly, and the refusal is the source of its power. It is a mill, but it is also a talisman, a totem, the concentrated prosperity of a whole land. It is the thing every society wants — endless wealth, endless plenty, the magical machine that solves the problem of scarcity forever. And the Kalevala's deepest political teaching is here, in Louhi's act of locking it away: that such a thing, once it exists, is never shared. The powerful lock the wonder inside a mountain, and grind its gold for themselves, and the ordinary lands starve. The Sampo is not just a magic mill. It is the Finnish image of hoarded prosperity — the wealth that exists but is kept from those who need it. And the entire rest of the epic is the story of the attempt to break that mountain open and share what is inside.

桑波是什麼？這首詩刻意拒絕確切地說，而那個拒絕，正是其力量的來源。它是一座磨，但它也是一個護符、一個圖騰、一整片土地所凝聚的豐饒。它是每一個社會都想要的那件東西——無盡的財富、無盡的豐足、那台能永遠解決匱乏問題的魔法機器。而《卡勒瓦拉》最深的政治教誨，便在此，在婁希將之鎖起的這個行動裡：這樣一件物，一旦存在，從不被分享。權勢者把那奇物鎖在一座山裡，為自己磨它的金，而平凡的土地挨餓。桑波不只是一座魔法磨。它是芬蘭關於「被囤積之豐饒」的意象——那種存在著、卻被藏在需要者之外的財富。而這部史詩其餘的全部，便是那場嘗試破開那座山、分享其中之物的故事。

And Ilmarinen, having forged the wonder and given it away, was given the maiden of the North in return — Louhi's daughter, whom he had come for. But the poem tells us, with quiet sadness, that he did not take her home. He looked at her, and he looked at the cold land she came from, and he left her there, and sailed back south alone. Why? The poem does not quite say. Perhaps he understood, even then, that a bride won by a forge was not a bride freely given. Perhaps the long work on the cold rock had emptied him of wanting. Perhaps he knew — and the next chapters will prove him right — that the Sampo could not stay locked forever, and that the marriage it bought was built on stolen prosperity, and would not hold. The smith goes home empty-handed. The wonder stays in the mountain. And the theft is already germinating in the warm lands to the south.

而伊爾瑪利寧，既已鍛成那奇物並將之交出，作為回報得到了北境的少女——婁希的女兒，他所來求之人。但這首詞告訴我們，帶著一份沉靜的悲傷，他並未帶她回家。他望著她，他望著她所來的那片寒冷之地，他把她留在那裡，獨自航回南方。為什麼？這首詩並未明說。也許他明白，即便在那時，一個以鍛爐贏來的新娘，並非一個被自由給予的新娘。也許那塊冷岩上漫長的工作，已掏空了他的欲求。也許他知道——而接下來的章節將證明他是對的——桑波不可能永遠被鎖著，而它所買來的婚姻，建立在偷來的豐饒之上，不會持久。那位鐵匠兩手空空地回家了。那奇物留在山裡。而那場盜取，已在南方溫暖的土地上萌芽。

Chapter 5 · 第五章

Pohjola & the Promise of Return

波赫尤拉與歸來之諾

Let us pause, before the next hero takes the stage, to understand the geography of the Kalevala's moral world — because that geography is the engine of everything that follows. There are two lands. There is Kalevala — the warm south, the land of the heroes, the land of song and forge and ordinary human life, where the forests grow and the lakes are full of fish and people farm and marry and die in their time. And there is Pohjola — the frozen North, the land of cold and dark, ruled by the witch Louhi, where the Sampo is locked away, grinding its gold for no one's benefit but her own.

讓我們，在下一個英雄登場之前，停下來理解《卡勒瓦拉》道德世界的地理——因為那個地理，是其後一切事物的引擎。有兩片土地。有卡勒瓦拉——溫暖的南方、英雄們的土地、歌與鍛爐與平凡人類生活之地，那裡森林生長、湖泊滿魚、人們在各自的時辰裡耕作、嫁娶、死去。還有波赫尤拉——冰封的北境、寒冷與黑暗之地，由女巫婁希所統治，桑波被鎖在那裡，只為她一人的利益磨著它的金。

And these two lands are not just places. They are principles. Pohjola is the place where good things are hoarded, where wonder is locked behind nine locks and made to serve only its keeper. Kalevala is the place where things are shared, where song passes freely from singer to singer, where the forge is open. And the whole epic is, in its deepest reading, the story of the long conflict between the sharing land and the hoarding land — the warm south trying to break open the cold north's mountain and free the wonder inside. This is not a war between good and evil, exactly; Louhi is not a demon, she is a keeper, a defender of what is hers. But the poem's sympathies are clear: prosperity that is hoarded is prosperity that has betrayed its purpose, and a land that locks its wonders away is a land that must, eventually, be opened.

而這兩片土地不只是地方。它們是原則。波赫尤拉，是好物被囤積之地，是奇物被鎖在九重鎖之後、只為其守護者效力之地。卡勒瓦拉，是事物被分享之地，是歌自由地從歌者傳到歌者之地，是鍛爐開放之地。而整部史詩，在其最深層的解讀中，是分享之地與囤積之地之間那場漫長衝突的故事——溫暖的南方試圖破開寒冷北方的山、釋放其中的奇物。這不完全是善惡之間的戰爭；婁希不是惡魔，她是一個守護者，一個她之物件的捍衛者。但這首詩的同情是清楚的：被囤積的豐饒，是背叛了其目的之豐饒；而一個將其奇物鎖起的土地，是一個終將被打開的土地。

And so the stage is set for the rest of the epic, and the poem tells us so plainly. The Sampo is in the mountain. The heroes are in the south. And the south is growing poor, year by year, while the north grinds gold in secret. Someone will have to go and take the wonder back. But before that theft — which will need all three of the Kalevala's great heroes together, the singer and the smith and the rash young warrior — the poem first gives us the story of that third hero, the rash one, whose adventures and death and strange restoration will harden him into the last member of the crew. His name is Lemminkäinen. And his story is the next stage of our journey.

於是，這部史詩其餘部分的舞台便搭好了，而這首詞坦白地告訴我們。桑波在山裡。英雄們在南方。而南方一年比一年貧窮，而北方卻在暗中磨著金。必須有人去把那奇物奪回來。但在那場盜取之前——那場盜取將需要《卡勒瓦拉》的三位大英雄一同前往，歌者、鐵匠，以及那位魯莽的年輕戰士——這首詞先給了我們那第三位英雄的故事，那位魯莽者，他的冒險、他的死亡、他那奇異的復生，將使他鍛成那個隊伍的最後一員。他的名字是勒明蓋寧。而他的故事，便是我們旅程的下一階段。

THE WONDER LOCKED AWAY

被鎖起的奇物

The smith who forged the sky came to the cold North on the wind of a phantom forge, and on a bare rock he hammered out, day after day, the wrong wonders — a crossbow, a ship, a heifer, a plow, all stained with the will to harm — until at last the forge gave him the right one: the Sampo, the mill that grinds salt and gold and flour without end. And the witch of the North took it, and locked it inside a mountain, behind nine locks, to grind its wealth for her alone. The warm south grew poor. The theft was already forming in the heroes' minds. But first, the poem says, you must meet the rash one — Lemminkäinen — whose death and strange return are the next stage of the song.
