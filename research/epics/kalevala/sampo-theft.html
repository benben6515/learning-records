THE SAMPO STOLEN & BROKEN

桑　波　之　盜　與　碎　裂

THE GREAT VOYAGE · THE KANTELE · THE BATTLE AT SEA

The three heroes sail together — the singer, the smith, and the warrior who has been to death and back — to break open the mountain of the North and free the wonder that has been locked away. On the voyage, the ancient singer kills a giant fish and makes from its jawbone the kantele, the magic harp, whose music makes the stones themselves weep. The Sampo is seized. The witch pursues. And over the black water, in the great battle, the wonder-mill shatters — its fragments washing to the southern shore, where they grind their wealth still. The Sampo is lost, and found, and broken, and shared, all at once. 三位英雄一同啟航——歌者、鐵匠、以及那個曾到死亡又歸來的戰士——要破開北境的山、釋放被鎖起的奇物。航程之中，古老的歌者殺了一條巨魚，以其頷骨造出康特勒琴，那把其樂音令石頭也為之泣的魔法豎琴。桑波被奪。女巫追來。而在黑色的水面之上，在那場大戰之中，那座奇蹟之磨碎裂了——它的碎片沖上南方的海岸，至今仍在彼處磨出財富。桑波，既是失去，也是尋得，既是碎裂，也是分享，同時。

Chapter 1 · 第一章

The Building of the Ship

造船

Väinämöinen resolved, at last, to go and take the Sampo back. The south had grown poor; the wonder was locked in the mountain of the North; and the time had come to break it open. And the three heroes gathered — the ancient singer, the eternal smith, and the rash young warrior who had been to death and returned — and they set about building the ship that would carry them north. And the poem tells us that the ship was built partly by craft and partly by song — Väinämöinen singing the planks into shape, the nails into place, the whole vessel into seaworthiness, while Ilmarinen and Lemminkäinen worked the wood.

維納莫寧，終於，決意去把桑波奪回。南方已貧；那奇物被鎖在北境的山裡；而時候到了，要破開它。而三位英雄聚集了——古老的歌者、永恆的鐵匠、以及那個曾到死亡又歸來的魯莽年輕戰士——而他們著手建造那艘將載他們北行的船。而這首詞告訴我們，那艘船，一部分由技藝而造，一部分由歌而造——維納莫寧把木板唱成形、把釘子唱到位、把整艘船唱到適航，而伊爾瑪利寧與勒明蓋寧則加工那木頭。

And the poem, here, gives us one of its recurring moral touches: the heroes must build their own ship, by their own labor, because no journey to the great confrontation can be undertaken by someone else's work. The ship that carries you to your defining act must be made by your own hands, or it will not hold. And the three work together — the singer, the smith, the warrior — each contributing what only he can. This is the Finnish ideal of cooperation, and it stands in deliberate contrast to the lonely heroism of so many other epics: in the Kalevala, the great deeds are done by a crew, not a champion, and each member of the crew is essential, and none of them could do it alone.

而這首詞，在此處，給了我們其反覆出現的道德之筆之一：英雄們必須建造自己的船，以自己的勞動，因為前往那場大對決的旅程，不能由別人的工作來承擔。承載你前往你那定義性之舉的船，必須由你自己的手所造，否則它將不牢。而這三人一同工作——歌者、鐵匠、戰士——各自貢獻唯有他能貢獻之物。這是芬蘭關於合作的理念，而它刻意地與許多其他史詩的孤獨英雄氣概形成對比：在《卡勒瓦拉》裡，偉大的功業是由一個隊伍所為，而非一位冠軍，而這個隊伍的每一個成員都是不可或缺的，沒有誰能獨力完成。

And the ship was launched, and the three set out across the cold northern sea, toward the land of Pohjola, toward the mountain where the Sampo was locked. And the poem tells us that Väinämöinen, as they sailed, steered by his knowledge of the deeps — he knew where every rock was, where every current ran, because he had been on the sea since before there was land to come home to. The ship moved north. The cold grew. And somewhere ahead, in the mountain of the witch, the wonder-mill waited, grinding its gold in the dark. The theft was underway.

而那艘船下水了，三人出發，越過寒冷的北方之海，朝向波赫尤拉之地，朝向那座鎖著桑波的山。而這首詞告訴我們，維納莫寧，在他們航行之時，以其對深淵的知識來掌舵——他知道每一塊岩石在哪裡、每一股洋流往何處，因為他自從還沒有可歸之陸地之時便一直在海上。那艘船向北行。寒冷漸增。而在前方某處，在那個女巫的山中，那座奇蹟之磨等待著，在暗中磨著它的金。那場盜取已經展開。

Chapter 2 · 第二章

The Giant Fish & the Jawbone

巨魚與頷骨

On the voyage, a giant fish rose from the sea — a pike so vast it threatened to capsize the ship, its jaws wide enough to swallow the vessel whole. And the poem gives us, in this moment, a small but characteristic scene. Lemminkäinen drew his sword to fight it, and the sword broke on the fish's scales. Ilmarinen seized an oar, and the oar splintered. The warrior's strength and the smith's tool both failed. And then Väinämöinen — the singer, the old man, the one whose weapon was never a blade — took his own knife and, with a precision born of his deep knowledge, stabbed the great fish in exactly the one spot where it could be killed.

航程之中，一條巨魚從海中升起——一條巨大到足以掀翻船的狗魚，它的頷足以將整艘船吞下。而這首詞，在這一刻，給了我們一個小而具特徵性的場面。勒明蓋寧拔劍要與之搏鬥，而那劍在魚鱗上折斷了。伊爾瑪利寧抓起一支槳，而那槳碎裂了。戰士的力量與鐵匠的工具，都失敗了。然後維納莫寧——那位歌者、那位老人、那個武器從來不是刀刃之人——取了他自己的刀，以一份源於其深知的精準，刺中那條巨魚唯一能被殺死的那一個點。

And the great fish died, and its vast body floated beside the ship. And Väinämöinen looked at the carcass, and he saw in it not waste but material. He took the jawbone of the giant pike — a bone as long as a man, white, resonant — and he brought it out, and he set about making from it a thing the world had never seen. He fashioned the bone into the body of an instrument. He strung it with hairs from the demon-horse Lemminkäinen had bridled. And he made, from the jaw of a monster that had tried to eat them, the first kantele — the magic harp of the Kalevala.

而那條巨魚死了，它巨大的身軀漂浮在船旁。而維納莫寧望著那具屍體，他在其中看見的不是廢物，而是材料。他取下那條巨狗魚的頷骨——一根與人等長的骨頭，白的、有共鳴——而他把拿出來，開始用它製造一件世界前所未見之物。他把那塊骨頭塑造成一個樂器的琴身。他以勒明蓋寧所套過之惡魔馬的毛來張弦。而他用一條曾試圖吞噬他們的怪獸之頷，造出了第一把康特勒琴——《卡勒瓦拉》的魔法豎琴。

This is one of the poem's most beautiful touches — the turning of the thing that attacked you into the instrument of your victory. The kantele, made from the bone of an enemy, will be the weapon that defeats the witch of the North, because the kantele's music is a power no sword can match. The Kalevala, again and again, insists on this: that the deepest power is not force but art, that the jawbone of a monster, in the right hands, becomes a harp whose music can move the world. The instrument is being forged for the moment, a few chapters on, when its sound will lull a whole fortress to sleep. The voyage continues. The harp is aboard. The theft is still ahead.

這是這首詞最美麗的筆觸之一——將那個攻擊你之物，轉化為你勝利的樂器。那把康特勒琴，以敵人之骨所造，將成為擊敗北境女巫的武器，因為康特勒琴的樂音是一種刀劍無法匹敵的力量。《卡勒瓦拉》，一再又再，堅持這一點：最深的力量不是武力，而是藝術；一塊怪獸的頷骨，在對的手中，會成為一把其樂音能感動世界的豎琴。那件樂器，正為了幾章之後的那一刻而被鍛造——那個它的聲音將使一整座堡壘沉睡的時刻。航程繼續。豎琴在船上。那場盜取，仍在前方。

Chapter 3 · 第三章

The Music of the Kantele

康特勒琴之樂

The kantele was made, but no one could play it. Lemminkäinen tried, and his fingers were too clumsy; the notes would not come. Ilmarinen tried, and his hands, used to the hammer, could not find the strings' music. The instrument sat silent, waiting. And then Väinämöinen — the singer, the old man — took the kantele in his hands, and he played. And the poem, here, gives us one of the most celebrated passages in all of Finnish literature: the description of the music of the kantele.

康特勒琴造好了，卻無人能彈。勒明蓋寧試了，他的手指太笨拙；音符不肯來。伊爾瑪利寧試了，他那雙習慣了鐵鎚的手，找不到琴弦的樂音。那件樂器靜坐著，等待。然後維納莫寧——那位歌者、那位老人——把康特勒琴捧在手中，而他彈了。而這首詞，在此處，給了我們全部芬蘭文學中最受頌揚的段落之一：對康特勒琴之樂音的描述。

And the music was so beautiful that everything heard it. The fish rose to the surface of the water to listen. The birds stopped flying and perched on the mast. The animals of the forest came down to the shore. And then — the poem's most haunting image — the very stones began to weep. The rocks, the boulders, the crags of the cliffs, hearing the kantele's music, cracked and wept tears. The trees bent toward the sound. The whole of creation leaned in to listen, because the music was the sound of the world's own longing, given voice at last. And Väinämöinen wept too, the poem tells us — the old singer wept at his own music — and his tears fell into the water and became blue gems, sapphires and pearls.

而那樂音如此之美，以至於萬物都聽見了。魚升到水面上來聽。鳥停止飛翔，棲息在桅杆上。森林裡的動物下到岸邊來。然後——這首詞最縈繞人心的意象——連石頭都開始哭泣。岩石、巨礫、懸崖的峭壁，聽見康特勒琴的樂音，裂開、流下淚來。樹木向那聲音彎下身。整個造物都傾身來聽，因為那樂音，是世界自身之渴望的聲音，終於被賦予了聲音。而維納莫寧也哭了，這首詞告訴我們——那位古老的歌者為他自己的音樂而泣——而他的眼淚落入水中，成了藍色的寶石，藍寶石與珍珠。

This is the moment the Kalevala has been building toward since its first page — the moment when the singer's power is shown, fully, for what it is. The kantele's music is not entertainment. It is the deepest force in the world. It makes stones weep; it bends creation; it is the voice of the longing that made the world in the first place. And it is this music — not the sword, not the smith's hammer — that will defeat the witch of the North, when the heroes reach her fortress. The Kalevala's great claim, made here in its purest form, is that art is the highest power: that the song is stronger than the blade, that the harp made from a monster's bone can do what a hero's sword cannot. The next chapters will prove it.

這是《卡勒瓦拉》自其第一頁以來一直在鋪陳的那一刻——那位歌者的力量被完整展示為其所是的那一刻。康特勒琴的樂音不是娛樂。它是世上最深的力量。它使石頭哭泣；它使造物彎身；它是那最初造就世界之渴望的聲音。而正是這樂音——不是劍，不是鐵匠的鎚——將在英雄們抵達那個女巫的堡壘之時擊敗她。《卡勒瓦拉》最偉大的主張，在此以其最純粹的形式被提出：藝術是最高的力量；歌比刀刃更強；那把以怪獸之骨所造的豎琴，能做到英雄之劍所不能。接下來的章節將證明它。

Chapter 4 · 第四章

The Sampo Seized

桑波被奪

The ship reached the shore of Pohjola, and the three heroes disembarked, and Väinämöinen took the kantele and began to play. And the poem gives us, here, the great scene of the theft. The music floated up from the shore toward the fortress of Louhi — over the walls, through the windows, into every room. And the witch heard it, and her household heard it, and one by one they were overcome. The guards fell asleep at their posts. The servants dropped where they stood. Louhi herself, the formidable mistress of the North, felt her eyes grow heavy, felt her limbs go soft, felt the music pulling her down into a sleep deeper than any she had ever known. And the whole fortress slept.

那艘船抵達了波赫尤拉的海岸，三位英雄下船，而維納莫寧拿起康特勒琴開始彈奏。而這首詞，在此處，給了我們那場盜取的偉大場面。那樂音從岸上飄向婁希的堡壘——越過城牆，穿過窗戶，進入每一個房間。而那個女巫聽見了，她的僕從聽見了，一個接一個地，他們被克服了。守衛在崗位上睡著了。僕人們在所站之處倒下。婁希本人，那位可怕的北境女主人，感覺到她的眼皮沉重，感覺到她的四肢發軟，感覺到那樂音把她拉入一個比她所知的任何睡眠都更深的沉睡。而整座堡壘睡了。

And the three heroes walked into the sleeping fortress, and they found the great rock hill where the Sampo was locked, and Lemminkäinen — the rash one, the one whose gift was the bold impossible act — set to work breaking it open. He seized the locks, one by one, and wrenched them free. And the poem tells us that the locks screamed as they broke — a sound that should have woken the witch, would have woken her, except that the kantele's music held her under. The music was stronger than the alarm. The art was stronger than the watch. And at last the hill was open, and there, inside, sat the Sampo — the wonder-mill, grinding its gold in the dark, exactly as Ilmarinen had left it.

而三位英雄走進沉睡的堡壘，他們找到了那座鎖著桑波的大岩丘，而勒明蓋寧——那個魯莽者，那個其天賦是大膽不可能之舉的人——著手破開它。他抓住那些鎖，一個一個，把它們扭開。而這首詞告訴我們，那些鎖在被破開之時尖叫——一個本該驚醒那個女巫的聲音，本會驚醒她，要不是康特勒琴的樂音把她壓在底下。那樂音比警報更強。那藝術比守衛更強。而終於那座丘開了，而在裡面，坐著桑波——那座奇蹟之磨，在暗中磨著它的金，正如伊爾瑪利寧所留下它的那樣。

And they seized it. The three heroes took up the Sampo — the wonder that one of them had forged and all of them had come for — and carried it out of the mountain, and down to the ship, and set it aboard. The theft was done. The wonder was free. And the ship pushed off from the shore of the sleeping North, and the three sailed south, toward home, carrying the prosperity of the world in their hull. But the poem does not let us rejoice for long. The witch will wake. The pursuit is coming. And the Sampo, the poem knows, is too great a thing to be carried whole across the sea.

而他們奪了它。三位英雄抬起桑波——那件其中一人所鍛造、三人同來所求之奇物——把它抬出山，抬到船上，置於船上。那場盜取完成了。那奇物自由了。而那艘船推離沉睡北境的海岸，三人航向南方，朝向故鄉，船艙裡載著世界的豐饒。但這首詞不讓我們歡慶太久。那個女巫會醒。追擊即將到來。而桑波，這首詞知道，是一件太大之物，無法完整地被運過大海。

Chapter 5 · 第五章

The Witch Pursues

女巫追來

And Louhi woke. The music's hold broke, and the witch of the North rose from her unnatural sleep, and she found the hill open, and the locks broken, and the Sampo — her Sampo, the wonder-mill that ground the wealth of her whole land — gone. And her fury, the poem tells us, was a thing that shook the sky. She called for her army, and they were slow. She called for her ships, and the wind was against them. And so Louhi, in her rage, did what only a witch of her power could do: she transformed.

而婁希醒了。那樂音的掌控破了，而那位北境的女巫從她那不自然的沉睡中起身，而她發現山開了，鎖破了，而桑波——她的桑波，那座磨著她整片土地財富的奇蹟之磨——不見了。而她的狂怒，這首詞告訴我們，是一種撼動天空之物。她召喚她的軍隊，而他們遲緩。她召喚她的船，而風與他們相逆。於是婁希，在她的狂怒之中，做了唯有她那般力量的女巫才能做的事：她變形了。

She turned herself into a great eagle — a vast bird of prey, with wings that darkened the sun, with talons that could tear a ship apart — and she rose from the fortress and flew out over the cold sea, toward the fleeing ship, faster than the wind. And the poem gives us, in this pursuit, one of its most thrilling passages: the great eagle gaining on the ship, the three heroes looking back in terror, the dark shape growing in the sky behind them. Lemminkäinen seized his weapons. Ilmarinen took the tiller. And Väinämöinen — the singer, the one whose power was never the sword — spoke to the sea and the wind, trying to speed their flight. But the eagle was faster. The witch was coming.

她把自己變成一隻巨鷹——一頭巨大的猛禽，雙翼遮暗了太陽，利爪足以撕碎一艘船——而她從堡壘中升起，飛越寒冷的大海，朝著那艘逃亡的船，比風更快。而這首詞，在這場追逐中，給了我們其最驚心動魄的段落之一：那隻巨鷹逼近那艘船，三位英雄驚恐地回望，那個黑色的形體在他們身後的天空中增大。勒明蓋寧抓起他的武器。伊爾瑪利寧掌住舵。而維納莫寧——那位歌者，那個其力量從來不是劍之人——向大海與風說話，試圖加速他們的逃亡。但那隻鷹更快。那個女巫來了。

And the poem tells us that Louhi, in her eagle-form, brought with her a great storm — the wind rising, the waves mounting, the sea turning black — and that the storm was the worst the three had ever faced. And here, in the chaos of the pursuit, comes the moment the whole theft has been hurtling toward: the moment when the Sampo, the great wonder-mill, will be fought over on the deck of a heaving ship, in the teeth of a witch's storm, and will not survive the fighting. The pursuit is not the climax. The pursuit is the road to the climax. And the climax — the breaking of the wonder — is the next chapter.

而這首詞告訴我們，婁希，在她的鷹形之中，帶來了一場大風暴——風起、浪湧、海變黑——而那場風暴是三人所面對過最糟的。而在這裡，在追逐的混亂之中，來到了整場盜取一直向其衝去的那一刻：那個桑波、那座偉大的奇蹟之磨，將在一艘起伏的船的甲板上、在一個女巫風暴的鋒牙之中被爭奪，而將無法在爭奪中存活的時刻。追逐不是高潮。追逐是通往高潮之路。而高潮——那奇物之碎裂——是下一章。

Chapter 6 · 第六章

The Breaking Over the Water

水面之上的碎裂

And the eagle caught the ship. Louhi, in her vast form, descended on the deck, and her talons closed on the Sampo, and the battle began. And the poem gives us, here, the climactic struggle of the whole epic — the three heroes and the witch fighting over the wonder-mill, on the deck of a storm-tossed ship, in the middle of the black sea. Lemminkäinen struck at the eagle with his broken sword. Ilmarinen held the Sampo and tried to pull it free. And Väinämöinen sang — sang to calm the storm, sang to weaken the witch, sang with everything he had.

而那隻鷹追上了船。婁希，在她巨大的形體中，降落在甲板上，她的利爪攫住桑波，而戰鬥開始了。而這首詞，在此處，給了我們整部史詩的高潮之爭——三位英雄與那個女巫，在一艘風暴顛簸的船的甲板上、在黑色大海的中央，為那座奇蹟之磨而戰。勒明蓋寧以他折斷的劍擊向那隻鷹。伊爾瑪利寧抓住桑波，試圖把它拉出。而維納莫寧唱著——唱以平息風暴，唱以削弱女巫，以他所有的一切唱著。

And in the struggle, the Sampo broke. The wonder-mill, fought over by forces too great for any object to hold, cracked — and then shattered — and then, the poem tells us, the pieces flew everywhere. Some fell into the sea, and sank to the bottom, and were lost. Some were carried off by the eagle, by Louhi, who seized what she could as the mill came apart. And some — and this is the heart of the matter — were washed, by the waves and the wind, toward the south, toward the shore of Kalevala, toward the land of the heroes who had tried to steal it.

而在那場爭奪之中，桑波破了。那座奇蹟之磨，被對它而言任何物件都無法承受的力量所爭奪，裂了——然後碎了——然後，這首詞告訴我們，那些碎片飛向四面八方。有些落入海中，沉到海底，失去了。有些被那隻鷹、被婁希帶走，她在那座磨崩解之時能搶多少便搶多少。而有些——而這便是事情的核心——被波浪與風，沖向南方，沖向卡勒瓦拉的海岸，沖向那些試圖盜取它的英雄們的土地。

The Sampo was broken. The wonder was not recovered whole. The great theft, which the whole epic had been building toward, did not end in victory or in defeat — it ended in both, and in neither. The heroes did not get the intact wonder; the witch did not keep her treasure. The mill was shattered, its pieces scattered between the lands, and no one possessed it whole ever again. This is the Kalevala's most characteristic move, and its deepest teaching about the great prizes of the world: that the things most worth having are not had, in the end, by anyone. They are broken in the争夺 over them, and their pieces are shared — imperfectly, unequally — among the very people who fought for them. The next chapter will tell us what the fragments do, washed up on the southern shore.

桑波碎了。那奇物並未被完整尋回。那場整部史詩一直在鋪陳的偉大盜取，沒有結束於勝利，也沒有結束於失敗——它結束於兩者，也結束於兩者皆非。英雄們沒有得到完整的奇物；那個女巫也沒有保住她的寶物。那座磨碎了，它的碎片散布於諸地之間，再也無人完整地擁有它。這是《卡勒瓦拉》最具特徵性的舉措，也是它關於世上大獎的最深教誨：那些最值得擁有之物，最終，不被任何人所擁有。它們在對它們的爭奪中碎裂，而它們的碎片，被分享——不完美地、不平等地——於那些為它們而戰的人之間。下一章將告訴我們，那些沖上南方海岸的碎片，做了什麼。

Chapter 7 · 第七章

The Fragments on the Shore

岸上的碎片

And the fragments washed ashore. The pieces of the Sampo that the waves had carried south landed, at last, on the coast of Kalevala — on the beaches and the rocks of the heroes' own land. And the poem tells us, in its closing of this great central act, what those fragments did. They did not lie dead on the sand. They ground. The broken pieces of the wonder-mill, lying in the southern shore, kept on grinding — salt, and gold, and flour — but in lesser measure than before, and only for the land where they had washed up.

而那些碎片沖上了岸。被波浪帶向南方的桑波碎片，終於落在卡勒瓦拉的海岸上——落在英雄們自己土地的沙灘與岩石上。而這首詞，在它對這場偉大核心之舉的收束裡，告訴了我們那些碎片做了什麼。它們沒有死躺在沙上。它們磨著。那座奇蹟之磨的破碎碎片，躺在南方的岸上，繼續磨著——鹽、金、麵粉——但比以前少了，而且只為它們所沖上之土地。

And so the south, which had been growing poor, became prosperous again — not as prosperous as it would have been with the whole Sampo, but prosperous enough. The salt fed the fisheries. The gold enriched the coffers. The flour fed the people. The land recovered. And the poem tells us, here, the deep truth of its central myth: that the wealth of the world is always broken, always shared imperfectly, always lying in fragments on whatever shore receives it. The Sampo is not a thing you can own whole. The Sampo is the way prosperity actually exists — scattered, partial, ground out in small amounts by the broken pieces of a greater whole, distributed by the wind and the waves to whoever happens to be standing on the shore.

於是那個一直在變貧的南方，又變得繁盛了——不如擁有整座桑波那般繁盛，但已足夠繁盛。鹽餵養了漁業。金充實了國庫。麵粉餵養了百姓。那片土地恢復了。而這首詞，在此處，告訴了我們其核心神話的深層真理：世界的財富，總是破碎的，總是不完美地被分享，總是以碎片的形式躺在任何接收它的海岸之上。桑波不是一件你可以完整擁有的物。桑波是豐饒實際存在的方式——散落的、部分的、由一個更大整體的碎片少量地磨出、由風與浪分配給任何恰巧站在岸上的人。

This is the Kalevala's great ambivalent ending to its great central act — and it is an ending that refuses to be either triumph or tragedy. The heroes did not win the Sampo. They won fragments of it. The witch did not keep her treasure; she kept fragments of it. The wonder was broken, and shared, and lost, and found, all at once — and the land of the heroes became richer than it had been, but poorer than it might have been, forever. This is, I think, the most honest image of how the great prizes of human life actually work: not possessed, but participated in; not whole, but in pieces; ground out, day by day, by the broken fragments of a wonder no one will ever hold entire. The Sampo is over. The poem turns, now, to its darkest story — the tragedy of Kullervo.

這是《卡勒瓦拉》對其偉大核心之舉那偉大而含糊的結局——而這是一個拒絕成為勝利或悲劇的結局。英雄們沒有贏得桑波。他們贏得了它的碎片。那個女巫沒有保住她的寶物；她保住了它的碎片。那奇物碎了，被分享了，失去了，又尋得了，同時——而英雄們的土地變得比以前更富，卻永遠比它本可能的更貧。我想，這是關於人生大獎實際如何運作最誠實的意象：不被擁有，而被參與；不完整，而以碎片；日復一日，由一件無人能完整擁有的奇物之破碎碎片所磨出。桑波結束了。這首詞，如今，轉向它最黑暗的故事——庫勒沃的悲劇。

BROKEN, AND SHARED

碎裂，且分享

The three heroes sailed north, and the singer made a harp from the jaw of a monster, and the harp's music made the stones weep and lulled a fortress to sleep. They broke the locks and seized the wonder. The witch woke and came as an eagle, and over the black water the great mill shattered — its pieces flying everywhere, some sinking, some seized, some washing south. And those southern fragments kept grinding, in lesser measure, and the land grew rich on the broken pieces of a wonder no one ever held whole. The Sampo is over. The darkest story follows.
