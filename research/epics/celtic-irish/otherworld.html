TÍR NA NÓG & THE LIVING MYTH

青　春　之　地　與　活　的　神　話

Niamh · Oisín's return · Yeats & Lady Gregory · the Celtic Revival

An old warrior, the last of the Fianna, met a golden-haired woman on a white horse who took him to Tír na nÓg, the Land of Youth — where three hundred years passed like a single afternoon, where no one aged, where the feasting and the hunting never ended. And then he went home — and found his father gone, his comrades dust, his whole world vanished — and dismounted from the horse, and aged three hundred years in an instant, and crumbled. This is the Celtic image of the Otherworld: always near, always beautiful, always impossible to keep. And then, in the nineteenth century, the poets gathered the old stories back, and gave them to a nation that needed them to remember who it was. 一位老戰士，菲安納的最後一人，遇見一位金髮女子騎著白馬，帶他去了 Tír na nÓg，青春之地——那裡三百年如一個午後，那裡無人變老，那裡的盛宴與狩獵永不結束。然後他回家了——發現他的父親不在了，他的戰友成了塵土，他的整個世界消失了——而從馬上下來，一瞬之間老了三百歲，化為塵土。這便是塞爾特關於異世界的意象：永遠在近處，永遠美麗，永遠無法留住。然後，在十九世紀，詩人們重新採集那些古老的故事，交給一個需要它們來記住自己是誰的民族。

Chapter 1 · 第一章

The Land of Youth

青春之地

Oisín, the poet of the Fianna, was hunting one day when a beautiful woman rode up to him on a white horse — Niamh of the Golden Hair, the tradition names her, daughter of the king of Tír na nÓg. And she had come for Oisín. She had heard of his beauty and his poetry, and she had crossed from the Land of Youth to the mortal world to take him back with her — to be her husband, to live in the land where no one grew old, where the feasting never ceased, where time itself ran differently.

奧伊辛，菲安納的詩人，某日正在狩獵，一個美麗的女子騎著白馬來到他面前——傳統稱她為金髮的妮芙，Tír na nÓg 之王的女兒。而她是為奧伊辛而來。她聽說了他的俊美與他的詩，而她從青春之地跨越到凡間，要帶他回去——做她的丈夫，住進那片無人變老、盛宴不息、時間本身以不同方式流逝的土地。

And Oisín, the tradition tells us, hesitated. He thought of his father, Finn, old and wandering. He thought of his comrades. But Niamh promised him he could return any time — that the white horse would carry him back whenever he wished — and the promise, and the beauty, and the pull of the Land of Youth, won him over. And he mounted the horse behind Niamh, and they rode — over the sea, the tradition says, the white horse galloping on the waves — and they crossed into Tír na nÓg, the Land of Youth, the land beneath the western sea, and Oisín entered a world where three hundred years would pass like a single afternoon.

而奧伊辛，傳統告訴我們，猶豫了。他想到了他的父親，芬恩，年邁而流浪。他想到了他的戰友。但妮芙答應他，他隨時可以歸來——那匹白馬會在任何他願意之時載他回去——而那個承諾、那份美、青春之地的牽引，征服了他。而他跨上馬，坐在妮芙身後，而她們策馬——越過大海，傳統說，那匹白馬在海浪上奔馳——而她們跨入了 Tír na nÓg，青春之地，那片西海之下的土地，而奧伊辛進入了一個三百年將如一個午後般過去的世界。

Tír na nÓg is the great image of the Celtic Otherworld, and it is unlike the afterlives of any other tradition in this garden. It is not a heaven of reward, nor a hell of punishment, nor a wheel of rebirth. It is simply a place where time does not work — where the body does not fail, where the feast does not end, where youth is eternal and sorrow unknown. And the deep teaching of the image is this: that the Otherworld is not far. Oisín did not die to reach it. He rode there, on a horse, over the sea. The Land of Youth is one journey away, for anyone — and the only price of staying is that you cannot, ever, come back to the mortal world and remain. The boundary is the whole point. And Oisín, in the next chapter, will test it.

Tír na nÓg 是塞爾特異世界的偉大意象，而它不同於這座園中任何其他傳統的來世。它不是一個獎賞的天堂，也不是一個懲罰的地獄，也不是一個重生之輪。它只是一個時間不運作之地——那裡身體不衰，盛宴不止，青春永恆而悲傷不知。而那個意象的深層教誨是：異世界並不遠。奧伊辛不是死後才到達它。他騎馬而去，越過大海。青春之地只隔一趟旅程，對任何人——而停留的唯一代價是，你永遠不能回到凡間而保全自身。那個邊界便是全部的重點。而奧伊辛，在下一章裡，將試探它。

Chapter 2 · 第二章

Oisín's Return

奧伊辛之歸

And after three hundred years — which had passed, for Oisín, like a season — the old longing came back. He thought of his father, and the Fianna, and Ireland, and he told Niamh he wished to visit, just to see his people once more. And Niamh, who knew, gave him the white horse, and one warning: do not dismount. Do not set foot on the mortal ground. For if you touch the soil of Ireland with your foot, the three hundred years will fall on you in an instant, and you will never return.

而在三百年之後——對奧伊辛而言，那如同一個季節——那個舊的思念回來了。他想到了他的父親、菲安納、愛爾蘭，而他告訴妮芙他想去看看，只是再見他的族人一次。而妮芙，她知道，給了他那匹白馬，和一個警告：不要下馬。不要讓腳踏上凡間的土地。因為如果你用腳觸及愛爾蘭的土壤，那三百年便會在一瞬之間落於你身，而你將永遠無法歸來。

And Oisín rode the white horse back to Ireland — and the tradition gives us the terrible moment of his arrival. The Ireland he found was not his Ireland. The Fianna were gone. Finn was gone — dead for centuries, the tradition tells us, the great hero fallen at last as all heroes fall. The great halls were ruins. The people spoke a changed tongue. And no one Oisín met had ever heard of the Fianna — they were legend, myth, a story the old ones half-remembered. And Oisín, on his horse, riding through a country that had forgotten his whole world, began to understand what Niamh had meant. He had been gone three hundred years. His people were dust. His age was over.

而奧伊辛騎著那匹白馬回到了愛爾蘭——而傳統給了我們他抵達的那個可怖時刻。他所找到的愛爾蘭不是他的愛爾蘭。菲安納不在了。芬恩不在了——死了數世紀了，傳統告訴我們，那位偉大的英雄終究如所有英雄般倒下了。那些大廳成了廢墟。人們說著一種改變了的語言。而奧伊辛所遇之人，無人聽說過菲安納——它們是傳奇、神話，一個老人半記得的故事。而奧伊辛，在他的馬上，騎過一個已經忘記他整個世界的國度，開始明白妮芙所說之意。他離開了三百年。他的族人是塵土。他的時代結束了。

And then the accident. The tradition tells us Oisín, riding, saw some men struggling to lift a great stone — and, remembering the strength of the Fianna, he leaned down from his horse to help them. And the saddle-girth broke, or his foot slipped — and he fell — and his foot touched the soil of Ireland. And in that instant, the three hundred years fell on him. His youth vanished. His body, that had been young for three centuries in Tír na nÓg, withered, aged, bent — and the strong warrior became a frail old man, lying on the ground, dying. The men who had been struggling with the stone fled in terror. And Oisín, the last of the Fianna, lay in the dust of an Ireland that had forgotten him, and aged, and knew he would never see Niamh again, nor Tír na nÓg, nor his father's face. He had touched the mortal ground. The boundary had claimed him. The next chapter tells what the boundary means — and why the Celtic imagination has been haunted by it ever since.

然後那場意外發生了。傳統告訴我們，奧伊辛，騎著馬，看見一些人在費力搬一塊大石——而，記起菲安納的力量，他從馬上俯身去幫他們。而馬鞍的肚帶斷了，或他的腳滑了——而他摔了下來——而他的腳觸及了愛爾蘭的土壤。而在那一瞬，那三百年落於他身。他的青春消失了。他那個在 Tír na nÓg 裡年輕了三世紀的身體，枯萎了、老了、彎了——而那位強壯的戰士成了一個虛弱的老人，躺在地上，將死。那些在搬石頭的人驚恐地逃了。而奧伊辛，菲安納的最後一人，躺在一個已經忘記他的愛爾蘭的塵土裡，老了，並且知道他再也見不到妮芙了，也見不到 Tír na nÓg，也見不到他父親的臉。他觸及了凡間的土地。那個邊界認領了他。下一章講述那個邊界意味著什麼——以及為什麼塞爾特的想像此後一直被它所縈繞。

Chapter 3 · 第三章

The Otherworld Always Near

永在近處的異世界

And Oisín's story is the key to the whole Celtic understanding of the Otherworld, and it sets the Irish imagination apart from every other in this garden. In Oisín's fall, the tradition reveals its deepest truth: that the Otherworld is not a place you go to after death. It is a place that exists right now, beside this one, and the boundary between them is the most dangerous thing in the world. To cross into the Otherworld is to leave mortal time — and to cross back is to have that time fall on you at once. You cannot live in both. You cannot have the eternal youth and the mortal love. The boundary is absolute.

而奧伊辛的故事是整個塞爾特對異世界之理解的鑰匙，而它使愛爾蘭的想像與這座園中其他每一個都不同。在奧伊辛的摔落裡，傳統揭示了它最深的真理：異世界不是你死後去的地方。它是一個此刻便存在、在此世界之旁的地方，而它們之間的邊界，是世界上最危險之物。跨入異世界，便是離開凡間的時間——而跨回來，便是讓那段時間一次落於你身。你不能兩者皆有。你不能同時擁有永恆的青春與凡間的愛。那個邊界是絕對的。

This is why the Celtic stories are so full of longing, and so full of loss. Every story of a mortal taken to the sídhe is a story of someone who gained eternity and lost everything mortal. Every story of someone who returns from the Otherworld is a story of ruin — Oisín crumbling, or a musician who plays the fairy-tune and finds a hundred years have passed, or a man who sleeps under a fairy mound and wakes to find his family gone. The Irish Otherworld is beautiful and it is a trap, and the tradition is honest about both. You can have the Land of Youth. You can never come home.

這便是為什麼塞爾特的故事如此充滿思念，也如此充滿失落。每一個凡人被帶入妖精之丘的故事，都是一個某人得到了永恆卻失去一切凡間之物的故事。每一個從異世界歸來之人的故事，都是一個毀滅的故事——奧伊辛化為塵土，或一個樂手演奏仙靈之曲而發現一百年已過，或一個人在妖精之丘下睡著而醒來發現他的家人都不在了。愛爾蘭的異世界是美麗的，也是一個陷阱，而傳統對兩者都誠實。你可以擁有青春之地。你永遠回不了家。

And this doubleness — the beauty always beside us, the boundary always absolute — is the thing that haunts the Irish landscape, and the Irish imagination, more than anything else. The green hills are beautiful because the Otherworld might be inside them. The mist is dangerous because you might step through it. The strange music from the hollow hill might be the sídhe, calling. The Celtic tradition does not put its gods far away. It puts them right here — one step, one wrong step, away. And this is why, of all the gardens in this collection, the Irish one feels the most alive — because the tradition insists that the magic never ended. The gods are under the hills. The Otherworld is beside us. And the stories, the tradition tells us, are not memories of something that was. They are descriptions of something that is.

而這份雙重性——那份總在我們身旁的美，那個總是絕對的邊界——是那個比任何其他事物都更縈繞愛爾蘭風景、也縈繞愛爾蘭想像之物。那些翠綠的山丘是美的，因為異世界可能就在它們裡面。那片迷霧是危險的，因為你可能踏過它。那從空心山丘傳來的奇異音樂，可能是妖精的呼喚。塞爾特傳統不把它的諸神放得很遠。它把它們放在這裡——一步、一個錯誤的步，之遙。而這便是為什麼，在這個集子的所有花園之中，愛爾蘭的那個感覺最活——因為傳統堅持，那份魔法從未結束。諸神在山丘之下。異世界在我們身旁。而那些故事，傳統告訴我們，不是某個曾經存在之物的記憶。它們是某個正在存在之物的描述。

Chapter 4 · 第四章

The Celtic Revival

塞爾特復興

And then, like the Kalevala, the Irish stories nearly died — and were saved, in the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, by people who refused to let them go. Ireland had been conquered, again and again — by the Normans, by the English — and the old language had retreated, the old stories had faded, and the Irish, like the Finns, were becoming a people who had forgotten who they were. And then a movement began — the Celtic Revival — and its figures are among the most important in modern literature.

然後，一如《卡勒瓦拉》，愛爾蘭的故事幾近死去——而在十九與二十世紀初，被那些不肯放手之人所救。愛爾蘭被征服了一次又一次——被諾曼人、被英格蘭人——而古老的語言退縮了，古老的故事褪色了，而愛爾蘭人，一如芬蘭人，正在成為一個忘記自己是誰的民族。然後一場運動開始了——塞爾特復興——而它的那些人物，是現代文學中最重要的之一。

W.B. Yeats, the great poet, devoted his early career to gathering and re-imagining the old stories — the Cúchulainn cycle, the Fenian cycle, the tales of the sídhe. Lady Augusta Gregory, his friend and collaborator, translated the myths into clear English prose so that the world could read them. Douglas Hyde founded the Gaelic League, dedicated to preserving the Irish language itself. And a whole generation of writers and artists — Synge, O'Grady, Stephens — turned to the old traditions and made them new. The stories of Cúchulainn and Finn, of the Tuatha Dé Danann and Tír na nÓg, were not merely collected. They were reimagined, reinterpreted, given to a new century as the foundation of a national identity.

W.B. 葉慈，那位偉大的詩人，將他早期的事業奉獻於採集與重新想像那些古老的故事——庫丘林循環、芬尼亞循環、妖精之丘的故事。奧古斯塔·葛瑞格里夫人，他的友人與合作者，將那些神話譯成清晰的英文散文，好讓世界能讀到它們。道格拉斯·海德創立了蓋爾聯盟，致力於保存愛爾蘭語本身。而整整一個世代的作家與藝術家——辛格、奧格雷迪、史蒂芬斯——轉向那些古老的傳統，使之更新。庫丘林與芬恩的故事、達南神族與 Tír na nÓg 的故事，不只是被採集。它們被重新想像、重新詮釋，作為一個民族認同的根基，交給了一個新的世紀。

And the revival was not merely literary — it was political. The Ireland that read the old stories, that remembered Cúchulainn and the Fianna, was the Ireland that rose, in 1916, against British rule, in the Easter Rising. The leaders of the Rising invoked Cúchulainn — tied to his stone, dying upright — as the image of the Ireland they wanted to be: unconquered, unbroken, willing to die standing. The road from the Celtic Revival to Irish independence runs, like the road from the Kalevala to Finnish independence, more or less straight. A people that recovers its myths recovers itself. The poets who gathered the old stories were not merely preserving literature. They were rebuilding a nation. And the stories, given back to the people, did what they had always done: they made the Irish remember who they were.

而那場復興不只是文學的——它是政治的。那個讀著古老故事、記得庫丘林與菲安納的愛爾蘭，便是那個在 1916 年、在復活節起義中起來反抗英國統治的愛爾蘭。起義的領袖們援引庫丘林——被綁在他的石上、挺立而死——作為他們想要成為的愛爾蘭的形象：未被征服、未被擊碎、願意站著死。從塞爾特復興到愛爾蘭獨立的那條路，一如從《卡勒瓦拉》到芬蘭獨立的那條路，或多或少是筆直的。一個尋回其神話的民族，尋回它自己。那些採集古老故事的詩人，不只是在保存文學。他們是在重建一個國家。而那些故事，被還給人民之後，做了它們一直所做之事：它們使愛爾蘭人記起他們是誰。

Chapter 5 · 第五章

The Myth That Will Not Die

不死之神話

And the stories live. This is the final truth of the Celtic tradition, and the thing that makes it, perhaps, the most tenacious of all the gardens in this collection: the Irish myths never quite became "mythology" — stories about the old gods, safely in the past, studied by scholars. They remained, and remain, alive — in the landscape, in the language, in the way the Irish still half-believe in the fairies, still warn their children about the hollow hills, still feel the thinness of the boundary between this world and the next. The Tuatha Dé Danann went under the hills, and they are still there, and the Irish still know it.

而那些故事活著。這是塞爾特傳統最終的真理，也是使它或許成為這個集子所有花園中最頑強之物的道理：愛爾蘭的神話從未完全成為「神話學」——關於古老諸神的、安全地置於過去的、由學者所研究的故事。它們保持著、並保持著，活著——在風景裡、在語言裡、在愛爾蘭人仍然半信妖精、仍然警告他們的孩子關於空心山丘、仍然感覺到這個世界與下一個世界之間邊界之薄的方式裡。達南神族走入了山丘之下，而他們仍在那裡，而愛爾蘭人仍然知道這一點。

And the tradition gives us, in its closing, the figure who embodies this — old Oisín himself, the last of the Fianna, lying dying in the dust after his return from Tír na nÓg. And the tradition tells us that St. Patrick — the saint who brought Christianity to Ireland, the representative of the new faith that replaced the old — found the dying Oisín, and tended him, and listened to him. And Oisín, in his last days, told Patrick everything — about Finn, and the Fianna, and the Salmon of Knowledge, and the warrior-band, and the age that was gone. And Patrick, the Christian saint, wrote it down. The new faith did not destroy the old stories. It preserved them. The very monk-scribes who copied the gospels also copied the Táin. And because they did, the old myths survived — the only complete Celtic mythology in Europe, saved by the people whose faith it was meant to replace.

而傳統，在其收束裡，給了我們那個體現此事的形象——年邁的奧伊辛本人，菲安納的最後一人，從 Tír na nÓg 歸來之後躺在塵土裡將死。而傳統告訴我們，聖派翠克——那個為愛爾蘭帶來基督教、那個取代了舊信仰的新信仰之代表——發現了將死的奧伊辛，照料他，並聆聽他。而奧伊辛，在他的最後日子裡，告訴派翠克一切——關於芬恩、菲安納、知識之鮭、戰士團，以及那個已逝的時代。而派翠克，那位基督教聖人，把它寫了下來。新信仰沒有毀掉那些古老的故事。它保存了它們。那些抄寫福音書的修士書記，也抄寫了《奪牛記》。而因為他們如此做，那些古老的神話存活了下來——歐洲唯一完整的塞爾特神話學，被那個其信仰本是要取代它之人所救。

This is the great paradox of the Celtic tradition, and its great gift. The old gods were defeated, and went under the hills, and became fairies. And then the fairies became stories. And then the stories were nearly forgotten. And then the stories were saved — by saints, and by poets, and by a nation that needed them. The Tuatha Dé Danann are under the hills. Cúchulainn is tied to his stone. Finn is wandering somewhere in the wild. Oisín told his stories to a saint and crumbled to dust. And the stories are still being told — in Irish, and in English, and in every language that has fallen in love with the green island and its ghosts. The Celtic myth is the one that, of all of them, most refuses to be merely past. It insists, to the end, that it is present — that the mist is thin, that the music is audible, that the gods are one step away. And so they are. And so they always will be.

這是塞爾特傳統的大悖論，也是它的大禮。古老的諸神被擊敗了，走入了山丘之下，成了仙靈。然後仙靈成了故事。然後故事幾近被遺忘。然後故事被救了——被聖人、被詩人、被一個需要它們的民族。達南神族在山丘之下。庫丘林被綁在他的石上。芬恩正在荒野某處流浪。奧伊辛把他的故事告訴了一個聖人，而後化為塵土。而那些故事仍在被講述——以愛爾蘭語、以英語、以每一種愛上了那座翠綠之島與其鬼魂的語言。塞爾特神話，是它們所有之中，最拒絕僅僅成為過去的那一個。它堅持，直到最後，它是現在的——那份迷霧是薄的、那份音樂是可聽見的、那些諸神只在一步之遙。而它們確實如此。而它們將永遠如此。

THE GODS ARE STILL ONE STEP AWAY

諸神，仍在一步之遙

A golden-haired woman took the last of the Fianna to the Land of Youth, where three centuries passed like an afternoon — and when he came home, his world was dust, and he dismounted, and aged three hundred years in an instant, and crumbled. The Otherworld is always near, and the boundary is absolute, and the stories refuse to become merely past. The saints wrote them down; the poets gathered them up; a nation remembered who it was. And the green hills still hold their gods, and the mist is still thin, and somewhere a white horse still waits by the sea. The Celtic cycle does not end. It is still happening, beside us, one step through the fog away.
