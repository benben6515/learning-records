THE DEATH OF THE HOUND & FINN'S FIANNA

獵　犬　之　死　與　芬　恩　的　菲　安　納

THE BROKEN GEASA · CÚCHULAINN'S DEATH · FINN MAC CUMHAILL · THE FIANNA

The boy who had held the ford was brought down at last — his sacred taboos broken one by one, trapped in the net of geasa that had made him great. He tied himself to a standing stone to die upright, sword in hand, and for three days his enemies dared not approach, until a raven landed on his shoulder and showed them he was dead. And then the story leapt forward, to a gentler age — to Finn mac Cumhaill and his warrior-band the Fianna, to the Salmon of Knowledge and the wisdom that comes by accident, to a cycle of hunting and poetry that would end, like the first, in a battle that broke the brotherhood forever. 那個守住渡口的男孩終究倒下了——他的神聖禁忌被一一打破，被困在那張使他偉大的 geasa 之網中。他把自己綁在一塊立石上，挺立而死，劍握於手，而他的敵人三日不敢逼近，直到一隻烏鴉落在他的肩上，告訴他們他已死。然後故事向前跳躍，來到一個較溫柔的時代——來到芬恩·麥克·庫梅爾與他的戰士團菲安納，來到知識之鮭與意外得來的智慧，來到一個以狩獵與詩為主的循環，而它將如第一個循環一樣，結束於一場永遠打碎那個兄弟會的戰役。

Chapter 1 · 第一章

The Plot Against the Hound

謀害獵犬

Cúchulainn had enemies — and the greatest of them were not the men he had beaten at the ford, but the children of those he had killed. The Irish tradition is faithful to its own logic of revenge: every hero who kills makes orphans, and the orphans grow up, and the orphans remember. And among the children of Cúchulainn's slain enemies were a group of sorcerous witch-children, the sons of Calitin, and they — aided by Medb, still nursing her grudge from the Táin — plotted the hero's destruction.

庫丘林有仇敵——而他們之中最偉大的，不是他在渡口所擊敗之人，而是那些他所殺之人的孩子。愛爾蘭傳統忠於它自身的復仇邏輯：每一個殺人的英雄都製造孤兒，而孤兒長大，而孤兒記得。而在庫丘林所殺之敵的孩子之中，有一群會魔法的巫童，卡里汀之子，而他們——在仍懷著《奪牛記》之恨的梅芙的幫助下——密謀那位英雄的毀滅。

And the plot was clever, because the plotters understood the one thing about Cúchulainn that mattered: that he could not be beaten in fair fight, but that he could be trapped by his own geasa. The geasa — the sacred taboos — were the hero's strength and his weakness at once: the rules that made him invincible also gave his enemies the exact blueprint for his destruction. To destroy a man bound by geasa, you do not need to overcome him. You need only to maneuver him into a situation where honoring one geis requires breaking another — and then either choice undoes him.

而那個密謀很聰明，因為那些密謀者明白關於庫丘林唯一要緊的一件事：他不能在公平的戰鬥中被擊敗，但他可以被他自己 geasa 的陷阱所困。geasa——那些神聖禁忌——同時是英雄的力量與他的弱點：那些使他無敵的規則，也給了他的敵人毀滅他的精確藍圖。要毀掉一個被 geasa 所縛之人，你不需要壓倒他。你只需要將他操縱進一個「履行一個 geis 便需要破壞另一個」的處境——然後無論哪個選擇都毀了他。

And so the witch-children and Medb began to lay their trap. They knew Cúchulainn's geasa — he must not refuse hospitality offered; he must not eat the flesh of a dog (his namesake, the thing he was); he must not be alone in a fight. And they began, day by day, to manufacture the situations that would force him to break them. The Irish tradition is unsparing about this: the hero's doom is not brought by a stronger warrior. It is brought by patient enemies who know the rules better than he does, and who wait, and arrange, until the rules themselves become the noose. The geasa that made Cúchulainn the Hound will, in the end, be the very things that hang him. The next chapter shows the noose closing.

於是那些巫童與梅芙開始設下他們的陷阱。他們知道庫丘林的 geasa——他不可拒絕所獻之款待；他不可吃犬之肉（他的同名之物，他所是之物）；他不可獨自作戰。而他們開始，日復一日，製造那些會迫使他破壞它們的處境。愛爾蘭傳統對此毫不留情：那位英雄的毀滅不是由一個更強的戰士所帶來。它是由那些比他更懂規則、且等待、且安排的耐心之敵所帶來，直到那些規則本身成了絞索。使庫丘林成為那隻犬的 geasa，最終，將成為絞死他的正是那些東西。下一章展示絞索的關閉。

Chapter 2 · 第二章

The Breaking of the Geasa

禁忌之破

And the trap closed. The tradition gives us the terrible sequence. Cúchulainn, riding out to meet his enemies, was met first by women — old women, the witch-children in disguise — who offered him hospitality: a meal of dog-meat. And here was the collision: he must accept hospitality (a geis), but he must not eat dog (a geis). Either choice broke a taboo. And Cúchulainn, the tradition tells us, took the meat and ate it — choosing to break the lesser geis to honor the greater one of hospitality — and the tradition tells us that with the first bite of dog, his strength began to leave him. He had eaten his namesake. He had consumed the thing he was.

而陷阱關閉了。傳統給了我們那個可怖的序列。庫丘林，策馬出去迎戰他的敵人，首先遇見的是女子——老婦，那些喬裝的巫童——她們向他獻上款待：一餐狗肉。而這裡是那場碰撞：他必須接受款待（一個 geis），但他不可吃狗（一個 geis）。無論哪個選擇都破壞了一個禁忌。而庫丘林，傳統告訴我們，接過那肉，吃了它——選擇破壞較小的 geis 以履行款待這個較大的——而傳統告訴我們，伴隨吃下狗肉的第一口，他的力量開始離去。他吃下了他的同名之物。他吞噬了他所是之物。

And then, as he rode on, weaker now, his enemies gathered against him in overwhelming numbers — and the tradition gives us the second geis breaking: he was alone. No charioteer, no companion, no brother-warrior at his side. The geis that said he must not fight alone was broken by the simple fact that there was no one left to fight beside him. And so the Hound of Culann, the greatest warrior Ireland had ever known, rode toward his last battle weakened, isolated, his sacred protections stripped away one by one by enemies who had understood that the way to kill an invincible man is to make him violate the rules that made him so.

然後，當他繼續前行，如今更弱了，他的敵人以壓倒性的數量聚集在他面前——而傳統給了我們第二個 geis 的破壞：他獨自一人。沒有御者，沒有同伴，沒有兄弟戰士在他身邊。那個說他不可獨自作戰的 geis，被「沒有人留下來在他身邊作戰」這個簡單事實所破壞。於是庫蘭之犬，愛爾蘭所曾擁有的最偉大戰士，騎向他的最後一戰，虛弱、孤立，他的神聖護衛被那些明白「殺一個無敵之人的方法，是讓他違反那些使他無敵的規則」之敵，一個一個地剝去。

This is the Irish tradition's deepest teaching about heroism and doom, and it is the opposite of the Greek. In the Iliad, Achilles is killed by an arrow to the heel — a single physical vulnerability in an otherwise invulnerable body. The Greek hero's doom is a weakness in his armor. But Cúchulainn's doom is not a weakness. It is his strength, turned against him. The geasa that made him the Hound — the rules that gave him his power — are the very things his enemies use to take that power away. The Irish hero is destroyed by his own code. The next chapter tells how he died — and the dignity with which he refused to die sitting down.

這是愛爾蘭傳統關於英雄主義與毀滅的最深教誨，而它與希臘的相反。在《伊利亞特》裡，阿基里斯被一支射中腳踵的箭所殺——一個在其他方面刀槍不入的身體上唯一的物理弱點。希臘英雄的毀滅是他甲冑上的一個弱點。但庫丘林的毀滅不是一個弱點。它是他的力量，被轉過來對付他自己。使他成為那隻犬的 geasa——那些給予他力量的規則——正是他的敵人用來奪走那份力量的東西。愛爾蘭的英雄被自己的法則所毀。下一章講述他如何死去——以及他拒絕坐著死去的尊嚴。

Chapter 3 · 第三章

The Stone & the Death

立石與死亡

And Cúchulainn fought, even weakened, even alone, and he fought magnificently — and his enemies fell around him in heaps — but there were too many, and he was wounded, mortally, and he knew he was dying. And here the tradition gives us one of the great death-scenes in all of literature. Cúchulainn, bleeding, dying, refused to die lying down. He would not give his enemies the sight of him fallen. And so he crawled — bleeding, mortally wounded — to a standing stone, a tall pillar by the road, and he tied himself to it, with his belt, so that he could die upright, on his feet, sword in hand.

而庫丘林，即便虛弱、即便獨自，仍奮戰，而戰得壯麗——而他的敵人在他周圍成堆倒下——但他們太多了，而他受了傷，致命地，而他知道他將死。而在這裡，傳統給了我們全部文學中最偉大的死亡場面之一。庫丘林，流著血，將死，拒絕躺著死。他不肯給他的敵人看見他倒下的樣子。於是他爬——流著血，致命受傷——到一塊立石，路旁的一根高柱，而他用他的腰帶把自己綁在上面，好讓他能挺立而死，雙腳著地，劍握於手。

And there he stood, dead but upright, sword still raised, for three days. And his enemies — the witch-children, Medb's host — circled him, and circled him, and dared not approach. For three days they believed he might still be alive, might still strike, and they kept their distance. And the tradition tells us that it was only when a raven — a bird of death, the Morrígan's bird — landed on Cúchulainn's shoulder that they knew he was truly dead. The raven was the sign. And then, and only then, did his enemies dare to close in, and take his head, and his sword.

而他在那裡站著，死了卻挺立，劍仍高舉，三天。而他的敵人——那些巫童，梅芙的軍隊——繞著他，繞著他，不敢逼近。三天裡他們相信他可能還活著，可能還會出擊，而他們保持距離。而傳統告訴我們，只有當一隻烏鴉——死亡之鳥，摩莉甘之鳥——落在庫丘林的肩上之時，他們才知道他真的死了。那隻烏鴉是徵兆。然後，也唯有那時，他的敵人才敢逼近，取了他的頭，和他的劍。

This is the death the Irish tradition has always remembered, and it is the death that defines the Celtic hero: not fallen, not conquered, but tied upright to a stone, refusing even in death to lie down. The Hound of Culann died as he lived — standing, fighting, refusing to give his enemies anything. And the raven on his shoulder is the Morrígan's final word — the war-goddess he had wounded, and unwittingly healed, claiming him at last, as she claims all the great warriors in the end. Cúchulainn was twenty-seven. The prophecy was fulfilled. The short bright life was over. And the Irish tradition, which had watched him from his naming to his death, mourned him as it has mourned no other — the boy who was too much for any body to hold, who burned out, who stood upright at the end. The Ulster Cycle closes. The Fenian Cycle begins.

這是愛爾蘭傳統一直所記住的死亡，而它是定義塞爾特英雄的死亡：不是倒下，不是被征服，而是被綁著挺立於一塊石上，即便在死亡之中也拒絕躺下。庫蘭之犬如他所活般死去——站立、戰鬥、拒絕給他的敵人任何東西。而他肩上的烏鴉，是摩莉甘最後的話——那個他所傷、又不知情地所癒的戰爭女神，終於認領了他，一如她最終認領所有偉大的戰士。庫丘林二十七歲。預言應驗了。那短暫灼烈之生結束了。而愛爾蘭傳統，那個從他的命名到他的死亡都注視著他的傳統，哀悼他，一如它未曾哀悼過任何人——那個對任何身軀都太多的男孩，那個燃盡了、那個在最終挺立之人。阿爾斯特循環結束。芬尼亞循環開始。

Chapter 4 · 第四章

Finn & the Salmon of Knowledge

芬恩與知識之鮭

And now the tradition leaps forward — past the death of Cúchulainn, past the fall of the Ulster heroes, into a new age, a gentler age, the age of the Fenian Cycle. And the hero of this age is Finn mac Cumhaill — and Finn's story begins, like so many great stories, with a fish. Finn's teacher, the druid Finnegas, had spent seven years trying to catch the Salmon of Knowledge — the fish that swam in a certain pool, and that, the prophecy said, would give to whoever ate it all the wisdom in the world. And at last, with young Finn as his pupil, Finnegas caught the salmon.

而如今傳統向前跳躍——越過庫丘林之死，越過阿爾斯特英雄的傾覆，進入一個新時代，一個較溫柔的時代，芬尼亞循環的時代。而這個時代的英雄是芬恩·麥克·庫梅爾——而芬恩的故事，一如許多偉大的故事，始於一條魚。芬恩的老師，德魯伊芬尼加斯，花了七年試圖捕捉知識之鮭——那條游在某個潭中的魚，而預言說，無論誰吃了它，都將獲得世上所有的智慧。而終於，帶著年少的芬恩作為他的弟子，芬尼加斯捉到了那條鮭魚。

And Finnegas gave the fish to young Finn to cook, with strict instructions not to eat any of it — for the wisdom was meant for the druid, the teacher, who had spent seven years earning it. And the boy cooked the fish, and in the cooking he burned his thumb on the fat that spattered from it, and instinctively put his burned thumb in his mouth to cool it. And in that instant — by accident, by the small reflex of a burned finger — the wisdom of the salmon passed to the boy and not to the druid. Finnegas, seeing what had happened, wept — but then laughed, and told the boy that the prophecy had been fulfilled, and that the wisdom was meant for him all along, and that Finn would be the greatest and wisest leader Ireland had ever known.

而芬尼加斯把那條魚交給年少的芬恩去煮，嚴格吩咐不可吃任何一口——因為那份智慧是為那個花了七年賺得它的德魯伊、那位老師而設的。而那個男孩煮那條魚，而在烹煮之中他被濺出的油脂燙到了拇指，而本能地把燙到的拇指放進嘴裡去涼它。而在那一瞬——出於意外，出於一個被燙手指的小反射——那條鮭魚的智慧傳給了那個男孩，而不是那個德魯伊。芬尼加斯，看見發生了什麼，流淚了——然後笑了，告訴那個男孩預言已經應驗，而那份智慧從一開始就是為他而設的，而芬恩將成為愛爾蘭所曾擁有過最偉大、最睿智的領袖。

And from that day, Finn had only to put his thumb in his mouth — the thumb that had tasted the salmon — and he would know whatever he needed to know. This is the Fenian Cycle's gift to the Irish imagination: a hero whose power is not the warp-spasm, not the berserk fury, but wisdom — the knowledge that comes by humility and accident, the wisdom that belongs to the one who did not seek it. Finn is the anti-Cúchulainn: where the Hound burned with a fury too great for any body, Finn is calm, knowing, a leader who rules by judgment rather than by force. And the warrior-band he gathers — the Fianna — will be the brotherhood of this gentler age, a band of hunters and poets who will, for one more generation, hold Ireland in glory. And then, like the Ulster heroes, they too will fall. But first, their age.

而從那天起，芬恩只需把他的拇指放進嘴裡——那個嚐過鮭魚的拇指——他便會知曉他所需知的一切。這是芬尼亞循環給愛爾蘭想像的禮物：一個其力量不是戰狂變形、不是狂戰士之狂、而是智慧的英雄——那份出於謙卑與意外而來的知識，那份屬於那個並未尋求它之人的智慧。芬恩是反庫丘林：當那隻犬燃燒著一份對任何身軀都太大的狂怒之時，芬恩平靜、知曉，一位以判斷而非武力來統治的領袖。而他所聚集的戰士團——菲安納——將是這個較溫柔時代的兄弟會，一群獵人與詩人，他們將再一個世代，把愛爾蘭保持在榮耀之中。然後，一如阿爾斯特的英雄，他們也將倒下。但首先，他們的時代。

Chapter 5 · 第五章

Oisín & the Age of the Fianna

奧伊辛與菲安納之時代

And the Fianna — Finn's warrior-band — were the soul of the Fenian Cycle, and the tradition gives us their life in loving detail: the hunting, the feasting, the poetry composed around the fire, the code of honor that bound them. To join the Fianna, a young man had to prove himself — not only in arms, but in knowledge and in courtesy. The tradition tells us the test: the candidate had to run through a forest, with the whole Fianna pursuing him, and if he let a single branch tear his clothes, or stumbled on a single root, or failed to recite a poem as he ran, he was rejected. The Fianna were warriors, yes — but they were also poets, and scholars, and gentlemen, and the Irish tradition remembered them as the ideal of what a warrior-band could be.

而菲安納——芬恩的戰士團——是芬尼亞循環的靈魂，而傳統以鍾愛的細節給了我們他們的生活：狩獵、盛宴、火堆旁所作的詩、約束他們的榮譽法則。要加入菲安納，一個年輕人必須證明自己——不僅在武藝上，也在知識與禮儀上。傳統告訴我們那個考驗：候選人必須跑過一片森林，整個菲安納追著他，而如果他讓一根樹枝撕破他的衣服，或被一根樹根絆倒，或一邊跑一邊未能背誦一首詩，他便被拒絕。菲安納是戰士，沒錯——但他們也是詩人、學者、紳士，而愛爾蘭傳統將他們記憶為一個戰士團所能成為之理想。

And Finn's son was Oisín — and Oisín, the tradition tells us, was the greatest poet of the Fianna, the one who would, after the age ended, tell the story of it to the people who came after. Oisín is the bridge between the heroic age and the age of memory: he lived the Fianna's glory, and then, in the next stage, he will outlive it, and spend his final days telling a new Ireland — a Christian Ireland — about the heroes who are gone. The tradition gives us Oisín as a young man hunting with his father, composing poems, fighting the Fianna's enemies, living the life that the whole cycle exists to celebrate.

而芬恩的兒子是奧伊辛——而奧伊辛，傳統告訴我們，是菲安納中最偉大的詩人，那個在時代結束之後，會向後來之人講述其故事的人。奧伊辛是英雄時代與記憶時代之間的橋梁：他活過了菲安納的榮耀，然後，在下一階段，他將比它長壽，並在他最後的日子裡，向一個新的愛爾蘭——一個基督教的愛爾蘭——講述那些已逝的英雄。傳統給了我們奧伊辛，作為一個與他的父親一同狩獵、創作詩歌、與菲安納之敵作戰、活著那整個循環之所以存在來頌揚之生活的年輕人。

And the tradition tells us that this age, too, was doomed — that the Fianna, for all their glory, were a power the kings of Ireland could not indefinitely tolerate. The Fianna were too strong, too independent, too much a state within the state. And the kings, the tradition tells us, began to chafe under the Fianna's dominance — and the stage was set for the final battle, the battle that would break the brotherhood as the Táin had broken Ulster. The Fenian Cycle, like the Ulster Cycle before it, ends in a great battle that solves nothing and destroys everything. The next chapter tells of the Battle of Gabhra — and the end of the age of heroes.

而傳統告訴我們，這個時代，也同樣註定毀滅——菲安納，儘管榮耀，是一種愛爾蘭諸王無法無限期容忍的力量。菲安納太強、太獨立、太像一個國中之國。而諸王，傳統告訴我們，開始在菲安納的支配之下感到不耐——而最終之戰的舞台便搭好了，那場將如《奪牛記》之於阿爾斯特般打碎這個兄弟會的戰役。芬尼亞循環，一如其前的阿爾斯特循環，結束於一場什麼也解決不了、卻毀滅一切的大戰。下一章講述加布拉之戰——以及英雄時代的終結。

Chapter 6 · 第六章

The Battle of Gabhra

加布拉之戰

And the Battle of Gabhra came. The high king of Ireland, Cormac mac Airt, and the Fianna, led now by the aging Finn, came to a final reckoning — and the tradition tells us that the battle was vast and terrible, and that the Fianna, for all their glory, were broken. The flower of the brotherhood fell. And the tradition tells us, in the most poignant detail, that Finn's own grandson, Oscar — the fiercest and most beloved of the young warriors — died in the battle, and that the old hero Finn wept over his grandson's body as Cúchulainn had wept over Ferdiad's.

而加布拉之戰來了。愛爾蘭的最高王，科馬克·麥克·阿爾特，與如今由年邁的芬恩所率的菲安納，來到了最後的清算——而傳統告訴我們那場戰役浩大而可怖，而菲安納，儘管榮耀，被擊碎了。那個兄弟會的精華倒下了。而傳統告訴我們，在最令人心酸的細節裡，芬恩自己的孫子，奧斯卡——那些年輕戰士中最兇猛、也最受人鍾愛的——死於那場戰役，而那位老英雄芬恩在他孫子的屍體旁流淚，一如庫丘林在費爾迪亞的屍體旁流淚。

And the tradition tells us that the Battle of Gabhra was the end of the heroic age in Ireland — that after it, the Fianna were no more, and the old warrior-band was scattered, and the age of heroes was over. The Fenian Cycle, like the Ulster Cycle, ends not in triumph but in a battle that destroys the very thing it was fought over. Ireland, after Gabhra, was a quieter, poorer place — the warriors gone, the poems still to be sung but about the dead rather than the living. And the tradition tells us that Finn himself survived the battle, but broken, and wandered, an old man, into the wilderness — and that his son Oisín, the poet, was the one who carried the memory of the age forward.

而傳統告訴我們，加布拉之戰是愛爾蘭英雄時代的終結——在它之後，菲安納不復存在，而那個古老的戰士團四散，而英雄時代結束了。芬尼亞循環，一如阿爾斯特循環，不是結束於勝利，而是結束於一場毀掉它所爭之物本身的戰役。愛爾蘭，在加布拉之後，是一個更安靜、更貧乏之地——戰士去了，詩仍要被唱，卻是關於死者而非活人。而傳統告訴我們，芬恩本人活過了那場戰役，卻破碎了，作為一個老人，流浪進了荒野——而他的兒子奧伊辛，那位詩人，是把那個時代的記憶帶向前之人。

And this is the pattern the Celtic tradition reveals, and it is the same pattern in both its great cycles: an age of heroes, a brotherhood, a glory that cannot last, and a final battle that destroys the brotherhood and ends the age — leaving only the poets, and the memory, and the mourning. The Ulster Cycle ended with Cúchulainn tied to a stone. The Fenian Cycle ends with Finn wandering, old and alone, into the wild. And in both cases, what survives is the story — the poem, the song, the keening — that the next generation carries. Oisín, the poet, will carry the Fenian age into the future. And in the next stage, he will carry it into the Otherworld itself, and back, and into the hands of the poets who would save the whole tradition. The heroic age is over. The age of poetry begins.

而這便是塞爾特傳統所揭示的模式，而它在它的兩個大循環裡是同樣的模式：一個英雄時代、一個兄弟會、一份不能持久的榮耀，以及一場打碎兄弟會、終結時代的最終之戰——只留下詩人、記憶，與哀悼。阿爾斯特循環以庫丘林被綁於一塊石上而結束。芬尼亞循環以芬恩，年老而孤獨，流浪進荒野而結束。而在兩種情況裡，所存活的都是故事——那首詩、那首歌、那場慟哭——那是下一代所承載的。奧伊辛，那位詩人，將把芬尼亞時代帶入未來。而在下一階段，他將把它帶入異世界本身，再帶回來，帶入那些會拯救整個傳統的詩人手中。英雄時代結束了。詩的時代開始。

THE HEROIC AGE ENDS; THE POET'S BEGINS

英雄時代終；詩人時代始

The Hound's enemies broke his geasa one by one, and he tied himself to a standing stone to die upright, sword in hand — and only a raven on his shoulder told his foes he was dead at last. Then the story turned to Finn, who gained the world's wisdom by burning his thumb on the Salmon of Knowledge, and to his Fianna, the brotherhood of hunters and poets. And that age, too, ended in a battle — Gabhra — that broke them, and left old Finn wandering into the wild. The heroic age was over. What survived was the poet Oisín, carrying the memory of it forward — into a land where three hundred years pass like a dream.
