THE BIRTH & FEATS OF CÚCHULAINN

庫　丘　林　之　生　與　功　業

SETANTA · THE HOUND OF CULANN · THE WARP-SPASM · SCÁTHACH

A boy called Setanta, walking to a feast, was attacked by a fierce guard-hound — and he killed it, bare-handed, by driving his playing-ball down its throat. And the hound's owner wept, for the beast had guarded his hall, and the boy — stricken with guilt — said: "Give me a new name. I will be your hound." And so Setanta became Cúchulainn, the Hound of Culann — and the greatest warrior Ireland would ever know began, at that moment, the short, blazing life that would end with him tied to a standing stone, sword in hand, refusing to die sitting down. 一個名叫塞坦塔的男孩，走向一場盛宴之時，遭到一頭兇猛守犬的攻擊——而他徒手殺了它，把他的玩球塞進它的喉嚨。而犬的主人流淚了，因為那頭獸曾守衛他的大廳，而那個男孩——滿懷愧疚——說：「給我一個新名字。我來做你的犬。」於是塞坦塔成了庫丘林，庫蘭之犬——而愛爾蘭所曾擁有的最偉大戰士，便在那一刻，開始了那短暫而灼烈的一生，那一生將以他被綁在一塊立石上、劍握於手、拒絕坐著死去而告終。

Chapter 1 · 第一章

Setanta & the Hound of Culann

塞坦塔與庫蘭之犬

His given name was Setanta — a boy of the Ulaid, the people of Ulster, nephew to King Conchobar at the great hall of Emain Macha. And the tradition tells us that from his earliest childhood he was more than other children: stronger, fiercer, more impossible to contain. He came to the king's hall at the age of five, the story goes, having heard the other boys were playing there, and demanded to be let into their games; and when they refused, he beat them all, every one, and was only stopped from hurting them further by the king's own intervention. This is the Irish hero as a child — already too much, already dangerous, already marked for the short bright life that would be his.

他的本名是塞坦塔——阿爾斯特人、阿爾伊德族的一個男孩，埃馬恩·瑪查大殿中康喬巴爾王的姪兒。而傳統告訴我們，從他最早的童年起，他便異於其他孩子：更強、更猛、更無法被約束。故事說，他五歲時來到王的大殿，聽說其他男孩在那裡玩耍，便要求加入他們的遊戲；而當他們拒絕之時，他打敗了他們全部，每一個，而只有王親自干預才阻止了他進一步傷害他們。這便是愛爾蘭英雄之童年——已經太多、已經危險、已經被標記為那將屬於他的短暫而灼烈之生。

And the naming happened on a night soon after. Conchobar was invited to a feast at the hall of a smith named Culann, and the boy Setanta was playing a game and said he would follow. The king went ahead, and at the feast, Culann asked if all his guests had arrived, and the king, forgetting the boy, said yes — and Culann released his great guard-hound, the fiercest in all Ireland, to patrol the grounds. And then Setanta came, alone, in the dark, walking up to the hall — and the hound leapt at him.

而命名發生在不久之後的一個夜晚。康喬巴爾受邀到一位名叫庫蘭的鐵匠的大廳赴宴，而那個男孩塞坦塔正在玩遊戲，說他隨後就到。王先走了，而在宴會上，庫蘭問他所有的客人是否都到了，而王，忘了那個男孩，說是——而庫蘭放出了他那頭大守犬，全愛爾蘭最兇猛的，去巡視場地。然後塞坦塔來了，獨自一人，在暗處，走向大殿——而那頭犬撲向他。

And the boy — five years old, alone, unarmed — did the thing that would define him. He did not run. He did not cry out. He drove his playing-ball, with all his strength, straight into the hound's open mouth, and the ball lodged in its throat and choked it, and the boy seized it by the hind legs and dashed its skull on the ground. And Culann, coming out, found his prized hound dead, and wept — for the beast had guarded his hall, and now the hall was unprotected. And the boy, seeing the man's grief, was stricken — and he said the words that gave him his name: "Give me a hound-shaped vessel, and I will guard your hall as the hound did. I will be the Hound of Culann." In Irish: Cú Chulainn. The boy Setanta took the hound's place. And the greatest warrior Ireland would ever know had his name, and his vocation, at five years old: to be the hound that guards.

而那個男孩——五歲，獨自一人，手無寸鐵——做了那件將定義他之事。他沒有跑。他沒有哭喊。他把他的玩球，用盡全力，直直地塞進那頭犬張開的嘴裡，而那球卡在它的喉嚨裡使它窒息，而那個男孩抓住它的後腿，把它的頭顱摔在地上。而庫蘭出來，發現他那頭珍貴的犬死了，流淚——因為那頭獸曾守衛他的大廳，而如今大廳無人保護。而那個男孩，看見那人的悲傷，被擊中了——而他說出了那句給他名字的話：「給我一個犬形的容器，我來像那頭犬一樣守衛你的大廳。我來做庫蘭之犬。」愛爾蘭語：Cú Chulainn。那個男孩塞坦塔取代了那頭犬的位置。而愛爾蘭所曾擁有的最偉大戰士，在五歲之時，便有了他的名字，與他的天職：做那隻守衛的犬。

Chapter 2 · 第二章

The Warp-Spasm

戰狂變形

Cúchulainn's power in battle was not merely skill. It was something else — something the Irish called the ríastrad, the warp-spasm, the battle-fury, and it is one of the strangest and most distinctive things in all of world mythology. When the boy-hero went into his battle-rage, his body literally transformed. The tradition describes it in loving, horrified detail: his body swelled and twisted; one of his eyes sank so deep into his head that a crane could not have reached it, while the other bulged out enormous on his cheek; his mouth stretched to his ears; his hair stood on end, each hair tipped with sparks or drops of blood; his calves and shins shifted to the front of his legs and his feet to the back.

庫丘林在戰鬥中的力量不僅是技藝。那是別的東西——愛爾蘭人稱之為 ríastrad，戰狂變形，戰鬥之狂，而它是全部世界神話中最奇異、也最具特徵的事物之一。當這位少年英雄進入他的戰鬥之狂之時，他的身體字面上變形了。傳統以鍾愛而驚恐的細節描述它：他的身體膨脹、扭曲；他的一隻眼深深沉入他的頭中，深到連鶴的喙也夠不到，而另一隻眼在他的臉頰上巨大地鼓出；他的嘴扯到他的耳朵；他的頭髮根根直立，每一根的尖端是火花或血滴；他的小腿和脛轉到腿的前面，而他的腳轉到後面。

He became, in battle, a monster — and this is the key to understanding the Irish hero, who is not the clean, beautiful warrior of the Greek or Arthurian tradition but something far stranger, far older, far more frightening. Cúchulainn in his fury is barely human. He is a force. And the tradition tells us that this fury was so terrible that it had to be contained — that when the boy came out of his first battle-spasm, still raging, the women of Ulster had to plunge him into vats of cold water to cool him, and the first vat burst from the heat of him, and the second boiled over, and only the third held. The hero's own people had to protect themselves from him, after the battle, before he could return to his right mind.

他在戰鬥中成了一頭怪獸——而這是理解愛爾蘭英雄的關鍵，那個英雄不是希臘或亞瑟傳統裡那種乾淨、美麗的戰士，而是某種遠更奇異、遠更古老、遠更可怖之物。庫丘林在他的狂怒中幾乎不是人。他是一股力量。而傳統告訴我們，這股狂怒如此可怖，以至於它必須被約束——當那個男孩從他第一次戰狂中出來、仍在狂怒之時，阿爾斯特的女子們不得不把他浸入一桶桶冷水來冷卻他，而第一桶因他的熱而爆裂，而第二桶沸騰溢出，而只有第三桶承受得住。英雄自己的族人，在戰鬥之後，不得不保護自己免受他之害，在他恢復理智之前。

This is the deepest difference between the Celtic hero and the heroes of the other gardens, and the tradition is honest about its cost. Cúchulainn is not a civilizing force. He is a barely-contained force of destruction, and the people he protects are also the people who must fear him. His short life — he will die at twenty-seven — is the story of a power too great for any body to hold, a fire that burns everything around it, including the one who carries it. The warp-spasm is not a gift. It is a doom. And the boy who became the Hound of Culann will, in the end, be consumed by the very fury that made him great. But first, he must be trained. And for that, he must leave Ireland.

這是塞爾特英雄與其他花園英雄之間最深的差異，而傳統對其代價是誠實的。庫丘林不是一股文明化之力。他是一股幾乎無法約束的毀滅之力，而他所保護的人民，也是必須畏懼他的人民。他的短暫之生——他將死於二十七歲——是一股對任何身軀都太大的力量、一把燒盡其周圍一切、包括那個攜帶它之人之火的故事。戰狂變形不是一個禮物。它是一個毀滅。而那個成為庫蘭之犬的男孩，最終，將被那使他偉大的狂怒本身所吞噬。但首先，他必須受訓。而為此，他必須離開愛爾蘭。

Chapter 3 · 第三章

The Training with Scáthach

師從斯卡哈

To be made into the warrior he had to become, Cúchulainn had to leave Ireland and cross the sea to the far north — to the land of Alba, to the isle of Skye, where there lived a woman named Scáthach. And Scáthach was the greatest teacher of warriors in the world. She ran a school — a fortress on a cliff, reachable only by a terrifying leap across a chasm — and the young men of all the lands came to her to learn the arts of war, and she taught them, and many of them died in the learning.

要被造就成他必須成為的戰士，庫丘林必須離開愛爾蘭，渡海到遙遠的北方——到阿爾巴之地，到天空之島，那裡住著一個名叫斯卡哈的女子。而斯卡哈是世上最偉大的戰士之師。她辦了一所學校——一座懸崖上的堡壘，只有跨越一道深淵的可怕跳躍才能抵達——而所有土地的年輕人都來到她這裡學習戰爭之藝，而她教導他們，而他們之中許多人死於學習之中。

And Cúchulainn made the leap, and entered the school, and Scáthach saw what he was — and she taught him everything. She taught him the leaps, the throws, the spear-forms. And she taught him, most importantly, the gae bolga — the terrible barbed spear that was Cúchulainn's signature weapon, a weapon that entered the body and opened inside it, that could not be removed without killing, that no one else in the world knew how to use. The gae bolga was Scáthach's gift to the boy who would use it, in years to come, against the one person he could not bear to fight. But that, too, is a later story.

而庫丘林跳了那一跳，進入了學校，而斯卡哈看見了他是什麼——而她把一切都教給了他。她教他跳躍、投擲、矛法。而她最重要的，教了他 gae bolga——那把可怕的倒鈎矛，那是庫丘林的招牌武器，一把進入身體、在體內張開的武器，一把不殺便無法拔出的武器，世上無人知曉如何使用的武器。gae bolga 是斯卡哈給那個男孩的禮物，那個男孩將在日後，用它來對付那個他不忍與之戰之人。但那，也是後話。

And here, at Scáthach's school, Cúchulainn met the man who would be his doom — or rather, his doom's instrument. His name was Ferdiad, and he was Cúchulainn's fellow-student, his sparring partner, his friend. They trained together, ate together, slept by the same fire, called each other brother. And the tradition tells us that their bond was the deepest thing in either of their lives — and that it was also, unbeknownst to them, the thing that would, years later, put them on opposite sides of a ford, forced to kill each other for a queen's pride. Scáthach trained them both. Scáthach gave Cúchulainn the gae bolga. And the gae bolga, in the fullness of time, would be the weapon that killed Ferdiad. The school of the warrior-woman forged, in one place and one time, both the hero and his tragedy. Cúchulainn returned to Ireland, mastered, armed, and carrying the seed of his own heartbreak.

而在這裡，在斯卡哈的學校裡，庫丘林遇見了那個將成為他的毀滅之人——或者更確切地說，他的毀滅之工具。他的名字是費爾迪亞，而他是庫丘林的同窗、他的陪練、他的朋友。他們一起訓練，一起吃飯，在同一堆火旁睡覺，互稱兄弟。而傳統告訴我們，他們的情誼是兩人生命中各自最深的東西——而它同時，在他們不知情的情況下，也是那件將在多年之後、把他們置於一處渡口兩端、為一個王后的驕傲而被迫彼此相殺之物。斯卡哈訓練了他們兩人。斯卡哈給了庫丘林 gae bolga。而 gae bolga，在時機成熟之時，將成為殺死費爾迪亞的武器。那位女戰士的學校，在一個地方、一個時間，鍛造了英雄與他的悲劇兩者。庫丘林回到了愛爾蘭，嫻熟、武裝、帶著他自身心碎的種子。

Chapter 4 · 第四章

The Curse of Macha

瑪查之咒

Before we come to the great deeds, the tradition pauses to give us a story that will determine everything — the story of the curse of Macha, which is the precondition for the entire Táin. And Macha was a woman — a goddess in disguise, the tradition suggests, or simply a woman of supernatural power — who married a farmer of Ulster and bore him a child. And her husband, foolishly, boasted at the king's feast that his wife could outrun the king's horses. And the king, insulted, demanded that she prove it — then and there, though she was heavy with child, in the final days of her pregnancy.

在我們來到那些偉大功業之前，傳統暫停一下，給了我們一個將決定一切的故事——瑪查之咒的故事，它是整個《奪牛記》的前提。而瑪查是一個女子——一個喬裝的女神，傳統暗示，或簡單地是一個有超自然力量的女子——她嫁了阿爾斯特的一個農夫，為他懷了一個孩子。而她的丈夫，愚蠢地，在王的宴會上吹噓他的妻子跑得比王的馬還快。而王，受辱，要求她證明——就在當場，儘管她身懷六甲，在她懷孕的最後幾日。

And Macha begged. She begged the king, on the life of the child she carried, to wait — to let her give birth first, to run the race after. And the king refused. He was a king, and his pride had been challenged, and he would have his race. And Macha, weeping, ran. And she won — she outran the king's horses, the tradition tells us, crossed the line first. And then, at the finish, she collapsed, in agony, and gave birth to twins there on the track, in her blood and her shame and her rage. And as she lay dying, she turned to the men of Ulster — all the men, all the warriors, all those who had stood by and watched a pregnant woman be forced to race for a king's vanity — and she cursed them.

而瑪查乞求。她以她所懷之孩子的性命乞求王等待——讓她先生產，之後再跑。而王拒絕了。他是個王，他的驕傲受到了挑戰，而他要他的賽跑。而瑪查，流著淚，跑了。而她贏了——她跑贏了王的馬，傳統告訴我們，率先越過終點。然後，在終點處，她崩潰了，痛苦不堪，而在跑道上，在她的血、她的恥、她的怒中，生下了雙胞胎。而當她躺在那裡將死之時，她轉向阿爾斯特的男子們——所有的男子、所有的戰士、所有那些站著旁觀一個孕婦被強迫為一個王的虛榮而跑之人——她詛咒了他們。

"For what you have done to me," she said, "every man of Ulster, in the hour of his greatest need, will feel the pangs I felt — the labor-pangs of a woman giving birth. For nine generations, whenever danger comes, the men of Ulster will fall to the ground, helpless, weakened, unable to fight, for the length of a queen's labor." And the curse took. And this is the wound that opens the entire Ulster Cycle — for when, in the next stage, the army of Connacht comes to steal the bull, every man of Ulster will be stricken by Macha's curse, lying on the ground in birth-pangs, unable to lift a weapon. All of them except one. Cúchulainn, for reasons the tradition debates, was exempt — perhaps because he was not truly of Ulster blood, perhaps because he was something other than fully mortal. And so, when the whole province lies helpless, the Hound of Culann will stand alone, against an entire army, at a ford. The curse of Macha is the reason there can be a Táin.

「因你們對我所做之事，」她說，「每一個阿爾斯特的男子，在他最需要的那個時辰，都將感覺到我所感覺之痛——一個女子分娩的產痛。九代之久，每當危險來臨，阿爾斯特的男子們都將倒在地上，無助、虛弱、無法戰鬥，持續一位王后分娩的時辰。」而那個詛咒應驗了。而這便是打開整個阿爾斯特循環的那道傷——因為當下一階段裡康諾特的軍隊來偷那頭公牛之時，阿爾斯特的每一個男子都將被瑪查之咒所擊，躺在地上產痛之中，無法舉起一件武器。除了一人之外。庫丘林，出於傳統所辯論的原因，被豁免了——也許因為他並不真正是阿爾斯特血脈，也許因為他是某種不完全是凡人之物。於是，當整個省無助地躺臥之時，庫蘭之犬將獨自站立，對抗一整支軍隊，在一處渡口。瑪查之咒，便是《奪牛記》之所以能夠存在的原因。

Chapter 5 · 第五章

Emer & the Bride-Price

艾梅與聘禮

And Cúchulainn, returning to Ireland a trained warrior, sought a wife — and the woman he sought was Emer, the daughter of a lord named Forgall, and she was, the tradition tells us, the one woman in Ireland who was his match in wit. And the tradition gives us their first meeting as a duel of words — Cúchulainn testing her with riddles, and Emer answering each one, matching him, refusing to be outdone, until the young hero realized he had found the one person who could speak to him as an equal. And he asked her father for her hand.

而庫丘林，以一個受過訓練的戰士之身回到愛爾蘭，求一位妻子——而他所求的女子是艾梅，一個名叫弗加爾的領主之女，而她，傳統告訴我們，是愛爾蘭唯一一個在機智上與他匹敵的女子。而傳統給了我們他們的初遇，作為一場言詞之鬥——庫丘林以謎語試她，而艾梅逐一回答，與他匹敵，拒絕被超越，直到那年輕的英雄意識到，他找到了那個能以平等之姿與他說話的唯一之人。而他向她的父親求她的手。

And Forgall, seeing the young warrior and fearing him — fearing, perhaps, the doom that hung over anyone who married into a fire like Cúchulainn — refused, and set an impossible bride-price, hoping the boy would give up. And Cúchulainn did not give up. He went away, and trained, and grew greater, and returned — and the tradition tells us that he took Emer by force, storming Forgall's fortress, leaping the ramparts, and carrying her off, and Forgall died in the attempt to stop him. The marriage was made. And Emer was faithful to Cúchulainn for the whole of his short life — even when other women, mortal and immortal, threw themselves at him, even when the queen of the Otherworld herself came to claim him. Emer knew who she was. She was his wife. And she would outlive him, and mourn him, and the tradition gives her, in the end, the dignity of the one who stayed.

而弗加爾，看見那年輕戰士而畏懼他——也許畏懼那籠罩任何嫁入庫丘林這般火焰之人之頭上的毀滅——拒絕了，並設下一個不可能的聘禮，希望那個男孩放棄。而庫丘林沒有放棄。他離開，訓練，變得更偉大，歸來——而傳統告訴我們，他以武力帶走了艾梅，攻陷弗加爾的堡壘，躍過城牆，把她帶走，而弗加爾在阻止他的嘗試中死去。那樁婚姻成了。而艾梅在庫丘林短暫的一生中對他忠貞——即便其他女子、凡人與不朽者，都向他投懷送抱，即便異世界之后親自來索取他。艾梅知道自己是誰。她是他的妻子。而她將比他長壽，為他哀悼，而傳統在最終給了她那份「留下來之人」的尊嚴。

And this marriage — the one stable thing in Cúchulainn's unstable life — is worth pausing on, because it is the ground from which all his tragedy grows. The hero who can hold nothing down, whose fury is too great for any body, who will die young and violent, has, in Emer, one fixed point. And the tradition knows that this is what makes his doom bearable — and what makes it doom. A hero with no one to come home to can die gloriously. A hero with a wife dies leaving a wife. The Irish tradition, here, gives us the hero not as a pure force but as a person — bound, loved, loving — and thereby makes his coming end not just spectacular but heartbreaking. The champion is ready. The marriage is made. The curse of Macha lies in wait. And the army of Connacht is gathering, to steal a bull.

而這樁婚姻——庫丘林不穩定生命中唯一穩定之物——值得在此停下，因為它是他所有悲劇生長的根基。那個什麼也留不住、其狂怒對任何身軀都太大、將年輕而暴力地死去的英雄，在艾梅那裡有一個定點。而傳統知道，這便是使他的毀滅得以承受之物——也是使它成為毀滅之物。一個沒有可歸之人的英雄可以輝煌地死。一個有妻之英雄死了，留下的是一個妻子。愛爾蘭傳統，在此處，給了我們這位英雄，不是作為一股純粹的力量，而是作為一個人——被約束、被愛、也愛著——而因此使他的終局不只是壯觀，而是令人心碎。英雄備就。婚姻已成。瑪查之咒在等待。而康諾特的軍隊正在聚集，要去偷一頭公牛。

Chapter 6 · 第六章

The Champion Ready

備就之英雄

Let us pause, at the end of this stage, to take the measure of the man who is about to hold a ford alone against an army. Cúchulainn is, by now, everything he will ever be: the Hound who took a dead beast's name, the warrior whose body warps into a monster in battle, the master of the gae bolga, the husband of Emer, the champion of Ulster. He is seventeen years old. The tradition tells us his feats in his boyhood alone were greater than most warriors achieve in a lifetime — that he killed the sons of Nechtan, that he held a fortress against a host single-handed, that his name was already feared across the whole island.

讓我們，在這個階段的結尾，停下來衡量一下這個即將獨自在渡口擋住一支軍隊之人。庫丘林，如今，是他將永遠所是的一切：那隻取了死獸之名的犬、那個在戰鬥中身軀扭曲成怪獸的戰士、gae bolga 的大師、艾梅的丈夫、阿爾斯特的英雄。他十七歲。傳統告訴我們，他在童年的功業，便已大過多數戰士一生所成——他殺了涅梅丹之子，他獨自守住一座堡壘抵擋一支大軍，他的名字已令全島畏懼。

And the tradition also tells us, with the heartbreaking honesty that runs through all of Irish myth, that he will not live long. There is a prophecy — and prophecies, in the Irish tradition, are never wrong — that Cúchulainn will die at twenty-seven, that his life will be short and famous, and that he will go down fighting. And the hero himself knows it. He has been told, by druids and by omens, that his fame and his life cannot both be long, and that he has chosen — willingly, gladly — the short bright life over the long obscure one. This is the Celtic ideal of heroism, and it is the opposite of the Greek kleos: not the glory that outlives you because your name is remembered, but the glory that burns you up, that consumes the very body it makes famous, that is, in the end, indistinguishable from self-destruction.

而傳統也告訴我們，帶著貫穿所有愛爾蘭神話的那份令人心碎的誠實，他活不長。有一個預言——而預言，在愛爾蘭傳統裡，從不會錯——庫丘林將死於二十七歲，他的一生將短暫而聞名，而他將戰死。而英雄自己也知道。他被告知，被德魯伊與徵兆，他的名聲與他的命不能兩者都長，而他選擇了——自願地、欣然地——短暫而灼烈之生，而非漫長而晦暗之生。這是塞爾特的英雄理想，而它與希臘的 kleos 相反：不是因為你的名字被記住而比你長壽的榮光，而是把你燃盡的榮光，消耗那個它使之成名的身軀本身，最終，與自我毀滅無從分辨的榮光。

And so the champion stands ready — young, doomed, armed, willing — at the edge of the story that will make his name forever. Across the island, in the west, a queen named Medb is comparing her wealth with her husband's, and discovering that she lacks one thing: a bull as fine as his. And her pride will not allow her to be second. And she will send an army east, into Ulster, to take the brown bull of Cooley. And the men of Ulster will fall, stricken by Macha's curse. And the Hound of Culann will stand alone at a ford, and fight, day after day, the whole army of Connacht, one champion at a time. The next stage is the central epic of the Irish tradition. The Táin is coming.

於是英雄備就——年輕、註定毀滅、武裝、自願——站在那個將永遠成就他之名的故事的邊緣。在島的那一頭，在西方，一位名叫梅芙的王后正在將她的財富與她丈夫的相比，並發現她缺一樣東西：一頭和他的一樣好的公牛。而她的驕傲不容她屈居第二。而她將派一支軍隊向東，進入阿爾斯特，去奪那頭庫利的褐公牛。而阿爾斯特的男子們將倒下，被瑪查之咒所擊。而庫蘭之犬將獨自站在一處渡口，日復一日地，與整支康諾特軍隊作戰，一次一位勇士。下一階段是愛爾蘭傳統的核心史詩。《奪牛記》即將到來。

THE HOUND STANDS READY

那隻犬，備就而立

A boy killed a hound with his playing-ball and took its name, and grew into a warrior whose body warped into a monster in battle. He crossed the sea to learn the gae bolga from a woman on a cliff, and met there the friend he would one day have to kill. He married the one woman who could match his wit. He knew, by prophecy, that he would die at twenty-seven — and he chose the short bright life anyway, gladly. Now, across the island, a queen's pride is gathering an army to steal a bull. And the men of Ulster will fall under an ancient curse. And the Hound of Culann will stand alone.
