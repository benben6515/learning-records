THE SONS OF MÍL & THE SÍD

米　爾　之　子　與　妖　精　之　丘

THE MORTAL GAELS · ÉRIU · THE RETREAT INTO THE MOUNDS

The gods had won Ireland. And then the mortals came — the sons of Míl, the ancestors of the Gaels, sailing out of Spain — and they did what no Fomorian had managed: they took the island from the gods themselves. But the gods did not die. They went into the hills — into the ancient burial mounds, the síd — and there they stayed, becoming the aes sídhe, the fairy-folk, the powers of the hidden Ireland that has existed, the tradition insists, ever since, beneath and beside the mortal one. From this moment, Ireland was two. And the mist between the two has never been thick enough. 諸神曾贏得愛爾蘭。然後凡人來了——米爾之子，蓋爾人的祖先，從西班牙航來——而他們做到了沒有一個弗莫爾人做到的事：他們從諸神手中奪取了這座島。但諸神沒有死。他們走進了山丘——走進古老的葬丘、妖精之丘——而他們留在那裡，成為妖精、仙靈、隱藏愛爾蘭的力量，那個愛爾蘭，傳統堅持，從此便一直存在於凡人愛爾蘭之下、之旁。從此，愛爾蘭是兩個。而兩者之間的迷霧，從未夠厚。

Chapter 1 · 第一章

The Mortal Gaels Arrive

凡人蓋爾人到來

The sons of Míl came from Spain — so the tradition says — descended from a wanderer who had once seen the green island from a high tower and sworn to reach it. They were mortals, not gods: warriors and poets and judges, the ancestors of the Gaelic people, and they came in a fleet of ships across the sea. And their poet, the first to set foot on the land, was a man named Amairgen, and as his foot touched the soil he spoke a poem — one of the most famous in the Irish language — that claimed the island and named everything in it.

米爾之子從西班牙而來——傳統如此說——一個曾從高塔上看見那座翠綠之島並誓要抵達它的流浪者之後。他們是凡人，不是神：戰士、詩人、判官，蓋爾人的祖先，他們乘著一支船隊渡海而來。而他们的詩人，第一個踏上這片土地之人，是一個名叫阿邁爾根的人，而當他的腳觸及土壤之時，他唸出了一首詩——愛爾蘭語中最著名的詩之一——那首詩聲索了這座島，並為其中的一切命名。

"I am the wind that breathes on the sea," Amairgen said. "I am the wave of the ocean. I am the roar of the sea. I am a stag of seven tines. I am a hawk on a cliff. I am a tear of the sun. I am the fairest of plants. I am a boar in valour. I am a salmon in a pool. I am a lake on a plain. I am the hill of poetry." And on and on — the poet becoming, in his words, every part of the land he was claiming. The poem is the Irish imagination in its purest form: the belief that to name a thing, to speak it, is to possess it and to be possessed by it. Amairgen did not conquer Ireland by the sword. He sang it into his people's hands.

「我是吹拂海上的風，」阿邁爾根說。「我是海洋的波浪。我是海的轟鳴。我是七叉角的雄鹿。我是懸崖上的鷹。我是太陽的一滴淚。我是最美的植物。我是勇猛的野豬。我是潭中的鮭。我是平原上的湖。我是詩歌之丘。」一個接一個——那位詩人在他的話語中，成為他所聲索之土地的每一個部分。這首詩是愛爾蘭想像的最純粹形式：那個相信「為一物命名、說出它，便是擁有它並被它所擁有」的信念。阿邁爾根不是以劍征服愛爾蘭。他把愛爾蘭唱進了他族人的手中。

And the Tuatha Dé Danann, seeing the mortals come, did not immediately fight. They did what they had done with the Fir Bolg — they offered terms. They would share the island. They would divide it. And the sons of Míl, being mortal and clever, agreed to negotiate. And the negotiation that followed is the strangest treaty in all of mythology — the treaty by which the gods gave away the daylight world and kept only the dark, the treaty that created the two Irelands that have existed ever since. But before the treaty, the three goddesses of the island had their say. And their say determined everything — including the very name of the land.

而達南神族，看見凡人到來，並未立即交戰。他們做了他們對弗摩爾人所做之事——他們提出了條件。他們會分享這座島。他們會瓜分它。而米爾之子，身為凡人又機敏，同意談判。而隨後的談判，是全部神話中最奇異的條約——諸神藉此讓出白晝世界、只保留黑暗的那個條約，那個創造了此後一直存在的兩個愛爾蘭的條約。但在條約之前，島的三位女神有她們的話要說。而她們的話決定了一切——包括這片土地的名字本身。

Chapter 2 · 第二章

The Three Goddesses of the Island

島之三女神

And as the sons of Míl moved inland, they met, one after another, three women — and each woman was a goddess, and each goddess was the island itself, and each asked the same thing. The first was Banba. She met the invaders and said: "If you take this land, name it for me." And they promised. The second was Fódla, who said the same: "Name the island for me." And they promised her too. And the third was Ériu — and Ériu, the tradition tells us, was the chief of the three, the goddess whose name would, in the end, win.

而當米爾之子向內陸推進之時，他們一個接一個遇見三個女子——而每個女子都是一位女神，而每位女神都是這座島本身，而每位都要求同一件事。第一位是班芭。她遇見入侵者，說：「若你們取此土地，以我之名名之。」而他們答應了。第二位是福德菈，她說了同樣的話：「以我之名名此島。」而他們也答應了她。第三位是埃里烏——而埃里烏，傳統告訴我們，是三者之首，那位其名最終將勝出的女神。

And Ériu spoke to the mortal leader, and she blessed him — and her blessing was also a prophecy. "This island," she said, "will bear my name forever. It will be called Ériu." And from her name comes Éire — the Irish name for Ireland — and from Éire comes, through the mouths of foreigners, the English word "Ireland" itself. The island is named for a goddess. Every time anyone, anywhere in the world, says the word "Ireland," they are speaking the name of Ériu, the goddess who blessed the invaders in exchange for immortality of a kind — the immortality of being spoken.

而埃里烏對那位凡人首領說話，而她祝福了他——而她的祝福也是一個預言。「這座島，」她說，「將永遠承載我的名字。它將被稱為埃里烏。」而從她的名字而來的是 Éire——愛爾蘭的愛爾蘭語之名——而從 Éire，經由外邦人之口，而來的是英語「Ireland」一詞本身。這座島以一位女神為名。世界上任何地方，任何時候有人說出「愛爾蘭」這個詞，他們便是在說埃里烏之名，那位為換取一種不朽——被言說的不朽——而祝福入侵者的女神。

And Banba and Fódla were not forgotten — their names survived as poetic names for the island, used by the poets for centuries afterward. But Ériu won, and Ériu's name became the name. And this little episode — three goddesses, three namings, one winner — is the Irish way of acknowledging something deep: that the land is older than the people on it, that the land was a goddess before it was a country, and that the mortals who took it did so with the goddess's permission, on the condition that they remember her every time they say where they live. The sons of Míl may own Ireland. But Ireland is named for Ériu. And Ériu, like all the Tuatha, is about to go into the hills.

而班芭與福德菈並未被遺忘——她們的名字作為這座島的詩意之名而存活，被詩人們在此後數世紀裡使用。但埃里烏贏了，而埃里烏之名成了那個名字。而這個小插曲——三位女神、三次命名、一位勝者——是愛爾蘭人承認某種深層之事的方式：土地比其上之民更古老，土地在成為一個國家之前曾是一位女神，而取它的凡人，是在女神的允許之下取之，條件是他們每次說出自己住在何處之時都要記得她。米爾之子或許擁有愛爾蘭。但愛爾蘭以埃里烏為名。而埃里烏，一如所有的達南神族，即將走進山裡。

Chapter 3 · 第三章

The Treaty at Tara

塔拉之約

And the two peoples met at Tara, the royal hill, to make their treaty. And the sons of Míl — clever, mortal, recognizing that the gods could not be beaten in open war — proposed the division that would define Irish cosmology forever. "We will divide the island," they said. "Not in half. We will divide it by a different measure." And the Tuatha, curious, asked what measure. And the sons of Míl said: "We will take the island above the ground. You will take the island below."

而這兩個民族在皇家山丘塔拉相會，以締結他們的條約。而米爾之子——機敏、凡人、認知到諸神在公開戰爭中無法被擊敗——提出了那個將永遠定義愛爾蘭宇宙觀的瓜分。「我們將瓜分這座島，」他們說。「不是對半。我們將以不同的度量來瓜分它。」而達南神族，好奇，問是什麼度量。而米爾之子說：「我們取地面之上的島。你們取地面之下的。」

The Tuatha, the tradition tells us, agreed. They had no choice, and they knew it — the mortals were too many, and the gods were tired of war, and the division, however unequal it seemed, was a way to survive. And so the treaty was made: the daylight world, the green hills and the fields and the towns, would belong to the mortals. The world below — the inside of the hills, the caverns beneath the bogs, the passages under the ancient burial mounds — would belong to the gods. And the two Irelands, the seen and the unseen, would exist side by side, sharing the same ground, separated by nothing but a skin of earth and a veil of mist.

達南神族，傳統告訴我們，同意了。他們別無選擇，而他們知道——凡人太多了，而諸神厭倦了戰爭，而這個瓜分，無論看起來多麼不平等，都是一條活路。於是條約訂下了：白晝世界，那翠綠的山丘、田野、城鎮，將屬於凡人。下面的世界——山的內部、沼澤之下的洞穴、古老葬丘下方的通道——將屬於諸神。而兩個愛爾蘭，可見的與不可見的，將並肩存在，共享同一片土地，只被一層地皮與一道迷霧之紗所分隔。

This is the foundational image of the Celtic imagination — and it is unlike anything in any other mythology in this garden. In Greece, the gods are on Olympus, above. In Mesopotamia, the gods are in the sky-temple. In the Norse world, the gods are in Asgard, across the rainbow. But in Ireland, the gods are not above or across. They are beneath. They went down. They live inside the hills you walk over every day, a few feet below the grass. The Celtic Otherworld is not far away. It is right here, under your feet, one step through the mist away. And this is why the Irish landscape, more than any other, feels haunted — because the tradition insists that the gods never left. They just went underground. And the mortal Ireland is built, literally, on top of the divine one. The next chapter tells how the gods made their descent.

這是塞爾特想像的奠基性意象——而它不同於這座園中任何其他神話裡的任何東西。在希臘，諸神在奧林帕斯，在上。在兩河流域，諸神在天上的神廟。在北歐世界，諸神在阿斯加德，彩虹的彼端。但在愛爾蘭，諸神不在上方，也不在彼端。他們在底下。他們走下去了。他們住在你每天走過的山丘內部，在草皮之下幾英尺處。塞爾特的異世界並不遙遠。它就在這裡，在你腳下，穿過迷霧一步之遙。而這便是為什麼愛爾蘭的風景，比任何其他地方，更令人覺得鬧鬼——因為傳統堅持諸神從未離開。他們只是到了地下。而凡人的愛爾蘭，字面意義上，是建在神聖愛爾蘭的上面。下一章講述諸神如何下凡。

Chapter 4 · 第四章

The Retreat into the Síd

退入妖精之丘

And so the Tuatha Dé Danann went down. One by one, the gods took their possessions and their people and descended into the great burial mounds of Ireland — the síd, the ancient passage-tombs that dotted the landscape, older than any memory. Each god took a mound. The Dagda took one. Brigid took one. Ogma, Lugh, Mannanán the sea-god — each found a hill, opened the way in, and went below. And inside the mounds, the tradition tells us, the gods' halls still stood — lit by an unchanging light, feasting eternal, where time ran differently and no one grew old.

於是達南神族走下去了。一個接一個，諸神帶著他們的財產與族人，下降到愛爾蘭那些巨大的葬丘——那些妖精之丘、那些遍布風景、比任何記憶都古老的通道墓——之中。每一位神取一座丘。大袞達取了一座。布麗姬取了一座。奧格馬、盧格、海神曼南南——每一位都找到一座山，打開入口，走了下去。而在那些丘的內部，傳統告訴我們，諸神的殿堂仍矗立著——被不變的光所照亮，盛宴永恆，那裡時間以不同方式流逝，無人變老。

And from that day, the Tuatha Dé Danann were no longer called gods. They were called the aes sídhe — the people of the mounds — or simply the sídhe, the fairy-folk. And the mortals, walking over the green hills above, knew that beneath their feet, in the hollow hills, the old powers lived on. And the two worlds touched — at the mounds' entrances, in the mist, at certain times of year — and when they touched, mortals could fall through into the Otherworld, and the sídhe could come up into ours. This is the source of every Irish fairy-story, every tale of being taken by the fairies, every account of a person who stepped into a mist and was never seen again. The whole vast literature of Celtic enchantment grows from this one fact: the gods did not leave. They are still here. They are under the hill.

而從那一天起，達南神族不再被稱為神。他們被稱為 aes sídhe——丘之民——或簡稱 sídhe，仙靈。而凡人，走在上方翠綠的山丘之上，知道在他們腳下，在那些空心的山丘裡，古老的力量仍活著。而這兩個世界相觸——在丘的入口，在迷霧中，在一年中的某些時刻——而當它們相觸之時，凡人可能跌入異世界，而妖精可能上來到我們的世界。這是每一個愛爾蘭仙靈故事、每一個「被仙靈擄走」的故事、每一個「某人走進迷霧便再也未被看見」之記述的源頭。整個龐大的塞爾特魔法文學，都從這一個事實中生長出來：諸神沒有離開。他們仍在這裡。他們在山丘之下。

And the tradition gives us, here, the most haunting figure of all: Mannanán mac Lir, the sea-god, who did not go into a mound but rode the waves between the two worlds, the ferryman of the boundary, the one who could pass freely between mortal Ireland and the Otherworld, who wore a cloak of mist and drove a chariot over the sea. Mannanán is the god of the threshold — the one who stands at the door between the two Irelands and decides who may pass. He is, in a sense, the patron of every story this cycle will tell — because every Irish story, sooner or later, involves someone crossing the boundary, and Mannanán is always there, at the edge of the mist, watching. The gods are under the hills. The sea-god rides between. And the mortals walk above, never quite sure whether the green hill they are climbing is solid all the way down.

而傳統，在此處，給了我們最縈繞人心的形象：曼南南·麥克·利爾，海神，他沒有走進一座丘，而是乘著兩個世界之間的波浪，邊界的擺渡人，那個能在凡人愛爾蘭與異世界之間自由通行之人，他穿著一件迷霧之斗篷，駕著戰車在海上。曼南南是門檻之神——那個站在兩個愛爾蘭之間的門口、決定誰可通行之人。在某種意義上，他是這個循環將講述的每一個故事的守護神——因為每一個愛爾蘭故事，遲早，都涉及某人跨越邊界，而曼南南總是在那裡，在迷霧的邊緣，注視著。諸神在山丘之下。海神在其間乘行。而凡人在上方行走，永遠不確定他們正在攀登的那座綠丘，是否一路實心到底。

Chapter 5 · 第五章

The Two Irelands

兩個愛爾蘭

And so, from this moment on, Ireland is two — and every story the Irish will tell, for the next two thousand years, will be haunted by that doubleness. There is the Ireland of the mortals: the farms, the cattle, the kings in their halls, the warriors, the poets, the feuds, the battles — the visible world, the world of history and weather and death. And there is the Ireland of the sídhe: the eternal halls under the hills, the unaging feasts, the music that never ends, the youth that never fades — the invisible world, the world of magic and timelessness and the strange.

於是，從此刻起，愛爾蘭是兩個——而愛爾蘭人在其後兩千年裡將講述的每一個故事，都將被那份雙重性所縈繞。有凡人的愛爾蘭：農場、牲畜、大廳裡的王、戰士、詩人、宿仇、戰役——可見的世界，歷史與天氣與死亡的世界。還有妖精的愛爾蘭：山丘之下永恆的殿堂、不老的盛宴、不絕的音樂、不褪的青春——不可見的世界，魔法與永恆與奇異的世界。

And the boundary between them is thin. The Irish have a word for this — and the word matters: thin places, they call them, the spots where the veil between the worlds wears through, where a mortal can step from one Ireland into the other without meaning to. A mist on a hillside. A door in a rock that was not there before. A strange music from inside a mound. A beautiful stranger who is too beautiful to be human. The thin places are everywhere, in the Irish imagination, and the whole literature of the Celtic cycles is, in a sense, the record of people who found them — who stepped through, and who came back changed, or did not come back at all.

而它們之間的邊界是薄的。愛爾蘭人有一個詞來稱呼這個——而這個詞很重要：他們稱之為「薄地」，那些兩個世界之間的紗被磨穿之處，那些一個凡人可以無意中從一個愛爾蘭踏入另一個之處。山坡上的一片迷霧。一塊石頭上一扇先前不在的門。一座丘內傳來的奇異音樂。一個美得不可能是人的陌生人。薄地，在愛爾蘭的想像裡，無處不在，而整個塞爾特循環的文學，在某種意義上，是那些找到它們之人的記錄——那些踏了過去、而歸來時已變、或根本沒有歸來之人。

This doubleness is the Celtic gift to the world's mythic imagination, and it sets the Irish stories apart from every other in this garden. In Greece, the gods are far away and the heroes visit them rarely. In the Norse world, the gods are doomed but distinct. In India, the divine takes human form by choice. But in Ireland, the divine is always here, just out of sight, one wrong step away. The Irish hero does not journey to the gods. The gods are next door. And the great fear — and the great wonder — of every Irish story is that the wall between the two Irelands might, at any moment, give way. The next stages of our journey will show us the mortal Ireland in its heroic age: the age of Cúchulainn, the Hound of Ulster, and the great cattle raid that nearly broke the island in two. But even in that most mortal of stories, the sídhe will be there — under the hill, in the mist, watching. They are always watching.

這份雙重性，是塞爾特給世界神話想像的禮物，而它使愛爾蘭的故事與這座園中其他每一個都不同。在希臘，諸神遙遠，英雄們很少造訪他們。在北歐世界，諸神註定毀滅卻截然分明。在印度，神聖自願取人形。但在愛爾蘭，神聖總是在此，就在視線之外，錯走一步之遙。愛爾蘭的英雄不是去諸神那裡旅行。諸神就在隔壁。而每一個愛爾蘭故事的大恐懼——以及大奇蹟——便是兩個愛爾蘭之間的那堵牆，可能在任何時刻崩塌。我們旅程的接下來幾個階段，將向我們展示凡人愛爾蘭在其英雄時代：庫丘林、阿爾斯特之犬的時代，以及那場幾乎將此島裂為兩半的偉大奪牛之戰。但即便在那個最凡人的故事裡，妖精也將在那裡——在山丘之下、在迷霧之中、注視著。他們總是在注視。

THE GODS WENT UNDERGROUND

諸神走入了地下

The mortals came from Spain, and the poet sang the island into their hands. Three goddesses asked their names be remembered, and one — Ériu — won, and her name became the island's forever. At Tara, the strangest treaty: above-ground to mortals, below to the gods. And the Tuatha Dé Danann descended, each into a burial mound, and became the fairy-folk, the sídhe, the powers of the hidden Ireland that has existed beneath the mortal one ever since. The sea-god rides between. The mist is thin. And now the story turns to the mortal Ireland — to a boy who killed a hound and took its name.
