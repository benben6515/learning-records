THE TÁIN

奪　牛　長　征　記

QUEEN MEDB · THE BROWN BULL OF COOLEY · CÚCHULAINN AT THE FORD · FERDIAD

A queen's pride. A bull she did not own. An army sent to take it. And, lying stricken by an ancient curse, a whole province that could not defend itself — except for one seventeen-year-old boy, who stood at a ford and fought the entire army of Connacht, one champion at a time, for the length of a cattle raid. And when, at last, they sent against him the one man he could not bear to kill — his foster-brother, his friend — the central epic of the Irish tradition became the central tragedy too. 一位王后的驕傲。一頭她不擁有的公牛。一支被派去奪取它的軍隊。還有，被一個古老詛咒所擊而躺臥的整個省份，無法自衛——除了一個十七歲的男孩，他站在一處渡口，與整支康諾特軍隊作戰，一次一位勇士，持續了一場奪牛之征。而當他們終於派來那個他不忍殺之人——他的義兄弟、他的朋友——愛爾蘭傳統的核心史詩，也成了核心悲劇。

Chapter 1 · 第一章

The Pillow Talk

枕邊之爭

The Táin — the great central epic of the Irish tradition — begins, famously, in bed. Queen Medb of Connacht and her husband King Ailill are lying together one night, and they begin, as couples sometimes do, to compare themselves. And the comparison escalates. They inventory their wealth — their land, their herds, their treasures, their household goods — and they find, item by item, that they are perfectly matched. Everything Ailill has, Medb has. Everything Medb has, Ailill has. They are equal in every thing, down to the last sheep and the last ring.

《奪牛記》——愛爾蘭傳統那偉大的核心史詩——著名地，開始於床上。康諾特的梅芙王后與她的丈夫阿伊里爾王某夜躺在一起，而他們開始，一如情侶有時所做，彼此比較。而那場比較升級了。他們盤點他們的財富——他們的土地、牲畜、珍寶、家財——而他們發現，一項一項地，他們完美地相當。阿伊里爾所擁有的一切，梅芙也擁有。梅芙所擁有的一切，阿伊里爾也擁有。他們在每一件事上平等，直到最後一隻羊與最後一枚戒指。

Until the bulls. Ailill has a magnificent white bull, the Finnbenach, the White-Horned, the finest in all his herds — and Medb, for all her wealth, has no bull to match it. Her herds are vast, but her greatest bull, the one that should have been hers, refused to be owned by a woman and went over to Ailill's herd of his own will. And so, in this one thing, Ailill is greater. And Medb, the tradition tells us, could not bear it. Her pride — and the Irish tradition is unsparing about this — was the equal of her wealth, and the one gap in that wealth was, to her, unbearable. She would not be second. Not even to her husband. Not even over a bull.

直到公牛。阿伊里爾擁有一頭宏偉的白牛，芬恩貝納赫，白角者，他所有牛群中最棒的——而梅芙，儘管富有，卻沒有一頭牛能與之匹敵。她的牛群浩大，但她最偉大的公牛，那頭本應屬於她的，拒絕被一個女子所擁有，自願走到了阿伊里爾的牛群之中。於是，在這一件事上，阿伊里爾更勝。而梅芙，傳統告訴我們，無法忍受。她的驕傲——而愛爾蘭傳統對此毫不留情——與她的財富相當，而那財富中唯一的一個缺口，對她而言，無法承受。她不要屈居第二。即便對她的丈夫。即便為了一頭公牛。

And so she sent messengers across Ireland to find a bull equal to the Finnbenach — and they found one. In the province of Ulster, in Cooley, there lived a great brown bull, the Donn Cúailnge, the Brown of Cooley, mightier than any bull in the land. And Medb sent to borrow him, offering a fair price and friendship. And the owner agreed. And then Medb's messengers, drunk on the queen's mead, boasted that Medb would have taken the bull by force anyway — and the owner, hearing this, broke the agreement and refused to lend. And Medb, her pride now wounded as well as her competitiveness, decided that if she could not borrow the bull she would take it. And she gathered the army of Connacht, and her allies from all of Ireland, and she marched east, into Ulster, to steal the brown bull of Cooley. The whole vast epic — the central story of Irish mythology — grows from a woman's inability to accept being second-best in one single thing.

於是她派使者走遍愛爾蘭尋找一頭與芬恩貝納赫相當的公牛——而他們找到了一頭。在阿爾斯特省，在庫利，住著一頭偉大的褐牛，唐恩·庫阿林傑，庫利之褐，比島上任何公牛都更強。而梅芙派人去借，提出一個公平的價錢與友誼。而那位主人同意了。然後梅芙的使者，醉於王后的蜜酒，吹噓梅芙無論如何都會以武力奪走那頭公牛——而那位主人，聽了這話，毀了協議，拒絕出借。而梅芙，她的驕傲如今與她的好勝一同受傷，決定如果她借不到那頭公牛，她就要奪走它。於是她召集了康諾特的軍隊，以及她來自全愛爾蘭的盟友，向東行軍，進入阿爾斯特，去偷那頭庫利的褐牛。整部浩瀚的史詩——愛爾蘭神話的核心故事——都從一個女子無法接受自己在一件單一之事上屈居第二而生長出來。

Chapter 2 · 第二章

The March of Connacht

康諾特之行軍

And the army of Connacht marched — vast, the tradition tells us, one of the greatest hosts Ireland had ever seen. Medb had gathered not only her own warriors but allies from every province: the men of Leinster, the men of Munster, exiles and mercenaries, a force so large that the tradition says they drank dry every river they crossed. And they marched east, toward Ulster, toward the brown bull — and the tradition gives us, in loving and ominous detail, the journey: the omens, the warnings, the strange encounters, the prophetesses who foretold disaster, the very landscape seeming to resist them.

而康諾特的軍隊行軍了——浩大，傳統告訴我們，愛爾蘭所曾見最偉大的軍隊之一。梅芙不只召集了她自己的戰士，還有來自每個省份的盟友：倫斯特的人、芒斯特的人、流亡者與傭兵，一支如此龐大的軍隊，以至於傳統說他們喝乾了他們所渡過的每一條河。而他們向東行軍，朝向阿爾斯特，朝向那頭褐牛——而傳統以鍾愛而不祥的細節給了我們這趟旅程：徵兆、警告、奇異的相遇、預言災難的女先知、連風景似乎都在抵抗他們。

And among the omens was a single figure — a young girl, the tradition says, who appeared to Medb and the army and foretold, in cryptic verse, that the whole expedition would be bloodied, that the brown bull would cost more than it was worth, and that a single warrior — unnamed, but clearly Cúchulainn — would hold the whole army at a ford. The figure was, the tradition hints, the Morrígan herself, the war-goddess, the phantom queen, who appears before every great battle to choose who will die. And she had chosen this one. The army marched on. But the shadow was already over them.

而在那些徵兆之中有一個形象——一個年輕女孩，傳統說，她出現在梅芙與軍隊面前，以隱晦的詩預言整場遠征將浴血，那頭褐牛的代價將超過它的價值，而一個單一的戰士——未被點名，但顯然是庫丘林——將在一處渡口擋住整支軍隊。那個形象是，傳統暗示，摩莉甘本人，戰爭女神，幻影之后，她在每一場大戰之前出現，選擇誰將死。而她選了這一場。軍隊繼續行軍。但陰影已然籠罩他們。

And the army crossed into Ulster, and looked for the brown bull — and found, to their confusion, that the herds of Cooley had been driven into the hills and hidden. And the tradition gives us, here, a small comic touch: the brown bull himself, sensing the invasion, was given a warning by a messenger and, in a detail the Irish tradition loves, the bull gathered his cows and fled into the mountains, refusing to be caught until the very end of the story. The prize the whole army came for was not even there to be taken. The brown bull was loose in the hills. And the army of Connacht, deep in enemy territory, with their prize vanished, would now have to deal with the thing they had not expected: a single boy, standing at a ford, who would not let them pass.

而軍隊越入阿爾斯特，尋找那頭褐牛——卻發現，令他們困惑的是，庫利的牛群已被趕入山中藏起。而傳統，在此處，給了我們一個小小的喜劇之筆：那頭褐牛本身，感覺到了入侵，被一個使者警告，而在一個愛爾蘭傳統所愛的細節裡，那頭牛聚集了他的母牛，逃入山中，直到故事的最後才肯被捉。整支軍隊所來求的那個獎品，甚至不在那裡可被奪取。那頭褐牛在山中亂跑。而康諾特的軍隊，深入敵境，獎品消失，如今將不得不對付他們所未預料之物：一個單一的男孩，站在一處渡口，不肯讓他們通過。

Chapter 3 · 第三章

Ulster Stricken

阿爾斯特之傾

And here the curse of Macha fell. The men of Ulster — every warrior, every champion, every farmer who could lift a spear — felt, all at once, the labor-pangs the goddess had laid on them generations before. They collapsed. They lay on the ground, curled around their bellies, groaning, weak as women in childbirth, utterly unable to fight. The entire fighting power of Ulster was nullified, in a single hour, by a curse laid for the vanity of a long-dead king. And the army of Connacht walked into a province that could not defend itself.

而在這裡，瑪查之咒降下了。阿爾斯特的男子們——每一位戰士、每一位英雄、每一位能舉矛的農夫——同時感覺到了那位女神在數代之前所加諸的產痛。他們崩潰了。他們躺在地上，蜷縮著肚子，呻吟，虛弱如分娩中的女子，完全無法戰鬥。阿爾斯特的全部戰力，在一個時辰之內，被一個為一個早已死去之王的虛榮而立的詛咒所取消。而康諾特的軍隊走進了一個無法自衛的省份。

The tradition gives us, here, the pathos of a stricken land — the great halls empty, the warriors groaning on their beds, the women weeping, the cattle undefended. This is what Macha's curse means: not that Ulster loses, but that Ulster cannot even fight. The humiliation is total. And into this helpless province, Medb's army swept, taking what they wanted, raiding the herds, gathering the brown bull's cows, moving toward the bull himself wherever he had hidden. The conquest was easy. The resistance was zero.

傳統，在此處，給了我們一片傾覆之地的哀婉——那些大廳空了，戰士們在床上呻吟，女子們哭泣，牲畜無人保護。這便是瑪查之咒所意味的：不是阿爾斯特會輸，而是阿爾斯特甚至無法戰。那份屈辱是全面的。而進入這個無助的省份，梅芙的軍隊掃過，取他們所要，劫掠牛群，聚集褐牛的母牛，朝那頭公牛不論藏於何處而行。那場征服是容易的。抵抗是零。

Except for one. The tradition is careful to tell us that Cúchulainn was not stricken. He was, the various explanations go, either not truly of Ulster blood, or asleep when the curse took, or something other than fully mortal — and whatever the reason, he alone of all the men of Ulster could stand. And so the seventeen-year-old boy, the Hound of Culann, stood up in a stricken province, took his weapons, and went out alone to meet the entire army of Connacht. The central image of the Irish epic is this: one boy, alone, against an army, at a ford. The tradition tells us he cut off the army's advance by a simple, brilliant tactic — he would stand at a narrow ford, where only one warrior could cross at a time, and he would fight each champion who came, single combat, one after another, day after day. The whole army could not pass while one champion held the ford. And so the boy held it. And the long, bloody series of single combats that is the heart of the Táin began.

除了一人。傳統謹慎地告訴我們，庫丘林未被擊倒。他，各種解釋是，要麼不真正是阿爾斯特血脈，要麼詛咒降臨時他正在睡覺，要麼是某種不完全是凡人之物——而無論原因是什麼，在所有阿爾斯特的男子之中，唯他一人能站立。於是那個十七歲的男孩，庫蘭之犬，在一個傾覆的省份中站起來，拿起他的武器，獨自出去迎戰整支康諾特軍隊。愛爾蘭史詩的核心意象便是這個：一個男孩，獨自，對抗一支軍隊，在一處渡口。傳統告訴我們，他以一個簡單而絕妙的戰術切斷了軍隊的前進——他會站在一處狹窄的渡口，那裡一次只能有一個戰士通過，而他會與每一個來的勇士作戰，單挑，一個接一個，日復一日。當一個勇士守住渡口之時，整支軍隊便無法通過。於是那個男孩守住了它。而那構成《奪牛記》核心的、漫長而血腥的單挑序列，便開始了。

Chapter 4 · 第四章

The Single Combats

單挑

And the single combats began, and they went on for days, and the tradition gives us each one. Champion after champion of Connacht came down to the ford, and Cúchulainn fought each, and killed each. The tradition tells us the boy fought with a ferocity that appalled the watching army — that his warp-spasm came upon him in each fight, that he became the monster, and that the champions of Connacht came to the ford knowing they were going to die. And the army could not pass. While the boy lived and held the ford, the cattle raid was stalled, day after day after day.

而單挑開始了，持續了數日，而傳統給了我們每一場。康諾特的勇士一個接一個來到渡口，而庫丘林與每一個交戰，殺了每一個。傳統告訴我們那個男孩以一種令觀戰軍隊驚駭的兇猛作戰——他的戰狂變形在每一場戰中降臨於他，他成了那個怪獸，而康諾特的勇士們來到渡口，知道他們將死。而軍隊無法通過。當那個男孩活著、守住渡口之時，那場奪牛之征便停滯了，日復一日，日復一日。

And the tradition gives us, woven through the combats, the Irish code of honor — the geasa, the sacred taboos that bound every warrior. Cúchulainn had his geasa: things he must do, and things he must not do, on pain of ruin. He must not refuse a challenge. He must not eat dog (for he was the Hound). He must accept hospitality. And the geasa, the tradition shows us again and again, are not rules to make a hero safer — they are the net in which the hero is finally caught. For the geasa conflict. A hero will be commanded to do a thing that violates one geis to honor another, and either choice destroys him. The Irish hero is a man walking a tightrope of sacred obligations, any one of which, broken, will kill him. And Cúchulainn, holding the ford, is walking that tightrope every day.

而傳統，編織在那些戰鬥之中，給了我們愛爾蘭的榮譽法則——geasa，約束每一位戰士的神聖禁忌。庫丘林有他的 geasa：他必須做的事，與他不可做的事，違則毀滅。他不可拒絕挑戰。他不可吃狗（因為他是犬）。他必須接受款待。而 geasa，傳統一再展示，不是使英雄更安全的規則——它們是英雄最終被捕捉於其中的網。因為 geasa 會衝突。一位英雄會被命令去做一件為了履行一個 geis 而違反另一個 geis 之事，而無論哪個選擇都會毀了他。愛爾蘭的英雄是一個走在神聖義務的鋼索上之人，其中任何一個被破壞，都會殺死他。而庫丘林，守著渡口，每一天都在走那條鋼索。

And the combats ground on, and the army grew frustrated, and Cúchulainn grew exhausted — for even the greatest warrior cannot fight forever without rest. And the tradition gives us, here, the famous compact: Cúchulainn would fight one champion a day, every day, and while he fought, the army could advance; but each night, he must be allowed to rest, and during his rest the army must halt. It was a bargain born of mutual respect — the army acknowledging that the boy was holding them through sheer impossible valor, and the boy acknowledging that even he could not hold forever without sleep. And so the Táin became a rhythm: a champion, a fight, a death, an advance; a night, a rest; and again. Until, at last, the army sent the one champion Cúchulainn could not bring himself to kill.

而那些戰鬥磨著，軍隊漸感挫折，庫丘林漸感耗竭——因為即便最偉大的戰士也無法永無止境地戰而不休。而傳統，在此處，給了我們那個著名的協定：庫丘林每天與一位勇士作戰，而當他作戰之時，軍隊可以前進；但每夜，他必須被允許休息，而在他休息之時軍隊必須停止。那是一個出於彼此尊重的協定——軍隊承認那個男孩是憑純然不可能的勇氣擋住他們，而那個男孩承認即便他也無法不眠地永遠守住。於是《奪牛記》成了一個節奏：一個勇士、一場戰、一次死亡、一次前進；一夜、一次休息；再來。直到，終於，軍隊派來了那個庫丘林無法狠心殺的勇士。

Chapter 5 · 第五章

The Morrígan's Attack

摩莉甘之攻

And into this grinding stand-off came the Morrígan — the war-goddess herself, the phantom queen, who had appeared to the army at the start and foretold blood. And the Morrígan, the tradition tells us, came to Cúchulainn in the form of a beautiful woman, and offered him her love. And Cúchulainn, devoted to Emer and suspicious of divine offers, refused her. And the Morrígan — whose vanity, like Medb's, could not bear refusal — turned to rage, and swore she would have her revenge.

而進入這場磨人的僵持的，是摩莉甘——戰爭女神本身，幻影之后，她在開始之時曾出現在軍隊面前並預言浴血。而摩莉甘，傳統告訴我們，以一個美麗女子的形象來到庫丘林面前，向他獻上她的愛。而庫丘林，忠於艾梅、且對神聖的提議心存戒備，拒絕了她。而摩莉甘——她的虛榮，一如梅芙的，無法承受拒絕——轉為狂怒，發誓她要報復。

And her revenge was the three-fold attack. The tradition gives us the famous scene: the next time Cúchulainn was at the ford, the Morrígan came against him in three forms — as an eel, that wrapped around his legs in the water to trip him; as a wolf, that stampeded the cattle against him; as a heifer, that charged him at the ford. And in each form, Cúchulainn — the greatest warrior — wounded her, gave her a wound that would not heal. And the goddess, bleeding, withdrew. But the Irish tradition knows that a goddess does not forgive a wound, and the Morrígan would return.

而她的報復是那場三重之攻。傳統給了我們那個著名的場面：庫丘林下一次在渡口之時，摩莉甘以三個形態來攻擊他——作為一條鰻魚，在水中纏住他的腿以絆倒他；作為一隻狼，驅趕牲畜衝向他；作為一頭小母牛，在渡口衝向他。而在每一個形態中，庫丘林——最偉大的戰士——都傷了她，給了她一個不會癒合的傷。而那位女神，流著血，退去了。但愛爾蘭傳統知道，一位女神不會原諒一個傷，而摩莉甘會歸來。

And return she did — not in battle, but in disguise, as an old woman milking a cow, with three wounds on her body matching the three Cúchulainn had given her. And Cúchulainn, not recognizing her (for the geasa bound him to accept hospitality and bless whatever he was offered), blessed her wounds, and they healed — and so the Morrígan, by the hero's own unwitting blessing, was restored, and her hatred of him was complete. This little episode is the Irish way of saying something the whole tradition insists on: that the hero is hemmed in by forces he cannot see, that the geasa themselves become weapons against him, and that the gods — even the war-goddess — are not above pettiness. Cúchulainn held the ford against men. But the goddesses moved around him, and through him, and the trap was closing. The next chapter brings the worst of it.

而她歸來了——不是在戰鬥中，而是偽裝著，作為一個擠牛奶的老婦，身上有三個傷，與庫丘林所給她的三個相符。而庫丘林，未認出她（因為 geasa 約束他接受款待、祝福任何被獻給他之物），祝福了她的傷，而它們癒合了——於是摩莉甘，因英雄自己不知情的祝福，復原了，而她對他的恨便完整了。這個小插曲是愛爾蘭人說出整個傳統所堅持之事的方式：英雄被他看不見的力量所圍困，geasa 本身成了對付他的武器，而諸神——即便戰爭女神——也不超脫於小氣。庫丘林擋住渡口抵擋人。但女神們在他周圍移動，穿透他，而陷阱正在關閉。下一章帶來其中最糟的。

Chapter 6 · 第六章

Ferdiad at the Ford

渡口的費爾迪亞

And Medb, frustrated beyond endurance, played her cruelest card. She sent for Ferdiad — Cúchulainn's foster-brother, his fellow-student under Scáthach, the man who had trained beside him, eaten beside him, called him brother. And Medb offered Ferdiad a great reward — her daughter, and vast wealth — to go down to the ford and fight Cúchulainn. And Ferdiad, who loved his foster-brother, refused at first. And Medb, knowing the way of warriors, sent messengers who implied that Ferdiad was a coward, that he was afraid — and the geasa of honor, and the shame, and the reward, broke him. He went down to the ford.

而梅芙，挫折到無法忍受，打出了她最殘酷的一張牌。她派人去請費爾迪亞——庫丘林的義兄弟、他在斯卡哈之下的同窗、那個在他身旁訓練、在他身旁吃飯、稱他為兄弟之人。而梅芙向費爾迪亞提供一個巨大的報酬——她的女兒，以及浩大的財富——去下到渡口與庫丘林作戰。而費爾迪亞，他愛他的義兄弟，起初拒絕。而梅芙，知道戰士之道，派人暗示費爾迪亞是個懦夫、他害怕——而榮譽的 geasa、那份恥、那份報酬，擊垮了他。他下到了渡口。

And the two friends met, at the ford, and the tradition gives us the most heartbreaking scene in all of Irish literature. They spoke, first. They wept. They remembered their time together under Scáthach — the training, the fires, the brotherhood. And then they fought, because they had to, because the geasa and the shame and the queen's demand left them no choice. And they fought for three days. The first day, they fought as equals, and at nightfall they embraced, and wept, and exchanged weapons, and slept side by side. The second day, the same — ferocious combat by day, embraces and tears by night. And on the third day, Ferdiad, who was a great warrior, nearly won — he had the upper hand — and Cúchulainn, in desperation, called for the gae bolga.

而那兩位朋友相遇，在渡口，而傳統給了我們全部愛爾蘭文學中最令人心碎的場面。他們先說話。他們流淚。他們回憶他們在斯卡哈之下的時光——訓練、火堆、兄弟之情。然後他們戰了，因為他們不得不戰，因為 geasa、那份恥、那位王后的要求留給他們沒有選擇。而他們戰了三天。第一天，他們以匹敵之姿作戰，而在黃昏之時他們擁抱、流淚、交換武器、並肩而眠。第二天，同樣——白天激烈的戰鬥，夜晚的擁抱與眼淚。而在第三天，費爾迪亞，他是個偉大的戰士，幾乎贏了——他佔了上風——而庫丘林，在絕望之中，呼叫了 gae bolga。

And the gae bolga — the terrible barbed spear that Scáthach had taught only to Cúchulainn, the weapon that no armor could stop — was cast, by Cúchulainn's charioteer, from behind Ferdiad, and it struck him, and opened inside him, and Ferdiad fell. And the tradition tells us that Cúchulainn, seeing his foster-brother dead by his own weapon, broke. He ran to the body. He keened over it — the Irish lament, the wild cry of grief — and he said, the tradition records, the words that are the heart of the Táin's tragedy: "O Ferdiad, betrayed to your death. It was not I who killed you — it was the queen's guile, and the gae bolga, and the shame they put on you. We were brothers. Now I am alone." The ford was held. But the Hound of Culann, who had fought an army, was broken by the one fight that mattered most. The grief would never leave him. And his own death, the tradition tells us, was now not far off.

而 gae bolga——那把斯卡哈只教給庫丘林的可怕倒鈎矛，那件任何甲冑都擋不住的武器——被庫丘林的御者從費爾迪亞身後投出，而它擊中了他，在他體內張開，而費爾迪亞倒了。而傳統告訴我們，庫丘林，看見他的義兄弟死於他自己的武器，崩潰了。他跑到屍體旁。他對著它慟哭——愛爾蘭的挽歌，那狂野的悲慟之喊——而他說，傳統記錄，那些作為《奪牛記》悲劇核心的話：「噢，費爾迪亞，被出賣至死。殺你的不是我——是那王后的詭計、是 gae bolga、是他們加諸於你的恥。我們曾是兄弟。如今我獨自一人。」渡口守住了。但庫蘭之犬，那個與一支軍隊作戰之人，被那場最要緊的一戰所擊碎。那份悲慟將永遠不離開他。而他自己的死，傳統告訴我們，如今已不遠了。

Chapter 7 · 第七章

The Bulls' Battle & the Waste

雙牛之戰與虛無

And the end came — not with a great human battle, but with the strange, fitting, anticlimactic ending the Irish tradition loves. The men of Ulster, at last, rose from their curse — the labor-pangs passed, the warriors could stand — and the two armies met in a final clash. But the tradition tells us, almost offhandedly, that the human battle was almost beside the point. The real climax had already happened, between the two bulls themselves.

而終局來了——不是以一場偉大的人類之戰，而是以愛爾蘭傳統所愛的那個奇異、貼切、反高潮的結局。阿爾斯特的男子們，終於，從他們的詛咒中起身——產痛過去了，戰士們能站立了——而兩軍在最後的衝突中相遇。但傳統告訴我們，幾乎是順帶地，那場人類之戰幾乎無關緊要。真正的高潮已經發生了，在那兩頭公牛之間。

For the brown bull of Cooley — the Donn Cúailnge, the prize Medb had come all this way to steal — met, on the way home, the white bull of Connacht, the Finnbenach, the bull Medb had wanted to match. And the two bulls fought. The tradition gives us the battle in epic detail: the two great beasts, tearing at each other, trampling the land, the brown bull goring the white, the white fighting back, the whole island shaking. And the brown bull won. He killed the white bull, tore it apart, and carried the white bull's remains on his horns — and then, maddened, exhausted, his heart burst, and he died. The brown bull made it back to Ulster, and collapsed, and died, with the white bull's flesh still on his horns.

因為庫利之褐牛——唐恩·庫阿林傑，梅芙千里迢迢來偷的獎品——在歸途上，遇見了康諾特的白牛，芬恩貝納赫，那頭梅芙想要匹敵的公牛。而那兩頭公牛交戰了。傳統以史詩般的細節給了我們那場戰：兩頭巨獸，彼此撕扯，踐踏大地，褐牛頂穿白牛，白牛反擊，整座島震動。而褐牛贏了。他殺了白牛，把它撕碎，把白牛的殘骸頂在角上——然後，發狂、耗竭，他的心臟爆裂，他死了。褐牛回到了阿爾斯特，倒下，死了，角上還掛著白牛的血肉。

And this is the great joke — and the great truth — of the Táin's ending: that the entire epic, the vast army, the queen's pride, the boy's stand at the ford, the death of Ferdiad, the whole blood-soaked cattle raid — was, in the end, for nothing. The brown bull died. The white bull died. Medb did not get to keep the prize she had come for. Ailill still had no rival. The cattle raid ended in waste. No one won. The Irish tradition, here, gives the world one of its most honest endings: the great quest, undertaken for pride, achieves nothing but corpses, and the very thing fought over destroys itself in the fighting. Medb and Ailill, the tradition tells us, went home, and made peace, and were equal again — exactly as equal as they had been before, when the whole thing started, in bed. Nothing was gained. Everything was lost. And the boy who had held the ford carried, from that day forward, a grief that would kill him. The next stage tells how.

而這便是《奪牛記》結局的偉大笑話——也是其偉大的真理：整部史詩，那支浩大的軍隊，那位王后的驕傲，那個男孩在渡口的堅守，費爾迪亞之死，整場浴血的奪牛之征——最終，為的是虛無。褐牛死了。白牛死了。梅芙未能保住她所來求的獎品。阿伊里爾仍然沒有對手。那場奪牛之征以虛無告終。沒有人贏。愛爾蘭傳統，在此處，給了世界它最誠實的結局之一：那場為驕傲而起的偉大追尋，所成就的不過是屍體，而那個被爭奪之物本身在爭奪中毀滅了自己。梅芙與阿伊里爾，傳統告訴我們，回家，議和，再度平等——正好與他們在一切開始之時、在床上、一樣平等。一無所獲。一切皆失。而那個守住渡口的男孩，從那一天起，帶著一份將殺死他的悲慟。下一階段講述如何。

THE RAID FOR NOTHING

為虛無而征

A queen's pride sent an army for a bull. A curse laid an entire province low, and one boy held a ford against the host, day after day, until they sent his foster-brother against him — and the gae bolga, the weapon his teacher had given only him, killed the one friend he could not bear to fight. And then the bulls themselves met, and the brown killed the white, and the brown died of a bursting heart. The raid ended in waste. No one won. The boy went home carrying a grief that would kill him. The central epic of Ireland is, in its end, the story of how pride destroys everything it touches, including the very prize it sought.
