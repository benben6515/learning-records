MODERN ECHOES

現　代　迴　響

SNORRI · WAGNER · TOLKIEN · GAIMAN · 1220–2017

The doomed gods refused to die. An Icelandic Christian preserved their stories out of love. A composer turned their curse into sixteen hours of music. A professor built the most beloved mythology of the modern age out of their bones. And a novelist retold them, at last, for everyone. 註定毀滅的諸神拒絕死去。一個冰島基督徒出於愛保存了他們的故事。一位作曲家將他們的詛咒譜成十六小時的音樂。一位教授以他們的骨骼建造了當代最受鍾愛的神話。而一位小說家，終於為每一個人，重述了他們。

Chapter 1 · 第一章

Snorri & the Preservation

斯諾里與保存

It is a strange fact that we know any of this at all. By the time Iceland officially converted to Christianity, around the year 1000, the old gods had been worshipped for perhaps three centuries and were on their way to being forgotten. The poets still knew the stories — the skalds, the singers, the men who could recite the deeds of Odin and Thor — but a new faith was spreading, and oral memory dies fast. We owe the survival of Norse mythology almost entirely to one man: Snorri Sturluson, an Icelandic chieftain, politician, and (for a man of his rank) unusually learned scribe, who around the year 1220 sat down to write a handbook for poets.

一個奇異的事實是：我們居然能知道這些故事中的任何一個。當冰島約在公元 1000 年正式改信基督教之時，舊諸神已被崇拜了約三個世紀，正走向被遺忘。詩人們仍然知曉這些故事——那些吟遊詩人、歌手，那些能背誦奧丁與索爾事蹟的人——但一種新的信仰正在傳播，而口述記憶消亡得很快。北歐神話之所以得以保存，幾乎完全歸功於一個人：斯諾里·斯圖魯松，一位冰島的酋長、政治家，以及（以他的階級而言）異常博學的書記，約在公元 1220 年坐下來，為詩人寫一本手冊。

That handbook, the Prose Edda, was meant to explain the old poetic diction to younger poets who had stopped understanding it — what "the horse of the sea" meant, and "the sword of Odin," and all the riddling kennings the skalds had used. But to explain those kennings, Snorri had to retell the myths themselves — all of them, from creation to Ragnarök — in clear, witty prose, so that no meaning would be lost. He was a Christian, writing about the gods his ancestors had abandoned. He did not believe in them. But he believed they should not be forgotten. Because of him, they were not. Without Snorri, Odin and Thor and Loki would be names carved on a few runestones, and nothing more.

那本手冊，即《散文埃達》，本意是向那些已不再理解舊詩歌語言的年輕詩人解釋它——「海之馬」是什麼意思、「奧丁之劍」是什麼意思，以及吟遊詩人所用的一切謎語般的隱喻。但要解釋這些隱喻，斯諾里必須重述神話本身——全部，從創世到諸神黃昏——以清晰、機智的散文，使任何意義都不致喪失。他是一個基督徒，書寫著他的祖先所拋棄的神。他不信他們。但他相信他們不應被遺忘。因為他，他們沒有被遺忘。若無斯諾里，奧丁、索爾、洛基，只不過是刻在幾塊盧恩石上的名字，僅此而已。

Chapter 2 · 第二章

Wagner's Ring

華格納的指環

In the middle of the nineteenth century, a German composer named Richard Wagner took the Völsung story and the curse of Andvari's ring, and reshaped them into a single, overwhelming work: Der Ring des Nibelungen — the Ring of the Nibelung — a cycle of four operas that take sixteen hours to perform, from beginning to end. He took the gold, the dragon, the betrayed hero, the fire-surrounded Valkyrie, the gods who know they will fall — and wove them, with the music of a hundred-piece orchestra, into one of the most ambitious artworks ever made by a human being.

在十九世紀中葉，一位名叫理查·華格納的德國作曲家，取了沃爾松格的故事與安達瓦里指環的詛咒，將之重塑為一部單一、壓倒性的作品：《尼伯龍根的指環》——一個由四部歌劇組成、從頭到尾演出需十六小時的循環。他取了黃金、惡龍、被背叛的英雄、被火環繞的女武神、明知將隕落的諸神——並以百人管弦樂團的音樂，將之編織成人類有史以來最具雄心的藝術作品之一。

Wagner changed the story as he went. He made the ring a symbol of power and its renunciation, a meditation on whether love or dominion will rule the world. He invented the idea — not in the original — that the gods fall because they took the gold, that Valhalla itself burns at the end because of the curse. He gave Brünnhilde a final act of self-immolation that redeems the world. He built, in Bayreuth, a theatre specifically designed to perform this one work. Whatever one thinks of Wagner the man — and there is much to think — Wagner the artist poured the Norse myths into a vessel vast enough to hold them, and they have been flowing through Western culture ever since. Every later "ring of power," including Tolkien's, owes something to Wagner's.

華格納邊寫邊改動故事。他讓指環成為權力及其捨棄的象徵，一場關於愛或統治將主宰世界的沉思。他發明了（原著中沒有的）這個想法：諸神之所以隕落，是因為他們取了黃金；瓦爾哈拉之所以在最終焚燬，是因為那個詛咒。他給了布倫希爾德一個救贖世界的自焚終局。他在拜羅伊特建造了一座專為演出這一部作品而設計的劇院。無論人們如何看待華格納這個人——而值得思考的很多——作為藝術家的華格納，將北歐神話傾入一個足以承載它們的巨大器皿，而它們自此便在西方文化中流淌。之後每一枚「力量之戒」，包括托爾金的，都欠了華格納一些什麼。

Chapter 3 · 第三章

Tolkien & Middle-earth

托爾金與中土大陸

A few decades later, an Oxford professor of Old English and Old Norse named J.R.R. Tolkien began, almost privately, to build a mythology of his own — a mythology for England, he said, since England had lost its own. And the bones of it were Norse. Middle-earth takes its very name from Midgard, the walled world of mortals. The One Ring, found by the hero, that corrupts every bearer, that can only be destroyed in the fire where it was made — that is the ring of the Völsungs, reimagined. The wizard Gandalf — grey-cloaked, broad-brimmed hat, wandering, wise — is Odin in everything but name.

幾十年後，一位名為 J.R.R. 托爾金的牛津古英語與古北歐語教授，幾乎私下地，開始建造一個他自己的神話——他說，那是為英格蘭而造的神話，因為英格蘭已失去了它自己的。而它的骨架，是北歐的。中土大陸之名，正取自米德加爾特，那個有牆的凡人之國。那枚被英雄尋得的「至尊魔戒」，腐蝕每一個持有者，只能在它被鍛造的火焰中銷毀——那便是沃爾松格的指環，重新想像而成。巫師甘道夫——灰斗篷、寬邊帽、流浪、智慧——除了名字之外，全都是奧丁。

The debt runs deeper than plot. The Silmarillion, Tolkien's great mythology of the elder days, follows the Norse pattern almost structurally: a created cosmos, a rebellion, a long war, a doom foretold. The names of his dwarves — Thorin, Dwalin, Balin, Dáin — he lifted directly from the Völuspá's catalogue of dwarves, the Dvergatal, a list in the old poem. the Rohirrim speak Old English and live in a mead-hall that could have stood in Beowulf. Tolkien did not hide his sources; he loved them, and he gave them new life. Because of him, more people now know the shape of a Norse myth — a ring, a dragon, a doomed king — than have ever read the Edda.

這份債，比情節更深。《精靈寶鑽》，托爾金那部遠古時代的偉大神話，幾乎在結構上追隨北歐的模式：一個被創造的宇宙、一場叛亂、一場漫長的戰爭、一個預言的毀滅。他的矮人名字——索林、德瓦林、巴林、達因——他直接取自《女占卜者之歌》裡的矮人目錄，Dvergatal，那是古詩中的一份清單。洛汗的驃騎說古英語，住在一座本可立於《貝奧武夫》之中的蜜酒殿堂裡。托爾金並未隱瞞他的來源；他愛它們，並給了它們新的生命。因為他，如今知道北歐神話之形——一枚指環、一條惡龍、一位註定毀滅的君王——的人，比曾讀過埃達的人還多。

Chapter 4 · 第四章

Neil Gaiman's Retelling

尼爾·蓋曼的重述

In 2017, the English novelist Neil Gaiman — already famous for weaving myth into fiction — published a slim book called, simply, Norse Mythology. He did for the Eddas what Snorri had done eight centuries before: he retold them, clearly, for an audience that no longer spoke the old language. Gaiman's versions are witty, fast, and humane; he lets Loki be charming as well as monstrous, lets Thor be funny as well as strong, lets the gods be the complicated, doomed, likeable creatures the original poems always suggested they were.

2017 年，英國小說家尼爾·蓋曼——已以將神話編織進小說聞名——出版了一本薄薄的書，書名就叫做《北歐神話》。他為埃達所做的，正是斯諾里八個世紀前所做的：他清晰地為不再說那古老語言的讀者重述了它們。蓋曼的版本機智、明快、富有人情味；他讓洛基既迷人又可怖，讓索爾既滑稽又強壯，讓諸神成為原始詩篇一直所暗示的那種複雜、註定毀滅、令人喜愛的生靈。

This had been a long time coming. Gaiman had spent decades drawing on the Norse myths elsewhere: in his Sandman comics, where Odin and Loki appear; in American Gods, where Odin walks America as "Mr. Wednesday," a grifter recruiting old gods for a last stand. Norse Mythology was his love-letter made plain — the tales told the way he would tell them to his own children. And it landed at exactly the right moment: a culture newly hungry for old stories, a generation that had grown up on Tolkien and Marvel and wanted the originals. Because of Gaiman, hundreds of thousands of readers who had never heard of Snorri now know why the wolf eats the sun.

這其實是醞釀已久的。蓋曼數十年來在其他地方汲取北歐神話：在他的《睡魔》漫畫中，奧丁與洛基現身；在《美國眾神》中，奧丁以「星期三先生」之姿行走美國，一個為諸神最後一戰招募舊神的騙子。《北歐神話》是他那封情書的直白呈現——這些故事以他會講給自己孩子聽的方式被講述。而它在對的時刻降臨：一個對古老故事新近飢渴的文化，一個在托爾金與漫威之下長大、想要原典的世代。因為蓋曼，數十萬從未聽過斯諾里的讀者，如今知道巨狼為何吞噬太陽。

Chapter 5 · 第五章

Thor in the World

世界之中的索爾

And then there is the strangest afterlife of all. The Marvel Comics Thor, invented by Stan Lee, Larry Lieber, and Jack Kirby in 1962, took the Norse thunder-god and turned him into a superhero — a blonde, cape-wearing, hammer-swinging Avenger, born of a union of Odin and the earth-goddess Gaea, defending the Marvel Universe from Loki's schemes. This Thor has almost nothing to do with the red-bearded, goat-driving, giant-brawling farmer's god of the Eddas. And yet he has done more to spread the names of the Norse gods than any poet or scholar in history. Ask a child anywhere on earth who Thor is, and they know.

然後是最奇異的後世。漫威漫畫的索爾，由史丹·李、賴瑞·李伯與傑克·柯比於 1962 年創造，取了北歐雷神，將之變成一個超級英雄——金髮、披斗篷、揮鐵鎚的復仇者，由奧丁與大地女神蓋婭所生，保衛漫威宇宙免於洛基的詭計。這個索爾與埃達中那位紅鬚、趕羊、與巨人鬥毆的農夫之神幾乎毫無關係。然而，他傳播北歐諸神之名的工作，比歷史上任何詩人或學者都多。問地球上任何一個孩子索爾是誰，他們都知道。

Beyond pop culture, the old gods have found stranger worshippers. In the late twentieth century, a religious movement called Ásatrú — "faith in the Aesir" — revived the worship of Odin and Thor as a living religion. In Iceland, Ásatrú is a state-recognized faith, with its own temples and priests. Across the world, small groups gather to blot, to sacrifice, to honour the gods their ancestors left a thousand years ago. And far uglier: white-supremacist groups have, at times, seized on the old Norse symbols for their own ends, twisting a mythology that was never about race into a banner for hatred. The myths, like the ring, pass from hand to hand — and not every hand uses them well. That, too, is what they are now. The story of the doomed gods is no longer only a story. It is a living, contested, ever-shaping thing — and it refuses, still, to end.

在流行文化之外，舊諸神找到了更奇異的崇拜者。在二十世紀末，一個名為「阿薩特魯」——「信奉阿薩神族」——的宗教運動，復興了對奧丁與索爾的崇拜，使之成為一種活的宗教。在冰島，阿薩特魯是受國家承認的信仰，有自己的神廟與祭司。世界各地，小群的人聚在一起行布洛特、獻祭、敬奉他們祖先千年前所拋棄的諸神。而遠為醜陋的是：白人至上主義團體有時也攫取舊日的北歐符號以達自己的目的，把一個從來與種族無關的神話，扭曲成仇恨的旗幟。神話，一如指環，從一隻手傳到一隻手——而並非每一隻手都善用它。這，如今也是它們的一部分。註定毀滅的諸神的故事，已不再只是故事。它是一個活的、有爭議的、不斷塑造之物——而它，依然，不肯結束。

THE GODS WHO REFUSED TO DIE

拒絕死去的諸神

The doom of the gods was written before the worlds began. And yet here they are — a thousand years after their worship ended — still being told, still being sung, still being argued over. The Norse taught that nothing escapes its fate. But they also taught, by their own survival, that a good story is the one thing that comes close. The old gods will burn, someday. Their names will not.

This is the end of the Norse path. Return to the start — or look onward to other worlds.
