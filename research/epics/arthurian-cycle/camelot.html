THE FOUNDING OF CAMELOT

卡　美　洛　的　締　造

GUINEVERE · THE ROUND TABLE · MORGAN LE FAY · MERLIN'S DOOM

The king had his crown. Now he needed his court — and the building of that court is the second tragedy of the cycle, because it is built, from the very first stone, on the things that will destroy it. A marriage entered for politics, not love. A sister nursing a grudge in the shadows. And a prophet who can see his own end coming, and walks into it anyway, because the woman who will trap him is also the woman he loves. 君王有了他的冠冕。如今他需要他的宮廷——而那座宮廷的建造，是這個循環的第二場悲劇，因為它從第一塊石頭起，便建立在那些將毀滅它的事物之上。一場為政治而非為愛而締結的婚姻。一位在陰影中懷恨的姊妹。還有一位能看見自己終局到來、卻依然走向它的先知，因為那個將困住他的女子，也是他所愛的女子。

Chapter 1 · 第一章

Guinevere & the Marriage of State

桂妮薇兒與政治婚姻

A king needs an heir, and an heir needs a queen, and a queen — for a king still consolidating a fragile hold on a fractured island — needs to bring an alliance. And so Arthur, on Merlin's counsel, married Guinevere, the daughter of a powerful neighboring lord, and the marriage was a treaty before it was a love-match. As a wedding-gift, Guinevere's father gave Arthur a great oaken table — the Round Table, which had a history older than either of them — and this, too, the legend notes, was a political transaction before it was a symbol. The founding of Camelot is, from the first, a piece of statecraft.

一位君王需要繼承人，繼承人需要王后，而王后——對於一位仍在脆弱地掌控一座破碎島嶼的君王而言——需要帶來一個聯盟。於是亞瑟，聽從梅林的諫言，娶了鄰邦一位權貴之女桂妮薇兒，而這樁婚姻，在成為愛情之前，先是一紙條約。作為婚禮之禮，桂妮薇兒的父親送給亞瑟一張巨大的橡木桌——圓桌，它有著比兩人都更古老的歷史——而這個，傳統也指出，在成為象徵之前，先是一樁政治交易。卡美洛的締造，從一開始，便是一樁治術。

And here the cycle plants the seed that will, in five stages' time, bring the whole thing down. The marriage between Arthur and Guinevere is not, the legend quietly insists, a love-match. They are fond of each other; they are compatible; they rule well together. But the consuming, reckless, ruinous love — the kind that makes a man throw away a kingdom — is not between them. It is waiting, instead, for a knight who has not yet arrived at court, a young man named Lancelot, who will see the queen and fall so completely that every other loyalty in his life will bend around that single fact. The legend sets up the marriage as the empty space into which the catastrophe will rush. Camelot is built, from day one, on a fault-line.

而在這裡，這個循環種下了一顆在五個階段之後將把整個事物拉倒的種子。亞瑟與桂妮薇兒之間的婚姻，傳統平靜地堅持，不是一場愛情。他們彼此好感；他們相處融洽；他們一同治理得當。但那種吞噬的、不計後果的、毀滅性的愛——那種會使一個人丟掉一個王國的愛——並不在他們之間。它反而，在等待著一位尚未來到宮廷的騎士，一個名叫蘭斯洛特的年輕人，他將看見王后並徹底淪陷，以至於他生命中所有其他的效忠，都將繞著這單一的事實而彎折。傳統把這樁婚姻設置為那個空缺，災難將湧入其中。卡美洛從第一天起，便建在一條斷層線上。

Yet it would be wrong to read Guinevere only as the cause of the fall. She is, throughout the cycle, one of its most complex figures — a woman of fierce will and sharp intelligence, trapped in a role that allows her power only through the men around her, and judged, again and again, by a double standard the male knights never face. The later tradition — particularly the women retellers of the twentieth century — has gone back to give her the inner life the medieval sources mostly withheld: the loneliness of a political marriage, the genuine love for Lancelot that she neither chose nor could control, the agony of being the reason a great court fell. The legend is honest enough to leave her, in the end, neither villain nor victim, but a person — acting in a world that gave her no good options.

然而，僅僅把桂妮薇兒讀作傾覆的原因，是錯的。她在整個循環中，是其最複雜的人物之一——一個意志激烈、智識銳利的女子，被困在一個只允許她透過周圍的男子來獲得權力的角色裡，並且，一次又一次，被那些男性騎士從未面對的雙重標準所審判。後來的傳統——尤其是二十世紀的女性再述者——回頭給了她中世紀來源大體上所保留的內在生命：政治婚姻的孤獨、對蘭斯洛特那份她既未選擇也無法控制的真愛、以及作為一座偉大宮廷傾覆之「原因」的痛苦。這個傳統足夠誠實，以至於在最終既不把她留作反派，也不留作受害者，而是一個人——活在一個沒有給她好選項的世界裡。

Chapter 2 · 第二章

The Round Table & the Oath

圓桌與誓言

The table was round for a reason, and the reason is the whole moral invention of the Arthurian legend. A rectangular table has a head, and the man at the head is above the others; a round table has no head, and so no knight — not even the king — sits above any other. At the Round Table, Arthur was first among equals, not a tyrant over subjects. The idea was radical for its time, and it remains radical now: that the people who hold power together should be bound to one another as brothers, accountable to a shared code, none of them able to claim precedence by birth or wealth alone. Camelot's deepest gift to the world was not its swords or its quests. It was this table.

那張桌子是圓的，有其原因，而這個原因，便是亞瑟王傳奇的全部道德發明。一張長方形的桌子有首座，坐在首座之人高於其他人；一張圓桌沒有首座，於是沒有一位騎士——連君王也不例外——坐在另一位之上。在圓桌之上，亞瑟是同儕之首，而非凌駕於臣民之上的暴君。這個理念在其時代是激進的，如今仍是：那些共同握有權力之人，應當如兄弟般彼此約束，對一套共同的法則負責，沒有任何人能僅憑出身或財富而主張優先。卡美洛給世界最深的禮物，不是它的劍，也不是它的遠征。是這張桌子。

And what bound them was an oath. The legend gives us versions of it, and the heart of it is this: each knight swore to never commit murder, nor cruelty, nor treason; to give mercy to those who asked it; to defend the weak; to fight for no unjust cause; to honor women; to keep faith with his brother-knights. It was a code of conduct, yes — but more than that, it was a moral experiment. Could you gather the most violent men of a violent age, bind them by an oath, and turn their violence into justice? The Arthurian answer is: for a while. For one shining generation. And then the violence, which the oath had only contained, breaks back out, and the experiment ends in a field of blood. But for that one generation, it worked — and the memory of it working has never let the world go.

而約束他們的，是一份誓言。傳統給了我們它的版本，而其核心是：每位騎士發誓永不犯謀殺、殘酷、叛國；向求饒者施以仁慈；護衛弱者；為任何不義之事而戰；尊重女子；對他的兄弟騎士守信。那是一套行為法則，沒錯——但不止於此，它是一場道德實驗。你能否聚集一個暴力時代裡最暴力的男子，以一份誓言約束他們，將他們的暴力轉化為正義？亞瑟王的答案是：可以，一段時日。一個閃亮的世代。然後那暴力——那份誓言只是約束住、並未消除的暴力——重新迸發，而這場實驗，終結於一片血野。但在那一個世代裡，它奏效了——而它奏效的記憶，從未讓世界放手。

This is why the Round Table haunts the Western imagination. It is the dream that violence can be civilized — that the strong can be made to serve the weak, that the warriors can be turned into guardians. The dream is, the legend admits, finally impossible; the oath cannot hold, because the men who swear it are men. But the legend refuses to call the dream worthless because it failed. Camelot's achievement, brief as it was, proved the dream possible at all — and that proof, the story says, is worth more than all the centuries of failure that came after. The Round Table is the West's great image of the noble failure: the thing we keep trying, knowing we will lose, because the trying itself is the point.

這便是為什麼圓桌縈繞著西方的想像。它是那個「暴力可以被文明化」的夢——強者可以被使喚去服侍弱者，戰士可以被轉化為守護者。這個夢，傳統承認，最終是不可能的；誓言守不住，因為發誓之人是人。但這個傳統拒絕因為這個夢失敗了，便稱它一文不值。卡美洛的成就，雖然短暫，卻證明了這個夢至少是可能的——而這個證明，故事說，比其後所有失敗的世紀都更有價值。圓桌，是西方關於「高貴之失敗」的偉大意象：我們不斷嘗試的那件事，明知會輸，因為嘗試本身，便是重點。

Chapter 3 · 第三章

The Sister in the Shadows

陰影中的姊妹

Every golden court casts a shadow, and Camelot's shadow had a name: Morgan le Fay. She was Arthur's half-sister — daughter of the same Igraine who had borne Arthur, but by her first husband, the Duke of Cornwall — and the circumstances of Arthur's begetting, the deception of that night at Tintagel, were a wound in her family that never healed. Morgan grew up learning the old magic — the herb-lore, the enchantments, the arts that the new Christian court of her half-brother preferred to call witchcraft — and she grew up nursing, year by year, a hatred of the brother whose existence was founded on the ruin of her own house.

每一座黃金宮廷都投下一道陰影，而卡美洛的陰影有一個名字：摩根勒菲。她是亞瑟的同母異父之姊——同一個伊格蓮的女兒，伊格蓮生了亞瑟，卻是與她的第一任丈夫、那位康沃爾公爵所生——而亞瑟被孕育的情境，廷塔哲那夜的欺騙，是她家族中一道永不癒合的傷。摩根長大，學習古老的魔法——那些草藥之學、那些咒術、那些被她同母異父之弟的新基督教宮廷偏好稱之為巫術的技藝——而她一年一年長大，懷著對那個弟弟的恨，那個弟弟的存在，建立在她自己家族的傾覆之上。

She is the great antagonist of the cycle, and she is far more interesting than a simple villain. In the oldest sources she is a healer — the island of Avalon, where Arthur will eventually be taken to cure his mortal wound, is her island, and she is the one who tends him there. Her enmity and her healing come from the same source: she is the keeper of the old ways, the pre-Christian magic of the land, and she hates the new court that has displaced her, even as she is the only one who can save its king when the new court's own ideals have failed him. The legend cannot decide whether she is Arthur's destroyer or his ultimate savior, and that indecision is her depth. She is the part of Britain the Round Table left behind.

她是這個循環的大反派，而她遠比一個簡單的反派有趣得多。在最古老的來源中，她是一位醫者——阿瓦隆之島，亞瑟最終將被送往那裡去醫治他致命之傷的那座島，是她的島，而在那裡照料他的，便是她。她的敵意與她的醫治來自同一個源頭：她是古老之道、大地前基督教魔法的守護者，她恨那個取代了她的新宮廷，即便當那個新宮廷自身的理想辜負了它的君王之時，她是唯一能救他之人。這個傳統無法決定她究竟是亞瑟的毀滅者還是他最終的救主，而那個無法決定，正是她的深度。她是圓桌所遺落的那部分布列顛。

Her early plots set the pattern: she stole Excalibur's scabbard — the blood-staunching one — and threw it into a lake, knowing that without it Arthur would one day bleed to death. (This is one of the cycle's quietest prophecies: Arthur's final wound is made possible, hundreds of pages in advance, by his sister's hatred.) She sent a magical mantle to the court, meant to burn the wearer to ash; a serving-maid put it on and died. She trapped knights in enchantments, set false Guineveres loose, sent accusing cups and green knights. Morgan is the steady drip of acid on the foundations of Camelot — never enough to topple it alone, always enough to weaken it, year by year, until the larger blows arrive. She is the proof the legend offers that a court's enemies are not always outside its walls. Sometimes they sit at its own table.

她早期的計謀定下了模式：她偷走了王者之劍的劍鞘——那止血之鞘——把它擲入一座湖中，深知沒有了它，亞瑟終有一日會流血而死。（這是這個循環最寧靜的預言之一：亞瑟最終之傷，在數百頁之前，便因他姊妹的恨而成為可能。）她送來一件魔法斗篷到宮廷，意在把穿著者燒成灰；一個侍女穿上它而死。她將騎士困於咒術，放出假的桂妮薇兒，送來控告之杯與綠色騎士。摩根是卡美洛根基上那道不斷滴落的酸——從不足以獨自將它推倒，卻總是足以逐年削弱它，直到更大的打擊來臨。她是這個傳統所提供的證明：一座宮廷的敵人，並非總是在它的牆外。有時，他們就坐在它自己的桌前。

Chapter 4 · 第四章

The Shining Age

閃亮的時代

But for all the shadows — the loveless marriage, the vengeful sister, the fault-lines beneath the foundation — Camelot, for a time, was real, and it was glorious. The legend gives us glimpses of the court at its height, and they are among the most beloved images in all of Western literature: the great hall full of knights in their colors; the feasts that went on for days; the tournaments where the greatest warriors in Christendom met to test themselves; the constant coming and going of questing knights, riding out to right some wrong and returning with a story to tell. It was a court where a person could believe, however briefly, that the world was governed by justice, and that the strong existed to protect the weak.

但儘管有那些陰影——那無愛的婚姻、那懷恨的姊妹、那地基之下的斷層線——卡美洛，一段時日，是真實的，而且是輝煌的。傳統給了我們這座宮廷在巔峰時的瞥見，而它們是全部西方文學中最受鍾愛的意象之一：滿是身著各色服飾騎士的大殿；延續數日的盛宴；基督世界最偉大的戰士們相會以試煉自己的比武；那不斷來去的遠征騎士，策馬而出去糾正某樁不義，帶著一個可述說的故事歸來。那是一座讓人得以相信——無論多麼短暫——世界是由正義所治理、而強者之存在是為了護衛弱者的宮廷。

And the legend is honest about why it could not last, in a way that is easy to miss. Camelot's shining age depended on a balance that was inherently unstable: the greatest knights in the land, bound by an oath, gathered in one place, with no great external enemy to fight. What happens to warriors with no war? They turn, sooner or later, on one another — or they go looking for a war, and the war they find is the Grail Quest, which will scatter them and break the Table. The very peace that Camelot created was the thing that would undo it. A court of perfect justice is a court with no work left for the just; and men with no work grow restless, and restless men make trouble. The shining age was always going to be brief, because it was, by its nature, a thing that could not sustain itself.

而傳統對於它為何不能持久，是誠實的，只是那誠實很容易被錯過。卡美洛的閃亮時代，依賴著一個本質上不穩定的平衡：全國最偉大的騎士，被一份誓言所約束，聚集於一處，而沒有偉大的外敵可戰。沒有戰爭的戰士會如何？他們遲早會彼此反目——或他們會去尋找一場戰爭，而他所找到的那場戰爭，便是聖杯之尋，它將使他們散去並打碎圓桌。卡美洛所創造的那個和平本身，便是將會瓦解它的事物。一座完美正義的宮廷，是一座對義者而言已無工作可做的宮廷；而沒有工作的人變得不安，而不安的人製造麻煩。那個閃亮的時代註定短暫，因為它，就其本性而言，是一件無法自我維持之物。

This is the melancholy that underlies every description of Camelot at its height: the knowledge, which the legend never lets the reader forget, that this is a golden age being remembered, not one being lived. When Malory or Tennyson describes the shining court, they are writing in hindsight, from the ruins — and the beauty of the description is inseparable from the grief of knowing it is gone. Camelot, in the imagination, is always already lost. That is what makes it Camelot: not a place that was, but a place that we wish had been, and that we keep trying to rebuild. The shining age is the West's great image of a paradise that existed only in the wishing for it.

這便是潛伏在每一段卡美洛巔峰描述之下的憂鬱：那份傳統從不讓讀者忘記的知曉——這是一個正在被回憶的黃金時代，而非一個正在被經歷的。當馬洛禮或丁尼生描述那座閃亮的宮廷時，他們是事後、從廢墟中書寫的——而那份描述之美，與知曉它已逝的悲慟不可分割。卡美洛，在想像中，總是已經失去了。那便是使它成為卡美洛的原因：不是一個曾經存在之地，而是一個我們但願它曾經存在、而我們不斷試圖重建之地。那個閃亮的時代，是西方關於一座僅存於盼望中之樂園的偉大意象。

Chapter 5 · 第五章

Merlin's Doom

梅林的終局

Even as Camelot shone, the man who had built it was walking toward his own end — and he knew it, which is the unbearable part. Merlin, in his old age, fell in love with a young woman named Nimue (some call her Viviane), a pupil of his own magic. He taught her everything he knew — the enchantments, the prophecies, the deep arts — and the more he taught her, the more he loved her, and the more he feared losing her. And she, learning from him, grew in power until she was his equal, and then his better.

即便卡美洛閃耀，建造它的那個人，正走向他自己的終局——而他知道，這才是無法承受之處。梅林，在他的晚年，愛上了一個名叫妮妙（有人叫她薇薇安）的年輕女子，他自己的魔法弟子。他把所知的一切都教給了她——那些咒術、那些預言、那些深奧的技藝——而他教她越多，便越愛她，也越怕失去她。而她，從他那裡學習，力量增長，直到她是他的匹敵，繼而超越他。

The legend is agonizingly clear about what happened, because Merlin himself foresaw it. He told Nimue, again and again, that one day she would trap him — seal him beneath a stone, or inside a tree, or in a tower of air, depending on the version — and that he would be there for centuries, alive but unable to move, waiting for a release that would never come. And she, hearing this, was troubled, and asked him why, if he knew, he did not flee. And Merlin answered with the line that is the whole tragedy of love in a single sentence: "Because I cannot help myself." He saw the trap. He walked into it. Because the woman who set it was the woman he loved, and a man in love will choose the doom he sees over the safety he cannot feel.

傳統對於所發生之事，痛苦地清晰，因為梅林自己預見了它。他一再告訴妮妙，終有一日她會困住他——將他封在一塊石下，或一棵樹中，或一座氣之塔裡，視版本而定——而他在那裡會待上幾個世紀，活著卻無法動彈，等待一個永不到來的釋放。而她，聽了這話，感到不安，問他，既然知道，為何不逃。而梅林以一句話回答，那句話便是一整場愛之悲劇：「因為我無法自拔。」他看見了陷阱。他走了進去。因為設下它的女子，是他所愛的女子，而一個戀愛中的男人，會選擇他所看見的毀滅，勝過他所感受不到的安全。

And so it happened. Nimue, having learned his arts, used them against him — sealed him away, alive, forever, in a cave beneath a great stone, or inside a hawthorn tree, or in a crystal tower. And Camelot lost its prophet, its architect, its seer — the one being who could have warned Arthur of everything that was coming. From this moment on, the king is on his own. The fall, when it comes, will come without Merlin's voice to interpret it. The cycle has, in this single story, given us its deepest teaching about foreknowledge: to see the future is not to escape it. The seer is the most trapped of all. And love — the love that built Camelot's king in deception, the love that will ruin Camelot's knight in adoration — love is the one power prophecy cannot master, because love makes us choose, willingly, the very thing we most fear.

於是它發生了。妮妙，學會了他的技藝，便用它來對付他——把他封了起來，活著，永遠，在一塊巨石下的洞穴裡，或一棵山楂樹裡，或一座水晶塔中。而卡美洛失去了它的先知、它的建築師、它的觀察者——那唯一能警告亞瑟一切將至之物的生靈。從此刻起，君王獨自一人。那場傾覆，當它來時，將在沒有梅林之聲去解讀它的情況下到來。這個循環，在這個單一的故事裡，給了我們關於預知的最深教誨：看見未來，並非逃離它。先知是所有人中最受困的。而愛——那份以欺騙建造卡美洛之君的愛，那份將以崇拜毀掉卡美洛之騎士的愛——愛，是預言所無法掌控的唯一力量，因為愛使我們自願地選擇我們所最恐懼的那件事物。

Chapter 6 · 第六章

The Wound Within

內在的傷

Let us pause, at the end of Camelot's founding, to take the measure of what has been built — and what is already broken. The court exists. The Round Table is set. The knights are gathering. The land is at peace. And yet, beneath the shining surface, three wounds have already been inflicted, and none of them will heal. The first: the marriage of Arthur and Guinevere is a political arrangement, not a love, and into that empty space a young knight named Lancelot will arrive and fall, and the queen with him. The second: Morgan le Fay, in the shadows, has already stolen the scabbard that keeps Arthur from bleeding — so the king's eventual death is already, quietly, in motion.

讓我們在卡美洛締造之末停一下，去衡量一下所建之物——以及已經破損之物。宮廷存在。圓桌已設。騎士們正在聚集。大地太平。然而，在那閃亮的表面之下，三道傷已經造成，沒有一道會癒合。第一：亞瑟與桂妮薇兒的婚姻是一樁政治安排，不是愛，而進入那個空缺，一位名叫蘭斯洛特的年輕騎士將到來並淪陷，王后與他一同。第二：摩根勒菲，在陰影中，已經偷走了那個使亞瑟不流血的劍鞘——於是君王的最終之死，已經，悄悄地，在運作之中。

And the third wound is the one we have just witnessed: Merlin, the seer, the architect, the only person who understood the whole shape of what was being built and what would destroy it — Merlin is gone, sealed away by love, leaving Arthur to face what is coming without the one voice that could have warned him. The king who was guided by prophecy must now rule by his own judgment alone, and his own judgment, the legend will show us, is good but not enough. He will make the decisions that a good man makes, and they will be the wrong ones, because the situations he faces have no right answers. Without Merlin, Arthur is a good man in an impossible position — and that is the most dangerous thing a good man can be.

而第三道傷，是我們剛剛所見證的那個：梅林，那位先知、那位建築師、那位唯一理解正在建造之物的整個形狀以及將毀滅它之物的人——梅林去了，被愛所封，留下亞瑟獨自面對將至之物，沒有了那唯一能警告他的聲音。那位曾受預言引導的君王，如今必須只憑自己的判斷來統治，而他的判斷，傳統將向我們展示，是好的，卻不夠。他將做出一個好人所做的決定，而那些決定將是錯的，因為他所面對的處境沒有正確的答案。沒有了梅林，亞瑟是一個處於不可能之境的好人——而那，是一個好人所能成為的最危險之物。

And yet — and this is the legend's saving grace — none of this makes the founding worthless. Camelot was built on wounds, and it would die of them; but for one generation it stood, and shone, and proved that such a thing could stand at all. The wound within is not a refutation of Camelot. It is the condition of its existence. Every good thing in this story — the just king, the round table, the oath — is built by flawed people in a flawed world, and the legend's greatness is that it never pretends otherwise. The shining age is real. The cracks are real. Both are true at once. And the next four stages of our journey will watch the cracks, slowly and inevitably, win.

然而——而這是這個傳統的救贖之恩——這一切都不使締造變得一文不值。卡美盛建立在傷口之上，而它將死於這些傷口；但在一個世代裡，它矗立著，閃耀著，證明了這樣一件事物至少能夠矗立。那道內在的傷，不是對卡美洛的反駁。它是它存在的條件。這個故事裡的每一件善事——那位公正的君王、那張圓桌、那份誓言——都是由有缺陷之人在一個有缺陷的世界裡所建造的，而這個傳統的偉大之處，在於它從不假裝不然。那個閃亮的時代是真實的。那些裂痕是真實的。兩者同時為真。而我們旅程的接下來四個階段，將看著那些裂痕，緩慢而無可避免地，獲勝。

BUILT ON WOUNDS

建於傷口之上

The table was round. The oath was sworn. The hall was full. And beneath the shining floor, three wounds were already bleeding: a marriage without love, a sister with a stolen scabbard, a prophet sealed away by the very love he could not refuse. Camelot stood, and would stand, for one generation — and the memory of its standing is the gift the legend gives to every age that comes after, which cannot rebuild it, and cannot stop trying.
