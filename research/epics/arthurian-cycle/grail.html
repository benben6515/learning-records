THE QUEST FOR THE GRAIL

聖　杯　之　尋

PERCEVAL · GALAHAD · THE FISHER KING · THE WOUNDED LAND

A covered cup appeared at the Round Table, radiant, and every knight swore to quest for it across the world. But the Grail could not be won by prowess — only by purity — and the quest that scattered the brotherhood in search of the holiest object in Christendom was also the quest that broke the Table forever. For when perfection is glimpsed, the mortal world cannot hold it; and the knights who saw the Grail came back, if they came back at all, as different men, and Camelot never sat whole again. 一隻覆著布的杯出現在圓桌之上，光輝燦然，每位騎士都發誓走遍世界去尋找它。但聖杯不能以武勇贏得——只能以純潔——而那場將兄弟會散布到基督世界各處、去尋找最神聖之物的遠征，也是那場永遠打碎了圓桌的遠征。因為完美一旦得見，凡人的世界便容不下它；而那些看見了聖杯的騎士，如果還回得來，回來時已是不同的人，而卡美洛再也未能完整地坐在一起。

Chapter 1 · 第一章

The Vision at Camelot

卡美洛的異象

It happened at the feast of Pentecost, while the whole court sat at table. Without warning, a great light filled the hall — a light brighter than any earthly fire, silent, sourceless — and through the light there passed, slowly, a covered object: a cup, hidden beneath a cloth of pure white, radiant with a light of its own. A smell of sweetness filled the room. And as the object passed each knight, the legend says, every man found his plate filled with the food he most desired, and yet no hand had touched a dish. And then the light withdrew, and the cup was gone, and the hall was ordinary again — and every man at the table felt, for the rest of his life, the absence of what he had just glimpsed.

它發生在五旬節的盛宴之上，那時整個宮廷都坐在桌前。毫無預警地，一道大光充滿了大殿——一道比任何凡間之火都亮的光，無聲，無源——而穿過那光，緩緩經過的，是一件覆著布的物件：一隻杯，藏在一塊純白的布下，自帶光芒。一股甜香充滿了房間。而當那物件經過每一位騎士之時，傳統說，每個人都發現他的盤中盛滿了他最渴望的食物，然而沒有任何一隻手碰過任何一道菜。然後那光退去，那杯不見了，大殿又恢復了平凡——而桌上的每一個人，在餘生之中，都感覺到他剛剛所瞥見之物的缺席。

And Gawain, in the stunned silence that followed, stood up and spoke — rashly, as was his way — and made the vow that would scatter the Round Table: "I swear," he said, "I will not sit at this table again until I have seen the Grail uncovered." And one by one, every knight in the hall rose and swore the same vow. Arthur, the legend tells us, was stricken. He knew — he had always known, perhaps — that the Grail Quest would empty his court. The adventures that had kept the knights sharp, and the kingdom safe, would be replaced by a single transcendent pursuit, and the brotherhood would ride out and not all of them would come back. He wept, the king did. He wept because he understood, in that moment, that the perfection the knights were chasing could only be reached by leaving the mortal court behind — and that the court, once left, could never be the same.

而高文，在隨後那驚愕的沉默之中，站起來，開口——一如他的作風，魯莽地——並立下了那將使圓桌散去的誓言：「我發誓，」他說，「在我親眼看見聖杯揭開之前，我不再坐回這張桌子。」而一個接一個，大殿裡的每一位騎士都站起來，立下了同樣的誓。傳統告訴我們，亞瑟，心如刀割。他知道——也許他一直都知道——聖杯之尋將掏空他的宮廷。那些使騎士們保持敏銳、使王國保持安全的冒險，將被一場單一而超然的追求所取代，而這個兄弟會將策馬而出，而他們之中不是所有人都會回來。他哭了，那位君王。他哭，因為他在那一刻明白，騎士們所追求的完美，只能透過拋下凡人的宮廷才能達到——而那個宮廷，一旦被拋下，便永遠不再相同。

This is the central paradox of the Grail Quest, and the legend states it without flinching. The knights swear a holy vow — and the holy vow destroys the brotherhood that swears it. The pursuit of the highest good empties the very court that made the pursuit possible. Perfection, the story tells us, is not compatible with the mortal world; the moment a society catches a true glimpse of it, the society begins to dissolve, because its members can no longer be content with what they had. The Grail appears at Camelot not as a reward but as a summons away from it. And the knights, being what they are, answer the summons — and ride out, most of them, to their deaths.

這便是聖杯之尋的核心悖論，而這個傳統毫不退縮地陳述了它。騎士們發下神聖的誓——而那神聖的誓，毀掉了發下它的兄弟會。對最高之善的追求，掏空了那使追求成為可能的宮廷本身。完美，故事告訴我們，與凡人的世界並不相容；當一個社會真正瞥見它的那一刻，那個社會便開始消解，因為它的成員再也無法滿足於他們曾擁有之物。聖杯出現在卡美洛，不是作為獎賞，而是作為一個遠離它的召喚。而那些騎士，因其本性，回應了那個召喚——而策馬而出，他們大多數人，走向了死亡。

Chapter 2 · 第二章

What the Grail Is

聖杯是什麼

What was the Grail? The answer shifted across the centuries of the legend's telling, and the shifting is part of the meaning. In the oldest Celtic sources, the Grail was simply a cauldron — one of the magical plenty-giving vessels of Welsh myth, a bowl that fed whoever owned it and brought the dead back to life. In these versions, the Grail is a symbol of the land's fertility: when the king is whole, the Grail overflows; when the king is wounded, the Grail stops, and the land becomes a waste. This is the deep pagan stratum beneath the Christian one — the Grail as the life of the earth itself, bound to the health of its king.

聖杯是什麼？這個答案在這個傳說被講述的數個世紀裡有所變動，而那變動，正是意義的一部分。在最古老的塞爾特來源中，聖杯不過是一個鍋——威爾斯神話中那些施法的、給予豐饒的容器之一，一個餵養其主、並使死者復生的碗。在這些版本中，聖杯是大地豐饒的象徵：當君王是完整的，聖杯溢流；當君王受了傷，聖杯停止，而大地變成一片荒原。這便是基督教地層之下的那個深層異教地層——聖杯即大地本身的生命，與其君王的健康相繫。

Then, in the 12th century, a writer named Robert de Boron Christianized it, and the Grail became the cup of the Last Supper — the very vessel from which Christ and his disciples drank on the night before the Crucifixion — and afterward, the cup in which Joseph of Arimathea caught the blood that flowed from Christ's side on the cross. In this version the Grail is holiness itself, a physical relic of God's sacrifice, carrying the power to heal and to feed and to make pure. And the quest for it became a pilgrimage, a search not for adventure but for grace — something no sword could win and no prowess could reach.

然後，在十二世紀，一位名叫羅貝爾·德·博隆的作者將它基督教化了，於是聖杯成了最後晚餐之杯——基督與其門徒在受難前夜所飲的那個器皿——以及後來，亞利馬太的約瑟用以承接基督在十字架上肋旁所流之血的杯。在這個版本中，聖杯即是聖潔本身，是上帝犧牲的一件物質遺物，帶著醫治、餵養、使人純潔之力。而對它的尋求，成了一場朝聖，一場不是為了冒險、而是為了恩典的追尋——那是任何劍都贏不來、任何武勇都達不到的東西。

The legend holds both meanings at once, and that is its genius. The Grail is the life of the land, and the Grail is the holiness of God; the wound that stops it is the wound of the king, and the wound is also the sin of the world. To heal the Grail-king is to heal the land, and to heal the land is to find grace, and to find grace is to be pure enough to look upon the holy. The quest, therefore, cannot be won by the greatest knight — for the greatest knight, Lancelot, is an adulterer, and the Grail will not show itself to a sinner. It can only be won by someone whose purity is complete. And the legend will spend the next chapters asking whether such a person can exist at all, and what it costs the world when one does.

這個傳統同時持有這兩個意義，而這正是它的天才。聖杯是大地的生命，聖杯也是上帝的聖潔；使它停止的那道傷是君王之傷，而那道傷也是世界之罪。醫治聖杯之王，便是醫治大地；醫治大地，便是尋得恩典；尋得恩典，便是純潔到足以仰望神聖。因此，這場遠征不能由最偉大的騎士贏得——因為最偉大的騎士，蘭斯洛特，是個通姦者，而聖杯不會向一個罪人顯現。它只能由一個純潔完整之人所贏得。而這個傳統將在接下來的章節裡探問：這樣的一個人是否真的可能存在，而當這樣一個人存在之時，世界要付出什麼代價。

Chapter 3 · 第三章

Sir Perceval & the Fisher King

珀西瓦爵士與漁夫王

The oldest and strangest version of the Grail story belongs to a knight named Perceval — and Perceval is, in his first appearance, an innocent fool. He was raised by his widowed mother alone in a forest, knowing nothing of knighthood or courtesy or the world; he could barely speak properly, and he was so strong and so naive that he was a danger to everyone he met. One day he saw a party of knights riding through his forest and, thinking them angels or gods, fell down and worshipped them — and from that moment was seized with the desire to be a knight himself. His mother, knowing what knighthood would do to her last son, died of grief as he rode away.

聖杯故事最古老也最奇異的版本，屬於一位名叫珀西瓦的騎士——而珀西瓦，在他初登場之時，是個天真的傻子。他由守寡的母親獨自在一片森林裡撫養長大，對騎士精神、禮儀或世界一無所知；他連話都說不周全，而他如此強壯、如此天真，以至於他對所遇的每個人都是一種危險。一日他看見一隊騎士策馬穿過他的森林，以為他們是天使或神，便俯身崇拜——而從那一刻起，他被自己也要成為騎士的渴望所攫住。他的母親，知曉騎士精神會對她最後的兒子做什麼，在他策馬離去之時，悲慟而死。

And Perceval, wandering, eventually came to a strange castle in a waste land, where a wounded king sat fishing — the Fisher King — and was taken in for the night. And in that castle, in a great hall, he saw a procession: a lance that dripped blood, candelabra, and finally a radiant Grail, covered in white cloth, passing before the wounded king. And Perceval, who had been taught (badly) by his earlier mentors that a knight should not ask too many questions, stayed silent. He did not ask whom the Grail served. He did not ask why the king was wounded. He did not ask the question that would have healed both king and land. He stayed quiet, out of a misguided courtesy — and in the morning, the castle was empty, and he was alone in the waste again.

而珀西瓦，流浪著，終於來到一片荒地上的一座奇異城堡，那裡坐著一位受傷的、正在釣魚的君王——漁夫王——他被收留過夜。而在那座城堡裡，在一座大殿中，他看見一列隊伍：一把滴血的長矛、燭台，最後是一隻光輝的聖杯，覆著白布，從受傷的君王面前經過。而珀西瓦，先前曾（被粗略地）教導一個騎士不該問太多問題，便保持沉默。他沒有問聖杯是為誰而服事的。他沒有問君王為何受傷。他沒有問那個本會醫治君王與大地之問題。他保持安靜，出於一種被誤導的禮貌——而到了早晨，城堡空了，他又獨自一人身處荒原。

And the legend tells us, in one of its most haunting moments, what Perceval did not yet know: that a single question — "Whom does the Grail serve?" — would have healed the Fisher King's wound, ended his suffering, and made the waste land bloom again. The land itself was waiting on a word from this naive boy's mouth, and he did not speak it. Years later, broken by the knowledge of what his silence had cost, Perceval would return, older and wiser, and ask the question at last — and the king would be healed, and the land would flower. But the legend lingers on the long years of his failure, because the failure is the point: the Grail is not won by strength. It is won by compassion — by the simple, courageous act of asking about another's suffering. The innocent fool failed because he was too concerned with being a proper knight to be a proper human. The land paid for his manners.

而這個傳統，在其最縈繞人心的一刻之一，告訴了我們珀西瓦當時尚不知道的事：一個問題——「聖杯是為誰而服事的？」——本會醫治漁夫王之傷、終結他的苦、使荒原重新開花。大地本身，正等待這個天真的男孩口中說出的一個字，而他沒有說。多年之後，因知曉自己的沉默所造成的代價而心碎，珀西瓦會歸來，年長而睿智，終於問出那個問題——而君王會被醫治，而大地會開花。但這個傳統駐足於他失敗的漫長歲月，因為那失敗才是重點：聖杯不是以力量贏得的。它是以悲憫贏得的——以那個簡單而勇敢的、詢問他人苦難之舉。那個天真的傻子失敗了，因為他太過在意做一個得體的騎士，而忽略了做一個得體的人。大地為他的禮儀付出了代價。

Chapter 4 · 第四章

Sir Galahad & the Siege Perilous

加拉哈德爵士與危座

The later tradition — and the version Malory would make canonical — answered the question "can any knight achieve the Grail?" with a single, astonishing figure: Sir Galahad. And Galahad's birth is itself a kind of miracle and a kind of sorrow. He was the son of Lancelot — the greatest and most sinful knight — by a woman named Elaine, who had tricked Lancelot into her bed by disguising herself as Guinevere. So Galahad was conceived in deception, the fruit of his father's weakness; and yet, the legend insists, he himself was born without stain. He was, in the language of the theology, the one sinless knight — and the legend draws the parallel, deliberately and uncomfortably, with Christ himself.

後來的傳統——以及馬洛禮將使之成為正典的版本——以一個單一而驚人的形象，回答了「有哪位騎士能成就聖杯嗎？」這個問題：加拉哈德爵士。而加拉哈德的誕生本身，便是一種奇蹟，也是一種悲傷。他是蘭斯洛特——那位最偉大也最有罪的騎士——與一位名叫伊萊恩的女子之子，那位女子喬裝成桂妮薇兒，騙蘭斯洛特上了她的床。所以加拉哈德孕育於欺騙之中，是他父親軟弱之果；然而，這個傳統堅持，他本人卻生而無瑕。用神學的語言來說，他是那唯一無罪之騎士——而這個傳統，刻意而令人不安地，將他與基督本身並列。

At the Round Table there was one seat — the Siege Perilous — that had always stood empty, with an inscription: only the knight who was wholly pure could sit in it, and anyone else who dared would be swallowed up by the earth. And at the feast of Pentecost, just before the Grail vision, an old man led a young man into the hall, and the young man walked straight to the Siege Perilous and sat down in it — and the earth did not swallow him. The letters on the seat rearranged themselves to spell his name: Galahad. The court knew, then, that this was the knight who would achieve the Grail — the one for whom the quest had been waiting. And Lancelot, looking on his own son, felt both a father's pride and a knife of shame, because he knew, as everyone knew, that the son's purity was a standing judgment on the father's sin.

在圓桌之上，有一個座位——危座——一直空著，上有銘文：唯有完全純潔的騎士能坐於其上，其他任何膽敢之人，都將被大地吞噬。而在五旬節的盛宴之上，就在聖杯異象之前，一個老人領著一個年輕人進入大殿，那年輕人徑直走向危座，坐了下去——而大地沒有吞噬他。座位上的字母重新排列，拼出他的名字：加拉哈德。宮廷那時便知曉，這便是將成就聖杯的騎士——那個遠征一直在等待之人。而蘭斯洛特，看著自己的兒子，既感到一個父親的驕傲，也感到一刀羞恥，因為他知道，一如每個人所知，那兒子的純潔，是對父親之罪的一個持續的審判。

And this is the legend's cruelest comfort. Perfection can exist, it says — but only as a stranger to the world that produced it. Galahad does not really belong to Camelot. He passes through it like a light through a window, achieves the one thing no other knight can achieve, and then leaves — because purity, having glimpsed its goal, cannot stay in a world of sin. The achievement of the Grail is, the legend knows, also the end of the Grail's presence among mortals. Galahad will see it, and be taken up, and the Grail will go with him, and Camelot will be left, forever after, with only the memory of what it once glimpsed. The sinless knight is the legend's proof that holiness is real — and its warning that holiness cannot live among us for long.

而這便是這個傳統最殘酷的慰藉。完美是能夠存在的，它說——但只能作為一個它所從出之世界的陌生人。加拉哈德並不真正屬於卡美洛。他穿過它，如光穿過窗戶，成就了沒有其他騎士能成就的那件事，然後離去——因為純潔，既已瞥見其目標，便無法留在一個罪的世界裡。聖杯之成就，這個傳統知道，也是聖杯臨在於凡人之間的終結。加拉哈德將看見它，並被接去，而聖杯將隨他而去，而卡美洛將從此只留下它曾瞥見之物的記憶。那位無罪之騎士，是這個傳統關於「聖潔是真實的」之證明——也是它關於「聖潔無法長久活在我們之中」之警示。

Chapter 5 · 第五章

The Tests of the Spirit

心靈之試煉

The Grail Quest was not a single adventure but a thousand, scattered across the world, and each knight who rode out met the tests suited to his soul. Sir Bors — Galahad's companion, and a man of ordinary virtue rather than miraculous purity — was tested in ways the legend recounts with a theologian's precision. He was offered, in one castle, a choice: rescue his brother from captivity, or rescue a maiden from rape. Both cried out to him at once. He chose the maiden — and arrived to find his brother killed, killed cursing Bors for the choice. And yet the Church, the legend tells us, judged Bors right: the greater harm was to be prevented, and he had chosen as a Christian must, even at the cost of his brother's love.

聖杯之尋不是一場單一的冒險，而是一千場，散布於世界之上，而每一位策馬而出的騎士，都遇見了適合他靈魂的試煉。鮑斯爵士——加拉哈德的同伴，一個有著平凡美德、而非奇蹟般純潔的人——以一個神學家的精準被這個傳統所記述的方式受了試煉。在一座城堡裡，他被給予一個選擇：救他的兄弟脫離囚禁，或救一位少女脫離強暴。兩者同時向他呼救。他選擇了那位少女——而趕到時發現他的兄弟被殺了，被殺時還在詛咒鮑斯的選擇。然而教會，這個傳統告訴我們，判鮑斯是對的：更大的惡必須被阻止，而他做了一個基督徒必須做的選擇，即便以他兄弟之愛為代價。

Lancelot, the greatest knight, was tested and found wanting — and the legend gives us the scene with devastating honesty. Lancelot came to a chapel where the Grail was being celebrated, and tried to enter; an invisible hand struck him down, and he lay outside, paralyzed, for twenty-four days, while within, his own son Galahad received the vision his father could not. Lancelot had been the greatest knight of the mortal world; in the presence of the Grail, his adultery made him unfit even to cross the threshold. The legend does not condemn him — it grieves him. The best knight who ever lived, brought low by the one sin that, in any other context, the legend has spent chapters refusing to condemn. Here, in the presence of the holy, that same love is the wall between him and grace. The Grail is unforgiving in a way the Round Table never was.

蘭斯洛特，最偉大的騎士，受了試煉，卻被發現有所不足——而這個傳統以毀滅性的誠實給了我們這個場面。蘭斯洛特來到一座正在舉行聖杯儀式的禮拜堂，想進去；一隻看不見的手把他擊倒，他躺在外面，癱瘓了二十四天，而在裡面，他自己的兒子加拉哈德領受了他父親無法領受的異象。蘭斯洛特曾是凡人世界最偉大的騎士；在聖杯的臨在之中，他的通姦使他連跨過門檻都不配。這個傳統不譴責他——它為他悲慟。世上最好的騎士，因那一樁罪而卑微——那樁罪，在任何其他脈絡中，這個傳統花了數章篇幅拒絕譴責。而在這裡，在神聖的臨在之中，那份同樣的愛，是他與恩典之間的牆。聖杯之不寬容，是圓桌從未有的。

And this is the deep theological turn of the Grail Quest, and the reason it is the most serious of the cycle's stages. Up until now, the legend has been about chivalry — about a mortal code, judged by mortal standards, where even adultery can be a kind of romance. The Grail Quest introduces a standard beyond the mortal: a holiness that judges every knight, that finds even the best of them wanting, and that can be satisfied by only one. The Round Table could hold Lancelot's love and Gawain's feud and Tristan's potion. The Grail cannot. The Quest, by introducing the absolute, makes every relative virtue of Camelot look like what it is — a beautiful compromise with sin, acceptable to men, unacceptable to God.

而這便是聖杯之尋深層的神學轉向，也是它是這個循環中最嚴肅之一階段的原因。直到此刻，這個傳統一直是關於騎士精神——關於一套凡人的法則，以凡人的標準來審判，在那裡，連通姦都可以是一種浪漫。聖杯之尋引入了一個超越凡人的標準：一種審判每一位騎士的聖潔，它發現連他們中最好的也有所不足，而只能被一人所滿足。圓桌能容納蘭斯洛特的愛、高文的宿仇、崔斯坦的靈藥。聖杯不能。這場遠征，藉由引入那絕對者，使卡美洛每一種相對的美德，看起來都像它本來所是的那樣——一種與罪的美麗妥協，為人所接受，卻不為上帝所接受。

Chapter 6 · 第六章

The Achievement of the Grail

聖杯之成就

At last, Galahad — with Bors and Perceval at his side, the three purest knights — came to the castle of Carbonek, where the wounded Fisher King had waited, for generations, for someone worthy to ask the question and receive the vision. And there, the legend tells us, the Grail showed itself, uncovered, to Galahad alone; and what he saw, he could not, or would not, fully describe — only that it was the fulfillment of every longing he had ever felt, and that to see it was to know, at last, why he had been born.

終於，加拉哈德——鮑斯與珀西瓦隨侍在側，這三位最純潔的騎士——來到了卡伯內克之堡，那裡，受傷的漁夫王已等待了數代，等待一個配得上問那個問題、領受那個異象之人。而在那裡，傳統告訴我們，聖杯向加拉哈德一人，揭開了它的布，顯現了自己；而他所見的，他不能、或不願，完全描述——只說那是他一切所曾感受之渴望的滿足，而看見它，便是終於知道他為何而生。

And the Grail healed the Fisher King, as it had always been destined to. The wound that had wasted the land for generations was closed by a single word from Galahad's lips. The waste land flowered. And then — and here the legend makes its most theologically daring move — Galahad, having achieved the Grail, did not return to Camelot with it. He prayed, instead, to be released from the world. And as he prayed, a hand came down from heaven and took the Grail from the earth, and took Galahad with it — and he was seen no more. The sinless knight, having glimpsed the perfect, could not stay in the imperfect world. He was translated. The Grail went with him.

而聖杯醫治了漁夫王，一如它始終注定要做的。那道浪費了大地數代之傷，被加拉哈德唇間的一個字所閉合。荒原開花了。然後——而在這裡這個傳統做出了它在神學上最大膽的一步——加拉哈德，既已成就聖杯，並未帶著它回到卡美洛。他反而祈求，從這個世界被釋放。而當他祈禱之時，一隻手從天而降，將聖杯從地上取去，將加拉哈德一同取去——而他再也不被看見。那位無罪之騎士，既已瞥見那完美者，便無法留在那不完美的世界裡。他被接去了。聖杯隨他而去。

And here is the legend's deepest theological claim, and its saddest: that the achievement of the highest good is also its removal from us. The Grail came to Camelot once, briefly, and then left forever, because the world could not hold it. Perfection, the story tells us, is not something we can keep. It is something that passes through our lives, briefly, like a light through a window, and the best we can do is glimpse it and remember. Galahad does not save Camelot by bringing the Grail back. He empties Camelot by taking the Grail away. The holiest achievement in the cycle is also the moment the court is condemned — because once you have glimpsed perfection and lost it, the beautiful compromises of mortal life are never enough again.

而這便是這個傳統最深的神學主張，也是它最悲傷的：最高之善的成就，也是它從我們之中被移除。聖杯曾來到卡美洛一次，短暫地，然後永遠離去，因為這個世界容不下它。完美，故事告訴我們，不是一件我們能保有之物。它是一件穿過我們生命之物，短暫地，如光穿過窗戶，而我們所能做的最好的事，是瞥見它並記住它。加拉哈德並未藉由把聖杯帶回來而拯救卡美洛。他藉由把聖杯帶走而掏空了卡美洛。這個循環中最神聖的成就，也是這個宮廷被定罪的那一刻——因為一旦你瞥見了完美又失去了它，凡人生命那些美麗的妥協，便再也不足夠了。

Chapter 7 · 第七章

The Cost of the Quest

遠征的代價

The Grail had been achieved, and Galahad was gone, and the knights who had ridden out so full of hope came drifting back to Camelot — those who survived. Many had not. The quest had killed them: not by combat, mostly, but by the simple attrition of years spent wandering, by enchantments, by despair. The brotherhood that had ridden out was a fraction of the brotherhood that returned. And those who returned were changed. They had glimpsed something that made the tournaments and the feasts and the adventures of the old court feel hollow. They sat at the Round Table again, but they sat differently — quieter, sadder, looking past one another at something the others could not see.

聖杯已被成就，加拉哈德已去，而那些滿懷希望策馬而出的騎士們，飄零地回到了卡美洛——那些活下來的。許多人沒有。那場遠征殺了他們：大多不是死於戰鬥，而是死於多年流浪的單純耗損，死於咒術，死於絕望。策馬而出的那個兄弟會，只是歸來的那個兄弟會的一小部分。而那些歸來的，變了。他們瞥見了某種東西，使舊宮廷的比武、盛宴、冒險，都顯得空洞。他們又坐在圓桌旁，但他們坐得不同了——更安靜、更悲傷，望過彼此，望向某種別人看不見的東西。

Arthur saw it, and grieved. The court he had built — the court of laughter and adventure and the constant testing of the code — had been emptied and aged by a single transcendent pursuit. The Grail Quest, which the knights had undertaken in holiness, had hollowed out the very thing it was supposed to ennoble. The Round Table still stood, but its heart had gone with Galahad. And into the space that the Grail had opened — the space of disillusionment, of knights who had seen perfection and could no longer be content with the mortal — flowed, quickly, all the old poisons that the adventures had kept at bay: the love of Lancelot and Guinevere, now harder to hide; the feuds of the Orkney clan, now sharper; the malice of those who wanted the secret exposed.

亞瑟看見了，悲慟。他所建造的宮廷——那個笑聲、冒險、不斷試煉法則的宮廷——被一場單一而超然的追求掏空、變老了。聖杯之尋，那場騎士們以聖潔之心所從事的遠征，挖空了它本應使之高貴的那個事物本身。圓桌仍矗立著，但它的心已隨加拉哈德而去。而流入聖杯所打開的那個空間——那個幻滅的空間、那個看見了完美而無法再滿足於凡人的騎士們的空間——的，很快地，是那些冒險曾擋在門外的一切舊毒：蘭斯洛特與桂妮薇兒的愛，如今更難隱藏；奧克尼氏族的宿仇，如今更為銳利；那些想要秘密被揭發之人的惡意。

This is the legend's most piercing irony, and it is not ironic but tragic. The Grail Quest was the holiest thing the Round Table ever did — and it was the thing that destroyed the Round Table. The pursuit of perfection, in a mortal institution, is corrosive; the glimpse of the absolute makes the relative intolerable; and a brotherhood of warriors, once it has been shown that there is something higher than prowess, can never again be content with prowess alone. Camelot did not fall because its knights were too wicked. It fell because they became, briefly, too good — and the goodness ruined them for the compromised world they had to keep living in. The next stage will watch the poisons, now unchecked, bring the whole shining structure down.

這便是這個傳統最刺骨的反諷，而它不是反諷，而是悲劇。聖杯之尋是圓桌所做過最神聖之事——而它正是毀了圓桌之物。在一個凡人的機構裡，對完美的追求是腐蝕性的；對絕對者的瞥見，使相對者變得無法忍受；而一個戰士的兄弟會，一旦被指出有比武勇更高的東西，便再也無法滿足於武勇本身。卡美洛之所以傾覆，不是因為它的騎士太邪惡。它之所以傾覆，是因為他們短暫地變得太好——而那份好，毀了他們去面對他們必須繼續生活其中的那個妥協之世界。下一階段將看著那些如今不再受阻的毒，將那整座閃亮的結構拉倒。

THE HOLIEST ACT, AND THE RUIN

最神聖之舉，與那場毀滅

A covered cup passed through a feast, and the knights swore to seek it — and the seeking scattered them, and killed half of them, and changed the rest beyond repair. The sinless son of a sinful father achieved it, healed a wounded king, and then was taken, with the cup, out of the world. Camelot, left behind, could not recover. The Grail had shown it perfection; and a court that has glimpsed perfection can never again be at peace with its compromises. The brotherhood sat at the round table once more, looking past each other. The fall was already begun.
