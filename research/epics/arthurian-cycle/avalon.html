AVALON & THE ONCE AND FUTURE KING

阿　瓦　隆　與　未　來　之　王

CAMLANN · EXCALIBUR RETURNED · MALORY · TENNYSON · T.H. WHITE

On a misted field in the west, a father and the son he had tried to kill destroyed each other, and the last of the Round Table died with them. A dying king ordered his sword thrown back into the lake, and a hand rose to take it. He was borne away to an island in the western sea, to heal of a wound that would not close. And then — six hundred years of storytellers refused to let the story end there. Arthur sleeps. Arthur waits. Arthur will return. This is the dream that the West cannot quite give up: that somewhere, the just king is only sleeping, and the hour of his return is not yet past. 在西方一片迷霧的戰場上，一位父親與他那他曾試圖殺死的兒子彼此毀滅，而圓桌的最後一批人也隨之而死。一位垂死的君王下令將他的劍擲回湖中，而一隻手升起接住了它。他被送往西海一座島嶼，去醫治一道不會閉合的傷。然後——六百年的說故事者拒絕讓故事在那裡結束。亞瑟沉睡。亞瑟等待。亞瑟將歸來。這是西方無法完全放棄的夢：某處，那位公正的君王只是睡著了，而他歸來的時辰，尚未過去。

Chapter 1 · 第一章

The Battle of Camlann

卡姆蘭之戰

The battle that the adder had begun was, the legend tells us, the worst thing that ever happened in Britain. Father fought son. Brother fought brother. The last survivors of the Round Table — the men who had sat together at the same board, sworn the same oath, ridden out on the same quests — now killed one another in the mud of a western field. By the end of the day, a hundred thousand men lay dead, the legend says (the numbers are legendary, the grief is real), and of all the great knights of the Table, only two were left standing on Arthur's side: Sir Bedivere, and Sir Lucan. And on Mordred's side, only Mordred himself.

那場由小蛇所開始的戰役，這個傳統告訴我們，是布列顛有史以來最糟的事。父戰子。兄戰弟。圓桌的最後倖存者——那些曾同坐一桌、共發一誓、同赴一征之人——如今在西方一片泥濘的戰場上彼此殺戮。傳統說，到了那日盡頭，十萬人橫屍（數字是傳奇性的，悲慟是真實的），而在圓桌所有大騎士之中，亞瑟這邊只剩下兩個人還站著：貝迪維爾爵士，與盧坎爵士。而在莫德雷德那邊，只剩下莫德雷德一人。

And the legend gives us the final exchange with terrible simplicity. Arthur saw Mordred, his son, across the field — and the king, knowing this was the moment, drove his spear through Mordred's body, pinning him to the earth. And Mordred, feeling the spear go through him, knowing he was dead, used his last strength to push himself up the shaft of the spear — closer, closer to his father — and drove his own sword into Arthur's skull. Father killed son; son killed father; and both fell together, in the same instant, on the field that had been the kingdom's whole hope and was now its grave.

而這個傳統以可怖的簡潔給了我們最後的交鋒。亞瑟在戰場彼端看見了他的兒子莫德雷德——而那位君王，知道這便是那個時刻，將他的矛刺穿了莫德雷德的身軀，將他釘在大地之上。而莫德雷德，感覺到那支矛穿過他，知道自己將死，用他最後的力量順著矛桿向上推——更近，更近他的父親——然後將他自己的劍刺入了亞瑟的頭骨。父殺子；子殺父；而兩者在同一瞬一同倒下，倒在那片曾是王國全部希望、如今卻是其墳墓的戰場上。

This is the legend's grimmest scene, and it is also its most theological. The king who was foretold to die by his son's hand dies by his son's hand. The prophecy that Merlin made, at the very beginning, is fulfilled — and nothing anyone did, not Arthur's attempted infanticide, not his mercy in taking Mordred in, not his war in France, nothing — could avert it. And the death is mutual: Arthur cannot kill Mordred without being killed himself. The just king and the unjust son destroy each other. The Round Table ends not in victory but in exhaustion — in the simple, terrible fact that the best king the world had seen, and the worst son, could only end together, and neither could survive the other. The battle of Camlann is the legend's final statement about the cost of a golden age: it always ends in a field of the dead, and the king who made it cannot outlive it.

這是這個傳統最陰沉的場面，也是它最具神學性的一幕。那位被預言將死於兒子之手的君王，死於兒子之手。梅林在最初所做的那個預言，被應驗了——而任何人所做的任何事，無論是亞瑟的未遂殺嬰、他收留莫德雷德的仁慈、他在法蘭西的戰爭，無論什麼——都無法迴避它。而那場死亡是相互的：亞瑟無法在不被殺的情況下殺死莫德雷德。那位公正的君王與那位不義的兒子彼此毀滅。圓桌不是結束於勝利，而是結束於耗竭——結束於那個簡單而可怖的事實：世上所見最好的君王，與最糟的兒子，只能一同結束，誰也無法在對方之後存活。卡姆蘭之戰，是這個傳統關於一個黃金時代之代價的最終陳述：它總是結束於一片死者之野，而造就它的那位君王，無法比它活得更久。

Chapter 2 · 第二章

Excalibur Returned to the Lake

王者之劍歸於湖

Arthur lay dying, the wound in his skull mortal, and he turned to his last surviving knight — Sir Bedivere (in some versions it is Griflet; the names vary, the scene does not) — and gave him a final command. "Take my sword Excalibur," the king said, "and throw it into the lake. Do not keep it. Do not bring it back. Throw it in, and tell me what you see." And Bedivere, heavy with grief, took the sword and went.

亞瑟躺著將死，他頭骨上的傷是致命的，他轉向他最後倖存的騎士——貝迪維爾爵士（有些版本是格里夫萊；名字各異，場面不變）——下了一道最後的命令。「拿我的劍王者之劍，」那位君王說，「把它扔進湖裡。不要留著它。不要帶回來。扔進去，然後告訴我看見了什麼。」而貝迪維爾，悲慟沉重，接過那柄劍，走了。

And Bedivere, the legend tells us, could not bring himself to do it. The sword was too beautiful, too precious — the hilt set with jewels, the blade the finest ever forged, the very sword the Lady of the Lake had given Arthur at the beginning of his reign. To throw such a thing into the water seemed a desecration. So Bedivere hid the sword and returned to the king and lied: "I threw it in. Nothing happened. I saw only the water." And Arthur looked at him and said, "You are lying. The sword is the most precious thing in the world. Go back, and throw it in, and tell me the truth." And Bedivere went again — and hid the sword again, and lied again. And Arthur knew, both times, and sent him back a third.

而貝迪維爾，這個傳統告訴我們，無法狠心這麼做。那柄劍太美、太珍貴——劍柄鑲滿寶石，劍身是有史以來鍛造得最好的，正是湖中妖女在亞瑟治世之初所賜之劍。把這樣一件東西扔進水裡，似乎是褻瀆。於是貝迪維爾藏起了劍，回到君王身邊，撒了謊：「我扔了。什麼也沒發生。我只看見水。」而亞瑟看著他，說：「你在撒謊。那柄劍是世上最珍貴之物。回去，把它扔進去，告訴我真相。」而貝迪維爾又去了——又藏起了劍，又撒了謊。而亞瑟兩次都知道，第三次又把他送了回去。

And the third time Bedivere did as he was told. He hurled Excalibur out over the dark water — and the legend gives us one of its most haunting images: an arm rose up out of the lake, clothed in white samite, caught the sword by the hilt, brandished it three times, and drew it down under the water. The sword had gone back to where it came from. The old powers of the land had taken back what they had lent. And Bedivere ran back to the king and told him — and Arthur smiled, for the first time in his dying, and said: "Now help me to the water's edge. A barge is coming for me." The sword had returned to the lake. The king was about to return to wherever kings go.

而第三次貝迪維爾照他所吩咐的做了。他把王者之劍擲過那片暗水——而這個傳統給了我們它最縈繞人心的意象之一：一隻手臂從湖中升起，覆著白色的絲綢，接住了劍柄，揮舞了三次，然後將它拉入水下。那柄劍回到了它所從來之處。大地的古老力量收回了它們所借出之物。而貝迪維爾跑回君王身邊告訴他——而亞瑟笑了，這是他垂死以來的頭一回，說：「現在扶我到水邊。有一艘船正為我而來。」那柄劍已歸於湖。那位君王即將回到君王們所去之處。

Chapter 3 · 第三章

The Once and Future King

未來之王

At the water's edge, a barge was waiting — full of women, all in black, weeping. And among them were three queens, veiled, whom the legend does not fully name but whom later tradition identifies as Morgan le Fay (Arthur's sister, his old enemy, now his attendant), the Queen of Northgalis, and the Queen of the Waste Lands. They had come, the legend says, to bear the wounded king away — not to burial, but to healing. They lifted Arthur into the barge. And Bedivere, watching, cried out: "My lord! My king! Where are you going? Do not leave me alone!"

在水邊，一艘船正等著——滿是著黑衣的女子，在哭泣。而其中有三后，覆著面紗，這個傳統未完全點名，但後來的傳統指認為摩根勒菲（亞瑟的姊妹，他的舊敵，如今他的侍者）、北加利斯之后，與荒原之后。她們來了，這個傳統說，是要把那位受傷的君王帶走——不是帶去安葬，而是帶去醫治。她們把亞瑟抬上了船。而貝迪維爾，觀望著，呼喊：「吾主！吾王！您要去哪裡？不要留下我獨自一人！」

And the barge moved off across the water, toward an island in the west — the island of Avalon — and the king was seen no more in Britain. Here is the legend's most beautiful ambiguity, and the source of its immortality. Arthur does not die, exactly. He is taken to Avalon, to have his wound healed — and the tradition insists that the wound is mortal, but also that it is being tended, that Morgan works over it, that the king sleeps but is not dead. The Latin chronicles say, in their famously maddening phrasing, that Arthur's death is "doubtful." No one saw him die. No one found a body. He simply went to an island in the west, and stayed there.

而那艘船駛過水面，向著西方的一座島——阿瓦隆之島——而那位君王在布列顛再也未被看見。這便是這個傳統最美麗的含糊，也是它不朽的源頭。亞瑟，嚴格說來，並未死去。他被帶到阿瓦隆，去醫治他的傷——而傳統堅持那道傷是致命的，卻也堅持它在被照料，摩根為它施作，那位君王睡著了，卻沒有死。那些拉丁編年史以其著名而令人發狂的措辭說，亞瑟之死是「可疑的」。無人見他死去。無人尋得遺體。他只是去了一座西方的島，然後留在了那裡。

And from that ambiguity grew the most powerful idea in the entire cycle: that Arthur is not dead, only sleeping, and will return. The "once and future king." He waits, in Avalon, for the hour of Britain's greatest need — and then he will wake, and come back, and the Round Table will be set again, and the golden age will return. This is the messianic hope at the heart of the Arthurian legend, and it is the reason the legend will not die. Every generation that feels its world is failing imagines that Arthur might wake. The just king is not gone — he is only asleep, and the dream of his return is itself a kind of resistance to despair. The legend refuses closure because the world it speaks to refuses closure. As long as there are people who long for a just kingship, Arthur cannot be allowed to be truly dead.

而從那份含糊裡，長出了整個循環中最有力量的觀念：亞瑟沒有死，只是沉睡，並將歸來。「曾經與未來之王」。他在阿瓦隆等待，等待布列顛最需要他的那個時辰——然後他會醒來，會歸來，圓桌會再被設起，黃金時代會回來。這是亞瑟王傳奇核心那個彌賽亞式的盼望，也是這個傳奇不會死去的原因。每一個感覺自己的世界正在失敗的世代，都想像亞瑟可能會醒來。那位公正的君王沒有離去——他只是睡著了，而關於他歸來的夢，本身就是對絕望的一種抵抗。這個傳奇拒絕閉合，因為它所訴說的世界拒絕閉合。只要還有人渴望一個公正的王權，亞瑟就不能被允許真正死去。

Chapter 4 · 第四章

The Endless Retelling

無盡的再述

The Arthurian legend is not one story but a thousand — a vast, accumulating tradition that every century has rewritten in its own image, and that has thereby never stopped being alive. In the 15th century, an English knight named Sir Thomas Malory, sitting in prison (probably for political crimes during the Wars of the Roses), gathered the whole sprawling French and English tradition into a single great book — Le Morte d'Arthur — and gave the legend the shape it has held ever since. Malory's Arthur is the Arthur most people know: the noble king, the doomed court, the great tragic arc from sword to lake. He wrote it in a cell, watching his own country tear itself apart, and the grief of that watching is in every page.

亞瑟王傳奇不是一個故事，而是一千個——一個龐大、積累的傳統，每一個世紀都以自己的形象重寫它，從而從未停止活著。在十五世紀，一位名叫托馬斯·馬洛禮爵士的英格蘭騎士，坐在獄中（大概是因為玫瑰戰爭期間的政治罪名），把整個蔓生的法蘭西與英格蘭傳統匯集成一本大書——《亞瑟之死》——並賦予了這個傳奇它此後一直持有的形。馬洛禮的亞瑟，是多數人所認識的亞瑟：那位高貴的君王、那個註定毀滅的宮廷、那條從石中之劍到湖的偉大悲劇弧線。他在一間囚室裡寫下它，眼看著他自己的國家撕裂自己，而那份觀看的悲慟，在每一頁之中。

In the 19th century, Alfred Lord Tennyson, the Poet Laureate, retold the whole thing as the Idylls of the King — a Victorian moral allegory in which Camelot becomes a parable about the fragility of civilization, and the fall becomes a warning about what happens when a society loses its ideals. Tennyson's Arthur is almost Christlike, almost too pure, and the poem's grief is the grief of an empire at its height wondering whether its own golden age is already passing. Then, in 1938, an Englishman named T.H. White, watching Europe march toward another war, wrote The Sword in the Stone — the first part of The Once and Future King — and made Arthur's story into a meditation on the tragedy of power: that the only way to stop violence is to use violence, and that using it corrupts the very thing it was meant to protect. White's Arthur is the Arthur of the musical Camelot and the Disney cartoon, and his book is the one most modern readers meet first.

在十九世紉，桂冠詩人阿爾弗雷德·丁尼生勳爵，將整個故事重述為《國王敘事詩》——一部維多利亞時代的道德寓言，在其中卡美洛成了一個關於文明脆弱性的寓言，而那場傾覆成了一個關於當一個社會失去其理想會發生什麼的警世。丁尼生的亞瑟幾乎像基督，幾乎太過純潔，而那首詩的悲慟，是一個處於巔峰的帝國在揣想它自己的黃金時代是否已然逝去的悲慟。然後，在一九三八年，一位名叫 T.H. 懷特的英國人，眼看著歐洲走向另一場戰爭，寫了《石中劍》——《曾經與未來之王》的第一部分——並將亞瑟的故事做成了一場關於權力之悲劇的冥想：阻止暴力的唯一方法是使用暴力，而使用它，腐蝕了它本意要保護的那個事物本身。懷特的亞瑟，是音樂劇《卡美洛》與迪士尼卡通裡的亞瑟，而他的書，是多數現代讀者最先遇見的那一本。

And the retellings continue. John Boorman's 1981 film Excalibur gave the legend Wagner and neon and a dark, mythic grandeur. Mary Stewart's Merlin trilogy told the whole story from the wizard's point of view. Marion Zimmer Bradley's The Mists of Avalon retold it entirely through the women — Morgan, Guinevere, Viviane — and in doing so exposed how much the male tradition had silenced. There are children's versions and academic versions and feminist versions and comedic versions (Monty Python's, beloved). The legend absorbs every retelling and is not exhausted by any of them — because, like the epics of India, it is not a single story but an argument, and every age enters the argument and adds its voice. Arthur is not a character. He is a conversation the West has been having with itself for nine hundred years.

而再述仍在繼續。約翰·布爾曼一九八一年的電影《王者之劍》給了這個傳奇華格納、霓虹，以及一種黑暗的、神話般的宏偉。瑪麗·斯圖爾特的梅林三部曲，從那位巫師的視角講述了整個故事。瑪麗昂·齊默·布拉德利的《阿瓦隆之霧》，完全透過那些女子——摩根、桂妮薇兒、薇薇安——來重述它，並在如此做之時，揭露了男性傳統所噤聲了多少。有兒童版本、學術版本、女性主義版本、喜劇版本（巨蟒劇團的，深受喜愛）。這個傳奇吸收每一次再述，而不被其中任何一次所耗盡——因為，一如印度的史詩，它不是一個單一的故事，而是一場爭辯，而每一個時代都進入那場爭辯，加上自己的聲音。亞瑟不是一個角色。他是西方與自己進行了九百年的一場對話。

Chapter 5 · 第五章

The Dream That Will Not Die

不死之夢

Why does this story — this particular story, of a king and a table and a sword and a cup and a fall — refuse to die? The Arthurian legend has lasted nine hundred years, through the collapse of the medieval world, through the Reformation and the Enlightenment and the industrial revolution and two world wars, through the end of empire and the beginning of everything else. And in every age, someone has picked it up and found it spoke to them. There must be something in it that is not bound to its own time.

為什麼這個故事——這個特定的故事，關於一位君王、一張桌子、一柄劍、一隻杯、一場傾覆——拒絕死去？亞瑟王傳奇延續了九百年，穿過中世紀世界的崩塌，穿過宗教改革與啟蒙運動與工業革命與兩次世界大戰，穿過帝國的終結與其他一切的開始。而在每一個時代，都有人拾起它，發現它對他們說話。它裡面必定有某種不受其自身時代所縛之物。

That something, I think, is the dream of the just king. Not Arthur specifically — the dream that there could be a ruler so good, and a court so fair, and a code so binding, that the strong would serve the weak and the world would be, for a while, governed by justice. The dream is impossible; the legend knows this, and shows us the impossibility, again and again — the loveless marriage, the sister's grudge, the knight's adultery, the son's treachery, the snake in the grass. And yet the dream will not die, because the alternative — a world in which the strong simply take what they can, and there is no justice but power — is too bleak to live in. So we tell the story of the king who almost made it work. We tell it knowing it ends in a field of the dead. We tell it because the telling is itself an act of defiance: a refusal to accept that the dream was always impossible, a insistence that it was real, briefly, and might be real again.

那個某物，我想，是「公正之君」的夢。不是特定的亞瑟——而是那個夢：可能有一位如此之好的統治者，一座如此公正的宮廷，一套如此有約束力的法則，以至於強者會服侍弱者，而世界會，一段時日，由正義所治理。這個夢是不可能的；這個傳奇知道這一點，並且一次又一次向我們展示這個不可能——那無愛的婚姻、那姊妹的宿怨、那騎士的通姦、那兒子的背叛、那草中的蛇。然而這個夢不肯死去，因為另一個選項——一個強者只管予取予求、除了權力之外別無正義的世界——太過慘淡，無法居住。所以我們講述那位差一點便使之成真的君王的故事。我們講述它，明知它結束於一片死者之野。我們講述它，因為講述本身便是一種反抗之舉：一種拒絕接受那個夢始終不可能的姿態，一種堅持它曾經真實過——短暫地——而可能再度真實的執拗。

And so Arthur sleeps in Avalon, and waits. He is not a character. He is a question the West keeps asking itself: could it be done? Could there be a just king, a fair court, a binding code, a round table where no one sits above another? The legend's answer is both — no, and try anyway. The dream fails. The dream is real. Both are true. And the dreamer, the once and future king, lies in his island in the west, wounded but tended, asleep but not dead, waiting for the hour — which may never come, which must always be imagined as coming — when the world will need him enough to wake him. That hour is the legend's true subject. And it is always, the legend tells us, just over the horizon. It is always nearly dawn. The king is always about to wake.

於是亞瑟在阿瓦隆沉睡，等待。他不是一個角色。他是西方不斷問自己的一個問題：它能被做到嗎？可能有一位公正的君王、一座公正的宮廷、一套有約束力的法則、一張無人高於他人的圓桌嗎？這個傳奇的答案兩者皆是——不能，但還是要試。這個夢失敗。這個夢真實。兩者皆真。而那位做夢者，那位曾經與未來之王，躺在西方他的島上，帶傷卻被照料，沉睡卻未死，等待那個時辰——那個可能永不會來、卻必須總是被想像為會來的時辰——那個世界足夠需要他、足以喚醒他的時辰。那個時辰，是這個傳奇真正的主題。而它，這個傳奇告訴我們，總是在地平線的那一邊。總是將近黎明。那位君王，總是即將醒來。

THE KING IS ONLY SLEEPING

那位君王，只是睡著了

A father killed his son, and the son killed him, and the Round Table died in a western field. A dying king threw his sword back to the lake, and a hand rose to take it. A barge of weeping queens bore him to an island in the west, where his sister — his old enemy — tends a wound that will not close. And for nine hundred years, the storytellers have refused to call him dead. He sleeps. He waits. He will return in the hour of greatest need — which is to say, in every hour, because the need is always great, and the dream of a just king never quite goes out. The sword is in the lake. The king is in the island. The story is in our hands.
