GILGAMESH

吉　爾　伽　美　什

KING OF URUK 烏魯克之王 · STANDARD BABYLONIAN, c. 1300–1000 BC

He who saw the deep, the foundation of the land; who knew all things, and journeyed to the edge of the world — two-thirds god, one-third man, and mortal after all. This is the oldest great story humanity ever wrote down, and it is about the fear of death. 他見過深淵，大地之根基；他知曉萬事，曾遠行至世界的邊緣——三分之二是神，三分之一是人，終究不免一死。這是人類寫下的最古老偉大故事，而它，講的是對死亡的恐懼。

Tablet I · 第一泥板

The Tyrant of Uruk

烏魯克的暴君

In Uruk-of-the-Sheepfold, the great walled city between the rivers, there reigned a king named Gilgamesh. He was, the poem tells us, two-thirds god and one-third man — a creature never seen before or since. No son was left to his father, for the king claimed him for his service; no bride was left to her bridegroom, for Gilgamesh, in his pride, took the first night as his right. He had built the mighty walls of Uruk with their foundations of seven sages, and the temple of the heaven-goddess; but he had also made the people groan beneath a tyranny that no man could curb, for who can stay a god's strength when it runs in mortal veins?

在兩河之間那座宏偉的圍城「羊圈之烏魯克」，統治著一位名叫吉爾伽美什的君王。詩中告訴我們，他三分之二是神，三分之一是人——一種前所未見、後亦未有的生靈。沒有一個兒子能留給其父，因為君王要徵用他服役；沒有一個新娘能留給其新郎，因為吉爾伽美什以他的驕傲，將初夜據為己有。他曾以七賢之根基建造了烏魯克宏偉的城牆，以及蒼天女神的神廟；但他也讓百姓在無人能制約的暴政下呻吟，因為誰能阻擋奔流於凡人血脈中的神之力呢？

The people of Uruk cried out to the gods: "Let them create a rival for him, a match for the storm of his heart, that he may turn his attention outward and leave Uruk in peace." And in the heavens, Aruru, the goddess of creation, heard. She washed her hands, she took a piece of clay, she pinched it in the steppe — and out of the very earth of Mesopotamia she shaped another being, as mighty as Gilgamesh but as wild as the beasts he would come to love.

烏魯克的百姓向諸神呼求：「讓他們為他造一個對手，一個與他心中風暴相匹敵之人，好讓他將心思轉向外面，使烏魯克得以安寧。」而在天庭，創造女神阿魯魯聽見了。她洗淨雙手，取了一塊黏土，在荒野中捏塑——就從兩河流域的大地本身，她造出了另一個生靈，與吉爾伽美什一樣強大，卻如他日後將會鍾愛的野獸一般狂野。

Tablet I · 第一泥板

The Wild Man of the Steppe

荒野的野人

His name was Enkidu, and when he stood up he was shaggy all over, his hair long like a woman's, his body matted with the grass of the open field. He knew nothing of cities, nothing of bread or beer or fine clothes. He ate grass with the gazelles, he drank at the waterhole with the beasts, he rejoiced in the company of living things. He was, in a word, the natural man — Gilgamesh's double, his mirror, set on the far side of civilization.

他的名字叫恩奇都，當他站起身時，渾身毛髮蓬亂，頭髮長如女子，身軀糾結著曠野的草莖。他不知城邦為何物，不識麵包、啤酒或華美之衣。他與羚羊一同吃草，與野獸一同在水窟飲水，在活物的陪伴中歡躍。一言以蔽之，他是自然人——吉爾伽美什的分身，他的鏡像，被安置在文明的彼端。

A trapper found him at his waterhole and was dismayed: this wild man kept freeing the animals from his snares, and the pits he dug were useless. Three times the trapper returned to his father in fear, and three times the father counselled patience — go to Uruk, he said at last, and ask King Gilgamesh for one of the temple-women, a woman of Ishtar, that she might tame the wild man as no snare ever could. The trapper went, and Gilgamesh, who already knew in his dreams that a friend was coming, granted him Shamhat.

一個獵人在他的水窟邊發現了他，大為苦惱：這個野人不斷從他的陷阱中放出野獸，他所挖的坑都成了無用之物。獵人三次滿懷恐懼地回到父親身邊，父親三次勸他忍耐——最後他說：去烏魯克吧，向吉爾伽美什王要一位神廟女子，一位伊什塔爾的女子，讓她以任何陷阱都做不到的方式馴服那個野人。獵人去了，而吉爾伽美什——他已在夢中預知一位朋友即將到來——便將莎姆哈特賜給了他。

Tablets I–II · 第一至第二泥板

The Wrestle at the Gate

城門前的角力

For six days and seven nights, Shamhat lay with Enkidu in the steppe, and when she was done with him, the wildness was gone from him. He turned to rejoin his gazelles — and they fled. He ran, and his knees failed him, for he could no longer bound as a beast. "You have grown wise, Enkidu," said Shamhat; "you have become like a god." She clothed him, she gave him bread and beer — strong drink that no wild thing had tasted — and led him by the hand toward the great walls of Uruk, where a king awaited his equal.

莎姆哈特與恩奇都在荒野中交合了六日七夜，當她完事之時，他的野性已蕩然無存。他轉身要回到他的羚羊之中——而牠們四散奔逃。他奔跑，雙膝卻不聽使喚，因他再也無法如野獸般騰躍。「你已變得有智慧了，恩奇都，」莎姆哈特說；「你已變得像個神。」她為他穿上衣裳，給他麵包與啤酒——那是野物從未嘗過的烈飲——並牽著他的手，走向烏魯克宏偉的城牆，那裡一位君王正等待著他的匹敵。

In Uruk that very night, the people had gathered for a wedding, and Gilgamesh, true to his custom, strode forward to claim the bride. But at the threshold stood a stranger — Enkidu, who had heard of the king's injustice and would not allow it. They seized each other like wild bulls. The doorposts shattered, the walls trembled; the dust of their struggle filled the street. Yet in that struggle, something stranger than victory happened: Gilgamesh bent his knee, and Enkidu's fury relented — and the two embraced, and kissed, and were friends from that hour. The goddess Ninsun, Gilgamesh's mother, took Enkidu as her own son. The tyrant, at last, had a companion.

就在那夜，烏魯克的百姓為一場婚禮聚集，吉爾伽美什依其慣例，大步上前要認領新娘。然而在門檻上站著一個陌生人——恩奇都，他已聽聞君王的不義，不容此事發生。他們如野牛般彼此抓住。門柱碎裂，牆壁震顫；他們搏鬥的塵土瀰漫了街道。然而在那場搏鬥中，發生了一件比勝利更奇異的事：吉爾伽美什彎下了膝，恩奇都的怒火也隨之平息——兩人擁抱、親吻，從那一刻起成了朋友。吉爾伽美什的母親寧孫女神，將恩奇都收為己子。暴君，終於有了一個同伴。

Tablets III–V · 第三至第五泥板

The Cedar Forest & Humbaba

雪松林與洪巴巴

Restless now, and eager for a name that would outlive him, Gilgamesh proposed a quest: they would travel to the far-off Cedar Forest, the dwelling of the gods, and there slay its guardian, the monster Humbaba, whom the lord Enlil had appointed to terrify mortals. The elders of Uruk begged him not to go; his mother Ninsun wept and laid upon Enkidu the solemn charge of bringing her son back alive. But Gilgamesh would not be turned. "Who, my friend, can scale heaven?" he asked. "Only the gods, in the days before the flood. But we are men, and our days are numbered, and so let us do a deed that will be remembered."

如今躁動不安、急於求取一個能比他長壽的名字，吉爾伽美什提議一項遠征：他們要前往遙遠的雪松林——諸神的居所——在那裡斬殺其守護者，怪獸洪巴巴，他是恩利爾所任命來威嚇凡人的。烏魯克的長老們懇求他莫往；他的母親寧孫流淚，並將把兒子活著帶回的莊嚴之責託付給恩奇都。然而吉爾伽美什不為所動。「我的朋友，誰能攀登上天？」他問。「唯有諸神，在洪水之前的日子裡。但我們是人，我們的時日有數，所以讓我們做一件會被銘記的事吧。」

The journey was long, and Gilgamesh dreamed terrible dreams each night — of a mountain falling, of a wild bull, of a thunder-bird — and each morning Enkidu interpreted them as good omens. At last they reached the Cedar Forest, where Humbaba's roar was like the flood, and his breath was death. They hewed the trees; the monster charged. But Shamash, the sun-god, Gilgamesh's protector, sent down thirteen winds to pin Humbaba in place, and the two heroes struck off his head. They took his terrible aura as a trophy, and felled the tallest cedars, and floated them home down the river to the gates of Uruk. Yet the deed carried a price they could not yet see — for Humbaba was Enlil's servant, and Enlil does not forgive.

旅程漫長，吉爾伽美什每晚都做可怕的夢——一座山崩塌、一頭野牛、一隻雷鳥——而恩奇都每個早晨都將之解為吉兆。終於他們抵達了雪松林，那裡洪巴巴的咆哮如洪水，他的氣息即是死亡。他們砍伐樹木；怪獸衝來。然而太陽之神、吉爾伽美什的庇護者夏馬修，降下十三股風將洪巴巴釘在原地，兩位英雄斬下了他的頭顱。他們取下他可怖的光環作為戰利品，砍倒最高的雪松，順河漂流，運回烏魯克的城門。然而這樁事帶著一個他們尚無法看見的代價——因為洪巴巴是恩利爾的僕人，而恩利爾不會寬恕。

Tablet VI · 第六泥板

Ishtar Spurned & the Bull of Heaven

伊什塔爾遭拒與天之公牛

Crowned with glory, Gilgamesh washed and donned his finest robe and his crown — and no sooner had he done so than the goddess Ishtar herself looked down and desired him. "Come, Gilgamesh, be my lover," she said, offering him a chariot of gold and lapis, kingship, and a palace of abundance. But Gilgamesh knew her history. He laughed in her face, and recited the long catalogue of her ruined lovers — the bird whose wing she broke, the lion whose pits she dug, the horse doomed to whip and spur, the shepherd turned to a wolf, the gardener Ishullanu struck to a dwarf. "And would you love me, and then ruin me too? You are a brazier that goes out in the cold, a door that lets in wind, a palace that crushes its guests."

頂著榮光，吉爾伽美什沐浴，穿上他最華美的袍子，戴上他的王冠——而他剛剛如此，伊什塔爾女神便俯視而下，傾慕於他。「來吧，吉爾伽美什，做我的情人，」她說，獻上一輛黃金與青金石的戰車、王權、以及一座豐饒的宮殿。然而吉爾伽美什知道她的歷史。他當面嘲笑她，背誦出她那些被毀的情人長長的名冊——那隻翅膀被她折斷的鳥，那隻為她挖下陷阱的獅子，那匹註定挨鞭受勒的馬，那個被變成狼的牧人，那個被變成侏儒的園丁伊舒拉努。「那麼你會愛我，然後也毀了我嗎？你是一盆寒天裡熄滅的火盆，一扇灌進冷風的門，一座壓垮賓客的宮殿。」

Enraged, Ishtar flew to heaven and demanded of her father Anu the Bull of Heaven — a monstrous beast that, if loosed, would eat the herds and dry the rivers and bring seven years of famine. "Give it to me," she threatened, "or I will smash the doors of the underworld, and the dead shall rise and outnumber the living." Anu, frightened, agreed; and the Bull descended onto the plain of Uruk. At each of its snorts, a pit opened that swallowed a hundred men; at the next, two hundred. But Enkidu seized it by the horns, and Gilgamesh drove in the knife between its horns, and the Bull of Heaven died. In triumph Enkidu tore off its thigh and hurled it at Ishtar's feet. The city rejoiced — and the gods grew cold.

伊什塔爾勃然大怒，飛上天庭，向其父安努索要天之公牛——一頭怪獸，一旦放出，將吞噬牲畜、吸乾河流、帶來七年的饑荒。「把它給我，」她威脅道，「否則我要砸碎冥府之門，讓死者復活，數目多過活人。」安努驚恐，應允了；公牛降下，落在烏魯克的平原上。牠每鼻息一次，便裂開一個吞沒百人的深坑；再鼻息一次，便是兩百。然而恩奇都抓住牠的雙角，吉爾伽美什在牠兩角之間刺下利刃，天之公牛便死了。恩奇都在勝利中扯下牠的大腿，擲到伊什塔爾腳下。城邦歡慶——而諸神卻漸漸冷了。

Tablet VII · 第七泥板

The Death of Enkidu

恩奇都之死

That night, Enkidu lay down and dreamed. He saw the gods assembled in council: Anu spoke first — "Because they slew the Bull and killed Humbaba, one of the two must die." Enlil answered, "Let Enkidu die, not Gilgamesh." But Shamash, the sun-god, rose to defend them: "Was it not at my command that they slew the Bull and Humbaba? Shall the innocent now die?" And Enlil turned his anger on Shamash. Enkidu woke, and knew. A sickness fell upon him that very day, and he understood that he would not rise again.

那夜，恩奇都躺下，做了個夢。他看見諸神聚於議會：安努先開口——「因為他們殺了公牛，殺了洪巴巴，兩人之中必有一死。」恩利爾回答：「讓恩奇都死，不是吉爾伽美什。」然而太陽之神夏馬修起身為他們辯護：「他們斬殺公牛與洪巴巴，豈不是奉我之命？無辜之人如今要死嗎？」恩利爾便將怒火轉向夏馬修。恩奇都醒來，知道了。就在那天，一場病降在他身上，他明白自己將再也無法起身。

In his fever, Enkidu cursed everything that had led him here — the great cedar gate he had made with his own hands, the trapper who first found him, and above all Shamhat, the woman who had civilized him. "May she never have a home," he raged, "may she stand in the street and drink ditch-water." But from heaven, Shamash rebuked him: "Enkidu, why do you curse the woman who gave you bread and beer, fine garments, and — greatest of all — Gilgamesh himself as a friend?" Struck by this, Enkidu relented, and blessed Shamhat instead. He dreamed once more of the underworld — the house of dust, where the dead eat clay and wear feathers, where the man with many sons is blessed and the unburied has no rest. Then, on the twelfth day, Enkidu died. And Gilgamesh, who had never known grief before, did not yet understand that he had just seen his own future.

在他的高燒之中，恩奇都詛咒了一切將他引至此處之物——他親手所造的那扇雪松大門、最先發現他的獵人，尤其是莎姆哈特，那個馴化他的女子。「願她永無歸宿，」他怒吼，「願她站在街頭喝溝渠之水。」然而夏馬修從天庭責備他：「恩奇都，你為何詛咒那個給你麵包與啤酒、華美衣裳，以及——最重要的——吉爾伽美什本人作為朋友的女子？」恩奇都被此言擊中，回心轉意，反而祝福了莎姆哈特。他又一次夢見了冥府——塵埃之屋，亡者在其中吃黏土、穿羽毛，多子者蒙福，未葬者不得安息。然後，在第十二日，恩奇都死了。而從未嘗過悲慟的吉爾伽美什，尚不明白自己剛剛看見的是他自己的未來。

Tablet VIII · 第八泥板

The Great Lament

大哀歌

Gilgamesh wept. He would not let them bury his friend. He circled the bier like a lioness whose cubs are taken; he tore his royal robes, he tore his hair, he poured out his grief like water. "You were the axe at my side," he cried, "the shield before my face. You were my brother's dagger on the finest of straps. Oh, Enkidu, what sleep has come upon you? You will not hear me, you will not answer me!" For seven days and seven nights he kept watch beside the body, refusing to believe Enkidu was truly gone, until at last a maggot crawled from the dead man's nose — and only then did Gilgamesh concede that his friend would not return, and let them take him to his grave.

吉爾伽美什哭了。他不肯讓他們埋葬他的朋友。他如失去幼崽的母獅般繞著停屍架；他撕碎他的王袍，扯亂頭髮，將悲慟如水般傾倒。「你是我身側的斧，」他哭道，「我面前的盾。你是我兄弟腰帶上最美的匕首。哦，恩奇都，怎樣的沉睡降臨在你身上？你聽不見我，你不回答我！」他守在遺體旁七日七夜，不肯相信恩奇都真的走了，直到一條蛆從死者的鼻中爬出——那時，吉爾伽美什才承認他的朋友不會回來了，才讓他們將他帶往墳墓。

And then, in the silence after the burial, the terrible thought rose up in him. Enkidu had died. Enkidu, who was as strong as he. If Enkidu could die — then so could he. The fear that mankind has carried ever since took root in that hour, in a king standing beside a fresh grave. "When I die," he whispered, "shall I not be like Enkidu? Sorrow has entered my heart. I am afraid of death. So now I will go — I will find Utnapishtim, the one the gods made immortal, the survivor of the flood. I will ask him: how did you find life?" He tore off his fine clothes, he put on the skins of beasts, and he walked out of Uruk alone.

然後，在葬禮之後的寂靜中，一個可怕的念頭在他心中升起。恩奇都死了。恩奇都，那個與他一樣強壯的人。如果恩奇都會死——那麼他也會。人類自那以來一直背負的恐懼，就在那一刻、在一個站在新墳旁的君王心中生了根。「我死的時候，」他低語，「我豈不像恩奇都一樣嗎？悲傷已進入我的心。我害怕死亡。所以現在我要出發——我要找到烏特納庇什提，那個諸神賜予永生的人，洪水的生還者。我要問他：你是如何得到生命的？」他扯下華美的衣服，披上野獸的皮，獨自走出了烏魯克。

Tablet IX · 第九泥板

The Road Past the Sun

越過太陽之路

He wandered. He crossed mountain ranges where no man had gone, he slew lions in the passes, he traveled until he came at last to the twin-peaked mountain called Mashu — the mountain through which the sun itself passes each day, rising in the east and setting in the west. At its gate stood the Scorpion-People, terrible guardians, half-man and half-scorpion, whose glance was death. But when they saw this strange traveller, two-thirds divine, wild-haired and grief-stricken, they did not strike him down. Instead they recognized him, and asked his errand, and listened.

他漫遊著。他越過無人到過的山脈，在隘口斬殺獅子，他一路前行，直到來到那座名為馬舒的雙峰山——太陽每日從中穿過的山，東升西落。在它的門口站著蠍人，可怖的守衛，半人半蠍，其目光即是死亡。然而當他們看見這個奇異的旅人——三分之二是神、披頭散髮、滿心悲慟——他們沒有將他擊倒。相反，他們認出了他，詢問他的使命，並傾聽。

"No mortal," they warned him, "has ever walked the path through the mountain. It is twelve leagues of utter darkness — no light, no dawn, nothing to see or to guide." But Gilgamesh would not turn back. He entered the darkness, and for twelve leagues he walked blind, the mountain's belly closing around him. At the end of each league the way grew dimmer; at the eleventh he could feel dawn approaching; and at the twelfth he burst forth into light — into a garden of the gods, where the trees bore fruit of carnelian and lapis lazuli, glowing like jewels. He had passed through the mountain of the sun. Beyond it lay the sea, and beyond the sea, the one man who had beaten death.

「沒有凡人，」他們警告他，「曾走過穿山之路。那是十二里格的絕對黑暗——沒有光，沒有黎明，無物可見，無物可引。」然而吉爾伽美什不肯回頭。他步入黑暗，盲目地走了十二里格，山的肚腹在他四周合攏。每里格盡頭，道路愈加幽暗；到了第十一里格，他能感覺到黎明將近；到了第十二里格，他衝入了光明——進入一座諸神的花園，那裡的樹結著紅玉髓與青金石的果實，如寶石般發光。他已穿過太陽之山。山的那一邊是大海，海的那一邊，是那個戰勝了死亡的人。

Tablet X · 第十泥板

Siduri & the Waters of Death

西杜麗與死亡之水

By the sea sat a woman named Siduri, the divine ale-wife, the maker of beer, who dwelt at the very edge of the world. When she saw this wild, grief-worn man approaching, she barred her door, fearing he was a murderer. But Gilgamesh told her his whole story — his friend, his death, his quest — and Siduri gave him the wisest counsel in the entire epic, a counsel he could not bring himself to take: "Gilgamesh, where are you hurrying? The life you seek, you will not find. When the gods created mankind, they set aside death for men, and kept life in their own hands. So fill your belly, Gilgamesh; be happy day and night; make every day a feast of joy; dance and play, and let your wife delight in your bosom. That is the work of the living."

海邊坐著一個名叫西杜麗的女子，神聖的酒肆女主人，啤酒的釀造者，她居於世界的盡頭。當她看見這個野性、被悲慟磨損的男人走近，她閂上門，怕他是個殺人犯。然而吉爾伽美什將他的整個故事告訴了她——他的朋友、他的死、他的追求——而西杜麗給了他整部史詩中最睿智的忠告，一個他無法令自己採納的忠告：「吉爾伽美什，你匆匆趕往何處？你所追尋的生命，你將尋不到。當諸神創造人類之時，他們為人保留了死亡，將生命留在他們自己手中。所以，填飽你的肚子吧，吉爾伽美什；日以繼夜地快樂；讓每一天都成為歡宴；跳舞、遊戲，讓你的妻子在你的懷中歡喜。那才是活著的人該做的事。」

But Gilgamesh would not be turned, and so Siduri sent him to Urshanabi, the ferryman of Utnapishtim, who alone could cross the Waters of Death — that deadly sea through which no hand might touch the surface and live. With Urshanabi's help, Gilgamesh felled long poles of pine, and they poled across the lethal water, breaking pole after pole, each one burned away the instant it touched the surface, until at last they rigged a sail from Gilgamesh's own garment and crossed to the far shore. And there, at the mouth of the rivers, he found the man he had crossed the world to meet.

然而吉爾伽美什不肯回頭，於是西杜麗將他送到烏特納庇什提的擺渡人烏爾夏納比那裡，唯有他能橫渡死亡之水——那片致命的海，任何手觸及其水面都會死去。在烏爾夏納比的幫助下，吉爾伽美什砍下長長的松木篙，他們以篙撐過那致命之水，一篙接一篙地折斷，每一根觸及水面便即刻燒毀，直到最後他們以吉爾伽美什自己的衣裳張起風帆，渡到了對岸。在那裡，在諸河之口，他找到了那個他穿越世界來尋見的人。

Tablet XI · 第十一泥板

Utnapishtim & the Flood Retold

烏特納庇什提與重述的洪水

He had expected a god — a shining figure radiant with immortality. Instead he found an ordinary-looking old man. "You are no different from me," Gilgamesh said, baffled. "Tell me — how did you come to stand among the gods, and find life?" And Utnapishtim told him the story you have already read: how he was the king of Shuruppak, how the gods decreed the flood, how Ea whispered the warning through the reed wall, how he built the boat and loaded every living thing, how the storm raged and the gods themselves wept, how he released the dove and the swallow and the raven, and made the offering on the mountain, and how Enlil, rebuked by Ea, at last relented and lifted Utnapishtim and his wife away to dwell "at the mouth of the rivers," granting them life as the gods do.

他原以為會見到一位神——一個散發著永生光輝的形象。然而他找到的，是一個看起來平凡的老者。「你和我沒有兩樣，」吉爾伽美什困惑地說。「告訴我——你是如何得以站在諸神之中，得到生命的？」於是烏特納庇什提講述了你已讀過的故事：他如何曾是舒魯帕克的君王，諸神如何降下洪水，伊阿如何透過蘆葦牆低語示警，他如何建造方舟、裝載一切活物，風暴如何肆虐、諸神如何哭泣，他如何放出鴿子、燕子和烏鴉，如何在山上獻祭，以及恩利爾如何在伊阿的責備下終於回心轉意，將烏特納庇什提夫婦帶到「諸河之口」居住，賜予他們如諸神般的生命。

"And at that time," Utnapishtim concluded gently, "the gods took me and set me here. But you, Gilgamesh — who among the gods will assemble for your sake? Who will grant you what was granted to me?" It was a kindness delivered as a wound. There was no secret, no technique, no herb of immortality. The flood survivor had been spared once, by a one-time decision of the gods, never to be repeated. Gilgamesh had crossed the world to learn that he could not be saved. And then Utnapishtim set him one final, impossible test — just to make sure he understood.

「而那時，」烏特納庇什提溫和地總結道，「諸神帶我來，將我安置於此。可是你呢，吉爾伽美什——諸神之中，誰會為你而聚集？誰會賜予你賜予我的那一切？」這是一份以傷口形式送達的善意。沒有秘訣，沒有方法，沒有不死之草。洪水的生還者曾一次得救，是諸神一次性的、永不重複的決定。吉爾伽美什穿越世界，只為得知自己無法被拯救。然後，烏特納庇什提給了他最後一個不可能的試煉——只為讓他確知，他已明白。

Tablet XI · 第十一泥板

The Test of Sleep & the Stolen Plant

不眠之試與被盜的仙草

"What will you do, Gilgamesh, to win eternal life? Here is a test as simple as it is humanly impossible: stay awake. Just seven days. Do not let sleep overtake you." Gilgamesh sat down on his haunches to try. And before he knew it, sleep fell upon him like a net — heavy, irresistible, the very thing his body was made to surrender to. For seven days he slumbered where he sat, while Utnapishtim's wife baked a fresh loaf each day and marked it on the wall, so that the king could not deny his failure. On the seventh day they woke him, and showed him the row of loaves — the first dried out, the second stale, the third wet, the fourth white with mould, the fifth mottled, the sixth barely cool, the seventh still hot. "Look, Gilgamesh," said Utnapishtim. "Even sleep, you cannot conquer. How then death?"

「你要做什麼，吉爾伽美什，來贏得永生？這兒有一個簡單到對人類卻不可能的試煉：保持清醒。只要七日。不要讓睡眠追上你。」吉爾伽美什蹲坐下去嘗試。而他在知覺之前，睡眠便如一張網落在他身上——沉重、不可抗拒，正是他的肉身生來便要屈服之物。他就那樣坐著沉睡了七日，烏特納庇什提的妻子每日烤一個新鮮的麵包，在牆上做記號，好讓君王無法否認自己的失敗。第七日，他們喚醒他，給他看那一排麵包——第一個乾癟，第二個陳舊，第三個濕軟，第四個覆了白霉，第五個斑駁，第六個剛涼，第七個猶熱。「看哪，吉爾伽美什，」烏特納庇什提說，「連睡眠，你都征服不了。何況死亡？」

Yet, seeing his despair, Utnapishtim offered one slender hope. At the bottom of the cosmic sea grows a thorny plant, hard to grasp — "this is your recovery, your second youth." Gilgamesh tied heavy stones to his feet and dove into the deep; he seized the prickly plant, slashed his hands to ribbons, and rose again clutching it. "I will call its name 'the old man grows young,'" he rejoiced; "I will eat it, and be young again — if not forever, then for a while." On the long road home, he stopped at a pool to bathe. And while he bathed, a snake caught the scent of the plant — and stole it, and slithered away, and as it ate, shed its old skin and emerged renewed. Gilgamesh sat by the water and wept. "For whom," he cried, "have my hands laboured? For whom has my heart's blood been spent? I have won no blessing for myself — and the snake has it now." He had held immortality in his torn hands, and lost it to the lowest of beasts.

然而，看見他的絕望，烏特納庇什提給了一絲渺茫的希望。在宇宙之海的底部長著一株多刺的草，難以攫取——「這是你的痊癒，你的第二青春。」吉爾伽美什將沉重的石頭繫於足上，潛入深淵；他抓住那株紮手的草，雙手被割成碎片，重新浮起，緊握著它。「我要叫它『老翁返少』，」他歡呼；「我要吃下它，再度年輕——若非永遠，便是一時。」在漫漫的歸途上，他停在一池水邊沐浴。而當他沐浴之時，一條蛇聞到了那草的氣味——便偷了它，蜿蜒而去，吃下之後，褪去舊皮，煥然一新。吉爾伽美什坐在水邊哭泣。「我的雙手，」他哭喊，「是為誰勞作？我的心血是為誰耗盡？我沒有為自己贏得任何祝福——而那蛇，如今擁有了它。」他曾把永生握在撕裂的雙手中，又把它輸給了最低等的野獸。

Tablet XI · 第十一泥板

The Walls of Uruk

烏魯克的城牆

Empty-handed, defeated, a man made old by grief and travel, Gilgamesh at last came home. He stood again beneath the walls of Uruk — the same walls he had built in his pride, before he had ever met Enkidu, before he had ever feared death. And now he turned to Urshanabi, who had walked the last road with him, and spoke the lines that close the oldest epic in the world: "Urshanabi, go up on the wall of Uruk and walk around. Examine its foundation, inspect the brickwork — is it not kiln-fired? Did not the seven sages lay its foundations? A square mile is city, a square mile is orchards, a square mile is clay-pits, and the temple of Ishtar: three square miles, and the open ground, comprise Uruk."

兩手空空、一敗塗地，一個被悲慟與跋涉催老的人，吉爾伽美什終於回到家鄉。他再次站在烏魯克的城牆之下——那正是他在結識恩奇都之前、在懼怕死亡之前，於驕傲之中所建造的城牆。如今他轉向一路同行的烏爾夏納比，說出了結束這部世界最古老史詩的詩行：「烏爾夏納比，登上烏魯克的城牆，走上一圈。察看它的根基，檢視它的磚工——它豈不是窯燒的嗎？七賢豈不是立了它的根基嗎？一平方里是城，一平方里是果園，一平方里是採泥場，以及伊什塔爾的神廟：三平方里，加上空地，便構成了烏魯克。」

This is the answer Gilgamesh found at last — not in the plant the snake had stolen, nor in the words of the man who survived the flood, but here, in his own city, all along. He had sought immortality as a private thing, something to hold in his own body forever, and he had failed. But the poem, by ending here, gives him a different immortality: the work of his hands. The walls he built still stand; the city still lives; and four thousand years later, a reader still reads his name. He did not get to live forever — but he did what only mortals can do: he made something that would. Gilgamesh, who saw the deep, who journeyed to the world's edge and back, learned at last that the only eternal life a human being can have is the one we give away.

這就是吉爾伽美什最終找到的答案——不在那條蛇所盜走的草中，也不在那個從洪水中生還的人的話語裡，而是在這裡，在他自己的城中，一直都有的。他曾將永生當作一件私有之物來追尋，某種可以永遠握在自己身體裡的東西，而他失敗了。然而這首詩，以在這裡收尾，給了他另一種永生：他雙手的作為。他建造的城牆依然矗立；城邦依然存活；四千年後，一個讀者仍在讀他的名字。他未能永遠活著——但他做到了只有凡人才能做的事：他創造了某個會永遠存在的東西。那個見過深淵、曾遠行至世界盡頭又折返的吉爾伽美什，終於明白：人所能擁有的唯一永生，是我們所能給予出去的那一種。

HE WHO SAW THE DEEP

他，見過深淵

A king sought to cheat death and could not. But the walls he built still stood, and his name was carved into clay that would outlast him by four thousand years. So the poem proved him right in the end — the only eternity we have is what we leave behind.
