ENUMA ELISH

埃　努　瑪　埃　利　什

WHEN ON HIGH · 當在高天之時 · STANDARD BABYLONIAN, c. 12TH CENTURY BC

When on high the heaven had not been named, and below no earth had been called — there were only the waters: sweet Apsu and salt Tiamat, mingling as one. From their union the gods were born; and from the dragon's sundered body, the world was made. 當在高天之上，天尚未有名；在下，地尚未被稱呼——唯有一片諸水：甘甜的阿普蘇與鹹澀的提雅瑪特，交融為一。諸神自其結合中誕生；而世界，自那巨龍被劈開的身軀之中造成。

Tablet I · 第一泥板

The Waters Before All

萬物之前的諸水

Enuma elish — "when on high." So begins the oldest creation song of Babylon, sung on the fourth day of the New Year festival while priests read it by torchlight. Before heaven was named, before earth was called by any word, there was only water. Not chaos exactly — the waters had a nature, a name, a gender. Above was Apsu, the fresh underground ocean, the first father; and Tiamat, the salt sea, the great mother who bore them all. Their waters mingled as one, unmixed with land, for no marsh had yet been reclaimed and nothing could yet be found that was not water.

「埃努瑪·埃利什」——「當在高天之時」。巴比倫最古老的創世之歌便如此開始，於新年祭典第四日，由祭司們在火炬光下誦讀。在天尚未被命名之時，在地尚未被任何詞語呼喚之時，唯有水。那並不盡然是混沌——諸水自有其本性、其名、其性別。上方是阿普蘇，地下的淡水大洋，最初之父；以及提雅瑪特，鹹澀的大海，孕育一切的偉大之母。他們的諸水交融為一，未與陸地相混，因為尚未有沼澤被開墾，也尚未有任何事物可被尋得，不是水的。

Before these two the poet names no ancestor. They are the absolute beginning, the Mesopotamian answer to the question every civilization must ask: what was there, when there was nothing? Two waters — sweet and salt, male and female — sleeping together, before even a god existed to wake them. Here already we may see a truth these people carried in their bones: that civilization itself, in their land between the rivers, was born of mingled water. The Tigris and the Euphrates were their Apsu; the marshes and the salt-flats, their Tiamat.

詩人不在這兩位之前再立任何祖先。他們即是絕對的開端，是這個民族對「什麼都不存在之時，有什麼？」這個每個文明都必須回答之問的兩河流域式答案。兩種水——甘與鹹、雄與雌——沉睡於一處，甚至尚無一神存在來喚醒他們。在此我們已可看見一個深植於這個民族骨髓的真理：在他們那兩河之間的土地上，文明本身即生於交融之水。底格里斯與幼發拉底是他們的阿普蘇；沼澤與鹽灘，是他們的提雅瑪特。

Tablet I · 第一泥板

The Birth of the Gods

諸神之誕

In the depths of their mingling, the gods were made — not spoken or thought into being, but born, as living things are born. First came Lahmu and Lahamu, whose names mean "the muddy ones," the silt of the riverbanks taking shape; and after long ages, Anshar and Kishar, "the whole of sky" and "the whole of earth," the horizon-circle of the world. From them was born Anu, the sky-god of the high heaven; and Anu begot Nudimmud — whom later ages would call Ea, or Enki — the lord of wisdom and of the sweet waters, who surpassed his father in every way.

在他們交融的深處，諸神造成了——並非被言說或被思惟而生，而是如活物一般被誕下。最先來的是拉赫穆與拉哈穆，其名意為「泥濘者」，是河岸的淤泥有了形貌；又經漫長歲月，安夏爾與基夏爾，「整個天空」與「整個大地」，世界的地平線之環。由他們生出了安努，高天之上的天空之神；安努又生了努丁穆德——後世稱之為伊阿，或恩基——智慧之神與甘水之主，在各方面都超越了其父。

So a genealogy unfolds, generation upon generation, the silent waters quickening into a household of rival gods. The younger ones were restless. They held banquets, they roamed, they filled the deep with their clamour. And here, in the noise of the children, the first seed of catastrophe was planted — for their revelry disturbed the ancient rest of the first father, Apsu, who could not sleep.

於是一份譜系展開，世代相承，沉默的諸水甦醒為一群彼此競爭的神祇。年輕的一輩躁動不安。他們設宴、漫遊、以喧囂填滿深淵。而就在這群孩子的喧鬧之中，災難的第一顆種子被埋下——因為他們的歡宴，驚擾了初代之父阿普蘇那古老的安息，使他無法入眠。

Tablet I · 第一泥板

Apsu's Murderous Plot

阿普蘇的殺機

The first father rose in fury and sought out his vizier Mummu, the minister who attended the waters. "They trouble me," cried Apsu; "by day I find no rest, by night no sleep. I will destroy them, I will put an end to their ways, that silence may return and I may sleep." Mummu, smiling, agreed — and indeed counselled something still more eager: to strike by day, while the young gods still trusted their father.

初代之父憤然起身，尋見他的大臣穆穆——侍奉諸水的宰輔。「他們煩擾了我，」阿普蘇叫道；「白日裡我不得安息，黑夜裡我不得安眠。我要毀滅他們，我要終結他們的作為，好讓靜默歸來，使我得以入睡。」穆穆微笑著，應允了——並且獻上更為急切的計謀：趁白日動手，趁年輕諸神仍信任其父之時。

But Tiamat, the great mother, heard this and was silent no longer. She was wroth, she cried out in grief; she raged at her husband for turning against the children of their own body. "Must we destroy what we ourselves brought forth?" But Apsu would not be moved, and Mummu only egged him on. So the plot was set in the deep waters — a father's plan to murder his own offspring, because their joy had become his torment.

但偉大的母親提雅瑪特聽聞此事，再不能保持沉默。她勃然大怒，悲痛地哭號；她因丈夫轉而對付他們親身的骨肉而狂怒。「我們豈要毀滅我們自己所生之物？」然而阿普蘇不為所動，穆穆更在一旁慫恿。於是在深水之中，計謀已成——一位父親謀殺自己子嗣的計畫，只因孩子們的歡愉已成了他的折磨。

Tablet I · 第一泥板

Ea Slays His Grandfather

伊阿弒祖

The plot was whispered in the deep — but the wise Ea heard it. Of all the gods, Ea alone knew all things; he drew a magic circle, he poured out a spell, a silence-charm of pure incantation, and laid it upon the waters. When he cast it upon Apsu, the first father grew drowsy, his strength failing, his splendour dimming; and Mummu, who had been so eager, sat powerless, unable even to struggle as the spell bound him.

計謀在深處被低語——然而睿智的伊阿聽見了。諸神之中，唯獨伊阿無所不知；他畫下一道魔法之環，傾倒出一道咒語，一道純然吟誦的靜默之咒，將之鋪於諸水之上。當他將之施加於阿普蘇，初代之父漸生倦意，力量衰退，光輝轉暗；而本來如此急切的穆穆，此刻只能無力地坐著，咒語縛住他時，甚至無力掙扎。

Ea stripped Apsu of his radiant mantle and crown, and took them for his own; and there, in the chamber of the sweet waters, he slew his grandfather. Upon the slain Apsu, Ea built his own dwelling — the great temple Esagila — and within it, with his wife Damkina, he begot a son. In the very house built on a father's corpse, the champion of the gods would be born. As for Mummu, Ea led him away by a nose-rope, captive, made to serve forever.

伊阿剝下阿普蘇光芒四射的披風與王冠，據為己有；在那甘水之廳，他弒殺了自己的祖父。在 slain 的阿普蘇之上，伊阿建造了自己的居所——宏偉的埃薩吉拉神廟——並在其中與妻子達姆基娜生下一子。正是在這座建於亡父屍身之上的宅邸中，諸神的英雄即將誕生。至於穆穆，伊阿以鼻繩牽走他，成為俘虜，永世為奴。

Tablet II · 第二泥板

Tiamat's Wrath and the Eleven Monsters

提雅瑪特之怒與十一怪獸

Now grief and rage together awoke the mother. The young gods, emboldened by their victory, had taunted Tiamat without ceasing, and the salt sea could bear it no longer. She gathered to herself a monstrous host, and out of her own body she fashioned eleven terrible things — serpents with sharp fangs whose venom was death, scorpion-men with stings, fish-men and bull-men, and chief among them all the mushhushshu, the furious dragon, scaled and serpent-tongued, the very beast that one day would grace the blue gates of Babylon. Whoever looked upon these creatures swooned; their glance was slaughter.

此刻，悲慟與暴怒一同喚醒了母親。年輕諸神因勝利而膽大，不斷嘲弄提雅瑪特，而鹹海再也無法忍受。她召集了一支怪獸的大軍，並從自身的軀體中塑造了十一頭可怖之物——毒牙銳利、其毒致死的巨蛇；帶刺的蠍人；魚人與牛人；而其中之首便是穆舒舒，那憤怒之龍，鱗甲覆身、蛇舌吐信，正是這頭猛獸，日後將妝點巴比倫的藍色城門。凡注視牠們之人無不昏厥；其目光即屠殺。

And Tiamat elevated one among the gods to stand at her side: Kingu, her new consort, the first among her children. Upon his breast she hung the Tablet of Destinies — that tablet on which the fate of all things was written, the right to decree what should be and what should not. "Your word, O Kingu," she swore, "shall be supreme." Thus armed, Tiamat readied for war against the children who had defiled her waters and murdered her mate.

提雅瑪特又抬舉一位神祇立於她身側：金古，她的新配偶，她子嗣之中的首位。她將命運之板懸於其胸前——那塊書寫萬物命運、有權決定何者當存、何者當亡的泥板。「你的言語，金古啊，」她起誓，「將是最高的。」如此武裝，提雅瑪特備戰，要對付那些褻瀆了她的諸水、謀殺了她伴侶的孩子們。

Tablets II–III · 第二至第三泥板

Marduk, the Young Champion

少年英雄馬杜克

The elder gods trembled. Anshar, the father of the sky, first sent his son Anu to confront Tiamat — but Anu went, saw her, and turned back in fear. Then Ea went, and likewise could not stand before her. At the last, the young Marduk, born in the temple of the slain waters, stepped forward — eager, burning, confident in his strength. "My father," he said to Ea, "send me, and I shall answer Tiamat."

年長諸神戰慄。天空之父安夏爾先派其子安努去面對提雅瑪特——然而安努去了，看見她，便恐懼而返。繼而伊阿前往，同樣無法在她面前站立。最後，年輕的馬杜克——生於被弒之水的神廟中——挺身而出，躍躍欲試、烈焰般熾烈、對自身之力充滿信心。「我的父親，」他對伊阿說，「派我去吧，我將迎戰提雅瑪特。」

But Marduk named his price. "If I am to be your champion and save your lives, then let my word be law. When I open my mouth, let it be fate — to destroy or to create. And after the victory, let my decree stand forever." To test him, the gods set a robe among them and bade him speak: "Let it be destroyed," said Marduk — and the robe vanished. "Let it be whole," he said — and it returned. Seeing this, the gods rejoiced and acclaimed him king; they feasted in his honour, and Anu armed him with a net, and the winds with which he would snare the dragon.

但馬杜克開出了他的代價。「若我要作你們的英雄，拯救你們的性命，那麼讓我的言語成為律法。當我張口之時，讓它便是命運——無論是毀滅或創造。而在勝利之後，讓我的旨令永遠有效。」為了試煉他，諸神在他們之間放下一件袍子，命他發令：「讓它毀滅，」馬杜克說——那袍子便消失了。「讓它完整，」他說——袍子便回來了。見此情景，諸神歡喜，尊他為王；他們設宴致敬，安努以一張網武裝他，又以諸風助他網住那巨龍。

Tablet IV · 第四泥板

The Dragon Split in Two

巨龍身軀裂為兩半

Marduk mounted the storm-chariot, drawn by the teams called Devastator and Trampler, and rode out to meet his great-great-mother in single combat. He had hung the net of Anu, and set the seven evil winds to ride behind him — the whirlwind, the tempest, the matchless flood. When Tiamat opened her cavernous mouth to swallow him, Marduk drove the imhullu wind straight into her jaws, so that she could neither close her lips nor breathe; her body swelled grotesque and vast. Then Marduk drew his bow and loosed a single arrow — it split her heart in two. He stood upon her carcass, the lord of all.

馬杜克登上風暴戰車，由名為「毀滅者」與「踐踏者」的戰馬牽引，馳赴戰場，去與他的曾曾祖母單獨決鬥。他掛起安努之網，令七股邪惡之風隨行於後——旋風、暴風、無匹之洪。當提雅瑪特張開她洞窟般的大口要吞噬他，馬杜克將伊姆胡魯之風徑直驅入她的咽喉，使她既不能闔上雙唇，也不能呼吸；她的身軀怪誕地膨脹起來，巨大無比。隨後馬杜克引弓，射出一箭——將她的心臟劈成兩半。他站立於她的屍骸之上，成為萬物之主。

From her split body he made the world. Half of her he stretched up and locked into place, calling it Sky — and there he set the waters above, the constellations, the calendar of the stars, the twelve months, the year. The other half he made into the Earth and the underworld below. From her two eyes flowed the rivers Tigris and Euphrates; her tail he bound into the great band of the firmament. Her monstrous host fled in terror and hid; Marduk trapped them in his net, broke their weapons, and led Kingu away in fetters. The Tablet of Destinies he took for his own. The world was now ready to be filled.

從她劈開的身軀，他造出了世界。他將她的一半拉起，鎖定於位，稱之為天——在那裡他安置了上方之水、群星、星辰的曆法、十二個月、一年。另一半他造為大地與其下的冥府。從她的兩隻眼睛，流出了底格里斯與幼發拉底河；她的尾巴，他繫成穹蒼的大帶。她的怪獸大軍驚恐逃散、躲藏；馬杜克以網擒住他們，折斷其兵器，將金古加上鐐銬牽走。命運之板，他據為己有。世界如今已準備好被填滿。

Tablets V–VI · 第五至第六泥板

The Making of Humankind

人類的造生

The world was made, but who would serve the gods, that they might rest? Marduk sought out Ea, and together they devised a creature to bear their labour. They brought forth Kingu — the consort who had urged on Tiamat — and tried him for his crime; they cut his veins and from his blood Ea kneaded the clay, and so made humankind. In us, then, runs the blood of a guilty god: we were made to lift the burden from the shoulders of the divine, to dig the canals, to tend the temples, to feed the gods with offerings so that they might sleep as Apsu never could.

世界已造，但誰來服侍諸神，使他們得以安息？馬杜克尋見伊阿，兩人共同設計了一種生靈來承擔他們的勞役。他們帶出金古——那位曾慫恿提雅瑪特的配偶——為他的罪受審；他們割開他的血管，伊阿以其血揉和黏土，於是造出了人類。那麼，在我們體內，流著一位有罪之神的血：我們被造，是為了將重擔從神祇的肩頭卸下，為了開鑿運河、照料神廟、以祭品供奉諸神，使他們得以安睡——正如阿普蘇從未能做到的那樣。

Then Marduk raised his city, Babylon — "the gate of the gods" — and within it the temple Esagila and the tower Etemenanki, the stairway to heaven. The gods assembled there and, in gratitude, proclaimed his fifty names, each one a title, a power, a facet of his lordship over all that is. Let the wise remember these names, said the poet; let them be handed down and pondered. And so the song closes: not with rest, but with the burden shifted — onto us. The waters are separated, the dragon is dead, the king of gods reigns in Babylon, and mankind labours below. So it was, and so, the tablets say, it shall be.

隨後馬杜克建立起他的城，巴比倫——「諸神之門」——於其中建了埃薩吉拉神廟與埃特曼安吉塔，那通天的階梯。諸神在那裡聚首，出於感恩，宣告了他的五十個名字，每一個都是一個尊號、一種力量、他對萬物統治權的一面。讓智者記住這些名字，詩人說；讓它們流傳下去，為人深思。於是這首歌落幕了：不是以安息，而是以重擔的轉移——轉到我們身上。諸水已分，巨龍已死，諸神之王統治著巴比倫，而人類在下方勞作。事曾如此，而泥板上說，事亦將如此。
