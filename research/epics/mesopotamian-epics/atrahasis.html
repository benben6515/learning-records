ATRAHASIS

阿　特　拉　哈　西　斯

"THE EXCEEDINGLY WISE" · 極智慧者 · OLD BABYLONIAN, c. 18TH–17TH CENTURY BC

When the gods themselves were labourers — digging riverbeds, bearing baskets of earth — they grew weary, and made mankind from clay and the blood of a slain god. But we were too many, too loud. And the sleepless lord of the air sent plague, then famine, then a flood to drown the noise forever. 當諸神自己還是勞工——開鑿河床、扛著一筐筐泥土——他們疲倦了，便以黏土與一位被殺之神的血造了人類。但我們人數太多，太過喧囂。於是那位不得安息的空氣之主，降下瘟疫，繼而饑荒，最後是洪水，要將那喧囂永遠淹沒。

Tablet I · 第一泥板

When the Gods Bore the Work

諸神尚作勞工之時

The poem opens with a world upside-down to our eyes: in the beginning, it was the gods who laboured. The great gods, the Anunnaku of the sky, commanded the lesser gods, the Igigu, to dig the riverbeds of the Tigris and the Euphrates, to clear the canals, to heap up the mountains. For forty years — or was it forty centuries? — they bent their backs to the baskets, and the marshes were tamed, and the land began to bloom. This is no myth of leisurely gods; the Mesopotamians knew, better than anyone, that civilization is built on sweat.

這首詩以一個在我們眼中顛倒的世界開場：起初，是諸神在勞作。天上的大神，安努納基，命令較小的神祇，伊吉古，去開鑿底格里斯與幼發拉底的河床，清理運河，堆起群山。整整四十年——抑或是四十個世紀？——他們彎腰背負筐籃，沼澤被馴服，大地開始繁茂。這並非一個眾神悠閒的神話；兩河流域的人比誰都清楚，文明是建立在汗水之上的。

But the Igigu grew weary. The work was endless, the nights brought no rest. At last, under the leadership of one burning spirit, they rose in rebellion — they heaped their tools, they set fire to their baskets, they marched in the night upon the house of Enlil, the lord of the air who ruled them all, and surrounded his shrine, crying: "We have borne this labour without equal! The work is too heavy!" The gods had gone on strike — the very first labour dispute in literature, and it happened in heaven.

然而伊吉古漸漸疲倦。勞作無盡，夜晚也帶不來安息。終於，在一顆燃燒之心的引領下，他們起而反叛——他們堆起工具，放火焚燒筐籃，在夜裡向統治他們全體的空氣之主恩利爾的居所進軍，圍住他的神龕，高喊：「我們已承受了這無與倫比的勞役！這工作太沉重了！」諸神罷工了——文學中第一場勞資糾紛，就發生在天上。

Tablet I · 第一泥板

The Labourers Made of Clay

泥造的勞工

Alarmed, Enlil summoned his counsellors — Anu the sky-father, Ea the wise, and Nintu the mother-goddess, also called Mami. A solution was needed. Ea spoke: the gods who rebelled must be given a substitute to bear their toil. Let the mother-goddess create a creature in the gods' own image — let her fashion a worker from the clay of the earth. But Nintu protested that she alone could not do it; she needed one thing more: a god's blood, a spirit to stir in the clay, so that the new creature would have reason and life.

恩利爾驚覺，召集他的謀臣——天空之父安努、睿智的伊阿、以及母親女神寧圖（又名瑪米）。必須尋得對策。伊阿說話了：起而反叛的諸神必須被給予一個替代者來承擔其勞役。讓母親女神按諸神的形象造一個生靈——讓她以大地的黏土塑造一個工人。然而寧圖抗議說她獨力無法做到；她還需要一物：一位神的血，一個能在黏土中攪動的靈，使新的生靈擁有理性與生命。

They seized a ringleader of the rebellion, a god named We-ilu, and slaughtered him. Nintu mixed his blood into the clay, and seven males and seven females she shaped, breathing into them the breath of life. Thus mankind was made — not in love, but to be a pair of hands, to lift the basket, to feed the gods with offerings. And the first thing the new creatures did was to take up the tools the gods had dropped. Labour had been handed down, and the gods could finally rest. But rest, as we shall see, is the one thing mortals would never again let them have.

他們抓住叛亂的首領之一，一位名叫韋·伊盧的神，將他屠殺。寧圖將他的血混入黏土，塑造了七男七女，向他們吹入生命之氣。於是人類造成了——不是出於愛，而是為了作一雙手，為了扛起筐籃，為了以祭品供奉諸神。而新造生靈所做的第一件事，便是拾起諸神丟下的工具。勞役被傳遞下去，諸神終於得以安息。然而正如我們將要看到的，安息，是凡人從此再也不會讓諸神擁有的唯一之物。

Tablets I–II · 第一至第二泥板

Plague and Famine

瘟疫與饑荒

Twelve hundred years passed, and mankind multiplied beyond counting. The land teemed, the cities roared — and above all, the noise rose. Enlil, the lord of the air, who had never quite forgiven the Igigu's strike, now found a new torment: the clamour of humanity kept him awake. "The noise of mankind has become too great," he complained. "I am losing sleep." And so he reached for the first tool at his disposal: plague.

一千兩百年過去，人類繁衍得不可勝數。大地熙攘，城邦喧囂——而最重要的是，喧鬧之聲升起。空氣之主恩利爾，從未真正原諒伊吉古的罷工，此刻又找到了一種新的折磨：人類的喧囂使他無法入睡。「人類的喧鬧太大了，」他抱怨道，「我失去睡眠了。」於是他伸手取用他手邊第一件工具：瘟疫。

But there lived a man named Atrahasis — "the exceedingly wise" — the king of the city Shuruppak, a favourite of the god Ea. Each time Enlil sent a plague, Ea whispered to Atrahasis a counter-spell: gather the people, cease all worship except to Namtar, the god of plague, and make him such an offering that he would stay his hand. The plague relented. Then Enlil sent famine — withholding the rains, drying the fields — and again Ea counselled Atrahasis, this time to honour Adad the rain-god. Twice the humans survived, twice Enlil's sleep was again robbed. His fury mounted. There would have to be a final solution.

然而住著一位名叫阿特拉哈西斯的人——「極智慧者」——舒魯帕克城的君王，伊阿神的寵兒。每逢恩利爾降下瘟疫，伊阿便向阿特拉哈西斯低語一道破解之法：召集百姓，停止一切祭祀，唯獨向瘟疫之神納姆塔爾獻祭，獻上足以令他罷手的供物。瘟疫便消退了。隨後恩利爾降下饑荒——扣留雨水，使田野乾涸——伊阿再次為阿特拉哈西斯獻策，這次要敬奉雨神阿達德。人類兩度存活，恩利爾的睡眠兩度再次被奪。他的怒火升騰。必須有一個最終的解決。

Tablet III · 第三泥板

Enlil Decrees the Flood

恩利爾降下洪水

Enlil called the gods to a great assembly and bound them all, every one, to a terrible oath of silence. This time there would be no warning, no god to whisper a countermeasure. "I will bring the flood," he swore, "and wipe mankind from the earth." No mortal was to know. The decree was sealed, and the oath taken — even Ea, the friend of humankind, was bound to keep the secret.

恩利爾召集諸神舉行盛大集會，將他們全體——每一個——都束縛於一道可怕的秘密之誓。這一次不會有預警，不會有神來低語破解之法。「我要降下洪水，」他起誓，「將人類從大地上抹去。」凡人無一可知。旨令被密封，誓言被立下——就連人類的朋友伊阿，也被縛於保守這個秘密。

But Ea was the lord of all cunning, and an oath sworn in council could not stop him from finding a loophole. He could not speak to a man directly; but he could speak to a wall. And in the reed hut of Atrahasis there stood such a wall, woven of reeds, through which a voice might pass undetected, plausibly denied. The wise god found a way to keep his vow and still save his favourite — by letting the wall overhear.

然而伊阿是所有狡計之主，議會中所立的誓約也無法阻止他找到漏洞。他不能直接對一個人說話；但他可以對一堵牆說話。而在阿特拉哈西斯的蘆葦小屋裡，立著這樣一堵牆，由蘆葦編織而成，一個聲音可以從中穿過而不被察覺，事後還可矢口否認。這位睿智之神找到了一個辦法，既能守住誓言，又能拯救他的寵兒——讓那堵牆「無意間」聽見。

Tablet III · 第三泥板

The Whisper Through the Reed Wall

穿越蘆葦牆的低語

"Reed-wall, reed-wall!" cried Ea, in a voice meant for no ear but the woven reeds. "Wall, hear me! Hut, attend! Tear down your house, build a boat. Abandon wealth, seek life. Spurn possessions, keep the soul alive. Take aboard the seed of all living things." It is one of the most elegant tricks in all of myth: the god speaks only to a wall, yet Atrahasis — conveniently standing within earshot — receives the message. No oath was broken; the wall, not the man, was addressed.

「蘆葦牆啊，蘆葦牆！」伊阿喊道，聲音是為了讓那編織的蘆葦聽見，不為任何耳朵。「牆啊，聽我說！小屋啊，留意！拆掉你的房屋，建造一艘船。拋棄財富，追尋生命。摒棄家產，保全靈魂。將一切活物的種子帶上船。」這是所有神話中最優雅的詭計之一：神只對一堵牆說話，然而阿特拉哈西斯——恰巧站在聽得見的範圍內——收到了訊息。誓言未被打破；說話的對象是那堵牆，而非那個人。

Atrahasis obeyed at once, though he trembled. "But, my lord," he asked Ea, "what shall I tell the elders and the city, when they see me build a ship and sail away from them to their doom?" Ea gave him a public answer to tell: that he was leaving because Enlil loved him and Enlil was angry at the city — that he would bless the earth wherever he went, while curses would rain on the place he left. With this cover, Atrahasis set his people to felling timber, hammering nails, pitching caulking, and loading the beasts — every creeping thing, winged and four-footed — until the boat groaned under its cargo of survival.

阿特拉哈西斯雖然顫抖，卻立刻遵從。「可是，主啊，」他問伊阿，「當長老和城民看見我造船、拋下他們駛向毀滅，我該對他們說什麼？」伊阿給了他一個公開的回答：他之所以離開，是因為恩利爾愛他，而恩利爾對這座城憤怒——無論他到何處，都將祝福那地，而咒詛將降在他所離開之處。以此為掩護，阿特拉哈西斯令百姓砍伐木材、釘釘、塗塞防水、裝載牲畜——每一種爬行的活物，有翼的與四足的——直到船在求生之貨的重壓下呻吟。

Tablet III · 第三泥板

The Boat and the Seven Days

方舟與那七日

The storm broke with a sound no mortal had ever heard. Adad the rain-god thundered from the clouds, and the subterranean waters of the deep burst upward through the earth, until sea and sky were one roaring wall of water. The brother could not see his brother; mankind, in its blindness, turned back to clay. And in the heavens, the very gods who had decreed this cowered in terror. Ishtar, the queen of heaven, wailed like a woman in childbirth: "The creatures of my own hands have turned to clay!" Even Anu, the sky-father, muttered that he could not have devised such a thing.

風暴以凡人未曾聽聞之聲爆發。雨神阿達德自雲中轟鳴，深淵的地下水從大地向上迸裂，直到海與天成為一道咆哮的水牆。兄弟看不見兄弟；人類，在其盲目之中，化回黏土。而在天庭，那降下此令的諸神自己也驚恐地瑟縮。天后伊什塔爾如分娩的婦人般哀號：「我親手所造的生靈，竟化為黏土！」就連天空之父安努也喃喃說道，他不可能設計出這樣的事。

For seven days and seven nights the flood raged. Then at last the rains ceased, the waters began to subside, and Atrahasis opened the hatch of his boat. The world was silent. He sent out birds — the dove, the swallow, the raven — and when the raven did not return, he knew there was dry land at last. He opened the vessel and released the surviving creatures into a drowned and empty world. And then, before anything else, he made an offering — the first sacrifice of the new age — and the gods, starving without their tribute, gathered around the smoke like starving men around a fire.

洪水肆虐了七日七夜。隨後雨勢終止，諸水開始退去，阿特拉哈西斯打開了船的艙口。世界一片寂靜。他放出鳥兒——鴿子、燕子、烏鴉——當烏鴉不再返回，他便知曉終於有了陸地。他打開船艙，將倖存的生靈釋放到一個被淹沒、空無一物的世界之中。隨後，在一切之前，他獻上祭品——新世代的第一個祭獻——而諸神，因沒有了貢品而飢餓，如飢民圍著火般聚攏在煙霧四周。

Tablet III · 第三泥板

A New Bargain with Mortality

與必死性的新約

When Enlil saw that a mortal had survived, his first reaction was fury — someone had broken the oath! Who warned him? But Ea rose and answered the king of the gods with rare plainness: he had not broken his oath; he had spoken to a wall. And more, he rebuked Enlil for his overreach — the sinner should be punished for his sin, not all mankind drowned for being noisy. A plague, a famine, yes — but a flood was a bludgeon where a scalpel was needed.

當恩利爾看見有一個凡人倖存，他的第一個反應是暴怒——有人違背了誓言！是誰示警的？然而伊阿起身，以少見的直率回答諸神之王：他並未違背誓言；他是對一堵牆說話。更有甚者，他責備恩利爾的窮追不捨——罪人當因其罪受罰，而非全人類因喧鬧而被淹沒。瘟疫、饑荒，可以；但洪水，是以棍棒代手術刀。

The two gods reached a compromise — and from it came a new order, the order under which we still live. Mankind would never again be destroyed wholesale. But to keep our numbers in check, new limits were imposed: a class of women forbidden to bear children; a demon to steal infants; a stillbirth here, an early death there. Death, which before the flood had come only as catastrophe, was now woven quietly into the fabric of every life. Atrahasis himself was taken out of the human bargain entirely — carried away to a paradise "at the mouth of the rivers," where he would live forever, the sole mortal to cheat the new rule. Under another name, in a later poem, we will meet him again — as Utnapishtim.

兩位神達成了妥協——由此誕生了一個新的秩序，一個我們至今仍生活其中的秩序。人類將不再被整體毀滅。但為了控制我們的數量，新的限制被加諸其上：一類被禁止生育的女子；一個偷竊嬰孩的惡魔；此處一個死產，彼處一個早夭。死亡，在洪水之前只作災難降臨，如今被悄然織入每一條生命的肌理之中。至於阿特拉哈西斯本人，則被完全帶出了人類的約定——被帶到「諸河之口」的樂園，在那裡永生，成為唯一騙過新規則的凡人。在另一個名字之下，在後來的詩篇之中，我們將再次與他相遇——作為烏特納庇什提。

THE SURVIVOR REMEMBERED

那生還者，被人銘記

One man walked off the boat and into immortality — and one day, a grieving king would travel to the world's edge to ask him how. That king's name was Gilgamesh.
