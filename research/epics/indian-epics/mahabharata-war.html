THE GITA & THE WAR

薄　伽　梵　歌　與　大　戰

THE BHAGAVAD GITA · KURUKSHETRA · THE HEART OF THE EPIC

On a field called the Field of Dharma, two armies stood ready — fathers against sons, teachers against students, cousins against cousins. And the greatest archer in the world looked out at the faces he would have to kill, and laid down his bow, and wept. Then his charioteer, who was God, spoke seven hundred verses — and in them the Indian imagination gave the world its most sustained answer to the oldest question: given that everything I love will die, why should I act at all? 在一片名為「正法之野」的戰場上，兩支軍隊蓄勢待發——父對子，師對徒，表兄弟對表兄弟。而世上最偉大的射手，望著那些他將不得不殺的面孔，放下他的弓，哭了。然後他的御者——即是神——說了七百頌——而在其中，印度的想像，給了世界對那最古老之問最綿長的回答：既然我所愛的一切終將死去，我究竟為何還要行動？

Chapter 1 · 第一章

The Field of Dharma

正法之野

The plain of Kurukshetra — the poem calls it dharmakshetra, the field of dharma, and the word is deliberate. This is not merely a battlefield. It is the place where the question of what is right will be decided in blood. Two armies had gathered: the Pandavas, seven divisions strong, under the banner of the monkey-god Hanuman; and the Kauravas, eleven divisions, vastly larger, led by grand Bhishma on his silver chariot. Between them, the space of a few bow-shots. Around them, every king of the age, every hero, every teacher who had ever trained either side. The whole world had come to watch a family destroy itself.

俱盧之野的平原——詩稱之為 dharmakshetra，正法之野，而這個詞是刻意的。這不僅是一片戰場。這是「何者為是」這個問題將以血來決定的地方。兩軍聚集：般度家，七個師，在猴神哈奴曼的旗幟之下；俱盧家，十一個師，龐大得多，由偉大的毗濕摩在他的銀色戰車上率領。在兩者之間，幾箭之遙的距離。在四周，這個時代的每一位君王、每一位英雄、每一位曾訓練過其中任何一方的老師。整個世界都來看一個家族毀滅自己。

And the poem opens, famously, with a sound: the blowing of the conches. As the armies stood ready, the great warriors raised their war-conches and blew — Bhishma's, vast and terrible; Drona's; Karna's; the conch of each great hero — and then Arjuna, on the Pandava side, blew his divine conch Devadatta, and Krishna blew his Panchajanya, and the sound, the poem says, "shattered the hearts" of the Kaurava host. The noise was the announcement: the war has begun. There is no turning back now. The conches are the epic's fanfare, and they are also its grief — because every man who blows one will, before eighteen days are out, be dead or grieving.

而詩，著名地，以一個聲音開場：海螺之鳴。當兩軍蓄勢待發之時，那些大武士舉起他們的戰螺吹響——毗濕摩的，巨大而可怖；德羅納的；迦爾納的；每一位大英雄的螺——然後，般度家這邊的阿周那，吹響了他的神螺天鳴，黑天吹響了他的五生螺，而那聲音，詩中說，「震碎」了俱盧大軍的心。那聲響即是宣告：戰爭開始了。如今已無回頭之路。那些海螺是史詩的號角，它們也是它的悲慟——因為每一個吹響它的人，在十八天結束之前，都將死去或哀悼。

And then Arjuna — the greatest archer alive, the man who has waited thirteen years for this moment, who has every reason to fight — does the thing no one expects. He asks Krishna, his charioteer, to drive the chariot out between the two armies, so he can look, one last time, at the men he is about to kill. Krishna drives him to the middle. And Arjuna looks. And what he sees breaks him.

然後阿周那——當世最偉大的射手，那個為此刻等了十三年、有千般理由要戰之人——做出了無人預料之事。他請他的御者黑天，將戰車駛到兩軍之間，好讓他最後再看一眼那些他即將殺的人。黑天將他駛到中間。阿周那望去。而他所見，擊碎了他。

Chapter 2 · 第二章

Arjuna's Despair

阿周那的絕望

He looked, and he saw them: his grand-uncle Bhishma, who had raised him; his teacher Drona, who had taught him to draw a bow; his cousins, his friends, the sons of his father's brother, the boys he had played with. On both sides. To win, he must kill the people who had given him everything he was. And the bow slipped from his hand. His limbs went weak. He sat down in the chariot and said the lines that open the Bhagavad Gita: "Krishna, I see evil omens. My limbs fail, my mouth is dry, my body trembles, my hair stands on end. I cannot stand. My mind is reeling. I see no good in killing my own people in battle."

他望去，他看見了他們：他的曾叔祖毗濕摩，那個撫養他長大的人；他的老師德羅納，那個教他拉弓的人；他的表兄弟、他的朋友們、他父親兄弟的兒子們、那些他一同玩耍的少年們。在兩邊。要贏，他就必須殺掉那些給予他今日一切的人。而弓從他手中滑落。他的四肢發軟。他坐倒在戰車裡，說出了開啟《薄伽梵歌》的那些詩行：「黑天，我看見不祥之兆。我的四肢衰敗，口乾，身體發顫，毛髮直立。我站不住。我心智暈眩。我看不見在戰鬥中殺戮我自己的親族有任何好處。」

What follows is not, the poem insists, cowardice. Arjuna is not afraid of dying. He is a warrior; he has stared down armies. What breaks him is moral clarity — the sudden, unbearable understanding that victory in this war will cost him everything that makes victory worth having. "We would rather be killed unarmed," he says, "than kill our own teachers and kin for a kingdom." And then he makes the most extraordinary offer: he will renounce the throne entirely. He will become a beggar. He will give up everything, rather than do this one thing. "I am confused about my dharma," he tells Krishna. "I ask you — tell me, decisively, what is good. I am your disciple. Teach me."

隨後所發生的，詩堅稱，不是懦怯。阿周那不怕死。他是個武士；他面對過大軍。擊碎他的是道德的清晰——那個突如其來、不可承受的明白：在這場戰爭中勝利，將耗去他一切使勝利值得擁有的東西。「我們寧可不執兵刃被殺，」他說，「也不願為一個王國而殺我們的老師與親族。」然後他做了一個最不尋常的提議：他要完全放棄王位。他要成為一個乞丐。他要放棄一切，也不願做這一件事。「我對我的正法感到困惑，」他告訴黑天。「我求你——決斷地告訴我，何者為善。我是你的弟子。教我。」

This is the moment the Bhagavad Gita is born — and it is worth pausing on why it matters. Arjuna's crisis is not his alone. It is the human crisis. Every person who has ever faced a duty that would wound the people they love; every person who has ever asked whether the right thing and the kind thing can really be different; every person who has ever wanted to throw down the tools of their life and walk away — that person is Arjuna, sitting in a chariot between two armies. The Gita is God's answer to him, and through him, to all of us. And the answer — which we are about to hear — is famous, subtle, contested, and not entirely comforting. It is, the tradition says, the most important seven hundred verses ever spoken.

這便是《薄伽梵歌》誕生的時刻——而值得在此停下，想想它為何重要。阿周那的危機不是他獨有的。它是人的危機。每一個曾面對一項會傷及他所愛之人的責任的人；每一個曾問過「對的事」與「善的事」是否真能不同的人；每一個曾想要擲下他生命的工具、轉身離去的人——那個人，都是阿周那，坐在兩軍之間的一輛戰車裡。《薄伽梵歌》是神對他的回答，並透過他，對我們所有人的回答。而那個回答——我們即將聽到的——是著名的、微妙的、有爭議的，且並不全然令人寬慰的。傳統說，那是曾被說出過的、最重要的七百頌。

Chapter 3 · 第三章

The Gita I — the Eternal Self

歌之一——永恆之我

Krishna begins where Arjuna's pain is sharpest — with the fact that he will have to kill. And the first thing Krishna says is the most famous line in the Gita, and perhaps in all of Indian thought: "You grieve for those who are not worth grieving for, and yet you speak words of wisdom. The wise do not grieve for the living or the dead." And then comes the heart of it: "Never was there a time when I did not exist, nor you, nor these kings; nor will there be a time when any of us ceases to be. As the embodied soul passes, in this body, from childhood to youth to old age, so does it pass, at death, into another body. The wise are not deluded by this."

黑天從阿周那最痛之處開始——從他將不得不殺人這件事開始。而黑天所說的第一句話，是《歌》中最著名的，也許是全部印度思想中最著名的：「你為那些不值得悲傷之人而悲，卻說著智慧的話。智者不為生者悲，亦不為死者悲。」然後是它的核心：「從未有過一個我不存在的時候，也不曾有過你，或這些君王不存在的時候；也永遠不會有一個我們之中任何一個停止存在的時候。正如具身之靈魂，在此身中，從童年到青年到老年這樣地走過，它也如此在死亡之際，走進另一個身體。智者不為此所惑。」

The teaching is staggering in its calm. The body dies, Krishna says — yes, as surely as old clothes are worn out and discarded. But the atman, the true self, the conscious witness within, is not the body. It was never born; it cannot die. "Weapons cannot cut it, fire cannot burn it, water cannot wet it, wind cannot dry it. It is eternal, all-pervading, unchanging, immovable, ever the same." Therefore, Krishna says, the killing you fear is not really killing — because what is real in your grandfather and your teacher cannot be killed. You will destroy their bodies. You cannot touch what they are.

這教誨以其平靜而驚人。身體會死，黑天說——是的，一如舊衣被穿破而丟棄。但 atman，那真實之我，那內在的覺知見證者，不是身體。它從未出生；它不能死亡。「刀不能割它，火不能燒它，水不能濕它，風不能乾它。它是永恆的、遍在的、不變的、不動的、始終如一的。」因此，黑天說，你所恐懼的殺，其實並非殺——因為你祖父與你老師身上真實之物，是不能被殺的。你會毀掉他們的身體。你觸不到他們是什麼。

Read carefully, this is not a comfortable teaching, and the Gita does not pretend it is. It is not saying "do not worry, no one really gets hurt." People's bodies will be torn apart; their families will mourn; the world will be poorer for their absence. The teaching is more radical than consolation: it is a complete re-description of what a person is. You are not the body that suffers and dies. You are the awareness that watches the body suffer and die, and that watches itself watch, and that was watching before this body and will watch after it. Act, then, Krishna says, from that place — from the place in you that was never born. Because that place cannot be wounded by anything you do.

仔細讀，這不是一個令人寬慰的教誨，《歌》也不假裝它是。它不是在說「別擔心，沒有人真的會受傷」。人的身體會被撕裂；他們的家人會哀悼；世界會因他們的缺席而更貧乏。這教誨比安慰更激進：它是對「一個人是什麼」的完整重新描述。你不是那個受苦與死亡的身體。你是那個觀看身體受苦與死亡的覺知，那個觀看自己觀看的覺知，那個在此身之前便在觀看、在此身之後仍將觀看的覺知。那麼，從那個地方行動吧，黑天說——從你之內那個從未出生的地方。因為那個地方，不會被你所做的任何事所傷。

Chapter 4 · 第四章

The Gita II — Karma & Dharma

歌之二——業與正法

But the eternal-self teaching, by itself, is not enough — because Arjuna's objection was never really about death. It was about action. "Even if they are eternal," he could say, "still I must do the killing; still my hand draws the bow. How can I act at all, knowing what action costs?" And so Krishna turns to the most famous teaching of the Gita: karma yoga, the discipline of action. "You have a right to action alone," Krishna tells him, "never to its fruits. Let not the fruits of action be your motive, nor let your attachment be to inaction."

但單憑永恆之我的教誨，是不夠的——因為阿周那的反對，從來不真正是關於死亡。它是關於行動。「即使他們是永恆的，」他可以說，「我仍得做那殺戮；我的手仍要拉弓。明知行動的代價，我怎能還行動？」於是黑天轉向《歌》中最著名的教誨：karma yoga，行動之瑜伽。「你對行動本身有權，」黑天告訴他，「對其果卻從來無權。莫讓行動之果成為你的動機，也不要讓你的執著落於不行動。」

This is the line that has governed Indian ethical life for two thousand years, and it is harder than it sounds. Krishna is not saying "do not care about results" — that would be absurd, you cannot live without caring. He is saying: do your duty because it is your duty, and let the results take care of themselves, because the results were never in your control anyway. The warrior's dharma is to fight. Whether he wins or loses, whether the war is just or unjust by some larger reckoning, whether the kingdom flourishes or burns — these are not his to decide. His is the action; the fruit belongs to the cosmos. To act with the whole heart, and to release the outcome completely — that, Krishna says, is the only freedom a human being ever has.

這是兩千年來主宰印度倫理生活的那句詩，而它比聽起來更難。黑天不是在說「不要在乎結果」——那是荒謬的，你無法不在乎地活著。他是在說：盡你的責任，因為那是你的責任，而讓結果自己去吧，因為結果本來也從來不在你的掌控之中。武士的正法是戰。無論他勝或敗，無論那場戰爭在更大的清算之下是義或是不義，無論王國興盛或焚燒——這些都不是他能決定的。屬於他的是行動；果，屬於宇宙。以全心去行動，並完全地釋放結果——那，黑天說，便是一個人所曾擁有的唯一自由。

And here Krishna makes the move that has troubled readers ever since: he tells Arjuna he must fight, specifically because it is his dharma as a warrior — and then, almost in the same breath, tells him that this very war, with these very deaths, is already woven into the fabric of the cosmos. "Except for you, all these warriors standing in either army will cease to be," Krishna says. "Therefore stand up. Win glory. Conquer the enemy and enjoy the kingdom. These have already been slain by me; you are but my instrument." It is a vertiginous teaching — Arjuna must act, and yet the acting is, in some sense, already done. He is responsible for drawing the bow; he is not responsible for the deaths, because the deaths were already ordained. Scholars have argued for millennia about whether this is genuine moral freedom or a cosmic fatalism in disguise. The Gita, characteristically, refuses to resolve it.

而在此，黑天做出了自此以來一直令讀者不安的一步：他告訴阿周那他必須戰，特別是因為這是他作為武士的正法——然後，幾乎在同一口氣中，告訴他這場戰、這些死，已然織入宇宙的結構之中。「除了你之外，這兩軍中站著的所有武士都將不復存在，」黑天說。「所以站起來吧。贏得榮光。征服敵人，享受王國。他們早已被我殺了；你不過是我的工具。」這是一個令人暈眩的教誨——阿周那必須行動，而那行動，在某種意義上，又已經完成了。他要為拉弓負責；他不必為那些死亡負責，因為那些死亡早已注定。學者們千年來一直在爭論：這究竟是真正的道德自由，還是披著偽裝的宇宙宿命論。《歌》，一如既往，拒絕去解決它。

Chapter 5 · 第五章

The Gita III — the Cosmic Form

歌之三——宇宙之相

Arjuna, overwhelmed, asks one more thing: "Show me what you truly are." And Krishna, who has been speaking as a friend and a teacher, grants the request — and the Gita gives us its most awe-ful passage, the Vishvarupa Darshana, the vision of the Cosmic Form. "Behold," Krishna says, "my hundreds and thousands of forms, of every kind, divine and wondrous, of every color." And the charioteer opens, for an instant, into what he really is: the entire cosmos, every face of every being, every star and every atom, the past and the future and the simultaneous now, all of it blazing from a single body that is also all bodies, the god who is also the demon, the creator who is also the destroyer.

阿周那，應接不暇，再求一事：「讓我看見你真實所是。」而黑天，那一直以朋友與老師之身發言者，應允了這個請求——而《歌》給了我們它最令人敬畏的段落，Vishvarupa Darshana，宇宙之相的異象。「看吧，」黑天說，「我的成百上千種形相，各色各樣，神聖而奇妙，各種顏色。」而那位御者，剎那間，敞開成祂真實所是：整個宇宙，每一個生靈的每一張臉，每一顆星與每一個原子，過去與未來與同時的此刻，全部從一個也是所有身體的單一身軀中灼燒而出，那個也是魔的神，那個也是毀滅者的創造者。

And in that vision, Arjuna sees, most terribly, the deaths he has been agonizing over — already accomplished. "I see all the sons of Dhritarashtra," he stammers, "and Bhishma and Drona and Karna, and all the warriors, rushing into your mouths, which are like the fires of the end of the world. Some are caught between your teeth, their heads crushed." And Krishna, in that blazing form, speaks the line that Oppenheimer would quote, two thousand years later, at the first atomic test: "I am Time, the destroyer of worlds, matured, here engaged in destroying these people. Even without you, none of these warriors will survive. Therefore arise, win glory; conquer your enemies and enjoy the kingdom."

而在那個異象中，阿周那最可怖地看見了——那些他一直在為之痛苦的死亡——已然完成。「我看見持國的所有兒子，」他結巴著說，「還有毗濕摩、德羅納、迦爾納，以及所有的武士，衝進你的口中，那口如世界末日之火。有的被你的牙齒夾住，頭顱被碾碎。」而黑天，在那灼燃之相中，說出了奧本海默在兩千年後、於第一次核試爆時所引用的那句：「我是時間，世界的毀滅者，已然成熟，在此從事毀滅這些人的工作。即使沒有你，這些武士也無一能存活。所以起來吧，贏得榮光；征服你的敵人，享受這個王國。」

This is the Gita's most disturbing gift, and it refuses to let itself be softened. The war is going to happen. The people Arjuna loves are going to die. And — this is the unbearable part — they are going to die not because Arjuna chooses to kill them, but because Time itself is consuming them, has always been consuming them, is consuming them right now, in this very moment, whether Arjuna draws his bow or not. The only freedom he has is whether to play his part in the cosmic action with full awareness, or to shrink from it in the illusion that shrinking will save anyone. Arjuna, broken open by the vision, falls to his knees. And then — at last — he picks up the bow. Not because he is no longer afraid. Because he understands, now, that his fear changes nothing, and that the only dignity left to him is to act.

這是《歌》最令人不安的禮物，而它拒絕讓自己被軟化。那場戰爭將要發生。阿周那所愛之人將要死去。而——這是無法承受的部分——他們將要死去，不是因為阿周那選擇殺他們，而是因為時間本身正在吞噬他們，一直都在吞噬他們，此刻正在吞噬他們，無論阿周那拉不拉弓。他唯一擁有的自由，是是否以完全的覺知去扮演他在宇宙行動中的角色，還是在「退縮便能拯救任何人」的幻覺中退縮。阿周那，被那異象擊開，跪倒。然後——終於——他拾起了弓。不是因為他不再害怕。而是因為他如今明白，他的恐懼改變不了任何事，而留給他的唯一尊嚴，便是行動。

Chapter 6 · 第六章

Bhishma on the Bed of Arrows

箭床上的毗濕摩

The Gita had ended. The bow was lifted. The war began. And the first to fall, on the tenth day, was Bhishma — the grand-uncle, the patriarch, the man whose terrible vow had set all of this in motion. Bhishma fought as no man had ever fought: he rained arrows like a storm, he single-handedly held the Kaurava line, he slaughtered thousands of Pandava soldiers a day. The Pandavas despaired. And Krishna, who had vowed not to fight himself, was so moved by the slaughter that he nearly broke his vow — he seized a wheel from a chariot and charged at Bhishma, and Bhishma, far from fleeing, knelt and offered his throat, overjoyed to die at the god's hand.

《歌》結束了。弓被舉起。戰爭開始了。而第一個倒下的，在第十天，是毗濕摩——那位曾叔祖、那位家長、那個其可怕的誓言啟動了這一切的人。毗濕摩如無人曾經戰過一般地戰：他的箭如暴風雨般落下，他獨力撐住俱盧家的陣線，他每日屠殺數千般度家的士兵。般度家絕望了。而黑天，那個曾發誓不親自作戰之人，被那屠殺感動得幾乎毀誓——他從一輛戰車上抓起一個輪子，向毗濕摩衝去，而毗濕摩非但不逃，反而跪下，獻上他的喉嚨，欣喜於能死於神之手。

But Bhishma's death, when it came, came another way — and it is one of the great images of the epic. Arjuna, following Krishna's counsel, had hidden behind Shikhandi — a warrior who had been born female and become male, whom Bhishma, by his chivalric code, would never raise a weapon against. And from behind Shikhandi's shield, Arjuna rained arrows down on the grandsire. Bhishma fell — but did not die. He had been granted the boon of choosing the hour of his own death, and he chose to wait. And so, riddled with arrows, he lay suspended above the earth, supported only by the shafts in his body — the famous "bed of arrows." There he lay, for fifty-eight days, through the rest of the war, teaching and blessing and watching the catastrophe he had helped to make, until at last, the war over, he let himself die.

但毗濕摩的死，當它來時，是另一種方式到來的——而那是史詩中最偉大的意象之一。阿周那，聽從黑天的計策，藏身於束髮之後——一個生而為女、後來變為男性的武士，毗濕摩依其武士之道，永不對此人舉器。而在束髮的盾之後，阿周那對那位大家長傾下箭雨。毗濕摩倒了——卻沒有死。他曾蒙恩，可自擇死亡之時，而他選擇等待。於是，遍體箭矢，他懸於大地之上，只由身中之矢撐著——著名的「箭床」。他在那裡躺了五十八天，穿過戰爭的其餘部分，教導、祝福、觀看著他所協助造成的災難，直到最後，戰爭結束，他才任自己死去。

The bed of arrows is the Mahabharata's symbol for itself. A great man, brought down by the very code he lived by (his refusal to fight Shikhandi, his refusal to break his vow), suspended between earth and sky by his own wounds, unable to die until he is ready, watching helplessly as the world he helped shape burns. There is no triumph in his fall. There is only the slow, dignified, terrible recognition that every choice has a cost, and that the man who set the chain in motion is not exempt from its final link. Bhishma dies well. But the poem does not let us forget that he dies because of a vow taken in love, half a lifetime before.

那張箭床，是《摩訶婆羅多》自身的象徵。一個偉大之人，被他所奉行之法則所擊倒（他拒戰束髮，他不肯毀誓），由自身的傷懸於天地之間，直到他願意才能死，無助地觀看著他所協助塑造的世界燃燒。他的倒下沒有勝利。只有那緩慢的、有尊嚴的、可怖的體認：每一個選擇都有代價，而那個啟動這條鏈的人，並不免於它的最後一環。毗濕摩死得其所。但詩不讓我們忘記，他死於半個人生之前、一個出於愛而立的誓。

Chapter 7 · 第七章

Drona, Abhimanyu & the Chakravyuha

德羅納、激昂與輪陣

After Bhishma fell, the Kaurava command passed to Drona — the old brahmin who had trained both Arjuna and the Kaurava princes in arms, and who loved Arjuna as a son even as he fought against him. Drona, too, was nearly invincible, and the Pandavas, knowing he could not be beaten by force, resorted to a lie. They spread the rumor that Drona's son Ashwatthama was dead — and Drona, heartsick, turned to Yudhishthira, the man who never lied, to confirm it. And Yudhishthira — the truthful, the upright, the eldest — spoke the lie. "Ashwatthama is dead," he said, adding under his breath, "the elephant" — though some say even that murmur was silenced by the drums. Drona laid down his weapons. And he was beheaded, in that instant of surrender, by a warrior who had no right to strike a man unarmed.

毗濕摩倒下之後，俱盧家的指揮權交給了德羅納——那位曾教過阿周那與俱盧諸王子武藝的老婆羅門，他即便與阿周那為敵，仍愛他如子。德羅納也幾近無敵，般度家知道不能以力勝他，便訴諸一個謊言。他們散布謠言說德羅納的兒子馬勇已死——而德羅納，心碎，轉向從不說謊的堅戰，求他證實。而堅戰——崇奉真實、正直的長子——說了那個謊。「馬勇死了，」他說，又在唇邊低聲補了一句，「那頭象」——雖然有人說，連那聲低語也被鼓聲蓋過了。德羅納放下了他的兵器。而他在那投降的一瞬，被一個無權攻擊手無寸鐵之人的武士斬了首。

And before Drona's fall, the war gave us its most heartbreaking death: Abhimanyu, Arjuna's son, a boy of sixteen. The Kauravas formed the chakravyuha — the wheel-formation, a rotating circle of soldiers that, once entered, could not be exited. Only Arjuna and Krishna knew how to break it; Arjuna was elsewhere that day. But young Abhimanyu knew how to enter it — he had learned it in the womb — though not how to escape. He broke in alone, fought every Kaurava champion single-handedly inside the formation, and was killed, at last, by many against one, in a violation of every warrior's code. His father Arjuna, learning of it, swore he would kill the killer by sunset or burn himself alive. The war, which had begun as a contest of dharma, had become a cascade of atrocity, each side's crime provoking the next, until the original question — who was right? — was buried under a mountain of mutual guilt.

而在德羅納倒下之前，戰爭給了我們它最令人心碎的一場死：激昂，阿周那之子，一個十六歲的少年。俱盧家布下了輪陣——那個輪形陣，一個旋轉的士兵之環，一旦進入便無法出去。只有阿周那與黑天知道如何破它；那天阿周那在別處。但年少的激昂知道如何進入它——他是在母腹中學會的——卻不知如何逃出。他獨自闖入，在陣中單挑每一位俱盧家的大將，最終被眾人合力殺死，違反了一切武士之規。他的父親阿周那聞訊，誓日落之前必殺其人，否則自焚。那場以正法之爭為始的戰爭，已成了一連串的暴行，一方之罪引發下一方之罪，直到最初的問題——誰是對的？——被一座共同罪咎的山所掩埋。

This is the Mahabharata's relentless argument, and Drona's death and Abhimanyu's are its proof. There is no clean war. The moment you decide that your cause is just enough to kill for, you have set in motion a logic that will, sooner or later, make you do the unjust thing — because the just thing cannot win. Yudhishthira lied. Arjuna's son was murdered by a mob. Drona was killed at surrender. Each side can point to the other's crime to justify its own; each side is, thereby, damned. The war does not vindicate the Pandavas. It corrupts them. And the poem, looking on, refuses to look away.

這是《摩訶婆羅多》無情的論證，而德羅納之死與激昂之死，是它的證據。沒有一場乾淨的戰爭。當你認定你的理由正當到足以為之殺人，你便啟動了一種邏輯，遲早會令你做出不義之事——因為不義之事才能贏。堅戰說了謊。阿周那之子被一群人謀殺。德羅納在投降中被殺。每一方都能指著對方的罪來為自己的罪辯護；而每一方，也因此，都 damned。那場戰爭並未為般度家平反。它腐蝕了他們。而詩，旁觀著，拒絕移開目光。

Chapter 8 · 第八章

The Midnight Massacre

午夜屠殺

The war ground on for eighteen days, and by its end nearly everyone was dead. Karna — Arjuna's secret half-brother, born of their mother before her marriage, raised in shame, fighting unknowingly against his own kin — fell to Arjuna's arrow after his chariot wheel stuck in the mud and Krishna told Arjuna to strike him defenseless. (Karna's death is, many readers feel, the moral nadir of the Pandava cause: a great and noble soul, killed at his most vulnerable, because Krishna judged that fair play could not be afforded against a warrior who had sided with adharma.) Duryodhana, the last of the hundred brothers, fled the field and hid in a lake, and was drawn out and fought Bhima in a mace-duel; Bhima, forbidden to strike below the waist, broke the rule and shattered Duryodhana's thighs, leaving him to die slowly.

那場戰爭磨了十八天，到了盡頭，幾乎所有人都死了。迦爾納——阿周那同母異父的私生兄長，生於母親婚前，在屈辱中長大，渾然不覺地與自己的親族作戰——在他的戰車輪子陷入泥中、毫無防備之際，倒於阿周那之箭下。（迦爾納之死，許多讀者覺得，是般度家事業的道德最低點：一個偉大而高貴的靈魂，在他最脆弱之時被殺，因為黑天判斷，面對一個站在非法一方的武士，公平是不可負擔的。）難敵，那一百個兄弟中最後的一個，逃離戰場，藏身於一個湖中，被引出來與怖軍以鎚搏鬥；怖軍，被禁止攻擊腰以下，違反了規則，擊碎了難敵的大腿，任他慢慢死去。

And then came the worst night in the epic. Drona's son Ashwatthama, maddened by grief and rage at his father's dishonorable killing, crept into the Pandava camp at midnight with three companions. There he found the five sons of Draupadi — the boys the Pandavas had fought the whole war to protect — sleeping in the belief that the war was won. And he butchered them in their sleep. All five. And when Draupadi woke to find her sons dead, her grief was such that the poem says even the gods in heaven wept. The Pandavas hunted Ashwatthama down; his death-sacrificial gem was torn from his forehead; he was cursed to wander the earth, wounded and immortal, for three thousand years. But the sons were dead. The war had been won. And the victory was ashes.

然後來了史詩中最糟的一夜。德羅納之子馬勇，因父親被不光彩地殺害而悲憤發狂，帶著三個同伴在午夜潛入般度家的營地。在那裡他發現了黑公主的五個兒子——那些般度家打了整場戰爭去保護的孩子——在「戰爭已勝」的信念中熟睡。他在睡夢中屠殺了他們。五個全殺了。而當黑公主醒來發現她的兒子們都死了，她的悲慟如此之深，詩中說連天上的諸神都為之泣。般度家追獵馬勇；他額上那顆獻死之寶被扯下；他被咒在世間流浪三千年，帶傷而長生。但兒子們死了。戰爭贏了。而那勝利，是灰燼。

The Mahabharata ends its war with a line that is its whole moral: Yudhishthira, the victor, goes to heaven, and there sees Duryodhana, the villain, enthroned and at peace — and is told that Duryodhana, having died a warrior's death on the field of dharma, has earned his place. The Pandava brothers, meanwhile, must pass through hell first. The poem is not being perverse. It is saying the thing no war story wants to say: that in a war between kin, no one wins. The righteous are stained by the means they used; the wicked are cleansed by the death they accepted. Victory and defeat have changed places. And Yudhishthira, the truthful king who won the war, looks at his ruin and asks, quietly, whether the whole thing was worth it. The epic never gives him an answer. It leaves the question with us.

《摩訶婆羅多》以一句詩為它的戰爭收尾，而那一句便是它全部的道德：勝利者堅戰上了天界，在那裡看見反派難敵，端坐於寶座之上，安寧——並被告知，難敵既在正法之野以戰士之身而死，已掙得他的位置。與此同時，般度五兄弟卻必須先經過地獄。詩並非在故弄玄虛。它在說每一個戰爭故事都不願說的事：在一場親族之間的戰爭裡，沒有人贏。義者被他們所用的手段玷污；惡者被他們所接受的死亡洗淨。勝利與失敗換了位置。而堅戰，那贏了戰爭的、崇奉真實的君王，望著他的廢墟，輕聲問，這一切究竟值不值得。史詩從未給他答案。它把那個問題，留給了我們。

VICTORY IS DEFEAT

勝利，即失敗

The god taught the warrior to act without attachment, and the warrior acted, and the war was won — and the winner's reward was a kingdom of corpses and a wife holding five dead sons. The villain sits in heaven. The hero must walk through hell. The Mahabharata has given the world its most famous teaching on duty, and its most unsparing portrait of what duty costs. There are no victors here. There are only people who did what they thought they had to, and who will never recover from having done it.
