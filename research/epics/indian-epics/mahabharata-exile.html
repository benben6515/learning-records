THE DICE & THE EXILE

骰　子　與　流　放

VYASA · THE MAHABHARATA · the edge of Kali Yuga

The Ramayana was a war between a man and a demon. The Mahabharata is a war between cousins — and that is why it is the darker, greater book. Here there are no clean lines of good and evil, only a family rotting from envy, a game of loaded dice, a woman dragged by her hair into a hall of men, and a question she asks that no one in three thousand years has been able to answer. 《羅摩衍那》是一個人與一個魔之間的戰爭。《摩訶婆羅多》是表兄弟之間的戰爭——而這便是它更黑暗、也更偉大的原因。這裡沒有善惡分明的界線，只有一個因嫉妒而腐爛的家族、一場作弊的骰子戲、一個被頭髮拖進男人議事廳的女子，以及她提出的一個問題——三千年來無人能答。

Chapter 1 · 第一章

The Seeds of the Feud

宿怨之種

The Mahabharata is the story of a single family — the Kurus — that has split into two branches who hate each other. On one side are the five Pandava brothers, sons of Pandu: Yudhishthira the truthful, Bhima the mighty, Arjuna the greatest archer of the age, and the twins Nakula and Sahadeva. On the other side are the hundred sons of Dhritarashtra, the blind king, led by Duryodhana. The two branches are cousins, raised under one roof, and their rivalry — begun in childhood over a ball game — will end in a war that kills nearly everyone either of them loves.

《摩訶婆羅多》是一個家族——俱盧族——分裂為兩支彼此相恨的支脈的故事。一方是般度之子般度五兄弟：崇奉真實的堅戰、力大無窮的怖軍、當世第一神射手阿周那，以及雙生子無種與偕天。另一方是瞎眼君王持國的一百個兒子，由難敵率領。兩支是表兄弟，在同一個屋簷下長大，而他們的對抗——始於孩童時的一場球戲——將終於一場殺死他們所愛幾近所有人的戰爭。

Before them, governing this combustible family, stands the figure of Bhishma — the grand-uncle, the patriarch, the greatest warrior of the previous generation — and his story is the moral foundation of the whole epic. As a young man, Bhishma had wanted his father to marry a fisher-girl he loved; the girl's father consented only on one condition: that the son born of the union, not Bhishma, would inherit the throne. And Bhishma, to give his father his happiness, took a terrible vow — the vow of lifelong celibacy, renouncing the throne and all his descendants' claim to it, forever. He swore it, and the gods rained flowers, and he was given the boon of choosing the hour of his own death. But his vow, the epic shows us again and again, was the original wound. By surrendering the throne, he set in motion the very succession crisis that would tear the family apart.

在他們之上，治理著這個火藥般的家族的，是毗濕摩這個人物——曾祖輩、家長、上一代最偉大的武士——而他的故事，是整部史詩的道德根基。年輕之時，毗濕摩想讓他的父親娶一個他所愛的漁家女；女方的父親只在一個條件下才答應：由這段結合所生的兒子，而非毗濕摩，將繼承王座。而毗濕摩，為了給父親他的幸福，立下了一個可怕的誓——終身獨身的誓，永遠放棄王座及其所有後代的繼承權。他發了誓，諸神降下花雨，他被賜予自行選擇死亡時辰的恩賜。但他的誓，史詩一再向我們展示，是最初的傷口。交出王座，他啟動了那場將撕裂整個家族的、關於繼承的危機本身。

So when Pandu died young and the question of the succession arose, the throne was, by Bhishma's vow, ambiguous — and the blind king Dhritarashtra, ruling as regent, could not bring himself to deny it to his own son Duryodhana, even though the eldest Pandava, Yudhishthira, was the rightful heir. To avoid bloodshed, the kingdom was split: the Pandavas were given a half, and built for themselves the magnificent city of Indraprastha. And there, in their splendor, they made the mistake that would cost them everything: they let Duryodhana see how beautiful their kingdom was. And envy, in the Mahabharata, is the one sin from which no recovery is possible.

於是當般度早逝、繼承之問浮現之時，王座，因毗濕摩的誓，是含糊的——而作為攝政統治的瞎眼君王持國，無法狠心將它從自己的兒子難敵身上剝奪，即便般度家的長子堅戰才是合法的繼承人。為了避免流血，王國被瓜分：般度家得到了一半，為自己建造了輝煌的天帝城。而在那裡，在他們的富麗之中，他們犯下了將使他們失去一切的錯：他們讓難敵看見了他們的王國有多美。而在《摩訶婆羅多》裡，嫉妒，是那一樁無可挽回之罪。

Chapter 2 · 第二章

The Hall of Illusions

幻象之殿

The Pandavas, at the height of their power, performed the Rajasuya — the great imperial sacrifice that proclaimed Yudhishthira emperor over all the kings of the earth. And they invited, among the guests, their cousin Duryodhana — who walked through the magic palace the Pandavas had built, a hall of illusions designed by the architect Maya, where pools of water looked like crystal floors and crystal floors looked like pools. And Duryodhana, the great prince, humiliated himself again and again: he lifted his robes to step into what he thought was water, and found it solid floor; he walked confidently onto what he thought was floor, and fell into a pool. The whole court, watching, laughed.

般度家在權力之巔，舉行了王祭——那場宣告堅戰為天下諸王之皇帝的盛大祭祀。他們在賓客中，也邀請了表兄難敵——他走過般度家所建的那座由建築師摩耶設計的幻術之宮，一座幻象之殿，那裡的水池看來如水晶地板，水晶地板看來如水池。而那位大王子難敵，一次又一次地使自己蒙羞：他提起衣袍要以為是水的地方踏去，發現那是堅實的地板；他自信滿滿地踏向以為是地板之處，卻跌入一池水中。觀看的整個朝廷，笑了。

And then Draupadi — the Pandavas' wife, whom they all five share by a strange twist of fate — laughed too. Or the tradition says she laughed; some versions say she only smiled, or that the laughter came from elsewhere. But the wound was the same. Duryodhana, already choking on envy, heard a woman's laughter in the moment of his humiliation, and something broke in him that would never mend. He went back to Hastinapura and would not eat or speak or rise from his bed, burning with a shame that curdled, day by day, into a plan to destroy his cousins utterly.

然後黑公主——般度五兄弟共同的妻子，因命運的奇異轉折而五人共之——也笑了。或者傳統說她笑了；有些版本說她只是微笑，或那笑聲來自別處。但那道傷是一樣的。難敵，本已在嫉妒中窒息，在他受辱之際聽見了一個女子的笑聲，於是他體內有什麼崩斷了，再也無法癒合。他回到象城，不吃、不語、臥床不起，焚燒於一種日復一日凝結成「徹底毀滅他表兄弟」之計畫的羞辱之中。

The Mahabharata is unsparing about this moment, and about what it means. A great man's pride, wounded by a laugh, becomes the engine of a war that will kill millions. There is no single villainous decision here — no demon whispering in an ear. There is only the ordinary, terrible arithmetic by which envy, once planted in a proud heart, grows until it consumes everything. The poem is telling us, with terrifying calm, that the great catastrophe of the Mahabharata began not with a sin of action but with a sin of feeling — and that the feeling was, at bottom, the most human one of all: the unbearable pain of being laughed at by someone you envy.

《摩訶婆羅多》對這一刻，以及對它的意義，毫不留情。一個偉大之人的自尊，被一聲笑所傷，成了那場將殺死數百萬人的戰爭的引擎。這裡沒有單一的反派決定——沒有魔在耳邊低語。只有那平凡的、可怖的算術，藉由它，嫉妒一旦種入一顆驕傲的心，便會長到吞噬一切。詩以可怖的平靜告訴我們：《摩訶婆羅多》的大災難，不是始於一行動之罪，而是始於一感覺之罪——而那感覺，歸根究柢，是最人性的那一種：被你所嫉妒之人嘲笑時那不可承受之痛。

Chapter 3 · 第三章

The Loaded Dice

作弊的骰子

Duryodhana's plan was simple and devastating. He knew Yudhishthira's one weakness — the eldest Pandava was addicted to gambling, though he was no good at it. So Duryodhana, through his father the blind king, invited Yudhishthira to Hastinapura for a friendly game of dice. And he arranged that his uncle Shakuni — a master of crooked dice, whose very dice were enchanted and would roll whatever Shakuni willed — would play in his stead. Yudhishthira, bound by the code of a king's hospitality, could not refuse. He sat down. And he began to lose.

難敵的計畫簡單而毀滅性十足。他知道堅戰唯一的弱點——這位般度家長子沉迷於博弈，卻並不擅長。於是難敵透過他的父親、瞎眼君王，邀請堅戰到象城來一場友好的骰子戲。他安排他的舅舅沙恭尼——一個作弊骰子的大師，其骰子本身便是施了法的，會擲出沙恭尼所要的任何點數——代他而擲。堅戰，受君王待客之禮所縛，無法拒絕。他坐下。而他開始輸。

The escalation of the game is one of the most chilling sequences in the epic, because each loss should have been a stopping-point, and none of them was. Yudhishthira lost his jewels, his chariots, his horses, his slaves, his army — and kept playing. He lost his brothers' wealth, his brothers themselves, one by one — Bhima, Arjuna, the twins, all staked and lost — and kept playing. And then, with nothing left of his own, he staked the one thing that was not entirely his to stake: he staked Draupadi, the common wife of all five brothers, the queen of Indraprastha. And Shakuni's crooked dice rolled, and Draupadi was lost. The hall fell silent. A woman had been wagered like a horse, and lost.

那場博弈的升級，是史詩中最令人不寒而慄的段落之一，因為每一次的輸，本該是一個停止點，卻沒有任何一次停下來。堅戰輸了他的珠寶、他的戰車、他的馬、他的奴僕、他的軍隊——繼續賭。他輸了兄弟們的財富，輸了兄弟們本身，一個接一個——怖軍、阿周那、雙生子，盡數押上、盡數輸掉——繼續賭。然後，自己的已一無所有，他押上了那件不完全是他的、可以拿來押的東西：他押上了黑公主——五兄弟共同的妻子、天帝城的王后。而沙恭尼的作弊骰子滾動，黑公主輸了。議事廳陷入沉寂。一個女子，如同一匹馬般被押注，並且輸掉了。

The Mahabharata gives us, in Yudhishthira, the most disturbing portrait of compulsion in ancient literature. He knows he is losing. He knows the dice are crooked. He knows he is destroying everything he loves. And he cannot stop — because the code says a king does not refuse a challenge, and because the gambler's sickness has him by the throat. The poem is not interested in condemning him. It is interested in showing us, with clinical clarity, how a good man, through a single weakness and a single rigid rule, hands everything over to his enemies. By the time the dice stop, the war is already inevitable. Everything that follows — the exile, the Gita, the blood — is just the long, slow playing-out of what this game set in motion.

《摩訶婆羅多》透過堅戰，給了我們古代文學中最令人不安的「強迫」之像。他知道他在輸。他知道骰子是作弊的。他知道他正在毀掉他所愛的一切。而他停不下來——因為那套法則說一位君王不拒絕挑戰，因為賭徒之疾已掐住了他的喉嚨。詩無意譴責他。它有意以臨床般的清晰，向我們展示：一個好人，如何透過一個弱點和一條死板的規則，將一切交到敵人手中。當骰子停下之時，戰爭便已無可避免。之後的一切——流放、薄伽梵歌、那血——不過是這場博弈所啟動之物、那漫長而緩慢的展開。

Chapter 4 · 第四章

Draupadi's Question

黑公主的問題

What followed is the moral center of the Mahabharata's first half, and one of the most famous scenes in all of world literature. Duryodhana, drunk on his victory, ordered that Draupadi be dragged into the hall of men — a queen, in her period, in a single garment, dragged by her hair by the servant Dushasana — and there, before all the elders, he commanded that she be disrobed. The hall sat frozen. Bhishma sat silent. Drona sat silent. The blind king Dhritarashtra sat silent. None of the great men, the patriarchs, the guardians of dharma, could bring themselves to speak — because the law was genuinely ambiguous: had Yudhishthira the right to stake her, when he had already lost himself?

隨後所發生之事，是《摩訶婆羅多》前半的道德核心，也是世界文學中最著名的場面之一。難敵，沉醉於勝利，下令將黑公主拖入男人議事廳——一位王后，正值月事，僅著單衣，被僕人難降拽著頭髮拖來——在那裡，在所有長輩面前，他下令將她剝衣。議事廳僵坐著。毗濕默不作聲。德羅納默不作聲。瞎眼君王持國默不作聲。那些偉大之人、那些家長、那些正法的守護者，沒有一個能開口——因為律法是真正含糊的：堅戰在已輸掉自己之後，還有權押她嗎？

And Draupadi, standing in that hall, asked the question that has echoed for three thousand years. She did not beg. She did not weep. She asked, with the precision of a philosopher: "Whom did my husband lose first — himself, or me? For if he lost himself first, he had nothing left with which to stake me. And if so, by what right am I here?" No one could answer. The wisest men in the world, gathered in that hall, looked at the floor. The silence is the point. The Mahabharata is telling us that dharma, in its hardest cases, does not yield a clean answer — and that the great men who should have defended it failed, because they preferred ambiguity to risk. Draupadi's question hangs over the entire epic, and over every society that has ever failed a woman in the name of the law's uncertainty.

而黑公主，立於那廳中，問出了那個迴響了三千年的問題。她不求饒。她不哭泣。她以一個哲學家的精準問道：「我丈夫先輸的是什麼——他自己，還是我？因為他若先輸了自己，他便已一無所有，拿什麼來押我？若如此，我憑什麼在這裡？」無人能答。世上最有智慧之人，齊聚在那廳中，望著地面。那沉默，正是重點。《摩訶婆羅多》在告訴我們：正法，在其最難的案子裡，不給出乾淨的答案——而那些本該護衛它的偉人，失敗了，因為他們寧要含糊，不願冒險。黑公主的問題，籠罩著整部史詩，也籠罩著每一個曾以「律法未明」之名而辜負了一個女子的社會。

Then Dushasana seized her sari and began to pull — and the Mahabharata gives us its first open miracle. As he pulled, the cloth did not end. Yard after yard of fabric poured from somewhere beyond the world, an endless sari, while Draupadi prayed to Krishna (who was, far away, aware of her), and Dushasana pulled and pulled until he collapsed exhausted in a mountain of cloth, and the hall was draped in red, and Draupadi stood covered. And Bhima, in a voice that shook the rafters, swore the vow that would seal the war: he would tear open Dushasana's chest and drink his blood. The dice-game was over. The thirteen-year exile was about to begin. And the war, now unavoidable, would be fought to answer the question no one in this hall had dared to.

然後難降抓住她的紗麗開始扯——而《摩訶婆羅多》給了我們它第一個公開的奇蹟。當他扯時，布沒有盡頭。一碼接一碼的織物，從世界之外的某處湧出，一條無盡的紗麗，而黑公主向黑天祈禱（他雖遠方，卻知曉她），難降扯了又扯，直到他精疲力竭地倒在一座布山之中，整座廳披掛著紅色，而黑公主立著，被遮蔽。而怖軍，以一個震動屋樑的聲音，立下了那將封定戰爭之誓：他要撕裂難降的胸膛，飲他的血。骰子戲結束了。十三年的流放即將開始。而那場如今已無可避免的戰爭，將為這座廳中無人敢答的那個問題而戰。

Chapter 5 · 第五章

The Thirteen-Year Exile

十三年流放

The blind king Dhritarashtra, shaken out of his paralysis by the horror of what had nearly happened, intervened at last and returned everything to the Pandavas — only to be talked around again by Duryodhana, and a second dice-game was played, with a single stake: the loser would go into exile for twelve years, and live a thirteenth year in disguise, and if recognized in that thirteenth year, the whole exile would begin again. Yudhishthira lost again. And the Pandavas — the five brothers and their shared wife — stripped off their royal robes and walked into the forest, beginning the long years that would harden them into the weapons they would need to be.

瞎眼君王持國，被那幾乎發生之事的可怖從癱瘓中震醒，終於介入，將一切都還給般度家——卻又被難敵說動，於是又賭了一局，只有一個賭注：輸者流放十二年，再喬裝隱居第十三年，若在那第十三年被認出，整個流放便從頭再來。堅戰又輸了。於是般度家——五兄弟與他們共同的妻子——脫下王袍，走入山林，開始那漫長的、將他們鍛成他們所必須成為之武器的歲月。

The forest years are where the Mahabharata becomes strangest and richest. Arjuna, the archer, journeyed into the Himalayas to obtain divine weapons — and there he fought Shiva himself in disguise, was recognized, and was given the Pashupata, the world-destroying weapon. He was taken up to Indra's heaven, where he learned the dance of the celestial nymphs and lived among the gods. Meanwhile, in the forest, the brothers starved and were tested: a yaksha, a spirit, seized a water-pool and demanded that each brother answer a philosophical question before drinking; four drank without answering and fell dead, and only Yudhishthira — patient, truthful — answered correctly and revived them all. The forest was a school. By the end of it, the Pandavas had been remade.

山林歲月是《摩訶婆羅多》變得最奇異、也最豐富之處。神射手阿周那，深入喜馬拉雅以取得神聖兵器——在那裡他與喬裝的濕婆搏鬥，被認出，獲賜濕婆法器 Pashupata，那足以毀滅世界之器。他被帶上因陀羅的天界，在那裡學習天女的舞蹈，生活在諸神之中。與此同時，在山林裡，兄弟們挨餓、受試：一個夜叉，一個精靈，佔據了一處水池，要求每一個兄弟在飲水之前回答一個哲學之問；四個人不答便飲，倒地而死，唯有堅戰——耐心、崇奉真實——答對了，使他們全部復生。山林是一個學校。到了盡頭，般度家已被重新鍛造。

And in the thirteenth year, they disguised themselves — Yudhishthira as a dice-master in the court of King Virata, Bhima as a cook, Arjuna (hiding his warrior's body) as a dance-teacher named Brihannala, the twins as stable-hands, and Draupadi as a hairdresser. They served in hiding, endured insults, and at the year's end — when the king's enemies attacked and only Arjuna, revealed, could save the kingdom — they emerged, unrecognized for the full thirteenth year, their exile complete. The contract was fulfilled. They had done everything that had been asked. And now they asked, in return, only for what was theirs: their half of the kingdom.

而在第十三年，他們喬裝——堅戰化作維羅吒王朝廷中的骰子師，怖軍化作廚子，阿周那（藏起他的武士之身）化作一個名叫大河女的舞蹈教師，雙生子化作馬伕，黑公主化作理髮女。他們在隱蔽中服役，忍受侮辱，而在那年盡頭——當國王的敵人來攻、唯有現身的阿周那能救王國之時——他們出現了，在整個第十三年裡未被認出，流放完成。契約已被履行。他們做到了所要求的一切。而如今他們作為回報，只求那本屬於他們之物：他們的那一半王國。

Chapter 6 · 第六章

The Embassy of Krishna

黑天的出使

The exile was over. The Pandavas had honored every term. And now, as kings, they sent emissaries to Hastinapura asking for the return of their kingdom. The elders of the Kuru court — Bhishma, Drona, the wise Vidura — all urged Duryodhana to make peace. Even the gods were uneasy. And so the Pandavas' cousin and friend Krishna — who was, the reader has by now understood, no mere mortal but the eighth avatar of Vishnu — offered to go himself, as a final embassy, to plead for peace.

流放結束了。般度家遵守了每一項條款。而如今，作為君王，他們派出使者到象城，要求歸還他們的王國。俱盧朝廷的長輩們——毗濕摩、德羅納、睿智的維杜羅——都力勸難敵議和。連諸神都感到不安。於是般度家的表兄兼摯友黑天——他，讀者至此已明白，不僅是凡人，更是毗濕奴的第八化身——主動提出親自前往，作為最後一次出使，為和平懇求。

Krishna came into Duryodhana's court and spoke with a restraint that makes the scene almost unbearable to read. He did not threaten. He did not boast. He asked for almost nothing — only five villages, one for each brother, out of the vast empire that was rightfully theirs. "Give them five villages," Krishna said, "and there will be peace. Refuse, and there will be a war that no one in this hall survives." And the elders agreed, and wept, and begged Duryodhana to accept. And Duryodhana, with the calm of a man whose envy has long since eaten his reason, refused. He would not give them, he said, so much as a needle-point of land without a fight. And he ordered his men to seize Krishna — an act of sacrilege so great that the god, for an instant, revealed his cosmic form in the court, blinding everyone, before vanishing.

黑天走進難敵的朝廷，以一種使這場面讀來幾乎無法承受的節制發言。他不威脅。他不誇口。他幾乎什麼也沒要——只要五座村莊，五位兄弟各一，出自那本就屬於他們的龐大帝國。「給他們五座村莊，」黑天說，「便有和平。拒絕，便有一場這廳中無人能生還的戰爭。」長輩們同意，流淚，懇求難敵接受。而難敵，帶著一個嫉妒早已吞噬其理性之人的平靜，拒絕了。他說，他連一個針尖大的土地，也不會不戰而給。並下令他的人捉拿黑天——一個如此褻瀆之舉，以至於那位神，剎那間在朝廷中顯露了祂的宇宙之相，令所有人目盲，旋即消失。

The embassy had failed. The war was now certain. And the Mahabharata pauses here, in this broken court, to let us feel the full weight of what is about to happen — because the poem does not want us to think the war was inevitable from the start. It wants us to know that peace was offered, humbly, by God himself, and refused. Duryodhana chose this. The blind king, his father, let him choose it. And now the drums would sound, and the armies would gather on the plain of Kurukshetra, and the greatest charioteer who ever lived would turn to the greatest archer who ever lived, and ask him why he had thrown down his bow.

出使失敗了。戰爭如今已定。《摩訶婆羅多》在此暫停，在這個破碎的朝廷裡，讓我們感到即將發生之事的全部重量——因為詩不願我們認為戰爭從一開始便無可避免。它要我們知道：和平曾被謙卑地獻上，由神親自獻上，而被拒絕了。難敵選擇了這個。瞎眼君王、他的父親，任他選擇了這個。而如今戰鼓將響，軍隊將聚集於俱盧之野的平原上，而那有史以來最偉大的御者，將轉向那有史以來最偉大的射手，問他為何擲下了他的弓。

THE PEACE THAT WAS REFUSED

被拒絕的和平

The dice were crooked, the queen was dragged by her hair, and her question went unanswered in a hall of silent elders. The exile was endured. The terms were honored. And when God himself came asking for nothing but five villages, he was refused — and the war became the only answer left. Two armies will gather now on the plain of Kurukshetra. And between them, in a single chariot, a warrior will throw down his bow and weep, while his charioteer begins the most famous speech in human history.
