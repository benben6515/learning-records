THE EPICS IN THE WORLD

史　詩　在　世　間

RAMLILA · TULSIDAS · SAGAR'S RAMAYAN · THE STILL-UNFOLDING ARGUMENT

These are not books on a shelf. They are sung in temples, danced in villages, burned in effigy every autumn, broadcast into a hundred million homes, reimagined by novelists and playwrights and filmmakers. A civilization that has never stopped telling itself who it is — through these two stories, again and again, for three thousand years. This is the final stage: not the end of the epics, but the proof that they have no end. 這些不是架上的書。它們在廟宇裡被傳唱、在村莊裡被起舞、每年秋天被燒成紙紮、被播送進一億個家庭、被小說家、劇作家與電影人重新想像。一個從未停止訴說自己是誰的文明——透過這兩個故事，一次又一次，三千年。這是最後一階：不是史詩的終結，而是它們沒有終結的證明。

Chapter 1 · 第一章

The Book That Contains Everything

無所不包之書

The Mahabharata begins, famously, not with its own story but with a frame — and that frame is the key to everything the book became. A bard named Ugrashrava arrives at a forest hermitage where a sacrifice is being performed, and there he is asked to tell the great tale. He agrees, and begins — and the tale he tells is itself a story of a tale being told: at the snake-sacrifice of King Janamejaya (who is, it turns out, the great-grandson of the very Arjuna whose war is being narrated), the sage Vaisampayana recites the Mahabharata to the king, having received it from its author Vyasa. Stories within stories within stories. The book is a set of Russian dolls, each mouth opening to reveal another mouth speaking.

《摩訶婆羅多》著名地，不是以它自己的故事開始，而是以一個框架開始——而那個框架，是它所成為的一切的鑰匙。一位名叫優婆沙羅婆的吟遊詩人來到一座正在舉行祭祀的森林道院，在那裡他被請求講述那個偉大的故事。他答應了，開始講——而他所講的故事，本身就是一個「故事被講述」的故事：在詹暖迦王的蛇祭之上（而這位王，原來正是那場戰爭被敘述的對象阿周那的曾孫），仙人護民子向君王誦出了《摩訶婆羅多》，他是從其作者廣博那裡領受的。故事套著故事套著故事。這本書是一組俄羅斯娃娃，每一張嘴張開，露出另一張正在說話的嘴。

This nesting is not a quirk; it is a theology. The Mahabharata claims for itself a scope so vast that no single teller could contain it — so it must be told by many mouths, each one a link in a chain stretching back to the original. And it makes, at its opening, the most famous claim any book has ever made: "What is found here may be found elsewhere; but what is not found here cannot be found anywhere else in the world." The book is asserting, with breathtaking confidence, that it contains all of human life — every situation, every dilemma, every emotion, every question a person could possibly face. To read it fully is to read everything. And three thousand years of readers have, by and large, agreed.

這種嵌套不是一個怪癖；它是一種神學。《摩訶婆羅多》為自己宣稱了一個如此浩瀚的範圍，以至於沒有任何單一的講述者能容納它——所以它必須被許多張嘴講述，每一張都是一條伸回源頭的鏈的一環。而它在開篇之處，做了一本書所能做過的最著名的宣稱：「此處所有者，或可於他處尋得；此處所無者，則任何他處皆不可尋。」這本書以令人屏息的自信斷言，它包含了全部的人類生活——每一種處境、每一個兩難、每一種情感、每一個人所可能面對的問題。完整地讀它，便是讀盡一切。而三千年來的讀者，大體上，都同意。

The Ramayana, by contrast, is a finished gem — a single voice, Valmiki's, telling a single story with the unity of a great novel. But both books share the quality that made them immortal: they are not merely stories. They are arguments — about dharma, about kingship, about marriage, about war, about what a person owes to their family and what they owe to their conscience. Every retelling in the centuries to come will not just repeat them; it will take a side in their arguments. That is why they live. A story that is only a story can be forgotten. A story that is an argument can never be settled — and so can never die.

《羅摩衍那》則相反，是一顆完成的寶石——單一的聲音，蟻垤的，以一部偉大小說的統一性講述一個單一的故事。但兩本書共享著使它們不朽的特質：它們不僅僅是故事。它們是爭辯——關於正法、關於王權、關於婚姻、關於戰爭、關於一個人對其家庭所欠之物與其對良心所欠之物。接下來幾個世紀的每一次再述，都不只是重複它們；而是會在其爭辯中選邊站。這便是它們活著的原因。一個只是故事的故事可以被遺忘。一個是爭辯的故事永遠無法被定論——於是永遠無法死去。

Chapter 2 · 第二章

The Bhakti Revolution

虔信的革命

For a thousand years after they were composed, the epics belonged largely to the learned — to brahmins who read Sanskrit, to kings who patronized the recitations, to scholars who argued the fine points of dharma. And then, beginning around the 6th century of the common era and cresting again and again for a thousand years, a revolution swept the subcontinent: the bhakti movement. Its central claim was radical and democratic: that God is reached not through ritual, not through birth, not through learning, but through bhakti — pure devotional love — and that this love is available to everyone, the untouchable no less than the brahmin, the woman no less than the man.

在成文之後的一千年裡，史詩主要屬於有學問之人——屬於讀梵文的婆羅門、屬於資助誦詠的君王、屬於爭辯正法細節的學者。然後，大約從公元六世紀開始，並在一千年裡一次又一次地達到高峰，一場革命掃過次大陸：虔信運動。它的核心主張是激進而民主的：通往神之路，不經由儀式，不經由出身，不經由學問，而是經由 bhakti——純粹的虔信之愛——而這份愛，人人可得，賤民不亞於婆羅門，女子不亞於男子。

And the bhakti saints needed stories to sing — and they found them in the epics. The poet-saint Tulsidas, in 16th-century Varanasi, retold the Ramayana not in Sanskrit but in Awadhi, the language of the common people, and called it the Ramcharitmanas, the Lake of the Deeds of Rama. It became, and remains, one of the most beloved books in North India — read in every household, chanted at every wedding, treated not as literature but as a living presence. Tulsidas's Rama is not Valmiki's austere prince; he is a tender, accessible, deeply personal god, the beloved of the devotee's heart. The Krishna cult, meanwhile, found its voice in the Gita Govinda of Jayadeva — a 12th-century poem of the love of Krishna and Radha that turned the god of the battlefield into the god of the dance, of the forest, of erotic longing.

而虔信的聖者們需要故事來傳唱——而他們在史詩裡找到了。詩聖杜勒西達斯，十六世紀的瓦拉納西人，不以梵文、而以平民的語言阿瓦第語，重述了《羅摩衍那》，稱之為《羅摩功行湖》。它成為、並至今仍是北印度最受鍾愛的書之一——家家戶戶讀，每一場婚禮誦，不被當作文學，而被當作一個活的臨在。杜勒西達斯的羅摩，不是蟻垤那個嚴峻的王子；他是一個溫柔、可親、深深人格化的神，是信徒心中的至愛。與此同時，黑天之崇拜，在十二世紀勝天的《牧童歌》中找到了它的聲音——一首關於黑天與羅陀之愛的詩，將戰場之神轉化為舞蹈之神、森林之神、情欲渴慕之神。

This is the great gift of the bhakti movement to the epics: it took them out of the academy and gave them to the people. And in doing so, it multiplied them. For every bhakti poet who sang Rama or Krishna added their own reading, their own emphasis, their own theological slant. The epics became not a single text but a vast, branching family of texts and songs and images — one root, ten thousand flowers. A village woman sweeping her courtyard while humming a Tulsidas couplet is, in that moment, as much a participant in the epic as any Sanskrit pundit. The bhakti revolution made the Ramayana and the Mahabharata into the common property of an entire civilization. They have stayed that way ever since.

這是虔信運動給予史詩的大禮：它將它們從學院裡取出，交給了人民。而如此一來，它使它們倍增。因為每一個傳唱羅摩或黑天的虔信詩人，都加上了自己的解讀、自己的強調、自己的神學傾向。史詩成了不只是一個文本，而是一個龐大的、分支的、由文本、歌謠與圖像構成的家族——一根，萬花。一個村莊婦女，一邊哼著杜勒西達斯的對句、一邊打掃她的庭院，在那個時刻，她與任何梵文學者一樣，是史詩的參與者。虔信革命使《羅摩衍那》與《摩訶婆羅多》成為一整個文明的共同財產。自此，它們便一直是如此。

Chapter 3 · 第三章

Ramlila & Dussehra

羅摩里拉與十勝節

Every autumn, across North India, something happens that has no real parallel in world culture: the Ramlila. For ten or more consecutive nights, an entire town gathers to watch a performance of the Ramayana — not in a theatre, but in the open air, processional, moving from location to location as the story moves. The boy who plays Rama is not an actor; he is chosen, often as a child, and treated for the duration of the performance as the god himself — fed, worshipped, bowed to. The audience does not watch the Ramlila so much as inhabit it; they are not spectators but participants in a sacred event that re-enacts, every year, the descent of Vishnu and the defeat of Ravana.

每年秋天，整個北印度，會發生一件在世界文化中少有真正對應之事：羅摩里拉。連續十個或更多的夜晚，一整個城鎮聚集起來觀看《羅摩衍那》的演出——不是在劇場裡，而是在露天，遊行式的，隨著故事的推進從一個地點移到另一個地點。扮演羅摩的男孩不是演員；他是被選出的，通常還是個孩子，在演出期間被當作神本身——被餵食、被崇拜、被鞠躬。觀眾與其說是觀看羅摩里拉，不如說是棲居其中；他們不是觀眾，而是一場神聖事件的參與者，這場事件每年重新演出毗濕奴的降凡與羅波那的敗亡。

And on the final night — the festival of Dussehra, or Vijayadashami — the climax is the burning. Towering effigies of Ravana, his brother Kumbhakarna, and his son Indrajit, built of bamboo and paper and stuffed with firecrackers, are set alight in the central square, and the crowd roars as the ten-headed demon goes up in flames. It is a ritual re-enactment of the victory of good over evil, light over dark, dharma over adharma — and it is also, the tradition knows, a yearly renewal. Ravana must be burned every year because evil, like the Hydra, grows back. The burning is not a memory of a victory long ago. It is a present-tense act. The demon is being defeated again, right now, by us, together.

而在最後一夜——十勝節，或勝利第十日——高潮是焚燒。高聳的羅波那、其弟恭婆羯哩娑那、其子因陀羅耆的紙紮巨像，以竹與紙製成、塞滿鞭炮，在中央廣場被點燃，而當十首之魔在烈焰中竄起，人群轟然歡呼。這是一場善勝惡、光勝暗、正法勝非法的儀式重演——而傳統也明白，它也是一年一度的更新。羅波那必須每年被燒，因為邪惡如九頭蛇，會長回來。那焚燒不是對很久以前一場勝利的記憶。它是一個現在式的行動。那魔，正被再次擊敗，此刻，由我們，一同。

There is no Western equivalent, and standing in the crowd at one is a strange, overwhelming experience. You are not watching a play about the past. You are inside a story that the community has decided is always happening. The boundary between audience and actor, between sacred and secular, between myth and history, dissolves. For the duration of the Ramlila, the Ramayana is not a story that was told. It is the story that is being told, by everyone, all at once, in the living present. This is what it means for an epic to be alive. It does not sit on a shelf. It walks through the town once a year, and the town walks with it.

這在西方沒有對等之物，而置身於這樣的人群中，是一種奇異而壓倒性的體驗。你不是在看一齣關於過去的戲。你在一個社群已決定「正在發生」的故事之內。觀眾與演員、神聖與世俗、神話與歷史之間的界線，消融了。在羅摩里拉進行的時段裡，《羅摩衍那》不是一個「曾被講述」的故事。它是「正在被講述」的故事，由每一個人，同時，在活的當下。這便是一部史詩「活著」的意思。它不坐在架上。它一年一次走過這個城鎮，而這個城鎮與它同行。

Chapter 4 · 第四章

The Modern Retellings

現代的再述

In 1987, something extraordinary happened to India. A television series began — Ramanand Sagar's Ramayan, broadcast on Sunday mornings on the state channel. And the country stopped. Streets emptied during the broadcast; buses were held; businesses closed; the whole nation sat down in front of whatever television set was nearest, and watched, together, the story they already knew by heart, performed by actors in costumes, and wept as if it were happening for the first time. Old women placed garlands around their television sets. When the actor playing Rama appeared, viewers touched the screen as if touching a deity. The series ran for seventy-eight episodes and was, by some measures, the most-watched television event in human history relative to population. The Ramayana, three thousand years old, had found a new medium — and conquered it instantly.

一九八七年，某件非凡之事降臨印度。一部電視劇開始了——羅摩難德·薩迦的《羅摩衍那》，週日早晨在國家頻道播出。而整個國家停了下來。播出時街道清空；公車暫停；商家關門；整個民族坐在最近的一台電視機前，一起，觀看那個他們早已倒背如流的故事，由穿戲服的演員演出，並且如同事頭一次發生般地流淚。老婦人在她們的電視機周圍掛上花環。當扮演羅摩的演員出現時，觀眾觸摸螢幕，如同觸摸一個神。這部劇播了七十八集，而從某些衡量標準來說，是相對於人口、人類歷史上觀看人數最多的電視事件。三千歲的《羅摩衍那》，找到了一個新的媒介——並且立刻征服了它。

And the modern reimagining did not stop with television. In 1985, the great director Peter Brook staged a nine-hour Mahabharata that toured the world — an international cast, a dirt-floor stage, a version that stripped the epic to its bones and found, beneath the Sanskrit and the spectacle, a story so universal that audiences in Paris and Tokyo and New York wept at it as Indians had for millennia. Shashi Tharoor, in 1989, wrote The Great Indian Novel, recasting the Mahabharata as the story of modern India's independence. Devdutt Pattanaik has spent a career retelling the myths for a business-school generation. And most importantly, the last decades have heard the voices the epics had silenced: Chitra Banerjee Divakaruni's Palace of Illusions retells the Mahabharata through Draupadi's eyes; Amish Tripathi humanizes the gods; dozens of women writers have given Sita, given Draupadi, given the silenced queens, the inner lives the male-authored epics never granted them.

而現代的再想像並未止於電視。一九八五年，偉大的導演彼得·布魯克上演了一齣九小時的《摩訶婆羅多》，巡迴世界——一個國際卡司、一片泥土地的舞台、一個將史詩削到只剩骨頭的版本，而在梵文與奇觀之下，找到了一個如此普世的故事，以至於巴黎、東京、紐約的觀眾為之泣，一如印度人千年來所做。夏希·塔魯爾，一九八九年，寫了《偉大的印度小說》，將《摩訶婆羅多》重新鑄造為現代印度獨立的故事。德夫杜特·帕特奈克以一個商學院世代的語言重述神話。而最重要的是，過去幾十年聽見了史詩所噤聲的那些聲音：齊特拉·班納吉·迪瓦卡魯尼的《幻象之宮》以黑公主之眼重述《摩訶婆羅多》；阿米什·特里帕蒂使諸神人性化；數十位女性作家，給了悉多，給了黑公主，給了那些被噤聲的王后，那些男性作者所寫的史詩從未授予她們的內在生命。

This, too, is the mark of a living epic: every generation does not merely preserve it, it rewrites it. The retellings argue with the original; they argue with each other; and the argument itself keeps the story alive. Draupadi's question — the one no elder could answer in the dice-hall — is finally being answered, three thousand years later, by the writers who refuse to let her remain only a question. The epic, like the cosmos it describes, does not end. It only takes new forms.

這，也是一部活著的史詩的標記：每一個世代不只是保存它，而是重寫它。那些再述與原典爭辯；它們彼此爭辯；而那爭辯本身，使故事活著。黑公主的問題——那個沒有一位長輩能在骰子廳中回答的問題——終於，在三千年後，被那些拒絕讓她僅僅是一個問題的作家們所回答。這部史詩，一如它所描述的宇宙，沒有終結。它只是取了新的形。

Chapter 5 · 第五章

The Still-Unfolding Argument

仍在展開的爭辯

So why do these two stories — one of an exiled prince and a stolen wife, one of a fratricidal war — still live? Why have they not, like the myths of Greece or Babylon, become the property of scholars and schoolchildren, beautiful but inert? The answer, I think, is that India never decided what they meant. The Ramayana and the Mahabharata are not answers. They are questions — and the questions are still open. Was Rama right to banish Sita? The country has argued about it for two and a half thousand years and has not agreed. Was the Mahabharata war just? The book itself cannot decide; it ends with the victor in hell and the villain in heaven. Every Indian who has ever faced a hard choice is, in some sense, re-fighting the war of the Pandavas, or weighing the exile of Sita, in their own life.

那麼，這兩個故事——一個關於被流放的王子與被劫的妻子，一個關於兄弟相殘的戰爭——為什麼還活著？為什麼它們沒有像希臘或巴比倫的神話那樣，成為學者與學童的財產，美麗卻無生氣？我想，答案是：印度從未決定它們意味著什麼。《羅摩衍那》與《摩訶婆羅多》不是答案。它們是問題——而那些問題仍然是開放的。羅摩流放悉多是對的嗎？這個國家為此爭論了兩千五百年，未曾達成共識。《摩訶婆羅多》的戰爭是正義的嗎？這本書自己也決定不了；它以勝利者在地獄、反派在天界收尾。每一個曾面對艱難選擇的印度人，在某種意義上，都在他自己的生命裡，重新打著般度家的戰爭，或重新權衡著悉多的流放。

This is why the epics resist being turned into doctrine. They are too honest for that. They show Rama as both god and flawed husband; they show Krishna as both divine teacher and political schemer who urges the breaking of rules; they show Yudhishthira as both the soul of truth and the man who lies to win. A story that contradicts itself cannot be reduced to a slogan, and so it cannot be killed by being "solved." The epics survive precisely because they refuse to resolve. They hold the contradictions open, the way a living culture holds its own contradictions open, and they invite each new reader to enter the argument rather than to end it.

這便是為什麼這些史詩抗拒被變成教條。它們對此太誠實了。它們既顯羅摩為神、又顯他為有瑕疵的丈夫；它們既顯黑天為神聖的導師、又顯他為敦促破壞規則的政治謀略家；它們既顯堅戰為真實之靈魂、又顯他為那個為了贏而說謊的人。一個自相矛盾的故事不能被簡化為一個口號，於是它也不能被「解決」而殺死。這些史詩存活，正是因為它們拒絕去解決。它們把矛盾保持開放，一如一個活的文化把它自己的矛盾保持開放，而它們邀請每一個新的讀者進入那個爭辯，而不是去終結它。

And so, three thousand years after Valmiki cursed a hunter over a dead crane, the Ramayana is still being told — and two thousand years after Vyasa dictated his hundred thousand verses to an elephant-headed god, the Mahabharata is still being argued. They are sung in temples and danced in villages and broadcast on television and retold by novelists who give voice to the silenced. They are, in the deepest sense, the autobiography of a civilization — the long, honest, unfinished story a people tells itself about how to live, knowing that the telling will never be done. The epics do not end. They wait, patiently, for the next person to pick them up and enter the argument. That person, now, is you.

於是，在蟻垤為了一隻死去的鶴而詛咒一個獵人之後三千年，《羅摩衍那》仍在被講述——而在廣博向一位象頭神口述他那十萬頌之後兩千年，《摩訶婆羅多》仍在被爭辯。它們在廟宇裡被傳唱、在村莊裡被起舞、在電視上被播送、被小說家再述以使噤聲者發聲。它們，在最深的意義上，是一部文明的自傳——一個民族關於「如何生活」對自己講述的、漫長、誠實、未完成的故事，明知那講述永無完成之日。史詩不終結。它們耐心地等待下一個人拾起它們、進入那個爭辯。那個人，此刻，便是你。

THE STORY THAT WILL NOT END

不會結束的故事

A prince obeyed an unjust exile. A warrior threw down his bow and God taught him why he must pick it up. A woman's question went unanswered for three thousand years, and is being answered now, by voices the original never heard. The Ramayana and the Mahabharata are not relics. They are the longest, most honest, most unresolved argument a civilization has ever had with itself — and the argument is still going, in every village and every language and every generation, and will go on as long as there are people who must choose between the throne and the one they love. Which is to say: forever.
