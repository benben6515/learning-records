THE FOUNDATIONS

宇　宙　根　基

THE VEDAS · THE TRIMURTI · THE AVATARS OF VISHNU

Before there was Rama or Krishna, before the great wars and the stolen wives, there was a worldview — and it is stranger and deeper than the stories built upon it. The Indian imagination does not begin with a creator making the world once. It begins with a cosmos that creates itself, dissolves itself, and creates itself again, endlessly, the way the ocean makes and unmakes its waves. To read these epics at all, you must first stand inside this view of time. 在羅摩與黑天之前，在大戰與被劫的王后之前，先有一個世界觀——而它比建構其上的故事更深邃、更奇異。印度的想像，並不從一位造物主「一次性地」創造世界開始。它從一個自我創生、自我消解、又自我創生的宇宙開始，無盡地，一如大海之造浪又散浪。要讀懂這些史詩，你必須先站進這個對時間的看法之中。

Chapter 1 · 第一章

The Three Faces of the Divine

神的三張面孔

In the Indian imagination, the divine is not one god but three functions — three faces of a single mystery. There is Brahma, the creator, who dreams the world out of the lotus that grows from Vishnu's navel. There is Vishnu, the preserver, who lies asleep on the cosmic serpent through the long night between worlds, and who wakes to keep the cosmos in order. And there is Shiva, the destroyer, whose dance is the fire at the end of time — not because he is cruel, but because nothing new can be made until the old has been burned away. Creation, preservation, dissolution: the three breaths of a single lung.

在印度的想像中，神聖並非一位神，而是三個職能——同一個奧秘的三張面孔。有梵天，創造者，從毗濕奴臍中生出的蓮花裡夢出世界。有毗濕奴，護持者，在世界之間的漫漫長夜裡臥於宇宙之蛇上沉睡，醒來以維持宇宙的秩序。還有濕婆，毀滅者，祂的舞是時間盡頭之火——不是因為祂殘酷，而是因為舊的不被燒盡，新的便無從誕生。創造、護持、消解：同一個肺的三次呼吸。

Notice what is missing. There is no single jealous creator here, making the world once and resting on the seventh day. There is no fall, no exile from Eden, no one-time rupture to be repaired. Instead there is rhythm — the inbreath and the outbreath of a cosmos that has always existed and always will. The world is not a thing that was made; it is a thing that is being made, and unmade, and made again, forever. The gods themselves are not outside this rhythm; they are its servants. And we — the humans in the stories to come — are not fallen creatures trying to get back to a lost paradise. We are one wave in an ocean that is always already waving.

注意其中所缺的。這裡沒有單一的、嫉妒的造物主，一次性地造了世界然後在第七日安息。沒有墮落，沒有被逐出伊甸，沒有待修補的一次性決裂。有的是節奏——一個始終存在、也將始終存在的宇宙的吸氣與呼氣。世界不是一個「被造之物」；它是一個「正在被造、又正在被拆、又再次被造」之物，永遠如此。諸神本身也不在這節奏之外；他們是它的僕人。而我們——故事中將出現的人類——不是試圖重返失落樂園的墮落受造物。我們是一個永遠正在起浪的海洋中的一個浪。

And of the three, it is Vishnu who concerns us most — because Vishnu, the preserver, is the one who refuses to let the world fall entirely into ruin. Whenever order (dharma) declines and chaos (adharma) rises, Vishnu takes a body and walks among us, to set things right. These walks are the avatars — the descents — and two of them, Rama and Krishna, are the heroes of the two epics we are about to read. To understand Rama and Krishna, you must first understand that they are not men who became gods; they are a god who, out of love for a world that keeps breaking itself, became men.

而在三者之中，與我們最相關的是毗濕奴——因為護持者毗濕奴，是不肯讓世界徹底淪於毀滅的那一位。每當秩序（dharma，正法）衰退、混亂（adharma，非法）升起，毗濕奴便取一身軀，行於我們之間，以撥亂反正。這些行走，便是化身——降凡——而其中兩位，羅摩與黑天，正是我們即將閱讀的兩部史詩的英雄。要懂羅摩與黑天，你必須先明白：他們不是變成了神的人；而是一位神，因著對一個不斷自我破碎的世界的愛，而變成了人。

Chapter 2 · 第二章

The Avatars of Vishnu

毗濕奴的化身

The most famous sentence in the Bhagavad Gita — Krishna's own — is this: "Whenever dharma declines and adharma rises, then I take a body and come forth." It is the key to all the avatars, and it is worth slowing down to feel what it says. God does not come because the world is good. God comes because the world is breaking. The descent is a rescue, not a triumph; it is the answer of love to a situation that love cannot bear to watch. And the form God takes is never arbitrary — it is always exactly the form the crisis requires.

《薄伽梵歌》中最著名的一句——黑天親口所說——是這樣的：「每當正法衰退、非法升起之時，我便取一身軀而降臨。」這是所有化身的鑰匙，值得放慢腳步去感受它所說的。神降臨，不是因為世界是好的。神降臨，是因為世界正在破碎。降凡是救援，不是凱旋；它是愛對一個愛所不忍旁觀之境的回應。而神所取的形，從不是任意的——它總是恰好是那場危機所需要的那個形。

So when the smallest life was drowning in the first great flood, Vishnu came as a fish, and tied Manu's boat to his horn. When the gods needed a foundation to churn the ocean of milk, he came as a tortoise, and bore the mountain on his back. When a demon had hidden the earth itself at the bottom of the sea, he came as a boar, and dove and brought it up. When a tyrant could not be killed by man or beast, he came as a man-lion — neither the one nor the other — and tore him with his claws. When another tyrant could be killed by neither god nor man nor animal, he came as a dwarf, asked for three paces of land, and in three steps covered the universe. These are the first five avatars, and they move, the tradition loves to point out, up the ladder of evolution — fish, amphibian, mammal, half-beast, early man — as if the divine were learning, life-form by life-form, how to meet the world's evil.

所以，當最微小的生命在第一場大洪水中溺沒，毗濕奴化作一條魚，將摩奴的船繫於祂的角上。當諸神需要一個根基來攪拌乳海，祂化作一隻龜，將山馱在背上。當一個魔將大地本身藏於海底，祂化作一頭野豬，潛下，將它托起。當一個暴君不能被「人或獸」所殺，祂化作人獅——既非此亦非彼——以爪撕裂他。當另一個暴君不能被「神、人、動物」所殺，祂化作一個侏儒，求三步之地，三步跨越了宇宙。這是前五個化身，而傳統樂於指出，它們沿演化的階梯上行——魚、兩生、哺乳、半獸、早期人類——彷彿神聖正在逐個生命形態地學習，如何面對世間之惡。

Then come the later avatars, in human form, and here the stories darken. Rama-with-the-axe came to purge a warrior caste that had turned cruel — and he did it by slaughtering them, generation after generation, until the earth ran red. Rama of Ayodhya — the hero of the Ramayana — came to kill Ravana, and his story is one of exile and war and a love tested past endurance. And Krishna — the hero of the Mahabharata, and the charioteer of the Gita — came to guide a catastrophic war and then to die himself, shot in the heel by a hunter, the god bleeding out under a tree. The avatars do not always end happily. They end as the world ends: in sacrifice. But they come. That is the whole point. The world breaks; and God, refusing to leave it broken, comes down, again and again, in whatever form the breaking requires.

接著是後來的化身，以人形出現，故事便由此轉暗。持斧羅摩降臨，是為了清除一個已變殘酷的武士階層——而他以屠殺完成此事，一代接一代，直到大地染紅。阿約提亞的羅摩——《羅摩衍那》的英雄——降臨以殺羅波那，他的故事是流放、戰爭，以及一段被試煉到超過忍耐之限的愛。而黑天——《摩訶婆羅多》的英雄、《薄伽梵歌》的御者——降臨以引導一場浩劫之戰，而後自己也死去，被一個獵人射中腳踵，神在一棵樹下流血而亡。化身並不總是圓滿收場。它們如世界一般收場：在犧牲之中。但它們會來。這便是全部的要點。世界破碎；而神，不肯任其破碎，一次又一次降臨，取那破碎所需要的任何形。

Chapter 3 · 第三章

The Wheel of the Yugas

紀元之輪

If you have understood that the Indian cosmos breathes, you must now understand that it breathes on a schedule — and the schedule is the yugas, the four ages. There is Krita Yuga, the age of perfection, when dharma stands on all four of its legs and people need no law because they do no wrong. There is Treta Yuga, when dharma stands on three legs and the first imperfections appear. There is Dvapara Yuga, when dharma stands on two legs and good and evil are evenly mixed. And there is Kali Yuga — our own age — when dharma stands on one leg and the world is mostly rotten, and people are short, greedy, and deceitful, and kings are tyrants, and the end is near.

若你已明白印度宇宙會呼吸，那麼你現在必須明白，它在一個時間表上呼吸——而那時間表便是紀元，四個時代。有圓滿紀元，完美之齡，正法以四足而立，人們不需要法律，因為他們不作惡。有三分紀元，正法以三足而立，最初的瑕疵出現。有二分紀元，正法以二足而立，善與惡勢均力敵。還有爭鬥紀元——我們自己的時代——正法以一足而立，世界大半腐爛，人們矮小、貪婪、詭詐，君王是暴君，而終局已近。

But the crucial word is wheel. The yugas do not run in a line, getting worse forever. They run in a circle. After Kali Yuga comes dissolution, and then — the wheel turns — and Krita Yuga returns, and perfection comes back, and the whole thing begins again. There is no final catastrophe and no final salvation; there is only the turning. This is, in some ways, the most vertiginous idea in the Indian imagination: that nothing is won once and for all, and nothing is lost once and for all. Every victory will be undone; every ruin will be rebuilt. The point of the story is never the ending. The point is how you conduct yourself while the wheel is turning.

但關鍵的詞是「輪」。紀元並非沿一條線越來越糟、永遠如此地運行。它們沿一個圓運行。爭鬥紀元之後是消解，然後——輪轉了——圓滿紀元再臨，完美歸來，整個過程重新開始。沒有最終的災變，也沒有最終的救贖；只有轉動。在某種意義上，這是印度想像中最令人暈眩的觀念：沒有什麼是一勞永逸地贏得的，也沒有什麼是一勞永逸地失去的。每一場勝利都將被取消；每一片廢墟都將被重建。故事的重點從來不是結局。重點是，當輪在轉之時，你如何自處。

The two epics fall at precise points on this wheel, and knowing where deepens them. The Ramayana belongs to Treta Yuga — the world is still mostly good, Rama is nearly perfect, the lines between hero and villain are clear, and the story can end in a golden age. The Mahabharata belongs to the edge of Dvapara and Kali — the world is already halfway to ruin, the heroes are deeply flawed, the war is fratricidal and morally poisoned, and the victory is indistinguishable from defeat. This is why the Mahabharata is the darker, greater book: it is the story of a world watching its own virtue drain away. Both epics are true; they are just true at different hours of the same long day.

兩部史詩落在這個輪上的精確位置，而知曉其位置，使它們更深。《羅摩衍那》屬於三分紀元——世界仍大半是好的，羅摩近乎完美，英雄與惡棍的界線分明，故事能以一個黃金時代收尾。《摩訶婆羅多》屬於二分與爭鬥紀元的交界——世界已半途走向毀滅，英雄們有著深深的瑕疵，那場戰爭是兄弟相殘且道德上已被毒化，勝利與失敗無從分辨。這便是《摩訶婆羅多》更黑暗、也更偉大的原因：它是一個正在看著自身美德流失的世界的故事。兩部史詩都是真的；它們只是同一個漫長之日的不同時辰上的真。

Chapter 4 · 第四章

Dharma & the Four Aims

正法與人生四目的

The word you will meet more than any other in these epics is dharma — and it is the hardest word in the Sanskrit language to translate. It means duty, law, righteousness, the order of the cosmos, the right way for a thing to be, the particular obligation that falls on a particular person because of who they are. A king's dharma is different from a warrior's; a wife's dharma is different from a husband's; a son's dharma may directly contradict his father's. There is no single rulebook. There is only the endless, anguished question — given who I am, and where I stand, and what is happening, what is the right thing to do? — and that question is the engine that drives both epics forward.

在這兩部史詩中，你會遇到的、比任何詞都多的詞，是 dharma——正法——而它是梵文中最難翻譯的詞。它的意思是責任、法、正義、宇宙的秩序、一物當然之道、因著「你是誰」而落在你身上的特殊義務。一位君王的正法不同於一位武士的；一位妻子的正法不同於一位丈夫的；一位兒子的正法可能直接與他父親的相抵。沒有單一的規則手冊。只有那無盡的、痛苦的問題——既然我是這樣的人，站在這個位置，事情是這樣發生著，那麼，正確的做法是什麼？——而那個問題，便是驅動兩部史詩向前行的引擎。

And dharma is only one of the four aims a human life is meant to pursue. There is also artha — wealth, power, success, the business of prospering in the world; and kama — pleasure, love, beauty, desire in its proper place; and moksha — liberation, the freedom of the soul from the whole wheel of birth and death. A complete life honors all four: it does its duty, it prospers, it loves, and — in the end — it seeks release. The epics are always weighing these aims against one another, because they constantly collide. When Rama's dharma (obey his father) collides with his love for Sita, what wins? When Yudhishthira's dharma (be truthful) collides with his duty to win the war, what should he do? These are not abstract puzzles. They are the daily agonies of being human, and the epics refuse to resolve them neatly.

而正法，只是一個人一生當追求的四個目的之一。還有 artha——利，財富、權力、成就、在世間興旺之事；以及 kama——欲、愛、美、欲望在其適當之處；以及 moksha——解脫，靈魂從整個生死之輪中得自由。一個完整的人生，要同時尊崇這四者：盡其責、興其業、愛其所愛，並且——最終——尋求解脫。史詩總在將這些目的彼此權衡，因為它們不斷相撞。當羅摩的正法（順從父親）與他對悉多的愛相撞，何者勝出？當堅戰的正法（說實話）與他贏得戰爭的責任相撞，他該怎麼做？這些不是抽象的謎題。它們是身為人、日常的痛苦，而史詩拒絕將它們乾淨利落地解決。

This is the deep reason the Indian epics feel so different from the Greek. In Homer, the heroic code is clear: win glory, die well, leave a name. The question is never whether to fight, only how. In the Indian epics, the question of whether is everything. Arjuna throws down his bow on the field of Kurukshetra and says, "I will not fight" — and the entire Bhagavad Gita is God's attempt to answer him, an answer so subtle and so uncomfortable that scholars have argued for two thousand years about what it actually means. Dharma is not a code you can follow. It is a weight you carry, and sometimes a wound. The epics honor that.

這便是印度史詩感覺如此不同於希臘的深層原因。在荷馬，英雄的法則是清楚的：贏得榮光，死得其所，留下一個名字。問題從不是要不要戰，而是如何戰。在印度史詩，要不要的問題便是一切。阿周那在俱盧之野擲下神弓，說：「我不戰。」——而整部《薄伽梵歌》便是神試圖回答他的嘗試，一個如此微妙、如此令人不安的回答，以至於學者們兩千年來一直在爭論它究竟意味著什麼。正法不是一套你可以遵循的法則。它是你背負的重量，有時是一道傷口。史詩尊崇這一點。

Chapter 5 · 第五章

The Two Sages & the Two Songs

兩位仙人與兩部詩

Every epic needs a singer, and the Indian tradition tells us, beautifully, who sang these. The Ramayana was the work of Valmiki — and the story of how he became a poet is itself the first poem. One day the sage saw a hunter shoot a crane in the act of mating; the bird fell dead, and its mate cried out in anguish. And something broke open in Valmiki — a grief and a fury he had never felt — and out of his mouth came a line, metrically perfect, cursing the hunter: "May you find no rest, O hunter, for all the ages, since you slew one of this pair while love was upon him." And he looked at what he had said, astonished. The verse had come unbidden; it was, the tradition says, the first shloka ever spoken — the birth of Sanskrit poetry itself, born not from joy but from compassion for a wounded bird.

每部史詩都需要一位歌者，而印度傳統美麗地告訴我們，是誰唱了這些。《羅摩衍那》是蟻垤（Valmiki）之作——而他如何成為詩人的故事，本身便是第一首詩。一日，仙人看見一個獵人射殺一隻正在交頸的鶴；那鳥倒地而死，牠的伴侶發出痛苦的哀鳴。蟻垤體內有什麼迸裂了——一種他從未感受過的悲慟與憤怒——從他口中迸出一句話，格律完美，詛咒那獵人：「獵人啊，願你萬劫不得安息，因為你在愛正降臨之時，殺了這一對中的一隻。」他驚訝地看著自己所說的話。那詩句是不請自來的；傳統說，那是人類說出的第一個輸洛迦——梵語詩歌本身的誕生，誕生不是出於喜悅，而是出於對一隻受傷之鳥的悲憫。

The Mahabharata, vaster and messier, has a different origin-story — and a different poet. Vyasa composed it, but could not write it down (the epic runs to a hundred thousand verses, ten times the Iliad and Odyssey combined). So he needed a scribe fast enough to take his dictation, and the god Ganesha — the elephant-headed remover of obstacles — volunteered, on one condition: Vyasa must never pause. If the poet stopped, Ganesha would lay down the pen and walk away. And so the two sat down together, the sage and the god, and the Mahabharata poured out — and whenever Vyasa needed a moment to think, he composed a particularly complex verse, knowing that Ganesha, that clever god, would have to slow down to puzzle it out, and so buy the poet his breath. The book, then, was a collaboration between human genius and divine memory, written in the gaps between two minds.

《摩訶婆羅多》，更浩瀚也更龐雜，有著不同的起源故事——以及一位不同的詩人。廣博仙人（Vyasa）構思了它，卻無法親自寫下（這部史詩長達十萬頌，是《伊利亞特》與《奧德賽》總和的十倍）。於是他需要一位夠快的書記來筆錄他的口述，而象頭神甘尼許——那位象頭的、移除障礙者——自願效勞，附帶一個條件：廣博絕不能停。詩人若停，甘尼許便放下筆走人。於是兩人一同坐下，仙人與神，而《摩訶婆羅多》傾瀉而出——每當廣博需要片刻思索，他便構造一句格外複雜的詩，深知那位聰明的甘尼許將不得不放慢下來去解開它，從而為詩人贏得喘息。於是這本書，是人類天賦與神聖記憶的合作，寫在兩個心靈之間的縫隙裡。

Two sages, two songs — and the contrast is the contrast of the epics themselves. Valmiki's Ramayana is a finished artwork, sung in a single voice, polished and unified, its lines as clean as a carved gem. Vyasa's Mahabharata is a vast accumulation, layered and contradictory, full of digressions and nested stories, a whole civilization's memory pressed into one sprawling book — a book that famously claims, at its opening, that "what is found here may be found elsewhere, but what is not found here cannot be found anywhere." Two ways of telling the truth. One by shaping it into a single perfect line. One by piling up so much truth that no single line could ever hold it.

兩位仙人，兩部詩——而這對比，便是兩部史詩本身的對比。蟻垤的《羅摩衍那》是一件完成的藝術品，以單一的聲音唱出，打磨而統一，其詩行如雕琢的寶石般潔淨。廣博的《摩訶婆羅多》是一個龐大的積累，層疊而矛盾，滿是離題與嵌套的故事，一整個文明的記憶被壓進一本蔓生的大書裡——一本在開篇便著名地宣稱：「此處所有者，或可於他處尋得；此處所無者，則任何他處皆不可尋。」兩種訴說真理的方式。一種，是將它塑造成一行完美的詩。另一種，是堆疊如此之多的真理，以至於沒有任何一行能容得下它。

BEFORE THE STORIES, THE WORLD

在故事之前，是世界

A cosmos that breathes. A god who descends. A wheel that turns. A duty that wounds. Two sages — one who sang from grief at a dead bird, one who dictated a hundred thousand verses to an elephant-headed god. With these foundations laid, we may now enter the stories themselves — first the Ramayana, the older, the cleaner, the tale of a perfect prince and a stolen wife.
