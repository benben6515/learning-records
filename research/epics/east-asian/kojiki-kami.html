THE AGE OF THE GODS

神　代　紀

IZANAGI & IZANAMI · AMATERASU · SUSANO-O · 712 AD

The first gods stirred the sea with a jeweled spear and bore the islands of Japan as their own children. The sun-goddess Amaterasu was born from her father's eye — and when her wild brother's outrages drove her into a cave, the whole world went dark, until the gods devised a ruse so ingenious, so funny, that she came out to see what everyone was laughing at. And from her line descended the emperors of Japan — the oldest unbroken monarchy on earth, still reigning today, twenty-six centuries on. This is the Kojiki — Japan's book of beginnings. 最初的神以瓊矛攪動大海，生下了日本諸島作為自己的孩子。太陽女神天照大神由其父之目所生——而當她的狂弟之暴行使她躲入洞中，整個世界陷入黑暗，直到諸神想出了一個如此巧妙、如此有趣的計策，使她出來看看大家在笑什麼。而從她的血脈傳下了日本的天皇——世上最古老的、從未中斷的王室，至今仍在位，二十六個世紀。這便是《古事記》——日本的開端之書。

Chapter 1 · 第一章

Izanagi in the Land of Yomi

伊邪那岐之黃泉

Izanami, the female-who-invites, died giving birth to the god of fire — burned from within, consumed by her own child. And her husband Izanagi, the male-who-invites, was stricken with grief, and did what every grieving spouse in mythology has done, from Orpheus to the mother of Lemminkäinen: he went to get her back. He descended into Yomi, the land of the dead, the Japanese underworld — a dark, polluted place, the realm of decay.

伊邪那美，那位「招聘之女」，在生下火神之時死了——從內部被灼燒，被她自己的孩子所吞噬。而她的丈夫伊邪那岐，那位「招募之男」，悲痛欲絕，而做了神話中每一個悲慟的配偶所做的事，從奧菲斯到勒明蓋寧的母親：他去把她帶回來。他降入了黃泉，死者之國，日本的冥府——一個黑暗、汙穢之地，腐朽的領域。

And he found her. And she spoke to him from behind a closed door, and said: "I have eaten the food of Yomi. I cannot leave. But I will plead with the gods of the dead to release me. Wait here. Do not look at me." And Izanagi waited. And then, unable to bear the not-knowing — just as Orpheus could not bear it, just as all the bereaved cannot bear it — he broke his promise. He lit a torch. And he looked. And what he saw, the tradition tells us, was his wife's body — rotting, infested with maggots and thunder-gods, swollen, monstrous. The beautiful goddess who had borne the islands of Japan was now a thing of horror. And Izanagi, the tradition tells us, was terrified — and he ran. And Izanami, humiliated and enraged by his disgust, pursued him, sending the hags of Yomi after him, and then the thunder-gods, and then the army of the dead.

而他找到了她。而她從一扇關閉的門後對他說話，說：「我已吃了黃泉之食。我不能離開。但我會向冥府之神懇求釋放我。在此等著。不要看我。」而伊邪那岐等著。然後，無法忍受那份不知道——一如奧菲斯無法忍受，一如所有喪親之人無法忍受——他違背了他的承諾。他點了一把火。而他看。而他所見的，傳統告訴我們，是他妻子的身體——腐爛的、爬滿了蛆與雷神、腫脹的、怪獸般的。那位生了日本諸島的美麗女神，如今是一件可怖之物。而伊邪那岐，傳統告訴我們，驚恐萬分——而他跑了。而伊邪那美，為他的厭惡所羞辱與激怒，追趕他，派出黃泉的醜女，然後雷神，然後死者之軍。

And Izanagi escaped — barely — by sealing the passage between the world of the living and the world of the dead with a great boulder. And from behind the boulder, Izanami cried out: "Every day, I will strangle a thousand of your people." And Izanagi answered: "Then every day, I will cause fifteen hundred to be born." And so the ratio was set: a thousand deaths, fifteen hundred births — the world's population growing, slowly, because the grief-stricken god loved his wife more than he could bear her decay, and because the goddess of death, humiliated by her husband's rejection, demanded her daily toll. This is the Japanese Orpheus myth — and it is more brutal than the Greek, because in the Greek, Eurydice simply vanishes. In the Japanese, she becomes the enemy of the living. The boundary between the worlds is sealed with a stone, and a divorce decree, and the endless war between death and birth.

而伊邪那岐逃了——勉強地——以一塊巨石封住了生者世界與死者世界之間的通道。而從那塊石頭後面，伊邪那美喊道：「每天，我要勒死你的一千個百姓。」而伊邪那岐回答：「那麼每天，我要讓一千五百個出生。」於是那個比例定了下來：一千死，一千五百生——世界的人口，緩慢地增長著，因為那位悲慟的神愛他的妻子勝過他能忍受她的腐朽，也因為那位死亡女神，為她丈夫的拒絕所羞辱，要求她每日的代價。這是日本的奧菲斯神話——而它比希臘的更殘酷，因為在希臘裡，尤麗狄絲只是消失。在日本裡，她成了生者之敵。兩界之間的邊界以一塊石、一紙離婚狀、以及死亡與出生之間那場永戰所封。

Chapter 2 · 第二章

The Three Pure Children

三貴子

And Izanagi, having escaped Yomi, felt himself polluted by the land of death — and so he went to a river to purify himself, in the first ritual of misogi, the Shinto purification by water that is still practiced today. And as he washed — his left eye, his right eye, his nose — three children were born from his cleansing, three beings of extraordinary purity and power. From his left eye: Amaterasu Omikami, the Great Sun Goddess, the radiant one, the ancestress of the imperial line. From his right eye: Tsukuyomi, the moon god, the lord of the night. And from his nose: Susano-o, the storm god, the howling one, the wild and uncontrollable.

而伊邪那岐，既已逃出黃泉，自覺被死者之國所污染——於是他去了一條河邊淨化自己，那是最早的「禊」，神道以水淨化的儀式，至今仍在實行。而當他洗滌之時——他的左眼、他的右眼、他的鼻子——三個孩子從他的清潔中誕生，三個具有非凡純潔與力量的生靈。從他的左眼：天照大神，偉大的太陽女神，那光芒四射的、皇室之祖。從他的右眼：月讀尊，月神，夜之主。從他的鼻子：素盞嗚尊，風暴之神，那個嗥叫的、狂野而無法控制的。

And Izanagi, looking at his three pure children, divided the world among them: Amaterasu was given Takamagahara, the High Plain of Heaven, to rule. Tsukuyomi was given the realm of the night. And Susano-o was given the sea. But Susano-o — and this is the hinge of the whole Kojiki — refused. He did not want the sea. He wanted to go to his mother, to Yomi, to the land of the dead where Izanami had gone. He wept and howled and refused his domain, until the tradition tells us that his weeping caused the green hills to wither, and the rivers to dry — for the storm god's grief was a drought upon the land. And Izanagi, furious at his son's refusal, banished him.

而伊邪那岐，望著他三個純潔的孩子，將世界在他們之間劃分：天照大神被賜高天原——天之高原——去統治。月讀尊被賜夜之領域。而素盞嗚尊被賜大海。但素盞嗚尊——而這是整部《古事記》的鉸鏈——拒絕了。他不想要大海。他想要去他的母親那裡，去黃泉，去伊邪那美所去的死者之國。他哭泣、嗥叫、拒絕他的領域，直到傳統告訴我們他的哭泣使翠綠的山丘枯萎、河流乾涸——因為風暴之神的悲慟是一場降臨大地之上的旱。而伊邪那岐，為他兒子的拒絕所激怒，放逐了他。

Chapter 3 · 第三章

Amaterasu & the Cave

天照與天岩戶

Before his banishment, Susano-o went up to Heaven to say farewell to his sister Amaterasu — and his farewell was a rampage. He destroyed her rice-fields, defiled her temples, broke the irrigation ditches, and — the worst insult, the tradition tells us — he flayed a heavenly horse and hurled its carcass through the roof of the hall where Amaterasu was weaving the garments of the gods. And one of her attendants, pricked by the shuttle in her terror, died. And Amaterasu — the sun herself, the greatest of the gods — was so grief-stricken and terrified by her brother's violence that she did the unthinkable: she hid. She went into a cave — the Heavenly Rock-Dwelling, the Amano-Iwato — and she sealed the door behind her, and the sun vanished from the world.

在被放逐之前，素盞嗚尊上天庭向他的姊姊天照辭行——而他的辭行是一場肆虐。他毀了她的稻田、玷汙了她的神殿、破壞了灌溉溝渠，而——最糟的侮辱，傳統告訴我們——他剝了一匹天馬的皮，把它的屍體從天照正在織神衣的大殿屋頂上扔了進去。而她的一個侍女，在驚恐中被梭刺到，死了。而天照——太陽本身，諸神中最偉大的——為她弟弟的暴力所如此悲痛與驚嚇，以至於她做了不可想像之事：她躲了起來。她走進了一個洞——天之岩戶——而她在身後封上了門，而太陽從世上消失了。

And the world went dark. The tradition tells us that without the sun, the whole cosmos fell into chaos — the gods could not see, the land withered, evil spirits filled the darkness, and terror reigned. And the eight hundred myriad gods gathered outside the cave and debated: how do you coax the sun out of hiding? And the plan they devised is one of the great scenes in world mythology. They gathered before the cave, and they did something the sun goddess did not expect: they threw a party. The goddess Ame-no-Uzume, the dawn-goddess, the divine dancer, mounted a tub — and she danced. And she danced so wildly, so hilariously, so provocatively, that the eight hundred myriad gods burst into uncontrollable laughter. The laughter shook the heavens.

而世界暗了。傳統告訴我們，沒有了太陽，整個宇宙陷入了混沌——諸神看不見、大地枯萎、惡靈充滿了黑暗、而恐怖統治著。而八百萬神聚集在洞外商議：你如何哄太陽出洞？而他們所設想的計畫，是世界神話中最偉大的場面之一。他們聚集在洞前，而做了一件太陽女神沒有預料到的事：他們開了一場派對。天宇受賣命，黎明女神，神聖的舞者，登上一個木桶——而她跳了。而她跳得如此狂野、如此滑稽、如此挑逗，以至於八百萬神爆發出無法控制的笑聲。那笑聲震動了天庭。

And inside the cave, Amaterasu heard the laughter. And she was puzzled — if the world was dark, if she was hiding, if everyone was grieving — then what was so funny? And she pushed the stone door open, just a crack, and asked: "Why are you laughing?" And Ame-no-Uzume answered: "We have found a goddess more wonderful than you!" And she held up a mirror — and Amaterasu, curious, drawn by the laughter and the light of her own reflection, stepped forward. And the hidden god behind the mirror — strong-armed Ama-no-Tajikarao — grabbed the edge of the rock and pulled it open. And the sun came out. And the mirror, and the dance, and the laughter brought the light back. The tradition tells us that the mirror — the Yata-no-Kagami — became one of the three Imperial Regalia of Japan, still kept at the Grand Shrine of Ise. The sun came out of the cave because the gods made her laugh. The world is saved by comedy.

而在洞中，天照聽見了笑聲。而她困惑——如果世界是暗的，如果她在躲，如果每個人都在悲慟——那麼什麼那麼好笑？而她把石門推開了一道縫，問：「你們在笑什麼？」而天宇受賣命回答：「我們找到了一位比你更偉大的女神！」而她舉起一面鏡子——而天照，好奇，被笑聲和自己倒影的光所吸引，向前走了一步。而在鏡子後面藏著的神——力大無窮的天手力男——抓住岩石的邊緣，把它拉開。而太陽出來了。而那面鏡子、那場舞、那笑聲，把光帶了回來。傳統告訴我們，那面鏡子——八咫鏡——成了日本三神器之一，至今仍保存在伊勢神宮。太陽出洞，是因為諸神使她笑了。世界因喜劇而得救。

Chapter 4 · 第四章

Susano-o's Exile

素盞嗚尊之放逐

Banished from Heaven for his crimes, Susano-o descended to the mortal world — to the land of Izumo, in western Japan — and there he met something that would define him: an old couple weeping, with their daughter between them. And he asked them why, and they told him: every year, an eight-headed dragon named Orochi comes from the river and devours one of their daughters. They had eight daughters. Seven were gone. This was the last. And Susano-o — the storm god, the banished one, the howler — looked at the weeping parents and the trembling girl, and he decided. He would fight the dragon.

因罪被逐出天庭，素盞嗚尊降到了凡間——到日本西部的出雲之地——而他在那裡遇見了將定義他之物：一對哭泣的老夫婦，他們的女兒在兩人之間。而他問他們為何哭，而他們告訴他：每年，一頭名叫八岐大蛇的八頭巨龍從河裡來，吞食他們的一個女兒。他們曾有八個女兒。七個已去。這是最後一個。而素盞嗚尊——那位風暴之神、被逐者、嗥叫者——望著那對哭泣的父母和那個發抖的女孩，而他決定了。他要與那龍搏鬥。

And he defeated it not by force but by cunning. He set out eight vats of sake — the strongest rice-wine — at the river's edge. And the dragon came, eight heads, eight maws, and each head drank from a vat, and the sake was so strong that the dragon fell into a stupor. And Susano-o, while it slept, hacked off all eight heads. And from the dragon's belly — from the fourth tail, the tradition specifies — he drew a sword: the Kusanagi, the Grass-Cutting Sword, a divine blade that would become the second of Japan's three Imperial Regalia. The storm god, exiled for his violence, redeemed himself by an act of protection — saving a girl from a monster — and the sword he found in the dragon's body became the symbol of the imperial authority it would, one day, be given to.

而他不是以力，而是以計敗之。他在河邊擺了八桶酒——最烈的米酒——。而那龍來了，八頭，八口，而每個頭飲了一桶，而酒烈到那龍陷入了昏睡。而素盞嗚尊，趁它睡時，砍下了全部八個頭。而從那龍的腹中——從第四條尾巴，傳統明確指出——他拔出了一把劍：草薙劍，一把神聖的刀，它將成為日本三神器之第二。那位因暴力而被逐的風暴之神，以一個保護之舉——從怪獸手中救出一個女孩——贖回了自己，而他在龍體中找到的那把劍，成了那個有朝一日將被賜予之帝國權威的象徵。

Chapter 5 · 第五章

The Descent of the Grandson

天孫降臨

And the line continued. Amaterasu, the sun-goddess, had a grandson — Ninigi — and the tradition tells us that she sent him down from the High Plain of Heaven to rule the land of Japan, carrying with him the three treasures: the mirror (her reflection, the sign of her presence), the sword (the Kusanagi, from the dragon's belly), and the jewel (the Yasakani, a curved comma-shaped gem). These three — mirror, sword, jewel — are the Imperial Regalia of Japan, and they are still, the tradition insists, kept in their respective shrines, unseen by common eyes, the physical proof of the divine descent of the imperial line.

而血脈繼續。天照大神，太陽女神，有一個孫子——瓊瓊杵尊——而傳統告訴我們，她從高天原派他下來統治日本之地，隨身帶著三件神器：鏡（她的倒影，她臨在的標記）、劍（草薙劍，來自龍腹）、以及玉（八尺瓊勾玉，一塊彎曲的逗號形寶玉）。這三件——鏡、劍、玉——是日本的三神器，而傳統堅持，它們仍被保存在各自的宮中，凡人之眼不得見，是皇族神聖降臨的物證。

And Ninigi descended — the tradition gives us the image of him standing on a floating bridge, lowering himself onto the peak of a mountain in Kyushu, the first of the divine line to walk on Japanese soil. And he brought with him rice — the grain that would sustain the Japanese people, the grain whose cultivation defines the civilization, the grain that is, in the Japanese imagination, the gift of the gods to the land they bore. And with the descent of Ninigi, the age of the wandering gods ended, and the age of the rulers began — the age in which the divine descendants would govern the land, generation after generation, as mortal kings with divine blood.

而瓊瓊杵尊降臨——傳統給了我們他站在天之浮橋上、降落在九州一座山頂的意象，他是神聖血脈中第一個踏上日本土地的。而他帶來了稻米——那將養活日本百姓的穀物、其種植定義了文明的穀物、那個在日本想像中是諸神給予他們所生之地的禮物。而隨著瓊瓊杵尊的降臨，流浪諸神的時代結束了，統治者的時代開始了——那個神聖後裔將一代接一代治理土地的時代，作為有著神聖血脈的凡人之王。

Chapter 6 · 第六章

The First Emperor

初代天皇

And the tradition tells us that, some generations after Ninigi, a descendant named Jimmu — the first human emperor — set out from Kyushu on a campaign eastward, to unify the land, and founded the Yamato court, the line of emperors that has, the tradition insists, never been broken. Emperor Jimmu ascended the throne in 660 BC — so the tradition says — and from him, in unbroken succession, descends the current emperor of Japan, making the Japanese imperial family the oldest continuous hereditary monarchy in the world.

而傳統告訴我們，在瓊瓊杵尊之後幾個世代，一位名叫神武的後裔——第一位人類天皇——從九州向東出征，統一了這片土地，建立了大和朝廷，那個傳統堅持從未中斷的天皇之脈。神武天皇於公元前六六○年登基——傳統如此說——而從他，在不斷的繼承之中，傳下了當今的日本天皇，使日本皇室成為世界上最古老的、持續的世襲君主制。

Whether the early emperors were historical or mythical is a question scholars debate — but the tradition itself does not distinguish between the two. The Kojiki, and its companion the Nihon Shoki, move seamlessly from the age of the gods to the age of the emperors, from the divine to the human, without a break — because the Japanese tradition insists that there IS no break. The emperor is divine-descended. The bloodline is unbroken. And the land itself — the islands born from the gods' own union — is sacred, the gods' own child, governed by the gods' own descendants. This is the foundation of Japanese identity, and it is the reason the Kojiki is not merely a mythology but a political document: the myth of divine descent is the myth of a nation's right to be itself, governed by its own sacred line, on its own sacred land. The Age of the Gods flows, without interruption, into the age of human history. The next stage of our journey leaves the gods behind, and turns to the most human — and the most heartbreaking — story in the Japanese tradition: the Tale of the Heike.

早期的天皇是歷史的還是神話的，這是學者辯論的問題——但傳統本身不區分兩者。《古事記》，及其同伴《日本書紀》，從神代到天皇代、從神聖到人間、無縫地移動，沒有斷裂——因為日本傳統堅持沒有斷裂。天皇是神裔。血脈未斷。而這片土地本身——由諸神自己的結合所生的諸島——是神聖的，諸神自己的孩子，由諸神自己的後裔所統治。這是日本認同的根基，也是為什麼《古事記》不僅僅是一部神話、而是一份政治文件：神聖降臨的神話，是一個民族有權成為自己、由它自己神聖的血脈、在它自己神聖的土地上被統治的神話。神代，沒有間斷地，流入人類歷史的時代。我們旅程的下一階段把諸神留在身後，轉向日本傳統中最人性的——也最令人心碎的——故事：平家物語。

THE LAND OF THE GODS

神之國

The first gods stirred the sea with a jeweled spear, and the islands were born. A grieving husband fled the land of death, and sealed its door with a stone, and set the ratio of births to deaths. Three pure children were born from a father's cleansing, and the sun-goddess hid in a cave, and was drawn out by laughter and a mirror. The storm god slew an eight-headed dragon and found a sword. And the grandson of the sun descended to the mountain, carrying the three treasures, and his descendant became the first emperor of a line that has never broken. Japan is the land of the gods — sacred, divine-descended, and governed by a bloodline that reaches back to the sun. But all that is sacred must also pass. The bell is about to ring.
