THE STONE MONKEY'S REBELLION

石　猴　的　反　叛

SUN WUKONG · WU CHENG'EN · 16TH CENTURY

Born from a stone egg on the Mountain of Flowers and Fruit, a monkey learned the seventy-two transformations, stole the peaches of immortality, named himself Great Sage Equal to Heaven, and defeated every army the Jade Emperor sent against him — until the Buddha himself came, and with a single palm, with a single wager, crushed the monkey's pride and buried him beneath a mountain for five hundred years. This is the first half of China's most beloved novel — the story of a being too powerful for any authority to contain. 由花果山上的一顆石卵所生，一隻猴子習得七十二變，偷了蟠桃，自封齊天大聖，擊敗了玉帝派出的每一支天兵——直到佛祖親自前來，以一掌、以一賭，碾碎了猴子的驕傲，將他鎮在山下五百年。這是中國最鍾愛的小說的前半部——一個強大到無法被任何權威所容之生靈的故事。

Chapter 1 · 第一章

The Stone Egg

石卵

On the Mountain of Flowers and Fruit, the tradition tells us, there was a stone — an ordinary stone, fed for ages by the forces of heaven and earth, absorbing the essences of sun and moon, until one day it split, and from it hatched a stone egg, and from the egg emerged a monkey. Not a monkey born of a mother, but a monkey born of geology — of the slow, patient accumulation of natural force over an inconceivable time. The monkey is, in a sense, the mountain's child, the earth's own offspring, a being made by the Dao itself, by the same flow that made the rivers and the stars. He is nature personified — and nature, in the Chinese imagination, is restless, powerful, and cannot be governed.

傳統告訴我們，在花果山上有一塊石頭——一塊平凡的石頭，被天地之力滋養了無數世代，吸收日月精華，直到有一天它裂開了，從中孵出了一顆石卵，而從那卵中出來了一隻猴子。一隻不是由母親所生、而是由地質所生的猴子——由自然之力在不可想像的時間裡緩慢而耐心地積累而成。這隻猴子，在某種意義上，是山的孩子、大地本身的後代，一個由道自身、由那同樣造就了河與星的流轉所造的生靈。他是自然的化身——而自然，在中國的想像裡，是躁動的、強大的、無法被治理的。

And the monkey's first act was to bow to the four quarters — a gesture of recognition, of the stone-born being acknowledging the world it had entered. And then, the tradition tells us, beams of golden light shot from his eyes, piercing straight up to the Palace of Heaven — and the Jade Emperor, the ruler of all the cosmos, sitting on his throne, saw the light, and was troubled. For the light meant that a being of extraordinary power had been born on earth — a being that would, one day, challenge heaven itself. And the Jade Emperor, looking down, saw only a monkey — and dismissed the concern. This is the first of the Jade Emperor's mistakes, and the tradition marks it: the powerful always underestimate the small. The stone monkey played with the other monkeys, found a waterfall-cave behind a curtain of water, and led them through it, and was crowned their king. And for a time, he was happy.

而猴子的第一個動作是向四方鞠躬——一個認知的姿態，由石所生的生靈承認它所進入的世界。然後，傳統告訴我們，金色的光從他眼中射出，直穿天庭——而宇宙的統治者玉帝，坐在他的寶座上，看見了那光，而不安。因為那光意味著一個力量非凡的生靈已在地上誕生——一個有朝一日會挑戰天庭本身的生靈。而玉帝，俯視而下，只看見一隻猴子——便打消了那個擔憂。這是玉帝的第一個錯誤，而傳統標記了它：權勢者總是低估小者。那石猴與其他猴子玩耍，在一片水簾後面找到了一個瀑布洞，帶領他們穿過去，而被冠為他們的王。有一段時日，他是快樂的。

Chapter 2 · 第二章

The Quest for Immortality

求長生

But happiness was not enough. The monkey, the tradition tells us, was sitting one day with his subjects at a feast, and a sudden grief came over him — for he realized that he would die. Even the Monkey King, even the ruler of the waterfall-cave, would one day grow old and pass into the underworld, under the rule of Yama, the king of death. And the thought was unbearable. Not death itself — the monkey was brave enough for that — but the waste of it, the thought that all his power and cleverness would simply end. And so he made a raft, and sailed across the sea, to find a teacher who could teach him how to not die.

但快樂不足夠。傳統告訴我們，那猴子某日與他的子民在宴會上，一陣突如其來的悲傷攫住了他——因為他意識到他會死。即便是猴王，即便是水簾洞的統治者，有朝一日也會老去、進入冥府，受閻羅——死亡之王——的管轄。而那個念頭無法承受。不是死亡本身——那猴子夠勇敢去面對——而是它的浪費，那個他的力量與聰明將簡單地終結的念頭。於是他造了一隻木筏，渡海而去，去尋一位能教他如何不死的老師。

And he found one. A Daoist immortal, the Patriarch Subhuti, took the monkey as his pupil, and taught him the seventy-two transformations — the power to become anything: a bird, a fish, an insect, a tree, another person, a god. And he taught him the cloud-somersault — the ability to leap one hundred and eight thousand li in a single flip, crossing the world in an instant. And the monkey learned everything, greedily, brilliantly, faster than any pupil the Patriarch had ever had. And the Patriarch, seeing what he had made — a being of limitless power and limitless ambition, with no matching wisdom to restrain it — grew afraid. He expelled the monkey. "Do not ever say you were my pupil," he warned. "For if you cause trouble, I will know, and I will not help you." The teacher who gave the weapon refused to be associated with the one who would wield it. The monkey went home, armed, immortal, and ready for the trouble the Patriarch had foreseen.

而他找到了一位。道教的仙人，須菩提祖師，收了那猴子為徒，教了他七十二變——化身萬物的力量：一隻鳥、一條魚、一隻蟲、一棵樹、另一個人、一位神。而他教了他筋斗雲——一個觔斗翻出十萬八千里、瞬間穿越世界的能力。而那猴子貪婪地、聰慧地、比祖師所曾有過的任何弟子都快地學會了一切。而祖師，看見他所造之物——一個擁有無限力量與無限野心、卻沒有相配的智慧來約束的生靈——害怕了。他把猴子逐出。「永遠不要說你曾是我的弟子，」他警告。「因為你若惹了禍，我會知道，而我不會幫你。」那個給了武器的人，拒絕與那個將揮舞它之人有所關聯。猴子回家了，武裝了，長生了，準備好了祖師所預見的那場禍。

Chapter 3 · 第三章

The Great Sage Equal to Heaven

齊天大聖

And then came the arrogance. The monkey, armed with his transformations and his cloud-somersault and his immortality, went first to the Dragon King of the Eastern Sea, and bullied him into surrendering the magical staff — the Golden-Hooped Rod, a pillar of iron that could shrink to the size of a needle or grow to fill the ocean, that had once been used to measure the depths of the sea. It weighed thirteen thousand five hundred pounds. The monkey wielded it like a toothpick. And then he went to the underworld, crossed out his name and the names of all his monkey subjects from the Register of the Dead, making himself and his people literally immortal, beyond the reach of death itself.

然後傲慢來了。那猴子，帶著他的變化、他的筋斗雲、他的長生，先去了東海龍王那裡，逼他交出那根法棍——如意金箍棒，一根可以縮成針的大小、或長到充滿大海的鐵柱，曾用以丈量海的深度。它重一萬三千五百斤。那猴子像一根牙籤一樣揮舞它。然後他去了冥府，把他自己和他所有猴子子民的名字從生死簿上劃掉，使他自己和他的人字面上長生不死，超越死亡本身的觸及。

And the complaints reached Heaven. The Dragon King, the King of Death — both appealed to the Jade Emperor, demanding justice against the monkey who had stolen and defied with impunity. And the Jade Emperor, making his second mistake, tried a soft approach: he offered the monkey a title. "Come to Heaven," he said, "and I will give you a post." And the monkey came, and was made the Keeper of the Heavenly Horses — a stable-boy. And the monkey, discovering the insult of his rank, rebelled, returned to his mountain, and declared himself a title that no emperor could give or take: Qi Tian Da Sheng, the Great Sage Equal to Heaven. He was not beneath Heaven. He was Heaven's equal. And the name itself was the declaration of war.

而那些控訴傳到了天庭。龍王、閻羅——都向玉帝上訴，要求對那個偷了又抗、卻不受懲罰的猴子伸張正義。而玉帝，犯了第二個錯，試了軟的一手：他給了猴子一個頭銜。「上天來，」他說，「我給你一個差事。」而猴子來了，被任命為弼馬溫——一個馬夫。而那猴子，發現了他品階的侮辱，反了，回到他的山上，宣告了一個沒有任何皇帝能給或能奪的頭銜：齊天大聖。他不在天之下。他是天的匹敵。而那個名字本身，便是宣戰。

Chapter 4 · 第四章

The Furnace & the Fiery Eyes

八卦爐與火眼金睛

And the war came. The Jade Emperor sent his armies — and the monkey defeated them all. He crashed the Peach Banquet, the greatest feast of Heaven, ate the peaches of immortality reserved for the gods, drank the jade elixir, swallowed Laozi's pills of longevity. Every attempt to contain him failed. Every army broke against him. And finally, the Jade Emperor sent for the one force the monkey could not defeat: Erlang Shen, the warrior god with the third eye, the most powerful fighter in Heaven. And Erlang and the monkey fought — a battle of transformations, each becoming greater than the other, the duel escalating until the whole cosmos shook.

而戰爭來了。玉帝派出了他的天兵——而那猴子擊敗了他們全部。他大鬧蟠桃宴——天庭最大的盛宴——吃了諸神專屬的蟠桃，喝了玉液，吞了太上老君的長生金丹。每一個控制他的嘗試都失敗了。每一支軍隊都在他面前崩潰。最後，玉帝派人請了那個猴子無法擊敗的唯一力量：二郎神，那個有三隻眼的戰神，天庭最強的戰士。而二郎與那猴子搏鬥——一場變化之戰，各自變得比對方更大，那場決鬥升級直到整個宇宙震動。

And the monkey was captured — by trickery, by Laozi's diamond snare, by the combined forces of all the gods. And the Jade Emperor ordered him executed — but the monkey could not be killed. The executioner's sword broke on his neck. The thunderbolts bounced off. He had eaten too many peaches, too many pills; he was immortal many times over. And so Laozi, the alchemist-god, proposed another solution: put the monkey in the Eight Trigrams Furnace, the great alchemical oven, and burn him — for forty-nine days, at the highest heat, to reduce him to ash and recover the elixir he had stolen. And the monkey was put in the furnace, and the fire was lit, and the days passed.

而那猴子被捉了——以詭計，以太上老君的金剛琢，以諸神的合力。而玉帝下令處死他——但那猴子殺不死。劊子手的刀在他脖子上折斷了。雷電彈開了。他吃了太多蟠桃、太多金丹；他長生了好幾重。於是太上老君，那位鍊丹之神，提出了另一個辦法：把猴子放進八卦爐裡，那座大鍊丹爐，燒他——四十九天，以最高的溫度，將他化為灰燼，回收他所偷的丹。而猴子被放進了爐裡，火點燃了，日子過去了。

And on the forty-ninth day, the furnace was opened — and the monkey leapt out, alive, blazing, his eyes now golden and smoking — for the smoke of the furnace had entered his eyes and given them a new power: the fiery golden eyes, that could see through every disguise, every illusion, every deception. The furnace that was meant to destroy him had made him stronger. The Jade Emperor's last attempt at force had failed. And now the monkey, blazing with fury and golden-eyed, stormed the very halls of Heaven itself, and no one — no god, no army, no weapon — could stop him. The Jade Emperor sat on his throne, and the tradition tells us he was afraid. He sent for the only being in the cosmos greater than the monkey. He sent for the Buddha.

而在第四十九天，爐開了——而那猴子跳了出來，活著，灼燒著，他的眼睛如今是金色的、冒著煙——因為爐的煙進入了他的眼睛，給了它們一種新的力量：火眼金睛，能看穿一切偽裝、一切幻象、一切欺騙。那個意在毀他的爐，使他更強了。玉帝最後一次以武力解決的嘗試失敗了。如今那猴子，燃燒著狂怒與金眼，衝進了天庭的殿堂本身，而沒有人——沒有神、沒有軍隊、沒有武器——能阻止他。玉帝坐在他的寶座上，而傳統告訴我們他怕了。他派人請了宇宙中唯一比那猴子更偉大的生靈。他派人請了佛祖。

Chapter 5 · 第五章

The Buddha's Palm

佛祖之掌

And the Buddha came. He did not come with an army, or a weapon, or a display of force. He came calmly, smiling, and he looked at the monkey — the Great Sage Equal to Heaven, blazing with fury, who had defeated every god in the cosmos — and he made a wager. "You are powerful," the Buddha said. "You can leap one hundred and eight thousand li in a single somersault. Very well. Leap from my palm. If you can reach the edge of the cosmos and return, I will acknowledge you as the supreme ruler of Heaven. And if you cannot — you will submit to my discipline."

而佛祖來了。他沒有帶著軍隊、或武器、或力量的展示。他平靜地、微笑著來，他望著那猴子——齊天大聖，燃燒著狂怒，那個擊敗了宇宙中每一位神的人——而他打了一個賭。「你有力量，」佛祖說。「你一個筋斗能翻出十萬八千里。好。從我的掌中跳出去。如果你能到達宇宙的邊緣並返回，我便承認你為天庭的最高統治者。而如果你不能——你便接受我的約束。」

And the monkey laughed. The palm of a hand — he could leap across the world in a single flip. He would be back before the Buddha could blink. And he leapt. He flew — faster than thought, across mountains and oceans and stars, until he came to the edge of everything, to five great pillars, pink and vast, that rose from the ground at the end of the cosmos. And he marked them — urinated on the middle pillar, wrote his name on it, to prove he had been there. And he leapt back, triumphant, and landed in the Buddha's palm. "I won," he said. "I reached the edge of the cosmos. I marked the pillars at the end of the world."

而那猴子笑了。一隻手掌——他一個觔斗便能跨越世界。他會在佛祖眨眼之前回來。而他跳了。他飛了——比思想更快，越過山與海與星辰，直到他來到了一切的邊緣，來到了五根巨大的、粉紅色的柱子，它們從宇宙盡頭的地面升起。而他在上面留下了記號——在中間的柱子上撒了尿，在上面寫了他的名字，以證明他曾到過那裡。而他跳了回來，得勝，落在了佛祖的掌中。「我贏了，」他說。「我到達了宇宙的邊緣。我在世界的盡頭的柱子上留了記號。」

And the Buddha smiled. "Look at my hand," he said. And the monkey looked — and there, on the Buddha's middle finger, was his urine, and his name, and the mark he had made. The five pillars at the edge of the cosmos had been the Buddha's five fingers. The monkey had never left the palm. The being who could defeat every army in Heaven, who had eaten the peaches of immortality and survived the furnace of the gods, had not, in all his vast power, moved beyond the hand of the Buddha. The monkey understood, in that instant, the full scale of his humiliation — that all his power was nothing against the kind of power the Buddha represented. And before he could react, the Buddha turned his hand over, and pushed the monkey down, and a mountain — the Five Elements Mountain — fell on him, and sealed him in. And on the mountain, the Buddha placed a seal. And the monkey was trapped.

而佛祖笑了。「看我的手，」他說。而那猴子看——而在那裡，在佛祖的中指上，是他的尿，他的名字，他所做的記號。那宇宙邊緣的五根柱子，竟是佛祖的五根手指。那個能擊敗天庭每一支軍隊、吃了蟠桃、在神之爐中存活的生靈，在他全部的巨大力量之中，從未離開過佛祖的掌。那猴子在那一瞬明白了他的屈辱的完整尺度——他所有的力量，在佛祖所代表的那種力量面前，什麼也不是。而在他能反應之前，佛祖把手翻了過去，把那猴子推下去，而一座山——五行山——落在他身上，把他封住。而在山上，佛祖放了一個封印。而那猴子被囚了。

Chapter 6 · 第六章

Five Hundred Years Under the Mountain

山下五百年

And for five hundred years, the Great Sage Equal to Heaven lay beneath the mountain. The tradition gives us the image with startling pathos: the monkey who had stormed Heaven, who had been too powerful for gods, now pinned under a rock, fed iron pellets by a local spirit, given copper juice to drink, exposed to the weather, unable to move. And the monkey waited. The monkey who could not sit still for five minutes, who had crossed the world in a single flip, who had declared himself the equal of Heaven — waited. For five hundred years. This is the Daoist-Buddhist lesson at the heart of the monkey's story: that power without wisdom is its own prison, and that the being who refuses all limits will, inevitably, be given a limit he cannot refuse.

而五百年，齊天大聖躺在那座山之下。傳統以驚人的哀婉給了我們這個意象：那個曾衝入天庭、曾強到諸神也奈何不了的猴子，如今被釘在一塊石頭之下，由一個地方小神餵鐵丸、飲銅汁、暴露於風雨、無法動彈。而那猴子等著。那個五分鐘也坐不住、一個觔斗便跨越世界、自封與天齊的猴子——等著。五百年。這便是猴子故事核心的道佛之教：無智之力是其自身的牢獄，而那個拒絕一切限制的生靈，必然，會得到一個他無法拒絕的限制。

And the tradition tells us that the monkey, in those five centuries, changed. Not completely — he would still be impetuous, still powerful, still given to fury. But the crushing, the waiting, the long lesson of being small — it taught him something. It taught him that there were forces greater than strength, and that the greatest of them was not another fighter but the quiet, smiling acceptance of things as they are. The Buddha had not fought him. The Buddha had shown him his own smallness, and that was enough. And the monkey, under the mountain, began — just barely — to understand. And then, one day, a monk came. A mortal monk, on a pilgrimage, sent by the Bodhisattva Guanyin to fetch the scriptures from the West. And the monkey called out to him from beneath the stone. And the monk lifted the seal. And the monkey was free — on one condition. He must serve the monk. He must protect him on the journey. He must wear a golden band around his head, that would tighten when he misbehaved, that the monk could control with a prayer. The monkey who could not be governed by Heaven would be governed by a mortal's prayer. The next stage of our journey begins.

而傳統告訴我們，那猴子在這五個世紀裡，變了。不是完全——他仍然會衝動、仍然強大、仍然易於狂怒。但那場碾壓、那場等待、那場「渺小」的漫長功課——它教了他一些東西。它教了他，有比力量更大的力量，而其中最大的，不是另一位戰士，而是那份「接受事物如其所是」的、安靜的、微笑的接受。佛祖沒有與他戰。佛祖給他看了他自己的渺小，而那就夠了。而那猴子，在山下，開始——只是勉強地——理解。然後，有一天，一個和尚來了。一個凡人僧人，在朝聖，由觀音菩薩派去西方取經。而那猴子從石頭下向他呼喚。而那僧人揭了封。而那猴子自由了——附一個條件。他必須服侍那僧人。他必須在旅途中保護他。他必須在頭上戴一個金箍，當他不規矩時會收緊，而那僧人可以用一句咒來控制。那個連天也管不了的猴子，如今要被一個凡人的咒所管。我們旅程的下一階段開始。

THE MONKEY LEARNED HE WAS SMALL

那猴子學到了自己是渺小的

Born from a stone, he learned the seventy-two transformations, stole the peaches of immortality, named himself the equal of Heaven, and defeated every army the gods could send — until the Buddha, without fighting, showed him that all his power had never left the palm of a single hand. Beneath a mountain, fed iron and copper, the Great Sage Equal to Heaven waited five hundred years — and learned, just barely, the lesson that power without wisdom is its own prison. And then a mortal monk came, and lifted the seal, and the monkey was freed — to serve, to protect, to wear a band that a prayer could tighten. The journey was about to begin.
