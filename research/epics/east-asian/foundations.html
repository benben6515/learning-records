THE DAO, THE KAMI & THE DHARMA

道　、　神　、　與　佛　法

PANGU · NÜWA · IZANAGI & IZANAMI · THE BUDDHA

Before the monkey, before the sun-goddess, before the bell — there were the foundations. And the foundations of the East Asian imagination are not one but three: a Chinese cosmos that makes itself out of its own chaos; a Japanese archipelago birthed, literally, by the first gods coupling on the floating bridge of heaven; and a teaching that came across the sea from India, carrying the single, devastating law that would color everything — that all conditioned things are impermanent, and that the very beauty of the world is inseparable from its passing. 在猴子之前，在太陽女神之前，在鐘之前——先有根基。而東亞想像的根基不是一個，而是三個：一個從自身混沌中自行造就的中國宇宙；一個字面上由最初之神在天之浮橋上交合而生的日本列島；以及一個從印度渡海而來的教誨，帶著那將染上一切的唯一、毀滅性的法則——一切有為法皆是無常，而世界的美，與其消逝不可分割。

Chapter 1 · 第一章

Pangu & the Cosmic Egg

盤古與渾沌之卵

In the Chinese beginning, there was no god speaking a world into being. There was, instead, an egg — a great cosmic egg, in which all the matter of the universe was mixed together, undifferentiated, heavy and chaotic, like a vast unhatched thought. And inside that egg, for eighteen thousand years, slept a giant named Pangu — neither god nor man, but something older, the first being, curled in the primordial chaos, waiting.

在中國的開端，沒有一位神說出世界。反而，有一顆卵——一顆巨大的宇宙之卵，其中宇宙所有的物質都混在一起，未分化、沉重而混亂，如同一個巨大的、未孵化的念頭。而在那顆卵裡，一萬八千年，沉睡著一個名叫盤古的巨人——非神亦非人，而是某種更古老的東西，最初的生靈，蜷曲於太初的混沌之中，等待著。

And then Pangu woke, and swung his great axe, and the egg split. And the light, pure part rose up and became the sky; the heavy, impure part sank down and became the earth. And Pangu stood between them, his head holding up the sky, his feet braced on the earth, and he grew — ten feet taller every day, the sky rising ten feet, the earth thickening ten feet — for another eighteen thousand years, until the two were finally set apart, never to close again. The separation was the first act. The cosmos begins, in the Chinese imagination, not with a creation but with a sorting — the untangling of a tangle that was always there.

然後盤古醒了，揮動他的大斧，那顆卵裂開了。而那輕的、純的部分升起成了天；那重的、不純的部分沉下成了地。而盤古立在兩者之間，他的頭頂著天，他的腳撐著地，而他生長著——每天長高十尺，天升高十尺，地加厚十尺——又一萬八千年，直到兩者終於被分開，永不再合。那場分離是第一個行動。在中國的想像裡，宇宙之始，不是一個創造，而是一場整理——一場對一個始終在那裡的糾結的解開。

And then, his work done, Pangu died — and his body became the world. His breath became the wind and the clouds; his voice became the thunder; his left eye the sun, his right eye the moon; his blood the rivers; his veins the roads; his flesh the soil; his hair the stars; his sweat the rain; and the fleas on his body, stirred by the wind, became the human beings. The whole physical world, in the Chinese imagination, is the body of the first giant, who sorted the chaos and then gave himself to become the place where life could happen. This is the deep frame of Chinese cosmology: the world is not made FOR us. The world is made OF someone. And we — the fleas on a giant's body, the afterthoughts of a sorting — live inside a corpse that is also a garden.

然後，他的工作完成，盤古死了——而他的身軀成了世界。他的氣息成了風與雲；他的聲音成了雷；他的左眼成了太陽，右眼成了月亮；他的血成了河；他的脈成了路；他的肉成了土；他的髮成了星；他的汗成了雨；而他的身體上的跳蚤，被風所攪動，成了人類。在中國的想像裡，整個物質世界，是那位最初巨人的身體，他整理了混沌，然後把自己獻出，成為生命可以發生之地。這是中國宇宙觀的深層框架：世界不是「為」我們而造。世界是「由」某個人所造。而我們——一個巨人身上的跳蚤，一場整理的後續——活在一具屍體之內，而那屍體也是一座花園。

Chapter 2 · 第二章

Nüwa Mends the Sky

女媧補天

And the creator of humankind, in the Chinese tradition, is not Pangu but a goddess named Nüwa — and Nüwa is one of the strangest and most beloved figures in all of mythology. She is depicted, the tradition tells us, with the head and arms of a woman and the body of a snake or a dragon — a being of two natures, the human above and the serpentine below, the meeting-point of the civilized and the wild. And she created human beings, the tradition says, because she was lonely.

而在中國傳統裡，人類的創造者不是盤古，而是一位名叫女媧的女神——而女媧是全部神話中最奇異、也最受鍾愛的形象之一。傳統告訴我們，她被描繪成一個有著女子的頭與臂、卻有著蛇或龍的身軀的生靈——一個二性的生靈，上面是人，下面是蛇，文明與野性的交會點。而她創造了人類，傳統說，因為她寂寞。

The tradition gives us the famous scene: Nüwa, walking by a pool, sees her reflection, and is seized by the desire to make something in her own image. And she kneels by the bank and takes the yellow clay, and begins to shape it, carefully, by hand, into the form of a small being — and the being comes alive, and dances, and calls her mother. And she makes another, and another, and another, each one unique. But the world was large, and her hands were slow, and so, the tradition tells us, she grew impatient — and she took a rope, and dragged it through the mud, and flicked the drops of clay into the air, and each drop became a person. The hand-made ones, the tradition says, became the nobles; the rope-flicked ones became the common people. The Chinese tradition, here, is characteristically unflinching: it tells the truth about inequality, and it makes the inequality itself part of the creation — and it does so with a dry humor that lets the reader decide whether to be comforted or offended.

傳統給了我們那個著名的場面：女媧，走在池邊，看見自己的倒影，被一種想要按照自己的形象造點什麼的渴望所攫住。而她跪在岸邊，取了黃色的黏土，開始仔細地、用手將它塑成一個小小的生靈之形——而那生靈活了過來，跳舞，叫她母親。而她又造了一個，又一個，又一個，每一個都獨一無二。但世界很大，而她的手很慢，於是，傳統告訴我們，她不耐煩了——而她取了一根繩子，拖過泥中，把黏土的滴點甩向空中，而每一滴都成了一個人。傳統說，那些手做的成了貴族；那些繩甩的成了平民。中國傳統，在此處，是典型的毫不退縮：它說出了關於不平等的真相，而它使那不平等本身成為創造的一部分——而它以一種冷峻的幽默來做這件事，讓讀者自己決定是要感到安慰還是被冒犯。

But Nüwa's greatest deed was not the making of humans. It was the mending of the sky. The tradition tells us that, in some ancient catastrophe, the pillar of heaven broke — the sky tilted, the earth cracked, fire raged unquenched, floods drowned the land, and the whole cosmos threatened to return to chaos. And Nüwa, the mother of humanity, would not let her children perish. She gathered stones of five colors — the five colors of the five elements — and melted them into a kind of cosmic plaster, and patched the broken sky. And she cut the legs off a great cosmic turtle and used them as new pillars to hold the sky up. The mother who made us also saved us. And the patched sky, the tradition says, is the sky we still live under — a sky that was once broken and is held together by a goddess's plaster and a turtle's legs. The Chinese imagination, here, gives us a cosmos that is fragile, that has been broken and repaired, and whose repair is the work of a woman.

但女媧最偉大的功業不是造人。而是補天。傳統告訴我們，在某場太古的災變中，天柱斷了——天傾了，地裂了，大火無法撲滅，洪水淹沒了大地，而整個宇宙威脅著要回到混沌。而女媧，人類的母親，不肯讓她的孩子們滅亡。她收集了五色的石頭——五行之五色——把它們熔成一種宇宙的灰泥，補好了破碎的天空。而她砍下一隻宇宙巨龜的腿，用它們作為撐起天空的新柱。造我們的母親，也救了我們。而那個補好的天，傳統說，便是我們仍生活於其下的天——一個曾被破碎、而由一位女神的灰泥與一隻龜的腿所撐起的天。中國的想像，在此處，給了我們一個脆弱的、曾被打破又被修補的宇宙，而其修補，是一位女子的工作。

Chapter 3 · 第三章

The Dao & the Flow

道與流轉

But beneath all the gods and giants, the Chinese imagination has a deeper current — a worldview that is not, strictly, a mythology at all, but a way of seeing the world that has shaped everything Chinese ever after. This is the Dao — the Way. And the Dao is not a god. It is not a person. It is the underlying pattern of reality, the way things flow, the way the river runs and the seasons turn and the breath moves in and out. "The Dao that can be named is not the eternal Dao," begins the great classic — and the point is that the Dao cannot be captured in words, because it is the thing the words are made of.

但在所有的神與巨人之底下，中國的想像有一股更深的潮流——一個嚴格說來根本不是神話、卻塑造了其後一切中國事物的世界觀。這便是道——the Way。而道不是一位神。它不是一個人。它是現實的底層模式，事物流轉的方式，河流運行的方式，四季更替的方式，呼吸進出的方式。「道可道，非常道，」那部偉大的經典如此開始——而重點是，道不能用言語捕捉，因為它是言語所由構成的那個東西。

And the Dao expresses itself through the interplay of two forces — yin and yang — which are not good and evil, but the two complementary halves of every whole: dark and light, cold and hot, resting and moving, female and male, earth and heaven. Nothing is purely yin or purely yang; everything contains both, and the balance shifts, forever, in a great turning. The yin-yang symbol itself — the taijitu, the circle with the two comma-shapes chasing each other, each holding a dot of the other — is the perfect image of this: a world in constant motion, in which every force carries the seed of its opposite, and in which nothing stays what it is.

而道透過兩種力量的交相作用來表達自身——陰與陽——它們不是善與惡，而是每一個整體的兩個互補之半：暗與明、冷與熱、靜與動、女與男、地與天。沒有什麼是純陰或純陽；每一物都包含兩者，而那平衡永遠在一個偉大的轉動中移動。太極圖本身——那個圓中有兩個逗號之形彼此追逐、各自含著對方一點的圖——便是此事的完美意象：一個永恆運動的世界，其中每一股力量都帶著其對立之物的種子，而其中沒有什麼停留在它所是之物。

This worldview — the flowing, the turning, the constant transformation — is the soil from which every Chinese story grows, and it is the reason the Chinese imagination is so different from the Western one. In the Greek cosmos, things are made and endure. In the Chinese cosmos, nothing endures; everything flows. The hero is not the one who builds something permanent. The hero — or rather, the sage — is the one who understands the flow, and moves with it, and does not waste strength fighting the current. This is the deep teaching that will shape the Journey to the West, and that stands behind every Chinese story in this cycle: that the world is a river, not a building, and that wisdom is the art of the swimmer, not the mason. The Dao flows. And the stories that follow are all, in their way, about learning to swim.

這個世界觀——那流轉、那轉動、那不斷的變化——是每一個中國故事所從出的土壤，而它是中國想像如此不同於西方的原因。在希臘的宇宙裡，事物被造並持久。在中國的宇宙裡，沒有什麼持久；一切流轉。英雄不是那個建造永恆之物的人。英雄——或者更確切地說，那位聖者——是那個理解那流轉、隨之而行、不浪費力氣與潮流相抗之人。這便是那將塑造《西遊記》、而立於這個循環中每一個中國故事背後的深層教誨：世界是一條河，不是一棟建築，而智慧是游泳者的藝術，而非泥水匠的。道流轉。而接下來的故事，各自以自己的方式，都是關於學習游泳。

Chapter 4 · 第四章

The Kami & the Spear of Heaven

神與天之瓊矛

Across the sea, the Japanese imagination begins differently — and it begins, like so many beginnings, with a couple on a bridge. The first gods, Izanagi and Izanami — the male-who-invites and the female-who-invites — stood on the Floating Bridge of Heaven and looked down at the formless waters below. And Izanagi took the Jeweled Spear of Heaven, and thrust it down into the sea, and stirred, and stirred, and when he drew the spear up, the brine dripping from its tip coagulated and piled up and formed the first island. On that island the two gods descended, and built a pillar, and around it they performed the first marriage.

在海的對岸，日本的想像以不同的方式開始——而它開始，一如許多的開始，以一對在橋上的男女。最初的神，伊邪那岐與伊邪那美——「招募之男」與「招聘之女」——站在天之浮橋上，俯視下方無形的水。而伊邪那岐取了天之瓊矛，刺入海中，攪動，攪動，而當他把矛抽起之時，從矛尖滴落的鹽水凝結、堆積，而形成了第一座島。在那座島上，兩位神降下，立了一根柱，而繞著它行了第一場婚禮。

And from their union the islands of Japan were born — not a world they made, but a place they bore, a country that is literally the child of its gods. This is the deep difference between the Chinese and the Japanese beginnings: in China, the world is made by a giant's body; in Japan, the country is born by a goddess's womb. The Japanese archipelago is not territory the gods conquered. It is their offspring. And this is why the Japanese tradition holds the land itself as sacred in a way few other traditions do — because the land is family, the land is the gods' own child, and to walk on Japan is to walk on the body of something divine.

而從他們的結合，日本諸島誕生了——不是一個他們所造的世界，而是一個他們所生的地方，一個字面上是其神之子的國度。這便是中國與日本開端的深層差異：在中國，世界由一個巨人的身體所造；在日本，這個國家由一位女神的子宮所生。日本列島不是諸神所征服的領土。它們是它們的後代。而這便是為什麼日本傳統以一種少有其他傳統能及的方式，視土地本身為神聖——因為土地是家人，土地是諸神親生的孩子，而走在日本之上，便是走在某種神聖之物的身體上。

And the kami — the spirits — are everywhere in this land. A kami is not a god in the Western sense, a person on a throne. A kami is a spirit, a presence, the aliveness of a thing: the kami of a river, the kami of a mountain, the kami of a tree. The Japanese landscape is not dead matter. It is alive, full of presences, and the human beings who live among them are not masters but neighbors. This is the foundation of Shinto, the Way of the Kami — and it is the worldview that will receive, in the next chapter, the teaching that crosses the sea from India. The kami fill the land. The land is the gods' child. And over the water, a new idea is coming: that all of this — the islands, the kami, the beautiful green land — is impermanent, and that its very beauty is the proof of it.

而那些神——那些精靈——遍布這片土地。一位神不是西方意義上的神，一個坐在寶座上的人。一位神是一個精靈、一個臨在、一個事物的活潑性：一條河的神、一座山的神、一棵樹的神。日本的風景不是死物。它是活的，充滿臨在，而生活於其中的，不是主人，而是鄰居。這便是神道——神之道——的根基，而它便是那個將在下一章裡，接收那個從印度渡海而來之教誨的世界觀。諸神充滿這片土地。這片土地是諸神的孩子。而越過水面，一個新的觀念正在到來：這一切——諸島、諸神、那片美麗的翠綠土地——是無常的，而它的美本身，便是它的證明。

Chapter 5 · 第五章

The Dharma Crosses the Sea

佛法渡海

And then, around the middle of the first millennium, a teaching crossed the sea from India into China, and from China into Japan — and it changed everything. It was Buddhism — the Dharma — and it carried, at its heart, a single, devastating idea: that all conditioned things are impermanent. Everything that is made, everything that depends on causes, everything that exists in time — all of it, without exception, is passing. And the passing is not a flaw to be repaired. The passing is the nature of things. And the beauty of the world — the cherry blossom, the morning, the youth, the empire — is beautiful precisely because it passes, and to grasp at it, to try to hold it still, is to suffer.

然後，大約在第一個千年的中葉，一個教誨從印度渡海進入中國，又從中國進入日本——而它改變了一切。它是佛教——法——而它在核心處帶著一個單一的、毀滅性的觀念：一切有為法皆是無常。每一個被造之物，每一個依條件而生之物，每一個存在於時間中的事物——全部，無一例外，都在消逝。而那消逝不是一個待修補的缺陷。那消逝是事物的本性。而世界的美——櫻花、清晨、青春、帝國——之所以美，正因為它消逝，而 grasping at it、試圖將它定住，便是受苦。

And this teaching met the two native traditions — the Chinese Dao, the Japanese kami — and did not destroy them. It married them. In China, Buddhism met the Dao, and the two produced Chan — what the Japanese would call Zen — a tradition of sudden enlightenment that fused the Indian teaching of impermanence with the Chinese love of flow. In Japan, Buddhism met the kami, and produced a peculiarly Japanese grief: the sense that the beautiful green land, the land of the gods, was itself transient, and that its very sacredness was a kind of sorrow, because what is sacred must also pass.

而這個教誨與兩個本土傳統——中國的道、日本的神——相遇，而沒有毀掉它們。它與它們結合了。在中國，佛教與道相遇，而兩者產生了禪——日本人所稱的 Zen——一個將印度關於無常的教誨與中國對流轉的愛融合在一起的頓悟傳統。在日本，佛教與神相遇，而產生了一種特殊的日本式悲愴：那種感覺，那片美麗的翠綠土地、諸神之地，本身是短暫的，而它那神聖本身便是一種悲傷，因為神聖之物也必將消逝。

And so the stage is set for all the stories that follow. The Chinese Journey to the West will be a Buddhist pilgrimage — a band of sinners walking the long road to enlightenment, learning, trial by trial, to let go. The Japanese Tale of the Heike will be a Buddhist elegy — the rise and fall of a great clan, told from the beginning as impermanent, the sound of a bell that rings the passing of everything. And the Japanese Age of the Gods will be a Shinto foundation — the birth of a land from the gods, the kami in every hill and river, the sacredness of place that makes the Japanese grief over its passing so much keener. The Dao flows. The kami fill the land. And the Dharma says: all of it passes. Hold on lightly. The foundations are laid. The monkey is waiting in his stone.

於是，為接下來所有故事的舞台便搭好了。中國的《西遊記》將是一場佛教的朝聖——一群罪人走著通往覺悟的漫長之路，一難一難地學習放下。日本的《平家物語》將是一首佛教的輓歌——一個大族的興衰，從一開始便被講述為無常，一座鐘聲，響著萬物之消逝。而日本的「神代」將是一個神道的根基——一片由諸神所生之土地，每一丘每一河中的神，那份使日本對其消逝的悲傷如此銳利的、地方的神聖。道流轉。諸神充滿這片土地。而法說：這一切都將過去。輕輕地執著。根基已立。那隻猴子在他的石頭裡等待著。

THREE STREAMS, ONE CURRENT

三股潮流，一道水流

A giant split an egg and died to become the world. A snake-goddess patched the broken sky with colored stones and the legs of a turtle. The Dao flows beneath everything, turning, never still. Across the sea, the first gods stirred the ocean with a jeweled spear and bore the islands as their own children. And then a teaching came across the water, carrying the law that colors it all: everything passes, and the beautiful is beautiful because it fades. Three foundations laid. Now the stories begin — and the first is about a monkey, born from a stone, who would not accept his place.
