THE BOOK THAT SURVIVES

菲　爾　多　西　與　不　朽　之　書

ABU'L-QASIM FERDOWSI OF TUS · c. 940–1020 AD

An empire fell. A religion was silenced. A language began to forget its own old words. And one old poet, in a town in the corner of Persia, decided that a people who lose their stories have lost themselves — and spent thirty years and sixty thousand couplets proving it. This is the story of how a book saved a civilization, and how the man who wrote it died believing he had failed. 一個帝國傾覆了。一種宗教被噤聲了。一種語言開始遺忘自己古老的詞語。而一位老詩人，在波斯一個角落的小鎮裡，認定一個失去故事的民族便已失去了自身——並用三十年與六萬聯詩句證明了它。這是一本書如何拯救一個文明的故事，也是寫下它的人，至死都相信自己失敗了的故事。

Chapter 1 · 第一章

The Coming of the Arabs

阿拉伯人的到來

In the year 636 of the common era, on a plain called Qadisiyya, the army of the Sasanian king met the army of the Arabs — men from the desert, fired by a new faith, carrying a book of their own. The battle was long and terrible. The Persians were armoured, trained, ancient; the Arabs were lean and hungry and believed. Rostam, the general of the Persian host (not the hero, but a namesake across twelve centuries), fell in the fighting; a sandstorm blew up; and by its end, the army that had stood for Iran since Ardashir was broken.

公元六三六年，在一個叫卡迪西亞的平原上，薩珊王的軍隊與阿拉伯人的軍隊相遇——那是來自沙漠的人，被一種新信仰所點燃，帶著一本他們自己的書。戰事漫長而可怖。波斯人披甲、訓練有素、古老；阿拉伯人精瘦、飢餓、且篤信。波斯大軍的統帥羅斯塔姆（不是那位英雄，而是跨越十二個世紀的同名者）戰死於陣中；一場沙暴颳起；而到它結束之時，自阿爾達希爾以來為伊朗而立的那支軍隊，已經潰散了。

The end came slowly and then all at once. Battle after battle; city after city. The last Sasanian king, Yazdegerd III, fled east, trying to rally the provinces, trying to hold together what was already falling apart. The fire-temples — those glowing sanctuaries that Ardashir had reopened four hundred years before, each one a fixed flame tended day and night by hereditary priests — were put out, one by one, as the new rulers advanced. The religion of Zoroaster, which had been the soul of Iran for a thousand years, was silenced in the land of its birth. The throne, the crown, the farr — all of it passed to the desert and the new book.

終局來得緩慢，然後忽然到來。一場又一場的戰役；一座又一座的城。最後一位薩珊王，雅茲德吉爾德三世，向東逃亡，試圖重整行省，試圖維繫已經在崩解之物。火祠——那些阿爾達希爾在四百年前重新開啟的光輝聖所，每一座都是一盆固定的火焰，由世襲的祭司日夜守護——隨著新統治者推進，一座一座地被撲滅。祆教，作為伊朗之魂已達千年，在它的誕生之地被噤聲了。王座、王冠、法爾——一切都歸於沙漠與那本新書。

Ferdowsi tells this part of the story with a controlled grief, like a man holding back tears at a funeral. He does not rage at the Arabs; he does not call them monsters. He simply records the loss — the lights going out, the flames dying, the throne emptying — and lets the reader feel the weight of it. Because his point, the whole point of the book he is about to give us, is that material power can be taken, temples can be destroyed, kings can be killed — but a story, once written down and kept, can outlive all of it. Iran was about to lose its throne. It was about to keep its poem.

菲爾多西講述這一部分，帶著一種克制的悲慟，如一個人在葬禮上強忍淚水。他並不對阿拉伯人發怒；他不稱他們為怪獸。他只是記錄那失去——燈熄了、火焰死了、王座空了——讓讀者感覺到它的重量。因為他的重點，他將要給我們的那本書的全部重點，在於：物質的權力可以被奪走，神廟可以被毀，君王可以被殺——但一個故事，一旦被寫下並保存，能比這一切更長壽。伊朗即將失去它的王座。它即將保住它的詩。

Chapter 2 · 第二章

The Death of the Last King

末代君王之死

Yazdegerd III, the last king, did not die like a king. He fled further and further east, a wandering sovereign in the ruins of his own empire, until he reached a mill on the edge of Merv. There, in 651, a miller — some say bribed, some say simply cruel — struck him down for his jewels. And so the line of kings that had begun with Gayumars on a mountain, that had passed through Jamshid and Fereydun and Rostam's masters and Sikandar and Ardashir, ended in a mill-yard, robbed by the man who ground its grain.

最後一位君王，雅茲德吉爾德三世，死得不像一位君王。他越逃越東，一個在自己帝國廢墟中流浪的君王，直到他到達木鹿邊緣的一座磨坊。在那裡，公元六五一年，一個磨坊匠——有人說是受了賄，有人說只是純然的殘忍——為了他的珠寶而將他擊殺。於是，那始於蓋尤瑪爾斯在山巔、經過賈姆希德、費雷敦、羅斯塔姆的諸王、亞歷山大、阿爾達希爾的列王之脈，終結於一座磨坊的院子裡，被磨其穀之人所劫。

Ferdowsi pauses on the ignominy, because it matters. This was not a death in battle, not a sacrifice, not even an execution — it was a squalid murder for trinkets. And the poem makes of it a parable: power, when it is gone, is worth no more than the gold on a man's body. The king who thought himself eternal was killed for his rings. The throne that had commanded armies was taken by a miller's blow. The farr had departed long before the fatal hour — departed the moment the king had nothing left to defend but himself.

菲爾多西在那屈辱上著墨，因為那很重要。那不是死於戰場，不是一場犧牲，甚至不是一場處決——那是為了飾物而進行的一樁齷齪謀殺。詩以此做成一則寓言：權力，當它逝去之時，其價值不過是一個男人身上的黃金。那自以為永恆的君王，為了他的戒指而死。那曾號令大軍的王座，被一個磨坊匠的一擊所奪。法爾早在那致命的時辰之前便已離去——在君王除了自身之外再無可守之物的那一刻便已離去。

And yet — and this is Ferdowsi's quiet counter-statement — the miller did not get to write the ending. The king died, but the memory of kings did not. The Sasanians fell, but the idea of Iran did not. Somewhere, in the houses of the learned, the priests and scribes who had served the old court were copying down, in the Pahlavi script, everything they could remember — the lists of kings, the deeds of heroes, the shapes of the old stories. They knew what was coming: the slow erosion of a language, the fading of a faith, the forgetting of a past. And they fought that erosion the only way they could — by writing. They did not know that one of their readers, three centuries later, would be a boy in Tus who would grow up to finish what they started.

然而——而這是菲爾多西寧靜的反向陳述——那磨坊匠並未能寫下結局。君王死了，但列王的記憶沒有。薩珊傾覆了，但伊朗的理念沒有。在不知何處的學者之家，曾侍奉舊朝的祭司與書記，正以巴列維文字抄寫他們所能記起的一切——列王的譜系、英雄的功業、古老故事的輪廓。他們知道即將到來的是什麼：一種語言的緩慢侵蝕、一種信仰的褪色、一段過去的遺忘。而他們以唯一能夠的方式對抗那侵蝕——書寫。他們不知道，他們的一位讀者，在三個世紀之後，將是一個圖斯的男孩，他將長大，完成他們所開始的事。

Chapter 3 · 第三章

The Age of Forgetting

遺忘的時代

Three centuries passed. Arabic became the language of power, of prayer, of learning; Persian retreated into the home and the village. The old stories, transmitted in Pahlavi, grew harder to read with each generation, for fewer and fewer could read the old script. The memory of Rostam, of the Seven Labors, of Zahhak and Kaveh, of Jamshid and the farr — all of it began to thin, like a voice calling across a widening river. This is the slow death that Ferdowsi feared most: not the burning of a temple, which is sudden and dramatic, but the forgetting of a word, which is quiet and irreversible.

三個世紀過去了。阿拉伯語成了權力、祈禱、學問的語言；波斯語退入了家庭與村落。以巴列維文傳承的古老故事，每一代都越來越難讀，因為能讀那古老文字的人越來越少。羅斯塔姆的記憶、七項試煉、扎哈克與卡維、賈姆希德與法爾——這一切開始變薄，如一個聲音在越來越寬的河對岸呼喊。這便是菲爾多西最恐懼的那種緩慢之死：不是一座神廟的焚毀（那是突然而戲劇性的），而是一個詞的遺忘（那是寧靜而不可逆的）。

And yet Persian did not die — and this, too, is part of the story. It changed. It took in new words, new rhythms, new forms; it became, slowly, the language we now call New Persian, written in the Arabic script but with its old grammar and its old soul. Poets began to write in it again, court by court, in the Samanid east and the Buyid west. And in the houses of the learned, the old Pahlavi chronicles still sat on the shelves, waiting — waiting for someone who could read them, and love them, and turn them into something that the new language could carry forward.

然而波斯語並未死去——而這，也是故事的一部分。它改變了。它吸收了新的詞、新的韻律、新的形式；它慢慢地，變成了我們現在稱之為新波斯語的那種語言，以阿拉伯字母書寫，卻保留著古老的語法與古老的靈魂。詩人開始再度以它寫作，一個朝廷接一個朝廷，在薩曼的東方與布伊的西方。而在學者之家，古老的巴列維編年仍靜臥架上，等待著——等待某個能讀懂它們、熱愛它們、並將它們轉化為新語言所能承載之物的人。

This is the age that made Ferdowsi necessary. Without it — without the three centuries of slow forgetting — there would have been no urgency, no need for the Shahnameh. A culture that remembers effortlessly does not need a poet to remember for it. It was precisely because Iran was forgetting that a man had to sit down and write down, in the new language, in the new script, all that the old language was losing. The Age of Forgetting was not a prelude to the poem; it was the reason the poem had to exist. And into that age, in a town called Tus, a boy was born who would grow up to be its answer.

這個時代使菲爾多西成為必要。若沒有它——若沒有那三個世紀的緩慢遺忘——便不會有緊迫，不會有對《列王紀》的需要。一個毫不費力便能記憶的文化，不需要一位詩人來為它記憶。正因為伊朗正在遺忘，才必須有一個人坐下來，以新語言、以新文字，寫下舊語言正在失去的一切。遺忘的時代並非這首詩的序曲；它是這首詩必須存在的理由。而在那個時代裡，在一個叫圖斯的小鎮，一個男孩出生了，他將長大，成為它的回答。

Chapter 4 · 第四章

A Poet in Tus

圖斯的詩人

His full name was Abu'l-Qasim Ferdowsi Tusi, and he was born around the year 940, in the district of Tus, in the north-east of Persia. He came, it seems, from a family of minor landholders — dehqans, the class of country gentry that had, for centuries, been the keepers of the old stories. The dehqans were the living archive of Iran: when the temples fell and the court fled, it was they, in their villages, who remembered. They told the tales to their sons, and their sons told them to theirs; and Ferdowsi, growing up among them, drank in the memory of Rostam and the kings as other children drank in milk.

他的全名是阿布·卡西姆·菲爾多西·圖西，約生於公元九四○年，在波斯東北圖斯地區。他似乎出身於一個小地主之家——德赫干，那個數世紀以來作為古老故事守護者的鄉紳階層。德赫干是伊朗的活檔案：當神廟傾覆、朝廷逃亡之時，正是他們，在他們的村莊裡，記住了一切。他們把故事講給兒子聽，兒子再講給他們的兒子聽；而菲爾多西，在他們之中長大，飲下羅斯塔姆與列王的記憶，一如別的孩子飲下乳汁。

He was, by all accounts, a man of learning — he could read the old Pahlavi, he knew the Arabic learning of his day, he had studied the chronicles that the Samanid court had begun to gather. And he had, it seems, one great wound that became his life's work: the realization that the language he loved was dying, and that the stories it carried were dying with it. A language can survive conquest, he understood; it cannot survive being unused. And the only way to make a language live is to make something beautiful in it — something so beautiful that people will want to learn it, just to read what was written.

據各方記載，他是一位博學之人——他能讀古老的巴列維文，他懂得當時的阿拉伯學問，他研習過薩曼朝廷已開始搜集的編年。而據說，他有一道大傷口，後來成了他一生的事業：他意識到他所愛的語言正在死去，而它所承載的故事正隨之死去。他明白，一種語言能挺過征服；它挺不過不被使用。而讓一種語言活下來的唯一方法，是在其中造出某種美的東西——美到人們會想學它，只為了讀懂所寫下的東西。

So he made a vow — and Ferdowsi, the man, is as important to his poem as any king in it. He would take the old chronicles, the dehqan's memory, the Pahlavi prose, and he would turn them all into verse. Not just any verse: verse in the purest Persian he could manage, using as few Arabic words as possible, so that the language itself — the old sounds, the old compounds, the old music — might be preserved inside the poem like a fly in amber. He did not know, when he began, that it would take thirty years. He did not know it would be sixty thousand couplets. He did not know he would die over it. He only knew that if he did not do it, no one would, and Iran would forget who it was.

於是他立下一誓——而菲爾多西這個人，對他的詩而言，與詩中任何一位君王同等重要。他要把古老的編年、德赫干的記憶、巴列維的散文，全部轉成詩。不是隨便的詩：是他所能寫出的最純粹的波斯語詩，盡可能少用阿拉伯詞，好讓語言本身——古老的音、古老的複合詞、古老的樂音——能如琥珀中之蟲，被保存在詩中。他開始之時，並不知道這要花三十年。他不知道會是六萬聯。他不知道他會為之而死。他只知道：若他不做，便無人會做，而伊朗將忘記自己是誰。

Chapter 5 · 第五章

Sixty Thousand Couplets

六萬聯詩句

For thirty years, Ferdowsi wrote. He wrote in a single meter — a rolling, majestic rhythm that carries the reader like a river through a thousand years of kings. He wrote in the purest Persian he could find, hunting for old words in the dehqan's speech, refusing the easy Arabic loanwords that would have made the work quicker. A line in the Shahnameh can take ten times as long to compose as a line in the fashionable style, because Ferdowsi was not writing for speed; he was writing for survival. Every old word he placed in the poem was a word rescued from extinction.

三十年，菲爾多西寫作。他以一種格律寫——一種滾滾而威嚴的節奏，承載讀者如一條河流穿越千年的列王。他以他所能找到的最純粹的波斯語寫，在德赫干的口語裡搜尋古老的詞，拒絕那些能使工作更快的阿拉伯借詞。《列王紀》中的一行，其創作可能要花上時髦風格一行的十倍時間，因為菲爾多西不是為速度而寫；他是為存續而寫。他放進詩中的每一個古老之詞，都是一個從滅絕中被救回的詞。

Sixty thousand couplets. Three times the length of the Iliad. The whole sweep of Iran's memory, from the first king on the mountain to the murder of the last king at a mill — every demon, every hero, every tragedy, every restoration — set down in a single great book that one old man composed, mostly alone, mostly poor, in a house in Tus. There were helpers, of course; there were patrons who paid intermittently; but the work, the vast fabric of it, was his. He carried it the way Rakhsh carried Rostam — a load no one else could bear.

六萬聯詩句。三倍於《伊利亞特》的長度。伊朗記憶的全部洪流，從山巔的第一代君王到磨坊邊最後一位君王之謀殺——每一個魔、每一個英雄、每一場悲劇、每一次復興——盡納於一部大書之中，由一個老人，多半獨自、多半貧窮地，在圖斯的一所房子裡寫成。當然有幫手；有間歇資助的贊助人；但那部作品，它浩瀚的織體，是他的。他扛著它，一如拉赫什扛著羅斯塔姆——一個旁人無法承受的重擔。

And here is the thing to understand: Ferdowsi was not writing history, and he knew it. He was writing memory. He took the chronicles and shaped them; he took the legends and gave them form; he took the scattered songs and made of them one Song. He was, in the deepest sense, an artist — and his art was the art of a man trying to save something larger than himself. The Shahnameh is not accurate history (the Pishdadian kings did not really live nine hundred years each); but it is true memory, in the way that only a poem can be true. It tells Iranians not what literally happened, but who they are — and that, Ferdowsi understood, is what a people most needs from its past.

而這裡有一點必須明白：菲爾多西不是在寫歷史，他知道這一點。他是在寫記憶。他取那些編年而塑之；他取那些傳說而賦之以形；他取那些散落之歌而使之成為一首歌。從最深層的意義上說，他是一位藝術家——而他的藝術，是一個試圖拯救某種比自身更大之物的人的藝術。《列王紀》不是準確的歷史（那些皮什達德王朝的君王並非真的各自活了九百年）；但它是真實的記憶，以一種只有詩才能為真實的方式。它告訴伊朗人的，不是字面上發生了什麼，而是他們是誰——而菲爾多西明白，這才是一個民族最需要從其過去得到的東西。

Chapter 6 · 第六章

The Last Stanza & the Garden Grave

最後一聯與花園之墓

The poem was finished around the year 1010, when Ferdowsi was an old man. He had intended it as a gift to the reigning sultan, Mahmud of Ghazna — but Mahmud, a Sunni with little love for the Shi'a Persians and their old heroes, mocked the work and paid the aged poet a pittance. Ferdowsi, in fury and humiliation, gave the money away — to a bathhouse-keeper and a beer-seller, it is said — and went home to Tus a broken man. He believed, in the end of his life, that he had failed: that the book he had given thirty years to would be forgotten, that Iran would forget itself anyway, that the whole vast labour had been for nothing.

這首詩約在公元一○一○年完成，那時菲爾多西已是個老人。他本欲將之作為禮物獻給在位的蘇丹、加茲尼的馬哈茂德——但馬哈茂德，一個對什葉派波斯人及其古老英雄少有敬遜的遜尼派者，嘲諷了這部作品，只付給年邁的詩人一點微薄的酬金。菲爾多西，在憤怒與屈辱之中，把錢散了出去——據說給了一個澡堂守門人和一個賣酒之人——而後帶著破碎的心回到了圖斯。在他生命的盡頭，他相信自己失敗了：他奉獻了三十年的那本書將被遺忘，伊朗反正會忘記自己，整個浩瀚的勞作都是白費。

He wrote his own ending — a short closing section in which he names himself, gives his age, prays for his soul, and asks that anyone who reads the book remember him kindly. It is one of the most moving passages in all of literature: the poet, old, poor, ignored, asking not for glory but for a kind word from a reader he will never meet. "I have reached the end of the Book of Kings," he wrote; "my hair is white, and my back is bent. I am sixty-five years old. I have set down all I remembered. May whoever reads it pray for my soul." And then, a few years later, around 1020, he died.

他寫下了自己的結尾——一段簡短的收束，他在其中報出姓名、道出年歲、為自己的靈魂祈禱，並請求任何讀這本書之人善待地記住他。這是全部文學中最動人的段落之一：那位詩人，年老、貧窮、被忽視，所求的不是榮耀，而是一位他永遠不會遇見的讀者的一句好話。「我已走到了《列王紀》的盡頭，」他寫道；「我的頭髮白了，我的背彎了。我六十五歲。我已記下我所記起的一切。願讀它之人為我的靈魂祈禱。」然後，幾年之後，約一○二○年，他死了。

And here is the ending Ferdowsi did not live to see. The local clerics, considering him insufficiently orthodox, refused him burial in the Muslim cemetery. So his body was laid, instead, in his own garden — among the trees and the water, in the corner of Tus — and a modest tomb marked the spot. But the book did not die. It was copied, and copied again, and carried into every Persian-speaking house, and read aloud to children, and learned by heart, and loved, until the language Ferdowsi had tried to save became the language that could not be imagined without him. Iran survived — not as an empire, not as a throne, not as a faith, but as a poem. And the old poet who thought he had failed saved more than any king in his book ever did. The last king died at a mill, robbed. The first poet of Iran died in a garden, and rose again every time someone opened the book.

而這是菲爾多西未曾活到看見的結局。當地的教士，認為他不夠正統，拒絕讓他葬入穆斯林的墓園。於是他的遺體被安放於他自己的花園——在樹木與水之間，在圖斯的一角——一座樸素的墓標記了那個位置。但那本書沒有死。它被抄寫，又一再抄寫，被帶進每一個說波斯語的人家，被朗讀給孩子聽，被背誦在心，被熱愛，直到菲爾多西所試圖拯救的那種語言，變成一種沒有他便無法想像的語言。伊朗活了下來——不是作為一個帝國，不是作為一座王座，不是作為一種信仰，而是作為一首詩。而那位自以為失敗的老詩人，所拯救的，比他書中任何一位君王所做的都多。最後一位君王死於一座磨坊，被劫。伊朗的第一位詩人死於一座花園，而在每一個有人翻開這本書的時刻，再度復生。

THE BOOK OUTLIVES THE KING

書，比君王更長壽

The last king died at a mill, robbed of his rings. The first poet died in a garden, refused a proper grave. And yet it is the poet who won. A thousand years after Ferdowsi laid down his pen, the language he saved is still spoken; the heroes he wrote down are still named; the book he believed had failed is read in every house where Persian is loved. Empires fall. Thrones empty. Temples go dark. But a book, once written and once loved, endures — and through the book, the people who wrote it endure too. This is the last thing the Book of Kings has to tell us: that the truest immortality a human being can have is the kind one gives away, in words, to strangers not yet born.
