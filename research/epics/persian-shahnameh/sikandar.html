SIKANDAR THE JUST

公　正　的　亞　歷　山　大

ALEXANDER, AS IRAN TELLS HIM

In the West he is remembered as the conqueror who wept for more worlds to subdue. In Ferdowsi's book he is something stranger and sadder: a Persian-speaking king who overthrew Iran's last emperor and then ruled the land more wisely than its native sons, who rode to the very edge of the world to seek the Water of Life, and who learned, at that edge, that no king — however great — may drink of it. 在西方，他被記為那個為沒有更多世界可征服而哭泣的征服者。在菲爾多西的書中，他是某種更奇異、也更悲傷的存在：一個說波斯語的君王，他推翻了伊朗末代皇帝，卻以比其本土之子更明智的方式統治那片土地，他策馬奔馳到世界的盡頭去尋找長生之水，而在那盡頭，他學到：沒有哪個君王——無論多麼偉大——配飲那水。

Chapter 1 · 第一章

The Fall of Darius

大流士之傾

The last of the native kings before Sikandar was Darius — Dara in the Persian — a great emperor weakened by treachery within his own court. Ferdowsi tells us that Dara was a just and able king, brought low not by foreign arms alone but by the corruption of his own ministers. When Sikandar crossed into Iran with his Macedonians, Dara met him in battle, and the battles were long and hard; for the Iranian king, though failing, was no coward. But his own generals betrayed him, and the empire cracked along the lines of its own internal rot.

在亞歷山大之前，最後一位本土之王是大流士——波斯語中稱達拉——一位被自己朝廷內的叛賣所削弱的偉大皇帝。菲爾多西告訴我們，達拉是一位公正而能幹的君王，他是被自己臣子的腐敗拉低的，不單是被外族的武力。當亞歷山大率領他的馬其頓人越入伊朗，達拉在戰場上迎他，戰事漫長而艱苦；因為這位伊朗之王，雖然衰退，卻不是懦夫。但他自己的將軍背叛了他，而帝國沿著自身內部腐爛的紋路裂開了。

And here Ferdowsi does something unexpected. In his telling, Sikandar is not a foreign barbarian but a kinsman — descended, on his mother's side, from the very Persian royal house he has come to defeat. The conquest, in the Shahnameh, is almost a homecoming. When Dara lies dying, stabbed by his own traitorous captains, Sikandar comes to him and weeps over his body; he hunts down the assassins and executes them; he marries Dara's daughter; and he takes up the throne of Iran not as a thief but as an heir, ruling by Persian law, in Persian dress, with Persian counsellors. The foreign conqueror becomes the legitimate king.

而在這裡，菲爾多西做了一件出人意料之事。在他的敘述中，亞歷山大不是外族蠻夷，而是一個親戚——從他母親一方算起，正是他所來擊敗的那個波斯王室之後。在《列王紀》中，這場征服幾乎是一場歸鄉。當達拉躺在地上將死、被自己叛變的統領所刺之時，亞歷山大來到他身邊，對著他的屍體流淚；他追捕刺客並將之處決；他娶了達拉的女兒；他坐上伊朗的王座，不是作為一個盜賊，而是作為一個繼承人，以波斯之法、著波斯之衣、用波斯之臣來統治。外族的征服者成了合法的君王。

This is one of the great gifts of the Persian telling of Alexander: it refuses the simple story of victor and vanquished, and insists instead on continuity. The throne does not fall to a stranger; it passes, painfully, to a man who will honour it. And Ferdowsi gives Sikandar a quality the Western tradition largely withholds: justice. The conqueror who wept for worlds is, in Iran, the king who ruled well, who listened to the poor, who judged fairly. The point is not to flatter a foreigner; the point is to say that kingship belongs to whoever serves the people, regardless of where he was born.

這是波斯版亞歷山大傳奇的一大恩賜：它拒絕了勝者與敗者的簡單故事，轉而堅持延續性。王座並非落於一個陌生人；它是痛苦地，傳給了一個會尊崇它的人。而菲爾多西賦予了亞歷山大一種西方傳統大體上吝於給予的特質：公正。那個為世界而哭的征服者，在伊朗，是那位統治得當、傾聽貧者、判案公允的君王。其重點不在於奉承一個外族人；而在於說：王權屬於任何服務人民之人，無論他生於何處。

Chapter 2 · 第二章

The Road to the Water of Life

通往長生之水的路

But kingship was not enough for Sikandar. He had conquered the known world; he had ruled it well; and yet a hunger remained, the hunger that has ruined every great king the Shahnameh has shown us. He heard, in some distant telling, of a spring hidden at the edge of the world — the Water of Life, the fountain of immortality, whose drinker would never die. And the king, who had everything, decided that the one thing he lacked was forever. He gathered his host and rode east, into lands no map had marked, toward the fountain that no king had ever found.

但王權之於亞歷山大並不足夠。他征服了已知的世界；他統治得當；然而一種飢渴仍在，那種毀掉《列王紀》所展現的每一位偉大君王的飢渴。他在某個遙遠的傳述中聽聞，世界盡頭藏著一處泉——長生之水，永生之泉，飲者永不死。而那個擁有一切的君王，決定他所缺的唯一之物便是永遠。他召集大軍，策馬向東，進入沒有地圖標記的土地，向著從未有君王找到過的那處泉水而去。

The journey was long and strange. Ferdowsi fills it with wonders — talking trees that prophesied; cities of women who needed no men; a kingdom of the virtuous poor who owned nothing and wanted nothing and pitied the king his gold. And in each wonder, the poem slips in the same quiet lesson, the one Sikandar will not yet hear: that the world is full of people who have what he is seeking, and they got it by not seeking it. The virtuous poor are immortal in their contentment; the women of the city need no conquest to be complete; the trees know the future the king cannot prevent. The Water of Life is everywhere except where he is riding.

旅途漫長而奇異。菲爾多西在其中填滿奇觀——會預言的會說話的樹；不需要男人的女子之城；一群有德之貧者的國度，他們一無所有、一無所求，反而可憐那君王擁有黃金。而在每一個奇觀中，詩都悄悄塞進同一個寧靜的教訓，那個亞歷山大尚聽不見的：世界充滿了擁有他所尋之物的人，而他們之所以得到它，正是因為不去追尋它。那有德之貧者，在其知足中已是永生；那城中的女子，無需征服便是完整的；那些樹知曉君王無法阻止的未來。長生之水無處不在，唯獨不在他策馬奔去之處。

And at last they came to the Land of Darkness — a place where the sun did not reach, a blackness so thick that torches died in it. Into this darkness Sikandar sent his most trusted companion, the wise Khizr, with instructions to find the spring. Khizr went, and in the dark he found it — a clear pool, a sweet fountain — and he drank, and became immortal, and vanished from history into legend. But Sikandar himself, searching nearby, missed it. He rode past the very spring he sought, in the dark, and did not know. And so the king who had everything lost the one thing he wanted most — not because it was hidden, but because it was right there, and he was looking too hard to see it.

終於他們來到了黑暗之境——一個太陽照不到的地方，一種厚得連火炬在其中都會熄滅的黑。亞歷山大派了他最信任的同伴、智慧的赫齊爾，進入這片黑暗去尋那泉水。赫齊爾去了，而在暗中他找到了它——一池清水，一處甘泉——他飲了，成了永生者，從歷史中消失，遁入傳說。但亞歷山大自己，在附近搜尋，卻錯過了它。他騎馬經過他所尋的那處泉水，在暗中，而他並不知道。於是那個擁有一切的君王，失去了他最想要的那唯一之物——不是因為它被藏起來，而是因為它就在那裡，而他尋找得太用力，反而看不見。

Chapter 3 · 第三章

At the Edge of the World

在世界盡頭

Onward Sikandar rode, past the darkness, to the very end of the earth — and there the sea stopped him. He had reached the place where the land ran out and there was nothing left to conquer. Ferdowsi gives him, at this moment, one of the poem's quietest and most famous images: the king climbs a high mountain at the edge of the sea, and at its peak he finds a single old man, sitting alone. And the old man speaks to him a truth no army can defeat.

亞歷山大繼續策馬，越過黑暗，直到大地的盡頭——而在那裡，海攔住了他。他到達了陸地耗盡、再無物可征服的地方。菲爾多西在此刻給了他，全詩中最寧靜也最著名的意象之一：君王攀上濱海的一座高山，而在山巔，他看見一個老人，獨坐。而那老人對他說出一句任何軍隊都無法擊敗的真理。

"You have conquered the world," the old man said (or so the poem has it), "and yet look at how little of it you have truly held. You have ridden across it; you have not possessed it. The land you crossed is still the land of those who lived on it before you came. The gold you took will belong to someone else the day after you die. And you, who could not find the Water of Life, will die as every king has died — and the world will not notice." And he showed Sikandar a skull, set upon a pole, with a little gold inside — and said, "This was a king, too. He thought the gold would follow him. It did not."

「你征服了世界，」老人說（或詩中如此相傳），「然而看看你真正握住了其中多少。你騎馬穿過了它；你並未擁有它。你所經過的土地，仍是你來之前住在那裡之人的土地。你所取的黃金，在你死後那日便將屬於他人。而你，那找不到長生之水的人，將如每一位君王那般死去——而世界不會察覺。」他給亞歷山大看一個骷髏，立在一根桿上，裡面有一點金——並說：「這也曾是一位君王。他以為黃金會跟著他。它沒有。」

And Sikandar, hearing this, was finally changed. He turned around. He began the long ride home. And the poem tells us, with no fanfare, that from that day forward he ruled with a new gentleness, a new awareness that his power had limits — that he himself was mortal, that the world would outlast him, that the only true conquest was the conquest of the hunger that had driven him to the world's edge in the first place. This is the Shahnameh's deep teaching about power, spoken not to tyrants but to the best of kings: even the just must learn to stop. Even the wise must learn that there is an edge, and that the wise thing to do at the edge is turn around.

亞歷山大聽了，終於改變了。他轉身了。他開始漫長的歸途。而詩中告訴我們，毫無喧闊地，從那天起，他以一種新的溫和、一種新的覺察來統治——覺察到他的力量是有盡的——他自己是會死的，世界會比他長壽，唯一真正的征服，是征服那最初將他驅趕到世界盡頭的飢渴。這是《列王紀》關於權力的深刻教誨，它不是對暴君說的，而是對最好的君王說的：即使是公正者，也必須學會停止。即使是智者，也必須學會有一個盡頭，而在盡頭，明智之事是轉身。

Chapter 4 · 第四章

The Death of Sikandar

亞歷山大之死

He did not, in the end, die in battle, as a hero might wish. Ferdowsi gives him a quieter, crueler death: sickness. After all his conquests, after the journey to the world's end, Sikandar fell ill — some say poisoned, some say by fever — and wasted, day by day, the greatest king of his age reduced to a man who could not lift his head from a pillow. The conqueror of nations could not conquer a fever. The seeker of the Water of Life died of thirst, in the end, of the ordinary kind.

他最終並非如英雄所願死於戰場。菲爾多西給了他一種更安靜、也更殘忍的死：疾病。在所有的征服之後，在世界盡頭的旅程之後，亞歷山大病了——有人說是中毒，有人說是發熱——日復一日地消瘦，他那個時代最偉大的君王，淪為一個無法將頭從枕上抬起的人。征服列國者，征服不了一場熱病。尋求長生之水的人，終究死於渴——那最平凡的一種。

And Ferdowsi gives him, on his deathbed, a final scene that the West rarely tells. The dying king called his generals around him and asked them to carry his coffin through the streets with his hands hanging loose outside it, uncovered, empty — so that the people might see that the man who had grasped the whole world was leaving it with nothing in his hands. "Let them see," he said, "that a king takes with him exactly what a beggar takes." The generals did as he asked; the coffin passed through Babylon; and the people wept, understanding at last what the old man at the world's edge had tried to tell their king.

而菲爾多西在他臨終之榻，給了他一個西方少有講述的最後場景。垂死的君王召集了他的將軍們，請他們將他的棺木抬過街道，讓他的雙手垂在棺外，不遮蓋，空空的——好讓百姓看見，那個緊握了整個世界的人，離開時兩手空空。「讓他們看見，」他說，「一個君王帶走的，正和一個乞丐帶走的一模一樣。」將軍們照他的話做了；棺木穿過巴比倫；百姓流淚了，終於明白了世界盡頭那老人曾試圖告訴他們君王的話。

His empire, of course, did not survive him by a generation; his generals divided it among themselves and fought over the pieces. The throne he had won passed away. But the lesson he learned at the edge — and taught, in death, to his people — stayed. This is what the Shahnameh asks of even its greatest: that they learn, before the end, the limits of their own greatness; that they go gently, hands open, having understood that the only kingdom worth keeping is the one inside a man who knows he must leave the rest behind. Sikandar, the just king, the failed seeker, the man who turned around — dies well, because he died understanding.

他的帝國，當然，在他身後連一個世代都未能撐過；他的將軍們瓜分了它，為那碎塊而戰。他所贏得的王座消逝了。但他在盡頭所學的那一課——以及他臨終之時教給他子民的那一課——留了下來。這便是《列王紀》對即使是最偉大之人的要求：他們要在終局之前，學會自身偉大的極限；他們要溫柔地離去，雙手張開，明白唯一值得保留的王國，是存在於一個知道自己必須把其餘一切留在身後之人心中的那個。亞歷山大，公正之王，失敗的求道者，那個轉了身的人——死得其所，因為他死時，是明白的。

Chapter 5 · 第五章

The Sasanians & the Restoration

薩珊王朝與復興

After Sikandar the throne fragmented, and for a long time Iran had no single king — only the squabbling heirs of his generals, the Seleucids and then the Parthian Ashkanians, who ruled from horseback and kept the land together loosely, more by tolerance than by glory. Ferdowsi treats this stretch briefly; it is not his heart. His heart is the next restoration: the Sasanians.

亞歷山大之後，王座碎裂了，很長一段時間伊朗沒有單一的君王——只有他將軍們彼此爭吵的繼承者，塞琉古，然後是安息的阿什康尼王朝，他們在馬背上統治，寬容地、而非以榮光將那片土地鬆散地維繫在一起。菲爾多西對這一段著墨簡略；那不是他的心之所繫。他的心之所繫，是下一場復興：薩珊王朝。

Ardashir was the man who restored it. A descendant of the old royal line, raised in the household of a powerful noble, he rebelled against the last feeble Ashkanian king and won, and crowned himself the first Sasanian emperor. With him came a deliberate, conscious return: the fire-temples were reopened, the old laws restored, the memory of Iran's greatness — of Jamshid, of Fereydun, of Rostam — made official again. The Sasanians understood what Ferdowsi will spend his whole poem arguing: that a people is held together not by armies or taxes, but by the story it tells itself about where it came from. And so they told the old stories, and commissioned the old songs, and gathered the old chronicles — and from those gathered memories, four centuries later, Ferdowsi himself would draw.

阿爾達希爾是復興之人。他是古老王室之後，在一個權貴之家長大，他起兵反抗最後一個虛弱的阿什康尼王，勝了，自封為第一位薩珊皇帝。隨他而來的是一場刻意而自覺的回歸：火祠重開，舊法恢復，伊朗偉大之記憶——賈姆希德的、費雷敦的、羅斯塔姆的——再度成為官方。薩珊王朝明白菲爾多西將以整部詩來辯證的事：一個民族之所以凝聚，靠的不是軍隊或賦稅，而是它對自己從何而來所講述的那個故事。於是他們講述古老的故事，委製古老的歌謠，搜集古老的編年——而從那些被搜集起來的記憶中，四個世紀之後，菲爾多西本人將汲取。

For four hundred years the Sasanians ruled — great kings and middling ones, some cruel, some wise, all of them conscious that they were the keepers of something older than themselves. And Ferdowsi, sketching them here at speed, lets us feel the weight that is about to fall. Because we know — and he knows — how this age ends. The throne will be taken not by a king, but by a wind from the desert: the coming of the Arabs, the coming of Islam, the end of Zoroastrian Iran. The light that Ardashir re-lit will go out. And then the only fire that can keep the old stories alive will be the fire of a single poet's stubbornness — sitting alone, in a town called Tus, writing down everything he can remember, before it is too late. That poet is the subject of our final stage.

薩珊王朝統治了四百年——偉大的君王與平庸的君王，有的殘酷，有的明智，全都意識到他們是某種比自身更古老之物的守護者。而菲爾多西，在此匆匆勾勒他們，讓我們感覺到即將落下的重量。因為我們知道——他也知道——這個時代如何結束。王座將被奪走，不是被一個君王，而是被一陣來自沙漠的風：阿拉伯人的到來，伊斯蘭的到來，祆教伊朗的終結。阿爾達希爾重新點燃的那團火將熄滅。然後，唯一能讓古老故事活下去的火，將是一位詩人孤身一人的固執之火——他獨坐在一個叫圖斯的小鎮，趁還來得及，寫下他所能記起的一切。那位詩人，便是我們最後階段的主題。

THE KING WHO TURNED AROUND

轉身的君王

He conquered the world and ruled it well, and still it was not enough; he rode to the edge of the earth for a fountain that would let him keep it forever — and rode right past it in the dark. An old man at the world's end taught him what no victory could: that a king leaves with empty hands. He died asking that his coffin show it. And after him, the throne was restored, the fire-temples opened again — for a time. The wind from the desert was already rising.
