THE FIRST KINGS

最　初　的　列　王

PISHDADIAN DYNASTY · THE FIRST AGE OF THE WORLD

Before Iran had history, it had memory. Ferdowsi opens his book at the very dawn — when the first man became the first king upon a mountain, clothed in a leopard's skin, and taught his children the two arts that separate human beings from the beasts: to kindle a flame, and to bury their dead. From this single mountain, all kingship descends. 在波斯擁有歷史之前，它擁有的是記憶。菲爾多西在破曉之初展開他的書卷——當第一個人成為第一代君王，登上山巔，身披豹皮，教導他的子民兩項將人與野獸分開的技藝：取火，與埋葬死者。一切王權，皆從這座山降臨人間。

Chapter 1 · 第一章

The Mountain of the First Dawn

第一道曙光之山

In the beginning, says Ferdowsi, there was a mountain — and upon that mountain, the first man. His name was Gayumars, and he was both the father of the human race and its first king; for in those days fatherhood and kingship were one thing, as the sun and its light are one. He did not wear robes of silk, for silk had not yet been woven; he clothed himself in a leopard's skin, and his people gathered around him on the heights, themselves dressed in the skins of the beasts they had taken. They had no swords and no thrones; they had kinship, and a leader, and the high clean air of a world still new.

菲爾多西說，太初之時有一座山——而在那山之上，是第一個人。他的名字叫蓋尤瑪爾斯，他既是人類之父，也是第一代君王；因為在那個時代，父親的身分與王者的身分是同一回事，一如太陽與其光是同一回事。他不穿絲袍，因為絲綢尚未被織造；他身披一襲豹皮，他的子民聚集在他周圍的山巔，同樣穿著他們所獵獲的獸皮。他們沒有刀劍，也沒有王座；他們擁有的是親族、一位首領，以及一個依然嶄新的世界裡那高遠清冽的空氣。

For thirty years Gayumars reigned in peace, and the world was as a garden in spring. He had a son, Siamak, tall as a cypress, and the king loved him as a king loves the kingdom's future made flesh. But there was a shadow in that dawn — a being of malice older than the mountain, the Black Demon, who could not bear that order should rise out of chaos. The demon came to Siamak in the field and struck him down; and when word reached the mountain, Gayumars's grief shook the earth itself. The first father had met the first death, and the world learned, at that hour, that mortality is the price of love.

蓋尤瑪爾斯治世三十年，天下太平，世界如春日之園。他有一子，名喚西亞馬克，高如柏樹，君王愛他，正如君王愛那化為血肉的國之未來。然而在那曙光之中有一道陰影——一個比山更古老的惡意生靈，黑魔，它無法忍受秩序從混沌中升起。惡魔在曠野中向著西亞馬克而來，將他擊殺；當消息傳到山上，蓋尤瑪爾斯的悲慟震動了大地本身。第一個父親遇上了第一場死亡，而世界在那個時辰學會了：必死性，是愛的代價。

Yet from grief came resolve. The whole of creation — the beasts of the field, the very trees — gathered to mourn, and from their mourning rose an army. At the head of it went Hushang, Siamak's son, a boy made into a man by his father's murder. And the grandfather, in his leopard skin, gave him his blessing, and a war began that would never wholly end: the war of the children of light against the dark. This is the first thing the Book of Kings has to tell us — that kingship is born not from conquest, but from a father standing between his children and the thing that would devour them.

然而從悲慟中生出了決心。整個造物——田野的走獸、連樹木本身——都聚集起來哀悼，而從那哀悼之中升起了一支軍隊。領軍的是胡尚，西亞馬克之子，一個被父親之謀殺催熟的少年。而祖父，身披豹皮，為他祝福，於是一場永不止息的戰爭開始了：光之子與黑暗之間的戰爭。這便是《列王紀》要告訴我們的第一件事——王權並非生於征服，而是生於一位父親挺身而出，擋在他子民與那要吞噬他們之物中間。

Chapter 2 · 第二章

Hushang & the Discovery of Fire

胡尚與火的發現

Hushang hunted the Black Demon across the world, and at last he found him, and slew him, and so avenged his father. But Ferdowsi does not linger on the killing — he hurries to the discovery that followed it, for that is the truer gift. One day, walking in the mountains with his hunting-party, Hushang saw a terrible serpent, black as a streak of ink, coiled upon a rock. He raised his hand and hurled a stone at it. The stone missed its mark and struck the rock face; and where the two flints ground together, a spark leapt out, caught the dry grass, and bloomed into flame. The serpent fled. Hushang stood still, staring at the fire he had not meant to make.

胡尚追獵黑魔，穿越世界，終於尋得他、斬殺他，為父報了仇。但菲爾多西並不停留於殺戮之上——他急著敘述隨之而來的發現，因為那才是更真實的禮物。一日，胡尚帶著獵隊行於山中，看見一條可怖的蛇，黑如一道墨痕，盤踞於岩石之上。他揚手擲出一塊石頭。石頭偏了，擊中岩壁；兩塊燧石相磨之處，一顆火星迸射而出，點燃了枯草，綻放成火焰。那蛇逃了。胡尚一動不動地站著，凝視著那團他無意間創造的火。

And the king, says the poem, fell down upon his face and gave thanks — not to the stone, not to the serpent, but to the hidden God whose mercy had placed fire within the rock for humankind to find. "From this stone," he cried, "the light has come; therefore let us keep this night as a feast forever." He ordered his people to gather, and they lit a great fire upon the mountain, and they danced around it; and that night is remembered still — it is the Sadeh festival, the festival of fire, celebrated by Persians three thousand years before Ferdowsi wrote it down and eight hundred years after he did.

詩中說，君王俯伏於地，獻上感恩——不向石頭，不向蛇，而是向那隱祕的上帝，祂的慈悲將火藏於石中，待人發現。「從這塊石頭，」他呼喊，「光來了；所以讓我們將今夜永遠定為節慶。」他下令召集子民，他們在山上點燃一堆大火，圍著它起舞；那一夜至今仍被銘記——那是薩德節，火之節，波斯人在菲爾多西寫下它之前三千年便已慶祝，在菲爾多西寫下它之後八百年仍在慶祝。

From fire, says Ferdowsi, came everything else: the forging of iron, the baking of clay, the cooking of food, the warming of houses through the winter. Hushang did not only discover fire — he taught his people to smelt metal, to till the soil, to irrigate the land. He drew the law that separates the wild from the civilized. A king who begins as an avenger ends as a teacher; and this is the lesson the Book of Kings returns to again and again: a true king leaves his people more capable of life than he found them. The Black Demon was dead. But the flame that was his true victory has never gone out.

菲爾多西說，從火之中，產生了一切：鐵的鍛造、黏土的窯燒、食物的烹煮、屋舍在冬日裡的取暖。胡尚不只發現了火——他教導子民冶煉金屬、耕耘土地、灌溉田畝。他制定了將野蠻與文明分開的律法。一個以復仇者之姿開始的君王，最終成了一位導師；而這正是《列王紀》一再回返的教訓：真正的君王，讓他的子民在離開時比相遇時更有能力活下去。黑魔死了。但那團作為他真正勝利的火焰，從未熄滅。

Chapter 3 · 第三章

Tahmuras Tames the Demons

塔赫穆拉斯馴服群魔

Hushang left the throne to his son Tahmuras, whom men called the Binder of Demons — for he did what no king had done and what many would not believe: he broke the power of the divs, the demons who had served the Black Master, and bent them to his will. Where Hushang had warred upon the dark, Tahmuras made it work for him. He captured a great demon in single combat, and by his authority — the farr, the royal glory that marks a true king — he compelled the creature to teach him the secrets no mortal knew.

胡尚將王位傳給了他的兒子塔赫穆拉斯，世人稱他為「縛魔者」——因為他做到了以前沒有君王做過、許多人也不肯相信的事：他打碎了群魔（迪夫）的力量，那些侍奉黑主之魔，並使它們臣服於他的意志。胡尚曾與黑暗作戰，而塔赫穆拉斯使黑暗為他效勞。他在單挑中生擒了一個大魔，憑藉他的威權——法爾，那標記真正君王的王者之榮——他迫使那生靈教給他凡人所不知的秘密。

From the bound demon, says the poem, Tahmuras learned writing — that strange magic by which a man's voice can cross a thousand years and speak to someone who was not yet born. He learned weaving, the spinning of thread and the making of cloth, so that his people need no longer dress in raw hides. He learned the taming of animals and the keeping of flocks. And, says Ferdowsi with quiet pleasure, he even taught the demons themselves a new trade: he made them carry his litter and grind his grain, so that the very powers of darkness were put to the service of the kitchens of Iran.

詩中說，從那被縛之魔身上，塔赫穆拉斯學會了書寫——那種奇異的魔法，藉由它，一個人的聲音能跨越千年，對一個尚未出生之人說話。他學會了紡織，學會了紡線與織布，使他的子民不必再穿著生獸皮。他學會了馴養動物、畜養畜群。而且，菲爾多西帶著一種淡然的愉悅說道，他甚至教群魔一門新營生：他讓它們抬他的轎子、磨他的穀物，於是黑暗的力量本身，被拿來為伊朗的廚房服役。

For thirty years he reigned, and the land knew prosperity such as it had never seen. And here Ferdowsi plants a seed that will grow into tragedy later in the book: every king, however great, sits upon a throne that the farr may withdraw from. The royal glory is not a possession; it is a presence, and it stays only with the worthy. Tahmuras was worthy; the farr stayed. But his descendants would learn, at terrible cost, that glory once claimed as a right is glory already lost. For now, though, the age is still golden, the demons pull the plough, and writing — that gift of a captive demon — begins its long work of remembering.

他治世三十年，大地繁盛，前所未見。而菲爾多西在此埋下了一顆將在本書後半長成悲劇的種子：每一位君王，無論多麼偉大，都坐於一個法爾可能撤走的王座之上。王者之榮不是一件財產；它是一個臨在，它只與配得上者同在。塔赫穆拉斯配得上；法爾留下了。但他的後代將會以慘痛的代價學到：一旦被當作權利來索取的榮光，便是已經失去的榮光。然而此刻，這個時代仍是黃金的，群魔拉著犁，而書寫——那個被擒之魔的禮物——展開了它漫長的記憶之工。

Chapter 4 · 第四章

Jamshid & the Throne to Heaven

賈姆希德與升天寶座

Jamshid, son of Tahmuras, was the greatest of the first kings, and the most beloved — until he became the most ruined. In his early reign the world reached a perfection it has not seen since. Ferdowsi lists his gifts with wonder: he made armour and weapons of war; he divided the day into hours; he built ships and sailed them; he discovered the working of silk and of perfume; and — most famous of all — he commanded the demons to carry him through the air in a throne of gold, so that on the day of the new-year festival he rose from his palace and flew from dawn to noon, and the whole world looked up and saw their king among the stars.

賈姆希德，塔赫穆拉斯之子，是第一代列王中最偉大、也最受愛戴的——直到他成為最毀滅的。在他統治初期，世界達到了一個此後再未見過的完美。菲爾多西滿懷驚嘆地羅列他的恩賜：他製造了甲冑與兵器；他將一天劃分為時辰；他建造了船隻並航行其上；他發現了絲綢與香水的製法；而最為著名的——他命令群魔在空中抬著一座金色的寶座載他飛行，於是在新年節慶之日，他從宮殿升起，自晨至午飛翔，整個世界仰首，看見他們的君王在群星之間。

For three hundred years, says the poem, Jamshid reigned, and in all that time no one died — for disease had not yet entered the world, and violence was unknown, and the farr clothed the king so brightly that death could not approach. His people multiplied, and the earth gave its fruit without labour. It was the true golden age, the dream that every later king would try and fail to restore. Jamshid had everything a mortal could want. And that, says Ferdowsi, was precisely his undoing.

詩中說，賈姆希德治世三百年，在那段時間裡無人死亡——因為疾病尚未進入世界，暴力亦屬未知，而法爾將君王披照得如此明亮，死亡無法靠近。他的子民繁衍，大地不費辛勞便結出果實。那是真正的黃金時代，是後來每一位君王都會嘗試卻無法恢復的夢。賈姆希德擁有凡人所想要的一切。而菲爾多西說，那正是他毀滅的根源。

One day, drunk on his own glory, Jamshid gathered his court and spoke the words that no king may speak and survive. "I am the source of all splendour," he said. "It is I who have given you fire and silk and the hours of the day. Worship me, for I am more than a king — I am your God." At those words, the farr — that silent presence that had crowned him for three centuries — slipped from him like a garment, and fled. The court fell silent. And in that silence, somewhere out in the deserts of the west, a young chieftain named Zahhak felt a new strength enter him, and began to ride toward Iran. The golden age was over; the serpent-king was coming. This is the central warning of the Shahnameh: glory belongs to the office, never to the man, and the instant a king forgets that, the kingdom is already lost.

一日，沉醉於自己的榮光之中，賈姆希德召集朝臣，說出了任何君王都不可說、說了便不能活的話。「我是一切輝煌之源，」他說，「是火、是絲綢、是時辰的賜予者。崇拜我吧，因為我不僅是君王——我是你們的神。」就在那些話出口的瞬間，法爾——那三個世紀以來為他加冕的無聲臨在——如衣袍般從他身上滑落，逃走了。朝堂陷入沉寂。而在那沉寂之中，西方沙漠的某個地方，一個名叫扎哈克的年輕酋長感到一股新的力量進入他體內，開始向著伊朗策馬而來。黃金時代結束了；蛇肩之王正在逼近。這便是《列王紀》的核心警示：榮光屬於那個位子，從不屬於那個人，而當一個君王忘了這一點的瞬間，王國便已經失去了。

THE GIFT AND THE FALL

恩賜與墜落

The first kings gave us fire, writing, woven cloth, the hours of the day. One of them nearly gave us paradise. But the moment a king reaches for the sky and calls it his own, the sky withdraws — and something worse than death comes walking out of the desert. So ends the first age. So begins the tyranny of serpents.
