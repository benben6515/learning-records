THE DEATH OF SOHRAB

索　拉　布　之　死

THE MOST FAMOUS TRAGEDY IN PERSIAN LITERATURE

Of all the stories in the Shahnameh, this is the one every Persian knows. A boy grows up fatherless and burning to meet the great hero who sired him; he rides to war to find him; and in the fog of combat, neither knows the other. They fight as only equals can. The story does not end well — and Ferdowsi tells it so that we may learn, with the father, the one thing no victory can teach: what it costs to win against the person you love. 在《列王紀》所有故事中，這是每個波斯人都知道的。一個失去父親、渴望見到生身英雄的男孩長大了；他策馬赴戰去尋他；而在戰場的迷霧中，彼此互不相識。他們以唯有勢均力敵者才有的方式搏鬥。故事的結局並不圓滿——而菲爾多西講述它，好讓我們與那位父親一同學到，沒有任何勝利能教的事：打敗你所愛之人，代價為何。

Chapter 1 · 第一章

Rostam in Samangan

薩曼甘之夜的羅斯塔姆

It began, as so many disasters do, with a hunt. Rostam, restless, rode out from Sistan with his great horse Rakhsh to chase wild asses in the steppe; and in the chase, the horse wandered off and was stolen. Furious and afoot, Rostam followed the thief's trail to the neighbouring kingdom of Samangan, where the king received him with great honour, begging the angry hero to spend the night in his palace. Rakhsh, the king promised, would be found by morning. Grudgingly, Rostam agreed.

它的開端，一如許多災禍，始於一場狩獵。羅斯塔姆，躁動不安，騎著他的神駒拉赫什離開錫斯坦，到草原上追獵野驢；而在追逐中，馬走失了、被盜了。憤怒、徒步的羅斯塔姆循著盜賊的足跡來到鄰國薩曼甘，那裡的國王以極大的敬意接待他，懇求這位憤怒的英雄在宮中過夜。拉赫什，國王保證，天明便會找到。羅斯塔姆勉強答應了。

That night the king's daughter came to Rostam's chamber. Her name was Tahmineh, and she was beautiful, and learned, and she had heard the songs of Rostam all her life and loved the hero before she ever saw him. She came openly, with no shame, and spoke plainly: "I have come for a child by you, for they say no man like you will ever live again, and I would bear the world a son as great as his father." Rostam, moved and astonished, agreed — but first he made her swear a vow: if the child were a son, she must raise him in secret, and must not tell him who his father was until the boy had proven himself. For Rostam knew his own life was a storm, and he would not have a son drawn into it before his time.

那夜，國王的女兒來到羅斯塔姆的寢室。她的名字是塔赫米內，她美麗、博學，她終生聽聞羅斯塔姆的歌，在見到他之前便已愛上了這位英雄。她坦然而來，毫無羞愧，直白地說：「我來，是為了向你求一個孩子，因為他們說像你這樣的人再也不會有了，我願為這世界生一個與其父一樣偉大的兒子。」羅斯塔姆，感動而驚訝，答應了——但他先要她立下一個誓：若生的是兒子，她必須秘密撫養他，在孩子證明自己之前，不可告訴他父親是誰。因為羅斯塔姆知道自己的一生是一場風暴，他不願兒子在時候未到之前被捲入其中。

He gave her an onyx amulet from his own arm, to be kept for the son, as a token. Then, when Rakhsh was found in the morning, Rostam mounted and rode home to Sistan, and the matter, as far as he knew, was closed. He had done what men do, and thought little of it. He did not know — could not know — that the seed of that single night would grow into the worst grief of his long life, and that the onyx he had given as a trifle would be the thing that broke his heart. Ferdowsi sets the trap with terrible patience: the tragedy is already born, in a comfortable palace, in a willing bed, while the father rides away believing he has done nothing of consequence.

他從自己臂上取下一枚縞瑪瑙護身符給她，為那個兒子保管，作為信物。然後，天明拉赫什被找到，羅斯塔姆翻身上馬，回到了錫斯坦的家，事情，就他所知，便了結了。他做了男人所做之事，並未多想。他不知道——也不可能知道——那一夜的種子將長成他漫長一生中最深的悲慟，而那枚他隨手給出的縞瑪瑙，將成為擊碎他心臟的東西。菲爾多西以可怖的耐心佈下陷阱：悲劇已經誕生了，在一座舒適的宮殿裡、在一張兩廂情願的床榻上，而那位父親騎馬離去，相信自己什麼要緊的事也沒做。

Chapter 2 · 第二章

The Boy Who Sought His Father

尋父的少年

The child was a son, and Tahmineh named him Sohrab, which means "one born of red dawn," for his beauty was like the sunrise. He grew faster than any boy the world had seen: by the time other children were learning to walk, Sohrab was wrestling foals to the ground; by the time they were playing with toys, he could lift a grown man off his feet. His mother told him, as he grew, only the outline of the truth — that his father was the greatest hero of Iran, named Rostam, and that one day he would go and find him.

孩子是個兒子，塔赫米內給他取名索拉布，意為「生於紅曙之人」，因為他的美如日出。他以世上前所未見的速度成長：當別的孩子在學走路之時，索拉布已能把馬駒摔倒在地；當他們在玩玩具之時，他能將一個成年男子舉離地面。隨著他長大，母親只告訴他真相的輪廓——他的父親是伊朗最偉大的英雄，名叫羅斯塔姆，有朝一日他會去找到他。

And the boy burned to do it. He did not want to wait, as Rostam had asked; he wanted to ride to Iran at once, find his father, and together — so ran his dream — they would overthrow the king of Iran and place Rostam on the throne, and Sohrab would be crown prince, and the whole world would know that the father had a son worthy of him. It was a young man's dream, simple and shining, and Ferdowsi lets us see its beauty even as he shows us its doom: the boy wants only to be loved and recognized by his father, and he has chosen, to win that love, the one road that will make it impossible — the road of war.

而那少年為此燃燒。他不願如羅斯塔姆所求那樣等待；他要立刻策馬前往伊朗，找到他的父親，然後一起——他的夢是這樣——他們將推翻伊朗之王，將羅斯塔姆扶上王座，而索拉布將成為太子，全世界都會知道，那位父親有一個配得上他的兒子。這是一個年輕人的夢，單純而閃亮，菲爾多西讓我們看見它的美，同時也讓我們看見它的毀滅：這少年要的不過是被父親所愛、所認可，而為了贏得那份愛，他選了唯一一條將使之成為不可能的路——戰爭之路。

So Sohrab gathered an army from Turan, the enemy land across the river, and rode west at its head, toward Iran. Tahmineh wept and tried to stop him, foreseeing what her woman's heart could see and her son's young eyes could not. She made him take the onyx amulet, the one Rostam had left — "wear it always," she said, "and if you meet your father, show it him, that he may know you." Sohrab laughed, confident, sure that the meeting would be joyful. He put the amulet under his armour, against his heart, and rode out. The trap, set so quietly in a palace bedroom, was now an army on the march.

於是索拉布從敵國突厥——河對岸的敵地——召集了一支軍隊，率軍西行，向著伊朗。塔赫米內流淚，試圖阻止他，她以女人的心預見了兒子年輕的眼所看不見的。她讓他帶上那枚縞瑪瑙護身符，羅斯塔姆留下的那枚——「永遠戴著它，」她說，「若你遇見你的父親，拿給他看，讓他認得你。」索拉布笑了，自信滿滿，確信那相見將是歡喜的。他把護身符放在鎧甲之下、貼著心，策馬而出。那個在一座宮殿臥房裡悄然佈下的陷阱，如今是一支行進中的軍隊。

Chapter 3 · 第三章

The Two Armies Meet

兩軍相遇

Word came to the court of Iran that a vast Turanian host had crossed the border, led by a young champion no one had ever seen, who fought like a demon and asked, after every victory, the same question of his captives: "Tell me — is Rostam among the Iranian army yet?" For the boy was searching. Every general he defeated, he hoped would be the last barrier between him and his father; every day without Rostam was a day wasted. The court of Iran grew alarmed — not at the army, which they could match, but at this unknown youth who fought as if the war were a personal errand.

消息傳到伊朗朝廷：一支龐大的突厥軍隊越過了邊境，由一個前所未見的年輕勇士率領，他戰如魔鬼，而每一次勝利之後，都向俘虜問同一個問題：「告訴我——羅斯塔姆在伊朗軍中了嗎？」因為那少年在搜尋。他擊敗的每一個將軍，他都希望是與他父親之間最後的屏障；沒有羅斯塔姆的每一天，都是被虛擲的一天。伊朗朝廷警覺起來——不是為那支他們尚能匹敵的軍隊，而是為這個作戰恍如一樁私人事務的無名少年。

Rostam, as was his way, was not at court when the news came; he was at home in Sistan. King Kavus sent for him, and the hero came — but slowly, in his own time, refusing to be hurried, refusing even to ride in the vanguard. Ferdowsi is careful to show us Rostam's dignity here, and also his fatal independence: the hero does not announce himself. He joins the army in its camp, an old champion among younger men, unrecognized by the enemy who is looking for him. And this is the cruelest detail of all: Sohrab is searching for Rostam everywhere, and Rostam is right there — but no one tells the boy, because no one knows that the boy is looking for his father. They think he is looking for a champion to defeat, for glory, for the usual reasons young warriors fight.

羅斯塔姆，按他的習性，消息傳來時並不在朝廷；他在錫斯坦的家中。卡烏斯王派人去請，英雄來了——但來得遲緩，按他自己的時間，不肯被催促，甚至不肯在先鋒中行軍。菲爾多西在此精心地向我們展示羅斯塔姆的尊嚴，也展示他那致命的獨立：這位英雄並不宣揚自己。他在營中加入軍隊，一個年輕人之中的老將，不被那個正在尋找他的敵人所識。而這是最殘忍的細節：索拉布到處在找羅斯塔姆，而羅斯塔姆就在那裡——但沒有人告訴那少年，因為沒有人知道這少年是在找他的父親。他們以為他在找一個要擊敗的勇士、找榮耀、找年輕戰士打仗的那些常見理由。

And so the armies drew up, and a challenge went out for single combat — champion against champion, to decide the war without a general slaughter. The Turanians named their champion: Sohrab. And the Iranians, looking among themselves, saw only one man who could possibly face him. Rostam stepped forward. He did not know, as he looked across the field at the tall, beautiful youth in the enemy's armour, that he was looking at his own face made young again. He saw only an enemy. The tragedy, Ferdowsi tells us, lives in the gap between what is true and what is known; and that gap is the cruelest country in the world.

於是兩軍列陣，一項單挑的挑戰發出——勇士對勇士，以一場決鬥決定戰爭，免於全軍屠戮。突厥方面點名了他們的勇士：索拉布。而伊朗方面，環顧自身，只見到一個人有可能面對他。羅斯塔姆走了出來。當他隔著戰場望向敵軍鎧甲中那個高挑、美麗的少年之時，他不知道，他正望著自己那張變得年輕的臉。他看見的只是一個敵人。菲爾多西告訴我們，悲劇活在真實與已知之間的縫隙裡；而那道縫隙，是世界上最殘忍的國度。

Chapter 4 · 第四章

The First Combat

第一場決鬥

They met on the field between the armies, and at the first sight of each other something passed between them — a strangeness, a pull, that neither understood. Sohrab looked at the weathered old champion across from him and felt a love he could not name; Rostam looked at the magnificent youth and felt, against all custom, a reluctance to strike. But the protocols of war were set, and a warrior does not refuse a combat because of a feeling. They closed.

他們在兩軍之間的戰場上相遇，初見彼此之時，有某種東西在他們之間流過——一種陌生，一種牽引，兩人都不明白。索拉布望著對面那飽經風霜的老將，感到一種無以名之的愛；羅斯塔姆望著那英挺的少年，違反一切慣例地，感到一種不願出手的遲疑。但戰爭的禮儀已定，一個勇士不會因為一個感覺而拒絕一場決鬥。他們逼近。

And Ferdowsi, in some of the most celebrated lines in the Persian language, tells us what happened: they fought as equals. The young man's strength was enormous, fresh, almost reckless; the older man's was tempered, deep, and cunning. Sohrab threw Rostam to the ground — a thing few had ever done — and could have killed him then; but he held back, wishing to spare the famous champion, hoping perhaps to take him alive and ask him where Rostam truly was. Rostam escaped. They wrestled and struck until the field was dark with their sweat, and the watching armies forgot their enmity in sheer awe at the spectacle. Sohrab, during a pause, asked the old man his name. "I am Rostam," said the hero — and the boy's heart leapt. But then the Iranians, fearing a trick, shouted that it was a lie, that this was only some minor captain hiding behind the great name. And Sohrab, hearing the denial, believed the denial — and the moment of recognition slipped away, unclaimed.

而菲爾多西，以波斯語中最著名的那些詩行，告訴我們發生了什麼：他們如勢均力敵者般搏鬥。那少年的力量巨大、新鮮、近乎魯莽；老者的力量則是淬鍊過的、深沉的、狡黠的。索拉布將羅斯塔姆摔在地上——這是少有人做到的事——本可當場殺了他；但他收了手，想饒過這位名將，或許希望將他生擒，問他羅斯塔姆究竟在哪裡。羅斯塔姆脫身了。他們扭打、出擊，直到戰場因他們的汗水而發暗，觀戰的兩軍在對那場面的純然敬畏中忘記了彼此的敵意。在一次暫歇中，索拉布問老人的名字。「我是羅斯塔姆，」英雄說——而少年的心一跳。但隨即伊朗方面，害怕這是計謀，大喊那是謊言，這只是某個小統領躲在偉大的名字之後。索拉布聽了否認，便相信了否認——而那相認的一刻，就這樣滑走了，無人認領。

This is the hinge of the whole tragedy. Ferdowsi makes us feel it like a door slammed by the wind: recognition was offered, openly, and rejected — not by malice, but by the confusion of armies and the mistrust of youth. Sohrab believed Rostam was still elsewhere, still to be found; Rostam, relieved that the boy had not pressed, assumed the matter settled. They agreed to fight again the next day. The night between the two combats is the loneliest in the poem: father and son, a field apart, each preparing to kill the other, each believing the other a stranger.

這便是整個悲劇的鉸鏈。菲爾多西讓我們感覺到它，像一扇被風甩上的門：相認曾被公開地獻上，又被拒絕了——不是出於惡意，而是出於軍隊的混亂與年少的疑心。索拉布相信羅斯塔姆仍在別處，仍待尋找；羅斯塔姆，慶幸少年沒有追問，便以為事情了結了。他們約定次日再戰。兩場決鬥之間的那一夜，是詩中最孤獨的一夜：父與子，隔著一片戰場，各自準備著殺死對方，各自相信對方是個陌生人。

Chapter 5 · 第五章

The Fatal Blow

致命的一擊

The next day they met again, and now Sohrab fought without the hope that had softened him — he believed the old man's name to be a lie, and so fought him as a mere enemy, and a formidable one. The combat was more terrible than the first. Sohrab was young and tireless; Rostam was older, and tiring. The boy pressed him hard, and at one moment hurled him down and sat upon his chest, and reached for his knife to end it. Rostam, in that extremity, did what heroes in extremity do: he reached for the one weapon left to him, which was his cunning.

次日他們再度相遇，而此刻索拉布的戰鬥已沒有昨夜軟化他的那份希望——他相信老人的名字是個謊言，於是只把他當作一個敵人、一個可畏的敵人來戰。這場決鬥比第一場更可怖。索拉布年輕而不知疲倦；羅斯塔姆年老，漸漸力乏。少年逼得他極緊，一度將他擲倒，跨坐於他胸口，伸手去取刀了結。羅斯塔姆在那絕境之中，做了絕境中的英雄所做之事：他伸向留給他的最後一件武器，那就是他的狡黠。

He spoke a formula that, in the old warrior's code, was held to be binding: he claimed that the laws of single combat forbade a champion to be killed while held down — that Sohrab must let him rise and fight again, or else forfeit all honour. It was a trick, a desperate stratagem, and Ferdowsi does not pretend otherwise; the poem tells us plainly that Rostam lied. But the young man, brought up to honour the warrior's code above his own life, hesitated — and in that hesitation, let the old man up. Rostam rose. And drawing on something deeper than muscle — the stored strength of a hundred battles, the fierce will not to die — he seized the boy, lifted him, and brought him down upon his own knee with such force that Sohrab's side burst open. The youth fell. He did not rise.

他念出一句套語，在古老的戰士之規中，那被視為有約束力：他宣稱單挑之法禁止勇士在被壓制之時被殺——索拉布必須放他起身再戰，否則便喪失一切榮譽。那是一個計謀，一個絕望的策略，菲爾多西並不假裝不然；詩中明明白白地告訴我們，羅斯塔姆說了謊。但那年輕人，自幼被教養得將戰士之規置於自己性命之上，猶豫了——而在那猶豫之中，放老人起身。羅斯塔姆站起來了。然後汲取一種比肌肉更深之物——百戰所蓄之力、那不肯死去的凜冽意志——他抓住少年，將他舉起，以如此之力將他摔在自己膝上，致使索拉布的脅下迸裂。少年倒下了。他沒有再起來。

Ferdowsi does not soften the moment. He lets it land. The youth lay in the dust, dying not of weakness but of his own honour — killed by the very code he had been raised to obey. And Rostam, standing over him, felt no triumph; only a strange, cold emptiness, as if the victory had cost him something he could not yet name. He had won. He had won as he always won. And yet, for the first time in his life, the great champion of Iran wished he had lost.

菲爾多西沒有軟化這一刻。他讓它落下。少年臥於塵土之中，將死，不是死於虛弱，而是死於他自己的榮譽——被他自幼被教養去遵從的那套法規所殺。而羅斯塔姆，站在他上方，沒有感到絲毫勝利的喜悅；只有一種奇異的、冰冷的空虛，彷彿這場勝利奪去了他某種尚無法名狀的東西。他贏了。他像以往一樣贏了。然而，這是伊朗的大英雄一生中頭一回，他希望自己輸了。

Chapter 6 · 第六章

The Recognition

相認

As Sohrab lay dying, he spoke — not in anger, but in a kind of weary warning. "You have won," he said, "but you will not long enjoy your victory. For I will tell you who it is you have killed: the son of Rostam. And when my father hears of this, he will tear the world apart to find you — and you will know, then, the grief you have given him." At the word "son," Rostam went cold. He begged the boy to say it was not so. And Sohrab, with his last strength, drew from beneath his armour the onyx amulet — the one Rostam had given Tahmineh in a palace bedroom a lifetime ago — and held it up.

索拉布將死之際，他開口了——不是出於憤怒，而是帶著一種疲憊的警告。「你贏了，」他說，「但你享受這場勝利不會太久。因為我要告訴你，你殺的是誰：羅斯塔姆之子。當我父親聽聞此事，他會撕裂這個世界來找到你——那時你便會知道，你給了他怎樣的悲慟。」聽到「兒子」二字，羅斯塔姆渾身冰冷。他求那少年說那不是真的。而索拉布，用盡最後的力氣，從鎧甲之下抽出那枚縞瑪瑙護身符——那是羅斯塔姆在一個世代之前、在宮殿臥房中交給塔赫米內的那枚——將它舉起。

And Rostam knew. He recognized his own amulet, his own seed, his own face in the dying boy's face. The hero who had slain a thousand champions, who had torn the White Demon apart with his bare hands, fell to the earth beside his son and tore his clothes and put dust on his head and wept as he had never wept. "I am Rostam!" he cried. "I am your father! The man you sought was here all along, and I did not know you, my son, my son!" And Sohrab, with his last breath, answered him gently, forgiving him — "Do not grieve, father. It is written. No man escapes his fate. Lay the blame not on yourself, but on the enmity of our two lands, which set a father and a son against each other in armour. Bury me. And when you return to my mother, tell her that I sought you, and found you, and that in the end we knew each other." Then he died.

而羅斯塔姆知道了。他認出了自己的護身符、自己的骨血、自己那張臉映在那將死少年的臉上。那斬過千員上將、徒手撕裂白魔的英雄，倒在兒子身旁的地上，撕裂衣裳，把塵土撒在頭上，哭了，如他一生所未曾哭過的。「我是羅斯塔姆！」他喊道。「我是你的父親！你所尋找之人一直都在這裡，而我竟不認得你，我的兒子，我的兒子！」而索拉布，以最後一口氣，溫柔地回答他，原諒了他——「不要悲慟，父親。這是註定的。無人能逃自己的命運。不要將責怪加於你自身，而要加在我們兩國的敵意之上，是它讓一對父子披著鎧甲彼此對立。埋葬我吧。等你回到我母親那裡，告訴她，我尋過你，找到了你，而最終，我們相認了。」然後他死了。

Rostam carried the body home to Sistan. He built a great tomb of black wood for his son, and mourned for years. And Ferdowsi, who has been building to this since the night in Samangan, lets the lesson land without commentary, for none is needed: this is what the world is. A father, searching for glory, slept one night in a palace and made a son he never knew; a son, searching for love, rode to war and found his father in the only form the world allows enemies to meet. They fought. The father won. And the father's victory was his ruin. This is the Shahnameh's deepest truth, truer than any of its monsters or its demons: the worst things that happen to us are done not by our enemies, but by ourselves, in ignorance, in the fog, against the very people we would have died to protect.

羅斯塔姆將遺體運回錫斯坦。他為兒子建了一座黑木大墓，哀悼了許多年。而菲爾多西，自薩曼甘的那夜起便一路鋪陳至此，讓這教訓落下，不加評說，因為無需評說：這便是世界的樣子。一位父親，尋求榮耀，在一座宮殿裡睡了一夜，造了一個他從未知曉的兒子；一個兒子，尋求愛，奔赴戰場，卻只在世界容許敵人相遇的形式裡找到了他的父親。他們搏鬥。父親贏了。而父親的勝利，便是他的毀滅。這是《列王紀》最深的真理，比其中任何怪獸或群魔都更深：降臨我們身上最壞的事，不是我們的敵人所為，而是我們自己，在無知之中、在迷霧之中，對著那些我們本願以死相護之人所做的。

WHAT THE FATHER LEARNED

父親所學

A boy sought his father and found him in armour. A father won a battle and lost everything he had. The amulet was there all along, against the boy's heart, and no one thought to look for it until it was too late. This is the tragedy every Persian knows by heart — not because it is unusual, but because it is ordinary: the grief we cause, in ignorance, to the ones we love. The next stages will tell of conquerors and of a poet. But this is the heart of the book.
