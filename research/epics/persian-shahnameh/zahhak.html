ZAHHAK & KAVEH

蛇　肩　暴　君　與　鐵　匠　卡　維

THE SERPENT KING · A THOUSAND YEARS OF DARKNESS

When the farr fled from Jamshid, it left the throne empty — and into the empty throne rode a man whose shoulders bore two living serpents, fed on the brains of the young. This is the story of how an empire fell to a monster, and how, at the very bottom of that long night, a blacksmith raised a leather apron on a spear and made it a flag. 當法爾從賈姆希德身上逃離，王座便空了——而騎馬坐上那空王座的，是一個肩頭長著兩條活蛇、以年輕人的腦髓為食的人。這是一個帝國如何淪落於怪獸之手的故事，也是在那漫漫長夜的最深處，一個鐵匠如何將一條皮圍裙掛上長矛，將它變成一面旗幟的故事。

Chapter 1 · 第一章

The Serpent's Kiss

蛇之吻

Zahhak was the son of an Arab king, and he was ambitious. Into that ambition came a stranger — a man of captivating speech, of dazzling counsel — who was, though Zahhak did not know it, Iblis, the accursed, the whisperer. The stranger bent close and promised the young prince his father's throne, and the kingdom beyond it, and fame that would outlive the mountains. "Only one thing is required," he murmured. "Your father is old. The world waits for the young." And Zahhak, his ears filled with honey, went into his father's chamber and smothered the old king in his sleep.

扎哈克是一位阿拉伯君王之子，而他野心勃勃。在那野心之中，來了一個陌生人——一個言辭迷人、獻計耀眼的人——他其實，儘管扎哈克並不知曉，是伊布利斯，那受詛咒的、蠱惑者。那陌生人湊近，向年輕的王子許下他父親的王座、王座之外的國度，以及比山脈更長壽的名聲。「只缺一件事，」他低語，「你父親老了。世界正等待著年輕人。」而扎哈克，耳中灌滿了蜜，走進父親的寢室，在老王熟睡之時將他悶死。

The throne was his — but Iblis was not finished. He came again, this time disguised as a learned cook, and presented himself at Zahhak's court. He prepared marvellous dishes, and the new king, delighted, asked what reward he wished. "I ask only this," said the cook, "that you permit me to kiss your two shoulders, in token of my devotion." Zahhak, puffed with pride, agreed. And where the stranger's lips touched the royal skin, two black serpents sprang forth, alive and hissing, each with a head of its own and a hunger no earthly food could sate. The cook — that is, Iblis — stroked his beard and vanished.

王位是他的了——但伊布利斯還沒有完。他又來了，這次喬裝成一位博學的廚子，在扎哈克的宮廷現身。他烹調出絕妙的菜餚，新王大悅，問他要什麼賞賜。「我只求一事，」廚子說，「請允許我親吻您的雙肩，作為我效忠的信物。」扎哈克，傲氣沖天，答應了。而當陌生人的嘴唇觸及王者的肌膚之處，兩條黑蛇蹦了出來，活生生、嘶嘶作響，各自有自己的頭，各有一種世上任何食物都無法滿足的飢餓。那廚子——亦即伊布利斯——捋了捋鬍鬚，消失了。

The king's physicians were summoned. They cut the serpents away, and two more grew back in their place. The court magicians tried fire and spells; the serpents only hissed the louder. At last Iblis returned, disguised now as a physician of great age, and pronounced his judgement: the serpents cannot be killed, for they are a part of you now; but they may be fed. "Give them each day the brains of two young men," he said. "It is the only food they will accept." And so the throne that Zahhak had murdered his father to win became a throne fed on the minds of a generation. The price of unchecked ambition, Ferdowsi tells us, is always paid in someone else's blood.

君王的御醫被召來。他們將蛇割去，原處又長出兩條。宮廷術士用火、用咒語；蛇只是嘶鳴得更響。最後伊布利斯又回來了，這次喬裝成一位年高德劭的醫者，宣布了他的判決：這兩條蛇殺不死，因為它們如今是你的一部分；但它們可以被餵養。「每天給它們兩個年輕人的腦髓，」他說，「這是它們唯一肯吃的食物。」於是，扎哈克為奪得而弒父的那個王座，變成了一個以一代人的心智為食的王座。菲爾多西告訴我們：不受節制的野心，其代價永遠是由別人的血來支付的。

Chapter 2 · 第二章

The Fall of Jamshid

賈姆希德的覆亡

With the farr gone from him, Jamshid's hold on Iran began to dissolve. His people, who had once looked up and seen their king flying among the stars, now looked and saw only a frightened old man, and they turned from him. Province after province sent its allegiance to the new force rising in the west — to Zahhak, the strong, the serpent-shouldered, who promised order and delivered terror. Jamshid fled. For a hundred years he wandered the earth, a king without a kingdom, the golden age gone thin and cold inside him like an ember blown upon too long.

法爾既已離他而去，賈姆希德對伊朗的掌控便開始瓦解。他的子民，曾經仰望並看見他們的君王飛翔於群星之間，如今仰望只看見一個驚恐的老人，於是背他而去。一個又一個行省將效忠獻給西方升起的新勢力——獻給扎哈克，強壯的、肩生雙蛇的，他許下秩序，交付的卻是恐怖。賈姆希德逃亡了。一百年他流浪於大地之上，一個沒有王國的君王，黃金時代在他體內變得又薄又冷，像一顆被吹得過久的餘燼。

At last Zahhak's hunters caught him at the edge of the sea, and brought him bound before the serpent-king. And here Ferdowsi pauses, for the moment is heavy with meaning. The greatest of the early kings — the inventor of silk and armour, the master of the flying throne — stood in chains before the monster who had taken his place. Zahhak did not grant him the dignity of a warrior's death. He ordered him sawn in half, lengthwise, between two planks; and Jamshid, the king who had once been a god, died in agony, his blood soaking into the sand of a kingdom he had believed would be his forever.

終於扎哈克的獵手在海邊捉住了他，將他五花大綁帶到蛇肩之王面前。菲爾多西在此停頓，因為這一刻意義深重。第一代列王中最偉大的那位——絲綢與甲冑的發明者，飛行寶座的主宰——戴著鎖鏈站在那個取代了他位置的怪獸面前。扎哈克並未賜予他戰士之死的尊嚴。他下令將他縱向鋸成兩半，夾在兩塊木板之間；而賈姆希德，那曾經為神的君王，在劇痛中死去，他的血浸透了他一直相信會永遠屬於他的那個國度的沙土。

And yet, says the poem, even in ruin Jamshid left behind something Zahhak could never touch. The arts he had given to humankind remained — the fire, the silk, the woven cloth, the measured hours. A king may fall, and his throne be taken by a monster, and his body be destroyed in shame; but what he gave to the world, that no tyrant can unmake. Zahhak sat upon Jamshid's throne, and the serpents upon his shoulders hissed with satisfaction — and did not know that already, somewhere, the line that would bring him down was being born.

然而詩中說，即使在覆亡之中，賈姆希德仍留下了扎哈克永遠無法觸及的東西。他賜給人類的技藝留存了下來——火、絲綢、織布、丈量的時辰。一個君王可能傾覆，他的王座可能被怪獸奪去，他的身軀可能在屈辱中被毀；但他給予世界的，沒有哪個暴君能將之收回。扎哈克坐上了賈姆希德的王座，肩上的雙蛇嘶嘶作響，滿意極了——卻不知在何處，那將帶來他覆滅的血脈，已經誕生了。

Chapter 3 · 第三章

The Brain-Tax of a Thousand Years

千年的腦髓之稅

Zahhak ruled for a thousand years, says the poem, and they were a thousand years of silence. Each dawn, two young men were taken from their homes — drawn by lot from every village, every street — and led to the king's chamber, where the royal cooks split their skulls and fed the warm brains to the serpents. A thousand years is a long time; Ferdowsi knows the number is too vast to be literal, and uses it to say something truer than a count: that under tyranny, time stops meaning anything at all. One day is the same as a century; the only measure is grief.

詩中說，扎哈克統治了一千年，那是一千年的沉默。每個黎明，兩個年輕人被從家中帶走——以抽籤的方式從每個村莊、每條街道抽出——帶到君王的寢室，在那裡，御廚劈開他們的頭骨，將溫熱的腦髓餵給雙蛇。一千年是很長的時間；菲爾多西知道這個數字大到不可能是字面的，他藉此說出比計數更真實的事：在暴政之下，時間失去了任何意義。一日等同於一個世紀；唯一的度量，是悲慟。

But even in that darkness, Ferdowsi finds the candle of mercy. Two of the king's cooks were men of conscience — Armail and Karmail, their names — and they could not bear the daily slaughter. So they devised a secret: each day, of the two youths condemned, they killed only one, and mixed his brains with the brains of a sheep for the serpents; the other youth they smuggled out of the palace by night, given a little money and a warning to flee to the mountains, never to return. From these spared young men, says the poem, descend the Kurds — a people born of mercy at the heart of cruelty.

但即便在那黑暗之中，菲爾多西仍找得到仁慈的燭光。君王的御廚中有兩個有良知的人——他們名叫阿爾梅爾與卡爾梅爾——他們無法忍受那每日的屠殺。於是他們想出了一個秘密：每天，在兩個被判死的青年之中，他們只殺一個，將他的腦髓與羊腦混在一起餵蛇；另一個青年，他們在夜裡偷偷送出宮去，給他一點錢和一個警告：逃到山裡去，永遠不要回來。詩中說，從這些被饒過的青年，傳下了庫德人——一個誕生於殘忍之心臟中的仁慈的民族。

And so the serpent-king's empire rotted gently from within, fed and fed again by the very cruelty that was meant to make it eternal. The cooks saved a people. The serpents were never satisfied. And somewhere in the hidden mountain-villages, the descendants of the spared multiplied and waited, and told their children the name of the boy-king who would one day come — Fereydun, the son of Abtin, born of the line that Zahhak had tried and failed to kill. Tyranny, Ferdowsi is telling us, always believes it is total; and it is always wrong, because it cannot reach into the kitchen where one person decides to be merciful.

於是蛇肩之王的帝國從內部緩緩腐爛，被那本欲使之永恆的殘忍一遍又一遍地餵養。廚子們拯救了一個民族。雙蛇從不滿足。而在隱密的山村裡，那些被饒過之人的後裔繁衍、等待，告訴他們的孩子那個有朝一日會降臨的少年之王的名字——費雷敦，阿布廷之子，誕生於扎哈克曾試圖殺絕卻未能如願的血脈。菲爾多西在告訴我們：暴政總以為自己是全面的；而它總是錯的，因為它無法鑽進那個廚房——那個有人決定行仁慈之事的廚房。

Chapter 4 · 第四章

The Dream of the Three Kings

三王之夢

The tyrant could not sleep. As the centuries ground on, Zahhak found rest harder and harder to come by; and one night, he dreamed. Three warriors rose up before him — the first was a king in royal robes, and the second bore the face of one who had seen too much, and the third was young, tall, leaning on a great mace whose head was the head of a cow. The young one struck Zahhak upon the head with the mace, and bound him in chains, and dragged him away. The serpent-king woke screaming, the sweat cold on him, his shoulder-serpents hissing in shared terror.

暴君無法入眠。隨著世紀的磨蝕，扎哈克越來越難得到安息；終於有一夜，他做了一個夢。三個武士在他面前升起——第一個是身著王袍的君王，第二個面容是那見過太多之人的臉，第三個年輕、高大，倚著一根大狼牙棒，棒頭是一顆牛頭。那年輕的一個用狼牙棒擊打扎哈克的頭，用鎖鏈捆住他，將他拖走。蛇肩之王尖叫著醒來，冷汗淋漓，肩上的雙蛇共鳴般地嘶嘶作響。

Terrified, Zahhak summoned every dream-interpreter in the realm, but none dared speak the truth — until one, braver than the rest, or perhaps more reckless, told the king plainly: the dream means your doom. The young man with the cow-headed mace is a king yet unborn, of your own royal enemy's blood, and he will be the one to bring you down. His name, the interpreter whispered, is Fereydun. The tyrant flew into a rage and ordered the death of every man of that line — but Fereydun's mother had already fled into the mountains, carrying the child in her womb, and Zahhak's hunters, like all tyrants' hunters, arrived always one night too late.

扎哈克驚恐萬分，召來了國境中每一個解夢者，但無人敢說實話——直到有一個，比其餘人更勇敢，或許也更莽撞，直白地告訴君王：這個夢意味著你的毀滅。那個手持牛頭狼牙棒的年輕人，是一個尚未出生的君王，屬於你的王室宿敵之血脈，他將是那個將你拉下馬的人。他的名字，解夢者低語，是費雷敦。暴君勃然大怒，下令殺盡那一脈的每一個男人——但費雷敦的母親已帶著腹中的孩子逃入了山中，而扎哈克的獵手，一如所有暴君的獵手，總是遲到一夜。

There is a quiet comedy in Ferdowsi's telling of tyrants — the way they chase the very prophecy they fear, and by chasing it, fulfil it. Zahhak, in hunting Fereydun's kin, only spread the boy's fame and hardened his mother's resolve. The dream that was meant to warn the serpent-king became instead a promise to the world. This is one of the deep comforts of the Book of Kings: that the powerful are never as powerful as they imagine, and that the future, which they so frantically try to murder, is already out of their reach. The boy is already growing. The mace is already being forged. The dream is already coming true.

在菲爾多西敘述暴君的方式裡，有一種寧靜的喜劇——他們追逐自己所懼怕的預言，而因追逐，反而應驗了它。扎哈克在追獵費雷敦的親族之時，反而傳揚了那少年的名聲，並使他母親的決心更加堅定。那本欲警告蛇肩之王的夢，反而成了對世界的一個承諾。這是《列王紀》深層的慰藉之一：權勢者從不如他們想像的那般有權勢，而未來——他們如此瘋狂地試圖謀殺的未來——早已在他們觸手之外。那少年已經在長大。那狼牙棒已經在鍛造。那個夢，已經在成真。

Chapter 5 · 第五章

Kaveh the Blacksmith's Revolt

鐵匠卡維的起義

By now Fereydun had grown to manhood in the mountains — strong, just, and aflame with the memory of a kingdom stolen from his line. Zahhak, hearing rumours of the young claimant, grew desperate to win the loyalty of his nobles, and called a great assembly. He offered them titles, lands, and a formal proclamation of his right to rule — a charter of lies, written to make the tyranny look like law. Most of the nobles, cowed, signed it. Among them was a simple blacksmith named Kaveh.

此時費雷敦已在山中長大成人——強壯、公正，心中燃燒著一個被從他血脈中奪走的王國的記憶。扎哈克聽聞那年輕覬覦者的傳聞，急於贏取貴族的忠誠，便召集了一場大會。他賜予他們頭銜、土地，並正式宣告他的統治之權——一份謊言的憲章，寫來讓暴政看起來像法律。多數貴族，被嚇服了，簽下了它。他們之中，有一個名叫卡維的鐵匠。

But Kaveh did not sign. He had had eighteen sons, and the serpents had taken seventeen of them, one by one, year by year, until only the youngest remained. Now the tyrant was asking him, with a parchment and a smile, to bless the very system that had eaten his children. Kaveh rose to his feet. He did not draw a sword, for he had none. He took off his leather blacksmith's apron — the rough skin he wore at his forge, scorched and sweat-stained — and he raised it on the shaft of his spear and held it high above the assembly. "I have no crown to raise," his silence said, "but I have this." And he turned, and walked out of the hall, and the people followed him.

但卡維沒有簽。他有過十八個兒子，雙蛇帶走了十七個，一個接一個，一年又一年，直到只剩最小的那個。如今暴君用一張羊皮紙和一個微笑，要他祝福那個吞噬了他孩子的體制本身。卡維站起身。他沒有拔劍，因為他沒有劍。他脫下了他的皮鐵匠圍裙——他在鍛爐旁穿的那張粗皮，焦灼、汗漬斑斑——將它掛在他的矛桿上，高高舉過大會之上。「我沒有王冠可舉，」他的沉默說道，「但我有這個。」然後他轉身，走出大廳，百姓跟著他走了。

That scorched leather apron became the Derafsh Kaviani — the Standard of Kaveh — and upon it the jewellers of Iran later set rubies and pearls and gold, so that a blacksmith's rough hide was transformed into the battle-flag of a nation. Kaveh marched at the head of the people straight to Fereydun in the mountains, and presented him the standard, and Fereydun took it, and rode down with an army no tyrant could stop. They broke Zahhak's hosts, and the young king struck the serpent-king down with the cow-headed mace just as the dream had foretold — but did not kill him. He bound him in chains, and carried him to the peak of Mount Damavand, and nailed him to the rock, there to scream until the world's end. The thousand years were over. The throne was restored to the line of light. And a blacksmith's apron had begun it all. The deepest teaching of the Shahnameh is here: a people's freedom begins not with a king, but with a working man who refuses, in public, to sign.

那張焦灼的皮圍裙成了「德拉夫什·卡維亞尼」——卡維之旗——伊朗的珠寶匠後來在它上面鑲嵌了紅寶石、珍珠與黃金，於是一個鐵匠的粗皮被轉化為一個民族的戰旗。卡維領著百姓徑直走向山中的費雷敦，將旗幟呈獻給他，費雷敦接過它，率領一支暴君無法阻擋的大軍策馬而下。他們擊破了扎哈克的軍隊，年輕的君王用牛頭狼牙棒擊倒了蛇肩之王，一如夢中所預言——卻沒有殺他。他用鎖鏈捆住他，將他帶到達馬萬德峰頂，釘在岩石上，讓他在那裡尖叫到世界盡頭。一千年結束了。王位歸還給了光之血脈。而一切，是由一個鐵匠的圍裙開始的。《列王紀》最深刻的教誨便在於此：一個民族的自由，並非始於一位君王，而是始於一個勞動之人，在公開場合，拒絕簽字。

THE APRON AND THE FLAG

圍裙與旗幟

A monster sat upon the throne for a thousand years, and was fed the minds of a generation. The cooks spared who they could; the women fled to the mountains; the boy grew up. But the tyrant fell only when one working man, in the public hall, refused to put his name to a lie — and raised his scorched leather to the light. From a blacksmith's apron a nation was reborn. The next age will belong to heroes.
