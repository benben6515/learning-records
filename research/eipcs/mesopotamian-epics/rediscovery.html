THE REDISCOVERY

重　見　天　日

LAYARD · GEORGE SMITH · NINEVEH, 1849–1872 AD

For two thousand years, Gilgamesh was forgotten — his name unreadable, his story buried under the dust of a fallen city. Then, in a single century, men dug him out of the ground, taught themselves to read his strange script, and discovered that the flood in the Bible was not the oldest story after all. 整整兩千年，吉爾伽美什為人遺忘——他的名字無人能讀，他的故事被掩埋在一座傾覆之城的塵土之下。而後，僅僅一個世紀之內，人們將他從地底挖出，自學讀懂他那奇異的文字，並發現聖經裡的洪水，竟不是最古老的故事。

Chapter 1 · 第一章

The City That Swallowed Itself

自我吞噬的城邦

Nineveh, the last great capital of Assyria, stood on the east bank of the Tigris — a city of walls so vast, the ancient boast went, that a man could walk along its top for a full day and not reach the end. For a few centuries it was the largest city the world had ever seen. Then, in 612 BC, a coalition of Medes and Babylonians broke through those walls and burned the city to the ground. The mud-brick palaces collapsed back into the very earth they had been made from; the carved stone guardians sank under wind-blown silt; within a few generations, no one remembered where Nineveh had even been.

亞述最後一座偉大的首都尼尼微，矗立在底格里斯河的東岸——一座城牆如此廣袤的城市，古人的豪語說，一個人沿著它的頂部走上整整一天也走不到盡頭。它曾是世界上前所未見的最大城市。而後，公元前 612 年，米底人與巴比倫人的聯軍突破了那些城牆，將城市焚燒殆盡。泥磚的宮殿塌回它們所由構成的大地本身；雕鑿的石頭守護者沉入風吹的淤泥之下；不出幾代，便再無人記得尼尼微究竟在何處。

The Greek historian Xenophon marched his ten thousand past its mounds in 401 BC and did not know what they were. The locals called the hills by other names — Kuyunjik, Nebi Yunus. They were just two long, low ridges of dirt across the river from Mosul, good for grazing, of no apparent interest. Beneath the feet of shepherds, the entire written memory of a civilization slept — thirty thousand tablets, baked in clay, the most durable writing medium ever invented by man. They could wait. And they did, for two thousand four hundred years, until a restless young Englishman wondered what was inside those hills.

希臘歷史學家色諾芬於公元前 401 年率其一萬大軍行經那些土墩之旁，卻不知它們是什麼。當地人以別的名字稱呼那些山丘——庫雲吉克、內比·尤努斯。它們只是摩蘇爾對岸兩道長而低的土脊，適於放牧，看來毫無趣味。在牧羊人的腳下，一整個文明的全部書面記憶沉睡著——三萬塊泥板，窯燒於黏土之中，那是人類曾發明的最耐久的書寫媒介。它們等得起。它們確實等了，整整兩千四百年，直到一個躁動不安的英國青年好奇那些山丘裡面藏著什麼。

Chapter 2 · 第二章

Layard Digs Up Nineveh

萊亞德挖出尼尼微

Austen Henry Layard was twenty-six years old, a failed lawyer turned wanderer, travelling through the Ottoman Empire on horseback, when he became convinced that the mounds opposite Mosul held something extraordinary. In 1845 he began to dig — at Nimrud first, then at Nineveh itself. Almost at once the spades struck walls. Out of the earth rose the great winged bulls with human heads, the lamassu, that had guarded the gates of kings; out of the earth came relief after relief of lion hunts and sieges and processions, an entire vanished world of stone.

奧斯汀·亨利·萊亞德二十六歲，一個由失敗律師轉為的浪跡旅人，騎馬穿越鄂圖曼帝國之時，深信摩蘇爾對岸的土墩裡藏著某種非凡之物。1845 年他開始挖掘——先在尼姆魯德，繼而在尼尼微本身。幾乎是立刻，鏟子便觸到了牆壁。從地底升起了那些有翼、人首的巨牛，拉瑪蘇，它們曾守護君王之門；從地底湧出一幅又一一幅的浮雕，獵獅、攻城、遊行，一整個消失於時間中的石頭世界。

And mixed in with the sculptures, by the hundreds, by the thousands, were pieces of baked clay — flat, pillow-shaped, covered on every face with dense little wedge-shaped marks. The Arab diggers called them "kinds of stones" and thought little of them. Layard, however, suspected they were writing. He could not read a word of it — no European yet could, the script would not be deciphered for another few years — but he had the instinct to pack them into crates anyway, hundreds and hundreds of crates, and ship them down the Tigris, across the sea, to the British Museum. He did not know that he had just rescued the oldest library on earth.

而與雕塑混在一起的，成百上千，是燒製的黏土碎片——扁平的、枕頭形的，每一面都佈滿密密麻麻、小小的楔形記號。阿拉伯工人稱之為「一種石頭」，對它們不以為意。然而萊亞德懷疑它們是文字。他一個字也讀不出來——當時尚無任何歐洲人能讀，這種文字還要再過幾年才被破譯——但他憑直覺還是把它們裝進箱子，一箱又一箱，順底格里斯河而下，渡海運往大英博物館。他不知道，自己剛剛搶救了地球上最古老的圖書館。

Chapter 3 · 第三章

The King Who Collected Books

收藏書籍的君王

As scholars in London slowly learned to read the wedges — a script called cuneiform, from the Latin for "wedge-shaped" — they realized that these tablets were no random archive. Most of them bore the same royal stamp, the same colophon: the library of Ashurbanipal, king of Assyria, who ruled from 668 to 627 BC. And Ashurbanipal was no ordinary warrior-king. He was, almost uniquely among the rulers of his age, a man who could read and write — who boasted of it on his own tablets — and who had made it his life's work to gather every text in his empire into one collection.

當倫敦的學者們慢慢學會讀懂那些楔形記號——一種被稱為「楔形文字」的書寫，源自拉丁文的「楔形」之意——他們意識到這些泥板並非雜亂的檔案。其中大多數都蓋著同樣的王室印記、同樣的卷尾題記：亞述王亞述巴尼拔的圖書館，他自公元前 668 年至 627 年在位。而亞述巴尼拔並非尋常的戰士之王。在那個時代的統治者之中，他幾乎是唯一一個能讀能寫的人——他在自己的泥板上以此自誇——並畢生致力於將其帝國中所有的文本聚集於一處。

He sent scribes to every temple in Babylonia to copy the old songs, the omen-texts, the medical recipes, the dictionaries, the hymns, the math tablets. He demanded originals by force when he had to. In his palace at Nineveh he assembled perhaps thirty thousand tablets — the first great library in history, organized with care, catalogued, shelved by subject. And because he wrote on clay, and baked it, the library was nearly indestructible. When his palace burned in 612 BC, the fire did not destroy the tablets — it baked them harder, baked them to last. The library of Ashurbanipal is the reason any of this survives. Among those thirty thousand tablets, kept safe in a room that a king built, were the twelve tablets of the Standard Babylonian Gilgamesh, copied by a scribe named, perhaps, Sin-leqi-unninni, two and a half thousand years before the man who would finally read them was even born.

他派遣書吏前往巴比倫尼亞的每一座神廟，抄錄古老的歌謠、占卜之文、醫藥配方、字典、讚美詩、數學泥板。必要時，他以武力索取原件。他在尼尼微的宮殿中聚集了約莫三萬塊泥板——歷史上第一座偉大的圖書館，精心整理、編目、按主題上架。而因為他書寫於黏土之上，並將之窯燒，這座圖書館幾乎不可摧毀。當他的宮殿於公元前 612 年焚毀，那場火並未毀掉泥板——反而將它們燒得更硬，燒得足以長存。亞述巴尼拔的圖書館，正是這一切之所以能存留的原因。在那三萬塊泥板之中，保存在一位君王所建的房間裡，有標準巴比倫語《吉爾伽美什》的十二塊泥板，或許由一位名叫辛·勒奎·烏寧尼的書吏所抄，比那個最終會讀懂它的人的出生，還早了兩千五百年。

Chapter 4 · 第四章

George Smith and the Flood Tablet

喬治·史密斯與洪水泥板

Into this story walks one of the unlikeliest heroes in the history of scholarship: George Smith, born in 1840, the son of a working-class London family, who at fourteen was apprenticed as an engraver of banknotes. Smith could not afford university. But he could walk to the British Museum on his lunch break, and there, behind the glass, were Layard's crates of clay tablets, slowly being sorted. The strange wedges fascinated him. He taught himself to read them — not from a tutor, but from staring at the cases year after year, until, by his early twenties, he had become perhaps the most skilled reader of cuneiform in England. The Museum, recognizing a self-made genius, hired him in 1867.

在這個故事中，走來了學術史上最不可能的英雄之一：喬治·史密斯，1840 年生於倫敦一個勞工階級家庭，十四歲時成為鈔票雕版學徒。史密斯讀不起大學。但他可以在午休時走到大英博物館，在那裡，玻璃櫃後面，是萊亞德一箱箱的泥板，正被慢慢地整理。那些奇異的楔形記號令他著迷。他自學讀懂了它們——不是由老師所教，而是年復一年地凝視著那些展櫃，直到二十出頭之時，他已成為或許是英格蘭最嫻熟的楔形文字讀者。博物館識得這位自學的天才，於 1867 年聘請了他。

And then, in 1872, came the moment. Sorting through a tray of broken tablets one evening, Smith found a fragment that told of a great flood, a boat, a man warned by a god, birds released to find land. His hands shook. The story was unmistakable — and it was not from Genesis. It was Babylonian. It was older than the Bible by a thousand years. The story goes that Smith leapt up, tore off his coat, and began to pace the room in such excitement that onlookers feared for his sanity. On December 3rd, 1872, he read his discovery aloud to the Society of Biblical Archaeology, and the Prime Minister himself, William Gladstone, was in the audience. The oldest story in the world, lost for two thousand years, had just spoken again.

然後，在 1872 年，那一刻來臨了。某個傍晚，史密斯在整理一盤碎泥板時，發現了一塊講述大洪水、一艘船、一個受神示警之人、放出鳥兒尋找陸地的殘片。他的雙手發抖。那個故事 unmistakable——而且它不是出自《創世記》。它是巴比倫的。它比聖經還早了一千年。傳說史密斯跳了起來，扯下外套，開始在房間裡踱步，興奮得令旁觀者都為他的神智擔憂。1872 年 12 月 3 日，他在聖經考古學會上朗讀了他的發現，連首相本人威廉·格萊斯頓都在聽眾席中。世界上最古老的故事，失落了兩千年，剛剛再度開口說話。

Chapter 5 · 第五章

The Expedition Paid for by a Newspaper

由報社出資的遠征

Smith's tablet was broken. Lines were missing — including, tantalizingly, the exact moment the raven fails to return. Smith believed the missing fragments must still lie in the dirt of Nineveh, and he longed to go and find them. But he was a working man without independent means, and excavations cost money that the Museum would not provide. Then something unprecedented happened: a London newspaper, the Daily Telegraph, stepped in and offered a thousand guineas to fund Smith's expedition outright — not out of scholarship, but because the story of a flood older than the Bible was exactly the kind of sensation that sold papers.

史密斯那塊泥板是殘破的。有些行已經缺失——其中誘人地，包括烏鴉不再返回的那個關鍵時刻。史密斯相信那些缺失的碎片一定還埋在尼尼微的泥土裡，他渴望親自去找。但他是一個沒有獨立財源的勞工，而挖掘需要博物館不肯提供的經費。於是，一件前所未有的事發生了：倫敦一家報社，《每日電訊報》，介入其中，主動提供一千堅尼全額資助史密斯的遠征——不是出於學術，而是因為「比聖經更古老的洪水」這個故事，正是那種能賣報紙的轟動話題。

In 1873 Smith sailed for Mosul. He had never excavated before in his life. And yet, against staggering odds, within days of opening trenches at Kuyunjik he found the very fragments he was looking for — pieces that joined his flood tablet and filled its gaps. A man had set out to find a lost sentence of a lost book, in a ruin the size of a city, on another continent, and he had found it within a week. He returned twice more to Mesopotamia, recovering thousands more tablets. On his way home from the third expedition, in 1876, he fell ill with dysentery in Aleppo and died. He was thirty-six years old — the same age, the ancient poem never tells us, that Gilgamesh might have been. The man who had given the flood back to the world did not live to see forty.

1873 年，史密斯啟航前往摩蘇爾。他這輩子從未挖掘過。然而，在極其渺茫的機率之下，他在庫雲吉克開挖壕溝不過數日，便找到了他正在尋找的碎片——那些接合他的洪水泥板、填補其空缺的碎片。一個人出發去尋找一本失落之書的一句失落的話，在一座城大小的廢墟裡，在另一個大陸上，竟然在一週之內找到了。他又兩度回到兩河流域，再尋回數千塊泥板。1876 年，第三次遠征歸途中，他在阿勒颇染上痢疾而死。年僅三十六歲——古詩從未告訴我們，那恰好也可能是吉爾伽美什的年紀。那個把洪水交還給世界的人，未能活到四十歲。

Chapter 6 · 第六章

Older Than Genesis

比創世記更古老

The consequences of what George Smith found radiated outward for a century. The flood in Gilgamesh, tablet eleven, was unmistakably the same story as the flood of Noah — same warning, same boat, same birds, same mountain, same offering — but it had been written down a thousand years before the oldest parts of Genesis. The Bible, it turned out, had not invented these stories from nothing; it had inherited them, transformed them, and folded them into its own theology. This was one of the discoveries that gave birth to modern biblical scholarship, the careful historical study of how a sacred text came to be.

喬治·史密斯所發現之物的影響，向外輻射了整整一個世紀。吉爾伽美什第十一泥板中的洪水，毫無疑問是挪亞洪水的同一個故事——同樣的示警、同樣的方舟、同樣的飛鳥、同樣的高山、同樣的祭獻——但它被寫下的時間，比《創世記》最古老的部分還早了一千年。原來，聖經並非憑空發明了這些故事；它繼承了它們、轉化了它們、將之摺入自己的神學之中。這是催生現代聖經研究的發現之一——那種仔細的歷史研究，探究一部神聖文本是如何形成的。

And it was not only the flood. Enuma Elish, with Marduk splitting the dragon Tiamat to make sky and sea, lit up echoes in Genesis, where God divides the waters above from the waters below — and in the Psalms, where Yahweh crushes the dragon Rahab or Leviathan. The Mesopotamians, it turned out, were not a footnote to the Bible; the Bible was, in part, a footnote to them. Today, thanks to a century more of work — to scholars like Andrew George, whose 2003 critical edition assembled every fragment from every museum — we can read the Epic of Gilgamesh as easily as we read Homer. A king feared death four thousand years ago, had his fear written on clay, and someone dug it up again. That is the only reason you have just read his story. The clay, in the end, won — just as Gilgamesh, looking at his walls, said it would.

而且不只是洪水。《埃努瑪·埃利什》中，馬杜克劈開巨龍提雅瑪特以造天與海，照亮了《創世記》中的回聲——在那裡，上帝將上面的水與下面的水分開——以及《詩篇》之中，雅威擊碎巨龍拉哈伯或利維坦。原來，兩河流域的人並非聖經的註腳；聖經，某種意義上，是他們的註腳。如今，多虧了一個多世紀以來的工作——多虧了像安德魯·喬治這樣的學者，他 2003 年的考訂本將每一座博物館裡的每一塊碎片匯集起來——我們可以像讀荷馬一樣輕易地讀到《吉爾伽美什史詩》。一位君王在四千年前懼怕死亡，將他的恐懼寫在泥上，而有人又將它挖了出來。這正是你剛剛得以讀到他的故事的唯一原因。泥，最終，贏了——正如吉爾伽美什望著他的城牆時所說，它會贏。

THE CLAY REMEMBERED

泥，銘記了一切

And so the journey closes — not in the world of the gods, but in a museum storeroom, in the hands of an engraver who taught himself to read. The oldest story we know survived because it was written on the most patient material in the world. Gilgamesh lost his immortality. But the clay kept it for him.

This is the end of the Mesopotamian path. Return to the start, or look onward to other worlds.
